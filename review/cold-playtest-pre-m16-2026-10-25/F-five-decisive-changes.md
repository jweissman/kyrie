# F. Five decisive changes

These are the five changes that would most improve the experience for someone opening Kyr for the first time. They're ordered by how much of the game each one unlocks.

### 1. Lead the player to a real adventure in the first ten minutes

Make sure a new player hears of a substantial adventure with a route, in town, from a person, within the first conversations. Either:
- offer the authored campaigns (*The Long Man* first, since it's already marked recommended) beside *Begin* on the title page, or
- have the first settlement's folk actually speak of the joined adventure beyond the march.

**Why it matters most:** today the best content is hidden behind `?dev` or past an unmarked edge-stone (C1). Every other improvement is wasted on a player who never finds the good part.

### 2. Make the first expedition survivable, and its failure intelligible

Start or quickly assemble the three-person company the handbook promises. Calibrate the first deep site so an ordinary starting party, playing sensibly, can usually reach its bottom. Fix the defeat account: name all the fallen with the protagonist first, give the true party count, show the final action, and report what Flee actually did (C2, C3).

**Why:** both of my adventures ended at the first real fight. Nothing else in the game — advancement, return, consequence — happens if the party never survives a dungeon.

### 3. Show what each person can do before you commit to them

Show a character sheet from creation onward. Give each calling coherent starting statistics (or let the player assign them). Show spell and ability effects in plain view rather than in hover tooltips. Let the player see a recruit's calling, kit and health before paying (C4).

**Why:** party play is the game's premise, yet I couldn't tell what my mage could do until she was already dead. Being able to plan "who goes and why" turns the fights from coin-flips into decisions.

### 4. Remove the template seams and the empty verbs

- No two people in one place give the same line.
- No placeholder strings in any view.
- Riddles agree with their answers.
- Dramatic objects answer "Listen" and "Look closely" with a specific sensory detail, or don't offer the verb.
- One working pole action instead of two look-alikes (C5, C6).

**Why:** the writing at its best (the tally, the winze, Brother Anselm's letter) is the reason to play. Repetition and "Nothing comes of it" teach the player to stop reading and stop inspecting — the two habits this game most needs.

### 5. Make the world's memory visible on return

When a rival band leaves, add a Notes thread and leave a trace the same day. Have the sponsor speak of them, and deliver their fate as news. Notes should record only what someone actually said, filed as heard rather than seen. The Story should keep real dialogue and survive a reload. "Begin again in this country" should either keep the country or stop claiming it does (C7, C8).

**Why:** the moment the brothers set out without me was the strongest in the game. It was followed by four days of silence. Kyr's distinctive promise is a world that changes and remembers, and the player has to *see* that happen for it to count.

---

**Why these five and not others.** The economy (C9), map duplicates (C10), small state contradictions (C11) and the atlas's tactical-zone work are real, but each of them affects a player who has *already* found an adventure, built a company, survived a dungeon, trusted the text and returned home. These five clear that path in order.
