# The High City: nine roads to the throne

**27 November 2026. Design chapter, not implemented succession rules or placed
quests.** This captures the owner's Seven Towers direction and extends
[The Empty Seat](NOTES.md), including [the mathematical fourth act](ACT-IV.md).
The city remains Dun Rìerdan, not a second capital. Its metropolitan overlay
blocker remains open.

The governing image: six towers can be counted from a kitchen window. The
seventh appears in the Black Pool. Above every ordinary room, the mayor is
trying to sleep while the royal household argues several floors below.

## What is inherited, and what is proposed

The [city canon](../../imbar/dun-rierdan.kyr) already names the Rì, Doors,
Cauldron, Judges, Harp, Scales and Teulu towers and their seated powers. Those
identities stay. The anatomical detail, nine new houses and succession
procedures below are proposals; Halyard is an existing family whose capital
household is proposed. No new pedigree overwrites a character's established
parentage.

The university occupies the Scales tower alongside the Exchange: reckoning,
surveying, writing and public teaching grew beside commerce. The College of Dun
Rì retains its canonical seat in Doors. It has teaching agreements with the
university, not a silently relocated campus. Its restricted precinct becomes
the mysterious seventh tower. The current expedition proves accessible lower
rooms, not the proposed visibility rules for that tower.

The High City means Dun Rì's upper wards. It is **not Cloudgate**, the lifted
town in [The Grand Tour](../the-grand-tour/cloudgate.kyr). A Halyard delegation
must travel from Sennet; neither the family nor its merfolk neighbours teleport
into the capital.

## A throne is not a prize for being interesting

Keep four questions separate: **may this person stand, can the claim be
proved, who will recognise it, and can they govern?** A coronet, prophecy or
victory answers none of them alone.

Proposed legal framework:

- The ordinary succession admits acknowledged children and proved collateral
  lines, but requires a public hearing and recognition by the city's powers.
  Gareth's acknowledgement and Aled's parentage remain different evidence.
- Marriage gives a consort's position, not automatic sovereignty. A marriage
  settlement must explicitly provide any shared authority, and the law must
  permit it. Consent is real and can be withdrawn before the vows.
- A living sovereign can designate or adopt a successor only through a
  witnessed, registered act. A private letter is evidence to examine, not a
  law capable of naming anybody king.
- If ordinary succession genuinely fails, the proposed Founder's Compact
  permits open nomination. Five of the seven seated powers must recognise a
  candidate after an open legal hearing. Using it while lawful heirs remain
  willing requires a public change to the settlement, not a secret loophole.
  The noble houses lobby and bargain; they do not own these six votes.
- The six ordinarily visible institutions can appoint a temporary regent
  without finding the seventh tower. The College can also send a representative
  from its public teaching rooms; an invisible building does not erase its
  political identity. Founding testimony can alter a case, but its absence
  cannot softlock government. A regent is not quietly treated as a monarch.
- Fraud can win a proclamation through collusion. It remains fraud: discovery
  threatens recognition, treaties and loyalties. The story must distinguish
  being crowned from having a lawful claim.

Nobody's background secretly turns out royal merely to make the plot work.
Service may earn nomination; it does not rewrite birth. An extraordinary person
can also refuse, lose, become a minister, or help another candidate rule.

## Nine contrasting candidates

These are possible continuations, not nine simultaneously compulsory campaigns.
Each has an entrance, a necessary bridge to sovereignty, and a price.

| Person and existing background | How the road opens | Necessary bridge; what can break it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gareth**, acknowledged royal heir and young warrior | He converts his coronation guard into a real wall muster. Soldiers see him accept a retreat rather than spend them for a handsome victory. | Ordinary succession, then recognition from civilians as well as the Teulu. He must surrender a house's promise to silence Aled. A victory cannot cancel his earlier choice to bar refugees. |
| **Aled**, a singer's concealed son, raised among the people outside the gate | His mother's account, Nerys's testimony and surviving witnesses bring his claim into the open. He acquires a constituency before acquiring a palace. | A hearing of actual parentage and a coalition willing to admit the people he will not abandon. Burned letters make living testimony indispensable. He can refuse a crown whose treaty requires a closed gate. |
| **Nerys**, the widowed queen who understands the court's machinery | An interim regency works because she releases stores, names the dead and takes responsibility for an unpopular evacuation. The regency's expiry forces a choice. | A public non-dynastic nomination or openly revised settlement, not inheritance from her husband. She must let the city examine her own concealed decisions. Her sons can support her without being declared incapable. |
| **Hest**, a working safehouse keeper | Refugees and carriers recognise the person whose records actually found beds for them. A deputation asks him to stand for mayor; competent civic service later makes an open crown nomination plausible. | Mayor and monarch remain distinct offices. He needs the Compact or a public reform, plus a coalition beyond Guild members. He must publish the accounts that also expose the Guild's compromises. He may choose to keep the keys instead. |
| **Cadi**, an adult hired scout without a noble pedigree | Her evacuation route becomes a district's survival story. At the Black Pool she witnesses a phenomenon later advertised as her destiny; she refuses to certify what she did not see. | Civic nomination and an open settlement, not a newly invented royal grandmother. The true route is organising people who can testify, feed a city and defend it. Tarian is not her reward or her compulsory consort. |
| **Tarian**, an adult courier who has sold a route | Lady Rhoswen Velloran offers marriage into a proved collateral line. A patron also offers a forged designation that would make him something more than her consort. | The honest marriage gives only negotiated authority; the forged route needs a bribed docket clerk, a false witness and a silenced courier. Exposure must have consequences. He can burn his own counterfeit claim and tell Cadi the truth, losing the palace rather than receiving instant absolution. |
| **Mared**, a mathematical researcher and Halm's former pupil | Wartime experiments make her useful; refusing an unsupported weapon claim makes her credible. Students and workshops ask why inherited authority should command expertise it will not hear. | A public nomination under a valid settlement. She must relinquish sole control of the instrument and submit a failed experiment as well as the successful one. An engine that moves a counterweight does not prove a right to rule. |
| **Mercy**, the Whelm's young adult merfolk resident, already attached to Perrin Halyard | News of their freely chosen match reaches the capital. A proposed collateral claim for Halyard makes their domestic choice a dynastic question; Mercy's delegation also demands recognition for submerged settlements. | Halyard's collateral genealogy is a new proposal requiring proof. Marriage would make Mercy a consort first; joint sovereignty needs a public settlement. Her independent alternative is open nomination under a treaty giving merfolk civic standing. Neither her grandmother's blessing nor Perrin's signet is a coronation. |
| **Fabian Murrey**, a frightened bookseller and guidebook reviser | His compared editions expose a succession precedent that important scholars copied incorrectly. He becomes the independent keeper of the hearing's account; factions then propose the inconvenient witness as a neutral candidate. | The precedent permits nomination, not inheritance from Dame Philippa. He must publish corrections under his own name, including facts that hurt his sponsors. He may accept the archive rather than the crown; a private love is not erased to supply a dynastic marriage. |

Mercy's family, rescue and match come from [the Whelm](../the-grand-tour/whelm.kyr)
and [the Night of Lanterns](../the-grand-tour/lanterns.kyr). Preserve whether
Perrin lives, where they chose to live, Imogen's knowledge, and the actual
relationship outcome. If those grounds are absent, a Halyard envoy can carry a
different petition; nobody fabricates a happy marriage for the council.
Fabian's [existing fear and authorship](../the-grand-tour/murrey.kyr) must likewise
survive his invitation.

### Four scenes that make these roads human

1. **The borrowed coat.** Tarian rehearses a noble introduction while Hest lets
   out its sleeves. Cadi asks whether the marriage settlement names her road
   among the properties being surrendered. The joke reveals a document worth
   reading; the scene can end with an honest refusal or a signed deception.
2. **The wrong chair.** The mayor's servants bring Mercy a bath instead of a
   seat. She asks who owns the water. The answer exposes a Caerbrith concession.
   Perrin can defend her without speaking for her; Imogen can give up her own
   chair. Warmth and embarrassment precede the treaty.
3. **One original sentence.** Fabian tries to read his nomination and discovers
   that its flattering first paragraph was copied from his grandmother.
   Mared crosses it out. He writes: "I saw the witness arrive." This gives the
   hearing an accountable author instead of another inherited authority.
4. **A crown between brothers.** Gareth and Aled repair the goose-damaged
   practice coronet. They can agree on an evacuation and still disagree about
   kingship. When the real summons arrives, one must decide whether the other
   is a rival he can live beside or a convenient reason not to choose.

## Ten noble houses of the High City

Each has a capital household, provincial obligations and people who do its
work. **None owns a tower outright.** Heraldry is fictional heraldry, not an
Atlas badge. Nine surnames below were checked against the adventure-name
instrument and were free; Halyard deliberately reuses the existing family.

She offers Tarian an honest consort's settlement; her uncle wants a fabricated designation making Tarian his dependent sovereign. Gareth's supporters want her genealogy admitted and her candidacy ignored. |
| **HC-H02 Caerbrith**; a cup with three channels | Reservoir concessions, millraces and grain leases. **Lady Maud Caerbrith** knows every sluicekeeper by name. | Her heir has married a miller's adult daughter and concealed the marriage from the pedigree office. She can keep disputed bodies unburied to pressure the council or fund shared mourning. Asterfen offers a prophecy that would make the disagreement disappear in public. |
| **HC-H07 Rhosden**; a wheel crossed by a spear | Cart contracts, remount farms and the army's supply foundries. **Count Alun Rhosden** boasts that his accounts are less romantic than his neighbours'. | His sibling commands an enemy-held district and sends a private plea. He may keep the roads supplied while bargaining for that person's safety, or sell the timetable. | **HC-H08 Iverlace**; a needle threading a seal | Court clothes, diplomatic households and a wide network of paid messengers. **Lady Nesta Iverlace** remembers what people wore when they lied. | Her secretary is also her adult lover and has been paid to alter one invitation. A genuine reflected tower would threaten the fake miracle rather than authenticate it. The house needs Merrowglass's optics and fears its measurements. |
| **HC-H10 Duskettle**; a kettle beneath a sun | Cookhouses, brewers, rented rooms and an ennobled victualling company. **Lady Bess Duskettle** still checks the ovens herself. | The family bought a pedigree and never bought the original clerk's silence. Bess can admit the purchase and stand on actual service, or support Tarian's counterfeit to normalise her own. Her younger brother wants to return the title; the older generation calls this extravagance. |

The houses' dependencies keep them from becoming ten interchangeable quest
dispensers: Caerbrith's water moves Rhosden's grain; Merrowglass prints
Penwold's forms; Iverlace sends Cinderward's death notices; Duskettle feeds the
night clerks; Asterfen's hostel pilgrims carry rumours; Halyard's provincial
past exposes the cost of raising a family above its neighbours.

At least one member of every house should be met at work or in embarrassment
before being met at a council. A house's political position can split between
generations, lovers, employees and provincial tenants. The Ferryman exploits
one such division; he does not secretly control every family.

## Seven towers: anatomy and paragon quests

These align with the [seven playable callings](../../catalog/classes.json), one
apiece, by the kind of excellence tested. **They are not class-exclusive
admission gates.** A warrior may prove civic stewardship; a thief may defend a
prisoner's rights. Each paragon role carries work, permissions and an obligation,
not a stamp to collect before the finale.

The mayor's penthouse is **the highest room in the city**. The university's
observatory is on its own roof, below that room. No accessible chamber in the
seventh tower supersedes the penthouse; misleading reflected height is not a
new floor in the city's ordinary geometry.

| Canonical tower | Institution and vertical anatomy | Calling resonance; proposed paragon role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rì** | Civics and the mayoralty share the old royal precinct. Petition hall and public counters below; municipal services and council chambers above; the mayor's penthouse highest. The royal oath hall remains lower down. Mayor, royal court and sovereign are distinct authorities. | **Ranger**: reading a living city's dependencies. **Keeper of the Waking City**. |
| **Scales** | The university and Exchange share a precinct. Classrooms and studios at ground level; library stacks below; teaching rooms, writing and scientific work above; an observatory on the roof. Public research is not the restricted College's entire business. | **Mage**: inquiry disciplined by evidence. **Rector of the Open Sky**. |
| **Judges** | Courts and public hearings near the street; gaols and records below; comfortable apartments for political prisoners at the top. Their windows and gardens make the injustice visible, not benign. | **Monk**: judgement that binds the powerful as well as the weak. **Advocate for the Absent**. |
| **Teulu** | Army muster, drill and stores at the base; strategic operations above; intelligence operations below ground. Civilian oversight has a door into the lower offices rather than only a ceremonial balcony. | **Warrior**: command that accepts responsibility for losses. **Captain of the Second Watch**. |
| **Cauldron** | The ecumenical council's tower: representatives and shrines for every known faith of the West, shared service spaces and disputation rooms. The Keepers retain the Cauldron and their own rites; they do not absorb other religions. | **Cleric**: care and covenant across disagreement. **Convener of the Many Shrines**. |
| **Harp** | Lore: workshops and museums at ground level, archives beneath, art galleries above. Very few are permitted into the upper levels, where access to disputed originals is controlled. The Bards' Hall keeps its canonical seat. | **Bard**: preserving memory without confusing it with flattery. **Keeper of the Unburnt Names**. |
| **Doors** | The seventh tower, seen only in particular light or in reflections on certain water, especially the Black Pool around which the earliest settlements formed. The College's ordinary shore entrances give access to restricted precincts; seeing the tower, entering it and knowing its ways are different achievements. | **Thief**: passage, attention and the recovery of what power concealed. **Doorward of the Black Pool**. |

### 1. Rì: The City That Can Sleep

**Entrance:** Hest finds the same refugee household on three rent rolls and no
bed list. At the petition counter, three officials insist their records agree.
They agree only about the existence of a fee.

**Movement:** Trace the household's actual route through the city; inspect the
water and grain bottlenecks rather than just correcting a name. Caerbrith can
release a sluice, Rhosden can reroute carts, and tenants can organise a working
night kitchen. Each solution spends time or relinquishes a concession.

**Reversal:** The mayor's penthouse is empty because its occupant has been
sleeping on a municipal floor. A patron proposes replacing that exhausted
person with a decorative heir. Decide whether to support, replace or expose
the mayor through a public procedure, not a private favour.

**Proof and aftermath:** A district can eat, sleep and be found in the same
records. The paragon receives authority to inspect municipal services and an
obligation to keep them accountable. Cadi or Hest earns grounds for nomination;
neither becomes king by fixing a drain.

> Hest: "They put the mayor above the weather. The complaints still get up."

### 2. Scales: The Night Without North

**Entrance:** A student in a ground-floor studio has drawn the observatory's
star chart upside down. It is the only copy agreeing with last night's log.

**Movement:** Compare observations on the roof, surviving notebooks in the
stacks and measurements from working instruments. Mared leads one test;
Fabian can establish which edition copied which mistake. Ordinary surveys,
independent witnesses and mathematical proof provide different evidence.

**Reversal:** A Merrowglass instrument works exactly as designed, but its
patented correction suppresses the anomalous light. The anomaly sometimes
reveals Doors in the Pool. Publishing the defect costs a patron its income;
advertising an unproved miracle would preserve the grant.

**Proof and aftermath:** Publish a reproducible observation and its limits.
Release a corrected chart and access to actual research, not the claim that
every mathematical device is understood. The university can scrutinise Act
IV's results; Mared's independent standing grows through candour.

> Mared: "We have discovered where north is not. Please do not found a kingdom there."

### 3. Judges: The Window Above the Gaol

**Entrance:** An upper political prisoner sends down excellent pears. A lower
prisoner sends up the name of the person who ordered the arrest.

**Movement:** Obtain the arrest docket, arrange counsel and hear witnesses in
their own circumstances. One witness is unsafe outside the prison; another
has been paid to stay comfortably inside it. Rescue, negotiation, appeal and
exposure can help, but an escape is not automatically an acquittal.

**Reversal:** Aderyn Penwold's child and the cellar prisoner acted under the
same order. The order is connected to Tarian's possible forged designation.
Releasing only the noble leaves the system intact; exposing the entire order
also damages a claimant the company may prefer.

**Proof and aftermath:** Secure an accountable hearing, correct the detention
record and obtain actual release or a lawful, reviewable sentence. Failure can
still preserve a deposition or protect a witness. The paragon gains standing
to represent people otherwise excluded from proceedings.

> Nerys: "A garden is not an acquittal. Nor is a cellar a conviction."

### 4. Teulu: The Order Nobody Signed

**Entrance:** Two patrols have received the same numbered order for different
roads. One brought back a cart; the other brought back its driver's coat.

**Movement:** Reconstruct the dispatch in the intelligence rooms below,
inspect the threatened approaches, and confront the strategic officers above.
Rhosden's timetable, Tarian's actual warning and a scout's ground observations
can contradict a beautiful map.

**Reversal:** The duplicate order was an authorised decoy, not necessarily an
enemy forgery. Its sponsor omitted who would be sacrificed. A quick assault
can rescue a trapped unit but expose a civilian crossing; a negotiated retreat
can save both groups while losing the ground.

**Proof and aftermath:** Lead an operation with actual orders, supply,
withdrawal routes and a reckoning of losses. Act IV's missing counterweight
changes this situation only if that exchange happened. The paragon gains
bounded command and must answer the survivors, not simply receive a medal.

> Gareth: "If it was my order, put my name on the coat."

### 5. Cauldron: A Funeral With No Empty Place

**Entrance:** A dead messenger is claimed by two traditions. Their household
asks for one thing neither delegation has asked: an ordinary shared meal.

**Movement:** Establish the deceased's expressed wishes, hear the people who
cared for them, and negotiate rites, sanctuary and practical shelter. Assemble
representation from the setting's existing faith communities, not a new cult
for every god in a catalog. Missing representation must remain visible.

**Reversal:** Cinderward can pay for the funeral only through an endowment
whose conditions exclude one community. Asterfen offers a convenient divine
announcement. The Cauldron's return of the dead is a separate, costly question:
neither a staged resurrection nor a coerced one settles the succession.

**Proof and aftermath:** Honour the person's wishes and establish a shared
sanctuary obligation the delegates actually sustain. They need not agree on
theology. The paragon can convene, not command, the shrines; recognition of a
ruler depends on a witnessed covenant rather than a miraculous birthmark.

> Seren: "Before you decide where his soul belongs, his sister needs a chair."

### 6. Harp: The Genealogy With a Missing Kitchen

**Entrance:** A conservator finds a laundress painted out of a royal portrait.
The public museum's copy retains her; the upper gallery's original does not.

**Movement:** Compare the workshop's restoration notes, archive payments,
household testimony and the restricted original. A public commission,
negotiated access, protective copying or clandestine entry opens different
routes. Stealing an original can expose it and destroy its chain of custody.

**Reversal:** The alteration concealed kindness as well as parentage. The
laundress protected Seren, but revealing every address now endangers her
family. Iverlace's letters and Duskettle's employment records connect the
people; they do not substitute for their testimony.

**Proof and aftermath:** Produce an attributed account that establishes what
matters without publishing a route to every witness. Aled's case, Halyard's
proposed collateral claim and Fabian's precedent each require their own
evidence. The paragon gains custodianship and must permit inconvenient readers.

> Fabian: "The queen was restored twice. The woman who washed her linen wasn't."

### 7. Doors: The Stair in the Other Water

**Entrance:** At the Black Pool, a washer sees a window reflected where the
sky has no building. An Asterfen proclamation has already described the
person expected to stand inside it. That person is not there.

**Movement:** Establish a repeatable viewing condition using witnessed light,
water and position. Survey the shore entrance; test a route with a cord and a
recoverable load. The early settlements' well foundations predate the present
court. Their records are an invitation to investigate, not instant proof of a
forgotten royal line.

**Reversal:** A restricted landing holds a founding agreement whose omitted
part concerns the people sharing the water. Another room appears occupied.
Its resident owns their evidence and may refuse to surrender it. Mercy can
interpret a lived water practice without becoming an oracle; Cadi can identify
a safe approach without becoming the subject of a true prophecy.

**Proof and aftermath:** Establish one usable passage and return an agreement
with provenance and the other party's consent. It may support an open
succession procedure or a water treaty. It does not crown its finder. Act IV's
Earth passage remains the only currently demonstrated destination; this quest
does not silently add a working route to Water.

> Cadi: "I saw a stair. The rest of the destiny arrived by printer."

## The Black Pool must behave like a place

The first settlement ring, well mouths, wash steps and old landing make the
Pool useful before it becomes uncanny. Fishers, cleaners, children and clerks
can notice it; expensive training is not the only entrance to wonder.

For future implementation, choose a stable, world-owned profile for the light
and reflection condition. Examples to develop: low autumn sunlight crossing a
still surface; a particular moon through thin cloud; a reflected lamp positioned
at an old survey mark. Disturbance may obscure the view, not erase a person.
A painting follows the engine's observed weather and visibility.

Distinguish seeing the tower, knowing its shore entrance, gaining permission,
crossing a passage, and finding a return. Save those discoveries. Once inside,
an interruption of the viewing condition must not strand the company without
an established physical or experimentally demonstrated exit. A reflecting
surface is not automatically a portal.

A rumour that the Pool "chooses kings" is allowed. Repeated observations can
disprove that rumour. Mathematics can establish the optical condition while
leaving occupancy, ownership and planar operation genuinely unknown.

Do not make every omen fraudulent. One glimpse may genuinely show Cadi later
carrying the crown down a stair. That image can be fulfilled by her own
accession, by an evacuation, or by taking it to another claimant. The glimpse
does not say which. Destiny supplies an unsettling invitation; the choices
give it its meaning. Asterfen's manufactured prediction is a separate fraud,
not the explanation that makes every wonder mundane.

## How the quests converge without becoming a checklist

Three pressures bring the institutions together:

1. **Whose name survives?** Harp's record, Judges' docket and the candidate's
   witnessed account establish different pieces of a claim. The safehouse
   protects living testimony.
2. **Whose city survives?** Rì's provision, Teulu's orders and Scales' bounded
   experiment must agree about the actual route and available supplies.
   Cauldron's sanctuary admits people whom a military plan might omit.
3. **Who was never asked?** Doors' founding agreement and the Black Pool's
   working neighbours challenge an inherited settlement, not merely an heir.

Do not require all seven paragon quests. Each can support, oppose or change a
candidate's programme. An uncompleted quest leaves the institution acting on
what it knows and possesses; another candidate or local ally can intervene.
The city continues during absence. A funeral, love affair or repaired kitchen
can matter without advancing the crown.

The final choice should be about a proposed government and the promises it can
keep, not which character collected the most endorsements. Refusing an office
can preserve a friendship or make a necessary minister available. A marriage
can fail while a treaty succeeds. A lawful heir can lose recognition without
becoming secretly evil.

## Deem and Kyr: the authoring boundary

**Deem proposes coherent variations:** a house's available patron, the disputed
document, who depends on a concession, the ordinary interruption at a hearing,
and an observed-light profile. Draw dependent details together; reuse them after
saving. Do not randomly marry two existing characters or regenerate parentage.

**Kyr should enact consequences:** testimony heard, permission granted or
revoked, a real released prisoner, a loan forgiven, stores spent, a route
disclosed, a marriage freely agreed, a delegation's arrival and a public
appointment. News travels; it does not make every village omniscient.

**Kits are candidates, not new implemented systems:** contested inheritance;
unequal confinement; civic provisioning; negotiated shared ritual; a witnessed
experiment; a restricted archive; and a conditional-view passage. Each needs a
small independent contract before reuse across towers.

Keep established persons as installed identities with their memories,
relationships and locations. New houses can supply genuinely new supporting
people. Their representatives need journeys into the city. House devices,
institutional membership and titles never declare Atlas facets; actual actions
and consequences supply the existing marks.

### Landing the design, eventually

First prove one tower encounter as a small, complete situation, not seven new
systems at once. Its end must change a person or institution and survive a
save. Then connect it to one existing claimant through the city overlay.
Implement visibility, succession and marriage only behind their own contracts.
This chapter captures the broader lore now; it does not claim those quests,
noble households or additional crown outcomes are playable.
