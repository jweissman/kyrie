# 1. What Westernesse is

*The authoring trail, page 1 of 9. [Start](../README.md) · next: [How it is built](02-architecture.md)*

**Westernesse** (*A History of Emprise*) is a literary fantasy role-playing game, a Seventh Star game set in the
world of **Imbar**. It runs in a browser page with a book's look over a persistent simulated world. **Kyrie** is its
engine, **Kyr** the language its world is written in, **Deem** the library that generates the parts of a world no one
wrote by hand. The repository's older pages say "Kyr" for the whole game; they mean this.

## What a player should feel

The owner's creative vision, [doc/design/VISION.md](../../design/VISION.md) (31 October), is the authority. In short:

- **The world is wider than the adventure.** One particular person arrives somewhere with a little money and a few
  abilities. The world existed before them and does not wait for them: people pursue their own ends, deadlines pass,
  what was done stays done (VISION §I).
- **Emprise: an undertaking that can grow beyond its first meaning.** A stolen bell leads to a dispute, the dispute to
  an older law, the law to an archive; or the bell comes home and a friendship begins. The world must allow both
  (§II).
- **The old-school art of adventuring.** Provisions, maps, rope and a crowbar matter; danger is not scaled to the
  company; warnings can be read, routes found, help recruited, retreat chosen. *One expedition should create the
  desire and the means for another* (§III).
- **A material world that resists and yields** to things not individually scripted (§IV); deep time and ruins with
  histories (§V); the magnificent, the grotesque and the ridiculous side by side (§VII); civilizations as living
  accomplishments (§VIII); literature that can be inhabited (§X).
- **The experience must live up to the world** (§XII): an interface that composes what the present moment means, not
  one that lists everything the simulation permits.

The [Living Design Atlas](../../design/ATLAS.md) and the [Aesthetic Doctrine](../../design/AESTHETICS.md) (25 October)
say the same at the level of design: one situated person who attends to a world, forms intentions and meets their
consequences; companions who come by who they are and what happens, never by filling party slots. The independent
product-design review of 4 November names the grammar the page should speak: *place → subject → intention → action →
result → changed place*.

## What it is today

Honestly, from the findings index and the Mission 19 [progress report](../../architecture/mission-19/progress.md):

- A new life begins as one person in a town of a generated country (`a1` is the reference seed). The first town's
  jobs, a 3D hex map for travel, generated wild country, ruins and deep sites, recruits, a resting scene, a wayfinder,
  combat, trade and an Encyclopedia are built and have been played through the page **by the builder**.
- Twenty-one authored adventures are installed as provinces of every generated first country, reached by road.
- No independent player and not the owner has accepted any build since the Mission 19 repairs (PD-14, open). The
  owner's verdict at the end of Mission 18 was that ordinary play "does not yet feel like a game"; Mission 19 is the
  work on that. Read every "Fixed" in the registers as the builder's claim.

## What this means for an author

You write for **one persistent world shared by everything**. An adventure is a place in that world that the player's
own company may walk into, not a separate game: it never supplies the company (page 4). A creature, a plant or an item
is a canonical record the whole world uses, not a local invention (page 6). What you write should give a curious
player something to notice, a reason to act, more than one way to act, and a consequence that is remembered.
