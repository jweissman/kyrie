### The road down into Barzelmizela — Day 1, 8:00 a.m.

The road comes down off the high ground in long zigzags, and Barzelmizela is below you at the foot of it: stone roofs, a green, the blue smoke of the charcoal hearths, the inn chimney smoking.

The herb-wife at Ramavor wrapped the feverwort in a cloth and put it in your hands. ‘The child at Barzelmizela has had the fever three days, and they have nothing left there,’ she said. ‘Don’t dawdle.’

**1. Take the road to Barzelmizela green**

Bess gets up, stretches, and comes over to push her nose into your hand.

### Barzelmizela green — Day 1, 8:10 a.m.

Barzelmizela climbs a slope from its green in steps of garden wall, with the blue smoke of charcoal hearths beyond the houses. It grew up beside the Marirzerem. The fells rise behind it. Next to the Laughing Skull is the shrine of Nuada, so close that the inn’s smoke goes over its roof.

A shepherd’s boy is telling anyone who will listen that something comes up out of Silkholme sink at night and takes a ewe off a pit in the ground, and that Garth means to burn it out.

The shutters of one house on the green are drawn in daylight, and a woman stands in its door, watching the road.

A cart goes by loaded with charcoal sacks, and everything it passes goes a little grey.

Vondal is sharpening an axe on a wheel, testing the edge on his thumbnail.

Harbek is sitting on the bank with a line in the water, not catching anything with great patience.

Dagny is sitting on the edge of the green mending a pot, with her wares spread on a blanket.

Bess, the inn’s dog, lies across the doorstep of the Laughing Skull with her chin on her paws, and watches who comes.

Here are Barendd, a mother with a sick child; and Thoradin, the sick child’s elder sister.
