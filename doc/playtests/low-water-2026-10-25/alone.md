# Low Water, left alone

Seed `alone`; 192 steps; ended at watch 1111 with the Mere at 9 spans, at the ending "Low Water". Written by `bun scripts/low-water-transcripts.ts` from the journey as the page told it.

*(The first page)*

The road comes over the lip of the Bowl and stops, because the world does. Below you the city goes down in four great steps, each a ring of roofs and gardens and wet stone, and between the rings the water: falling, turning, held. You can hear it before you understand it, a sound like a great many people breathing out. At the bottom, very far, the Mere lies like a coin somebody dropped. There is a pale ring round it. It takes you a moment to see that the ring is mud.

**To the head of the Stair**

The head of the Stair: a stone landing the size of a market, where the Ninefold, brought along the Rim in a cut channel, drops into the first of the Nine Locks and goes down the Bowl in steps of white water. Stairs for people go down beside it. The spray makes a rainbow over the first lock at this hour and a cold mist at every other. The rainbow hangs over the first lock, so steady the carters use it to tell the time. A woman in a grey coat is standing at the head of the Stair with a brass rod under her arm, as if she had been there all morning and would be there all afternoon. Isaura Vant, a Clerk-Measurer, Third Grade, of the Office of Measures, is here.

**Talk to Isaura Vant**

Isaura looks up from her ledger, finishes the line she was writing, and then looks up properly.

**Ask the woman in grey if she is waiting for you**

‘You’re the Assessor.’ It is not a question. ‘Isaura Vant. Office of Measures, Third Grade. I’m to accompany you, record your findings, open what you need opened, and stop you being robbed. The Registrar would like your Finding at the Hearing, which is at noon in six days’ time.’ She glances down at the Mere. ‘It was at the fourth step of the Ward landing when I was a girl. There are twenty-nine painted steps above it now. I’ll show you the twenty-ninth. That’s the kind of thing I’m for.’

**Ask Isaura Vant to join the company**

Isaura tucks the brass ell-rod under her arm and falls in beside you, half a step behind, where she can see what you see and also write it down.

*(10 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Over the whole Bowl, terrace below terrace, the Sounding’s bells go into the Hymn, and under them, faint and enormous, the choir.

*(6 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

An hour passes. It is a long time since anyone slept. The company is weary, and will fight the worse for it until it sleeps.

*(3 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Down in the Ward the last boat on the Mere has gone over on her side in the mud: Dov Vant’s Tench.

*(12 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Seven o’clock, and the Sounding’s bells go into the Hymn again, as they have every evening for three thousand years; and up and down the terraces, at every lock and sluice, keepers put their hands on their wheels.

*(14 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

In the Ward, an old woman called Marra Fen signs her plot over to the Guild in Basset’s ledger, with her daughter’s hand on her shoulder. The plot is the one the dig is on.

*(5 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At the dig it comes to cudgels and shovels. It is short and ugly. When it is over, two diggers are bleeding into the mud, the bailiffs have Dov Vant down with his arms tied behind him, and they march him up the Ward steps to the Office cells. The dig is the Guild’s.

*(3 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

The Hymn. Somewhere below you a dog howls along with the bells, and is hushed, and howls again.

*(10 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Word comes down the terraces, and up them: the Ward well has failed. In Tench Row the handcarts are coming out.

*(12 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

The Hymn, at seven. Down in the Ward, where it used to come back off the Mere, nothing comes back; the mud takes it, and the handcarts in Tench Row stop, all together, while it lasts.

*(16 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

From the Sounding the noon bell, and from the Office the long single note of the Office horn: the Hearing.

*(1 wait in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Word comes, wherever you are: the Hearing sat and the Assessor did not come, and Registrar Fenn gave the Office’s own finding in their place. The Guild’s Fulling Channel, the cause; the Channel to be closed; the charter not renewed. The mills are out on the Rim, and the Ward is drinking to the Office in the Full Measure.

*(4 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

The bells go into the Hymn, the first since the Hearing, and the Sounding must be full to the doors: you can hear the singing from the Rim to the Ward, and in the Ward, for once, people are singing too.

*(20 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Far down, the Mere draws back from the Ward like a held breath let go, and does not come back. Every bell in Ammerlin rings at once. Nobody is at the ropes. The sound goes round the Bowl and comes back off the Rim, and under it, from the water itself, from the Stair and the cisterns and every pipe and basin in the city, a chord comes up that nobody living has heard: the whole Long Hymn, in one breath, sung by the water. Then nothing.

*(2 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

The Hymn, at seven. The bells are the same and the choir is the same, and something under it is not: the water does not answer. Down through the terraces, where for three thousand years the cisterns rang with it, there is only stone.

*(6 waits in which nothing is told)*

**To the north road, at the Rim**

The north road ends at a gate in a low wall, and past the gate the ground goes out from under the world. Carters water their horses at a trough fed by a bronze spout older than the wall. From the Rim the ring of mud round the Mere is wider than the water now, grey, with things standing up in it.

**Go up out of the Bowl by the north road**

The count closed on its own, in the night, with every bell in Ammerlin ringing and nobody at the ropes and nobody in the city who knew what they were hearing. The Mere went down six spans in one breath and stopped. It stands at 9 spans on the Ward gauge, a pond at the bottom of a Bowl of mud, and the Ward is gone up the terraces. Under it the round door stands open on still water. In the Office, a Clerk-Measurer, Third Grade, has written a line in her tally under the forty ells that she could never put anywhere: Ceased. She has not written why. Nobody has asked her.
