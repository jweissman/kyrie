## Why
The first hour is a good run of small discoveries: a letter, a rhyme, a lamp, then a huge dark hall. Every trap offered a safe way past, either the pole or a skill check. The dungeon told its story through a notebook, scratched marks and tripwires. What pulled the experience down was the interface. Look rows often timed out, so I never read the tally-board that is probably the errand's goal, and I missed some loot.

## What worked
1. The aims at the top of every page, and the gold follow row.
2. Pole-defusing of traps, with a stated risk ("about 7 harm… Adrian has 4 left: it could kill").
3. Hireable companions who showed their stats, and combat where Sweeping Strike and Scald did what they said.
4. Leoric's "noon in a jar" rhyme turning into an aim.
5. Searching rooms, and the "Look closely" rows that revealed a way past.
## Surprise
A dungeon that tells its story through tripwires and a tally-board, where a pole answers a trap and goblins can be parleyed with at an even chance. I did not expect a small mining ruin to hold a hidden store behind loose planks.
