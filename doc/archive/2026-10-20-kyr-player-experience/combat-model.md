# B. Intended combat experience and minimum coherent rules

**D — proposed product model, for review.** This is not a description of today's `Battle`. Current combat is nonspatial; the [gap analysis](capabilities-and-authoring.md) and [earlier arithmetic audit](../../playtests/cold-2026-10-19/combat-and-roadmap.md) separate implemented rules from proposals.

Combat should turn the expedition's choices into a tense, readable local problem: who is threatened, what outcome we need, what each companion can contribute, which resource/risk to spend, and when to leave. The drama comes from a dangerous situation and changing decisions, not from a required fight in every room or a long HP pool.

## 1. Spatial alternatives

| Model | What decisions it supports | Cost / limitation | Judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Abstract engagement** | Target priority, buffs/debuffs, resources, protection, committed threat response. | Lowest migration cost. Cannot represent a valve beyond a guard, a firing position or meaningful crossing distances. Card placement must not imply formation. | Appropriate current renderer and valid simple-encounter fallback; insufficient alone for the intended physically meaningful RPG. |
| **Front/back formation** | Protecting fragile members, melee access constraints. | Cheap-looking but requires reach/targeting rules; fixed rows can contradict an open room, flank, doorway or pursuit. Adds a second spatial model disconnected from places. | Do not adopt as universal baseline. |
| **Feature-based connected zones** | Access to passage/mechanism, reach, protection/choke points, pursuit and escape, local movement without exact squares. | Requires real zone topology, occupancy/visibility and action rules; authors must define useful features. | **Recommended minimum intended model.** Usually 2–4 zones for a meaningful encounter; one-zone encounters remain legitimate. |
| **Metric/grid tactics** | Exact ranges, areas, paths, cover edges, detailed formations. | Major geometry, movement, targeting, AI, UI and authoring burden. Current place-node coordinates do not supply it. | Defer until a demonstrated need cannot be expressed clearly with zones. |

A zone is an authored part of a real place or threshold, not a decorative row of combat cards. Examples might be a workshop's gallery steps, central floor and boiler arch, but those zones are **new authored data**, not inferred from current room prose. Zone labels and local landmarks must explain connections. A diagram is explicitly schematic; it does not imply exact meters, collision or facing.

## 2. Recommended baseline rules

These decisions establish a coherent rule boundary for an experiment. Exact numerical ability balance follows separately; builders must not infer rules from the wireframes.

### Space, reach and access

- Combatants occupy a zone subordinate to a canonical place. Encounter projection references world location; it does not maintain an independently mutable duplicate position. Objects and thresholds have zone anchors when relevant.
- An encounter has a bounded extent with visible/known zones and connected exit thresholds. Initial placement comes from approach, actual locations and an authored fallback, not an arbitrary front line. In a one-zone scene all eligible combatants share reach as the current abstract model does.
- Melee targets are in the same zone unless an ability explicitly grants adjacent-zone reach. Ranged attacks can reach a visible zone along a declared line of sight within their listed zone range; graph path alone does not establish visibility. Closed doors, smoke or full cover can block that line only if represented.
- A normal movement action crosses one traversable zone connection. Movement does not also grant a full attack by default; an explicit rush/charge technique can do both with its own cost/risk. Entering a zone containing an enemy is permitted unless a declared guarded threshold/capacity rule blocks it. There are **no implicit opportunity attacks** in the baseline.
- A guard on a passage is represented by a threshold condition and encounter stance. If bypass is possible, its method/condition is authored. Ash's guarding must not become a magical control zone over every adjacent route.
- Cover in the first zone model is one explicit `covered from` relation for relevant connections, with an authored mechanical modifier. No half-cover geometry or guessed sight lines. It is optional per encounter, not a requirement to add cover to every room.

### Turn order, time and economy

- Stable speed-based order, one active turn per living/acting combatant per round. Ties use a fixed declared ordering so save/replay is reproducible. Show the queue; skipped/incapacitated/added combatants update it visibly. A faster actor acts earlier, not indefinitely more often without a separate ability.
- One major action per turn: attack, spell, Guard, Protect, move, use item, operate feature, negotiate/surrender or initiate withdrawal as legal. Inspect/select/preview is free. There is no separate bonus-action vocabulary in the baseline.
- A round is six seconds of world time. Actions are ordered within it; world events and condition clocks resolve at their declared boundaries. Ended partial rounds have an explicit committed elapsed interval (minimum one round for an encounter containing a resolved action). Do not charge an unrelated fixed ten-minute campaign duration for both a one-action massacre and a long encounter.
- Readied equipment is used by its listed action. Drawing/swapping a stowed weapon or passing gear costs a major action unless a specific technique says otherwise. No free inventory reorganization during a paused fight. Rest/recovery remains outside active hostile engagement.
- Owner-turn, world-time and encounter durations are distinct. A “three owner turns” condition shows its actual expiration boundary; Guard lasts until the actor's next turn begins. Persistent outside-combat effects explicitly state whether their combat-turn counter pauses or converts; never silently reinterpret turns as hours.

### Readable threats, protection and roles

- Enemies expose committed next intentions when their behavior supports telegraphing: action, visible preparation, affected zone/target policy and timing. Store the commitment; do not merely predict a heuristic AI choice. A fixed target/policy cannot secretly retarget after player mitigation. Cancellation/replanning after target loss, incapacity or state change produces a visible updated intent.
- Not every exact ability is public. An unknown attack can be “drawing breath across the ledge” with a known threatened zone and uncertainty; a practiced foe may feint only under an explicit rule, not because the UI's forecast is unreliable. Surprise/immediate opening actions require an authored trigger and readable danger boundary; no promise that a player always gets the first turn.
- Guard halves incoming damage under the listed rules until next turn. Protect spends a turn to intercept the next eligible single-target physical strike against one named ally in the same zone, before the protector's next turn. Redirect to protector and use actual defenses; consume protection. One protector per target, no redirection chains, area attacks not intercepted, incapacity/moving out of reach cancels it. This is a proposed new rule, not current Guard behavior.
- Roles arise from useful decisions. A sturdy companion can protect/control access or pressure an enemy; an agile one can exploit reach/access, use a viable ordinary attack or work a feature; a caster manages limited control/damage/support; a medic handles casualties. Any class can have overlaps; do not enforce a mandatory trinity or make baseline competence depend on a random exceptional attribute.
- Enemy selection should consider actual effectiveness, intent and objective, not just effect-type scores. An area attack against one target is not automatically preferable. A hound must not flee merely because bite is cooling down; give it an actual fallback or authored disengagement policy.

### Defenses, effects and environmental action

- Preserve typed damage, defenses, saves, conditions, costs and cooldowns. An attack-roll technique reports roll/hit/miss; a direct-damage spell/attack reports actual resolved damage and mitigation, not an invented hit. Conditions include effect and clock, not only name.
- Preview player-known range/eligibility and effects. Do not expose hidden exact HP/defenses by default simply because the engine knows them. Recommended baseline shows exact companion resources, enemy condition bands plus established traits; detailed exact enemy values can be an optional rules mode or granted knowledge. Evaluation experiments may show known fixture HP, but that does not make all product enemies omniscient statistics.
- Record full resolved damage separately from HP loss (and overkill). A 16-damage breath against a 6-HP target is not a “6-damage breath.” Link the save to its controlled effect; resisting bleeding does not cancel independent bite damage.
- A feature action names the object, zone/reach, capability/tool, action cost, opposed risk if any, state transition and resulting effects. A valve can be operated in combat only if authored as an encounter action with authoritative world consequences. A fire effect on a bridge must cause the same persistent transition whether used by a monster or another supported source.
- Do not implement arbitrary free-form environmental physics. Start with one lever/door/valve intervention that changes a genuine encounter choice. It must affect current encounter state immediately, not only write a post-battle prose line.

### Objectives, retreat, surrender and loss

- Encounter success is an authored predicate: recover item, free captive, hold passage for a duration, reach an exit, force retreat, secure agreement, disable hazard or eliminate opposition. Participants can have different goals. UI displays only known stakes/conditions; outcome event records actual reason and world changes.
- **Withdrawal** is an explicit company intention, not a self-target Flee that ambiguously ends both sides. Preview destination, available exit, known response, able carriers and anyone/anything left. The baseline withdrawal resolves one declared enemy response window, then moves the withdrawing company through a currently usable exit and records costs. It fails/changes only for specified state changes, explained to the player; no silent teleportation or guaranteed escape through a ruined bridge. Exact response-window scheduling must be tested with turn order before production; instant abstract disengagement can remain a clearly labelled interim mechanic.
- One carrier per incapacitated companion under PX06. Leaving someone/gear records real location and exposure; later rescue/retrieval is possible only while that state remains. No automatic transfer of all bodies and inventories with a sole survivor.
- A surrender offer consumes an action and invokes a typed social disposition: accepted terms, refused with reason/uncertainty, or counteroffer. Acceptance changes hostility, custody, property and commitments atomically. It is not a universal escape button. Creature/institution definitions declare whether such approaches exist and what known evidence supports them.
- **Zero HP = incapacitated. Death requires an explicit death event**, such as a lethal finishing action, specific destructive hazard or declared defeat aftermath. Constructs may instead become destroyed under their explicit rule. No generic “fell, therefore died” fallback.
- Every authored encounter needs a defeat disposition: killed, captured, left incapacitated, or rescued under an actual local rule/actor. No invented savior. Full incapacity ends player control and resolves that disposition with a persistent record. Lethal endings are allowed and must be coherent with the established threat. A single-player save can be resumed from before loss, but reload is a player history branch, not an in-world resurrection.
- After hostile control ends, supported first aid/spell/service can restore incapacitated people; rest alone does not resurrect the dead. Stabilization/treatment costs and any ongoing hazard clock must be declared. The initial product need not add an automatic death-save minigame; timed critical bleeding can be a later explicit rule. Recovery must identify who can act, supplies, travel constraints and remaining injuries.

## 3. Actual examples and proposed improvements

**Pell's rack / Ash's passage:** Current rescue methods and hunter/food approaches are valuable. Keep them outside combat where possible. Under the intended model, a rescue attempted during hostility uses an authored rack action at its zone, consuming turns; companions can protect the worker or distract the guard only through actual actions. Do not make every rescue hostile to advertise the combat system. Ash may defend its passage without attacking everyone who enters the gallery; calming/feeding or another route can succeed without a kill.

**Sentinel:** Current recorded fight lasted 15 actions and inflicted only three total party HP damage; adding a grid or more HP would not create good tactics. First repair readable Guard/status/queue and useful character actions. Existing flame blade, physical resistance, spells, debuffs and optional bypass support preparation choices now. Current sentinel does not guard the sword/exit by rule: taking the blade and leaving is a legitimate approach, not an exploit to conceal.

Proposed authored encounter: an inspector bars access to a particular store while a mechanism threatens that area. If the author wants this conflict, add its purpose, guarded threshold, mechanism and alternatives explicitly; do not merely rename existing behavior. Zones could separate arch, floor and control station. A committed sweep threatens floor; one companion protects a worker, another reaches/operates the control, a caster spends control magic or the party chooses exit. Outcome may be securing access/closing a hazard without destroying the construct. **All of the purpose/mechanism/zone integration is new authoring and rules work**, not a demonstrated current event.

**Calder:** Observed tribute buys passage, hunting creates a time window, rope creates alternate egress, fire destroys the bridge. Preserve the dragon as a potentially overwhelming creature, not an automatically balanced level-one duel. The intended entry view makes obvious scale, scorch marks, visible exit and known warning matter before explicit attack. Under zones, breath threatens declared parts of ledge; a feature can shield only if authored as actual cover with line-of-sight/area rules. Do not invent a convenient rock to make the recorded defeat survivable. A bridge burning removes the corresponding escape option immediately and persistently. A theft-triggered raid exists in current source but was not played; intended product needs credible evidence/report and local responses rather than omniscient quest updates.

## 4. Adoption boundary

Current-engine readability, attribution and selection repairs are foundational. Zone data, movement/reach, shared clocks, committed intentions, protection, objective outcomes, surrender and casualty locations are new product capabilities. Prototype **one small multi-enemy, two-zone encounter** after the simpler rendering contract passes; compare against an honestly abstract equivalent with the same information.

Accept the zone model only if players can name a meaningful movement/protection/environment decision and predict eligibility without moderator explanation. If it adds bookkeeping without changing plans, keep that encounter one-zone and revise the rules/content. Do not ship a visually spatial battle before authoritative occupancy, targeting, world persistence and save/resume all agree.
