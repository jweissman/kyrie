# The first hour of eleven shipped adventures, played as the novice

Method: `bin/kyrie play data/campaigns/<campaign>.campaign.json --seed s1 --choices "<label>" ...`, taking only what the page offers, about ten to fifteen choices each (the pitch, the first room, the first people, the first walk). "Third Night" is the campaign that ships Wrayford. Shipped party in brackets. Scores are "would a reader keep going", 1 to 5, before and after this pass's fixes.

## Per adventure

**Winter in Carth.** Opens on the Cross: Hern, a barefoot heretic, and Beadle Sutcliffe. Goal clear in two screens (bring Hern to the Fell Gate, eight shillings). The first ten choices include a real decision (take custody, or tell the beadle no), a gain (50 experience), and a walk with a question ("Can one man be sorry for another?") answered by a three-way pick. Seven "Ask Hern" topics, all different. The cleric's first leaf of the Register is not reachable by the bard and the first hour never hints at it. No dead openings.

**Wrayford.** The ranger comes home to the bridge-city. Northgate has six exits but the page makes the right ones loud (the silent carters; Wain Row sends you to your mother's kitchen). Every room has a face; the asks are specific ("when she wrote", "about Kit"). Lone-company beats are not met in ten choices. Stake: your brother's raw hands. Strongest first hour of the set for a reader who wants a mystery.

**Book of Faces.** Customs Steps, Strangers' Square, the sergeant: a perfect three-beat opening (the rule, the crime, the contract). First goal (find the Book, nine days) is clear at the sergeant. Weak point: after "Look." the novice has nine exits and no direction; Counting Row has eleven. The thief-only second lock and the Bursar report cannot be reached by the shipped warrior. The Hospice has six named people, mostly talk-only in the first hour.

**Gosse's Company.** The green: Gosse, Hepzibah, Pudding, Patience the bear, the chained wagon. Goal in one answer: Lem has fled with the hat; pay the eight-coin reckoning at the Wheatsheaf or find him. The comedy does the work. Dead end: "Examine the wagon: Looked at closely, it is just as it seems." The cleric's requiem is not reachable by the bard.

**Hollins.** Notice, then the giant's doorstep. Bootscrape street is a 260-word paragraph, the longest first screen in the set, but it holds the whole hook (four days since the giantess came home, the village watching the chimney). Ten choices include a ladder, a letterbox and the Mat. No dead openings.

**Half the Hive.** Smoke End carries the notice (SEARCHERS WANTED... GET SCENTED FIRST), the Apron is a fine set-piece, and the Scenting House is the free gate to the Comb. The Smoker's Rest had no scene text at all ("Martin Sayle is here"). The cleric carrying office and the Merrit capping are far past ten choices; the lone-ranger routes are not findable in the first hour.

**Quiet in the Sky.** Larum's street opened with two long observations that did nothing: a gloss on Sixways curtain-cloth and a general remark about listening. Nobody says what the novice might do. Jory's house and the Hark are good rooms; paying six coin to go up is the first real act, at choice 6, after talk-only choices. The Whitlock lines are good but the street gave no reason to seek him.

**Patient Star.** The Shift Market had three sellers and seven exits and said nothing about the uncle or the debt. "Talk to Rusk" gives a stage direction with no content. The goal arrives only at the third interaction (Dagny in the hall). The thief's door and the clerk are far beyond ten choices. Strong later.

**The Lull.** The best arrival (the beech valley, the mill with its windows dark) is behind a wait. In Sixways the street is two screens of custom and the "LULL IN 2" board is the only signpost. The reader waits two days on trust. Street choices are almost all the same shape (talk, buy a pasty, ask). The sit-up-with-Hester route and Tobias are inside the valley.

**Thrume.** Three companions, a salt town, and a market square of one sentence with eight doors. The bounty notice is the only action. The Red Lantern ("people pay to sleep") is the adventure's real hook and the first screen did not mention it. Rooms are short and sharp (the Salt Office map with CLOSED crossed out is excellent), but the novice has no order to try them in.

**Hessel.** A farce of a spa. The pitch says there is nothing to do; the first three rooms (bridge foot, Quay Lane, Stall Street) are pretty and goalless. The hat on the peg in Hetty's shop ("or are you looking for him?") is the first thread, off the main path. The bard's voice fires in Stall Street. The banns, the real plot, were not mentioned until the Circus.

## Cross-cutting findings

- **Goals live in pitches.** In five of the eleven (Patient Star, Lull, Thrume, Hessel, Quiet) the first room did not restate the pitch's goal. Objective lines mostly appear only once a fact is known.
- **The new material is rarely signposted.** No calling-unique beat (thief leaf, cleric office, requiem) shows in the first ten choices of a lone novice, and none of the lone-company routes is advertised either. Hive's notice and Winter's Cross are the model of a signpost that works.
- **Text walls:** Hollins street (260 words), Larum street, Sixways street, Smoke End, the Apron. All are good prose; only Larum and Sixways carried material that was not scene.
- **Dead openings:** Gosse's wagon; Patient Star's Rusk, Ezh's aunt and barrow boy `Talk to` lines are stage business.
- **Choices that are all the same:** the Hospice at Ombra (six "Talk to"), the Lull street, the Hark (eleven asks).

## What was changed (Kyr only)

- **Patient Star:** `quell.kyr`: new observe `square_hook` on the Shift Market (your name and twenty-eight crowns on Dagny's board, she has sent twice, Garret held eleven months, the crew goes in three or four days); the barrow boy's role reads as a role.
- **The Lull:** `sixways.kyr` street scene points at the Fold-stone and the Waiting Woman's window; `fellowship.kyr` curtains observe shortened; `acts.kyr` objective `obj_wait_for_lull`.
- **Thrume:** `saltgrave.kyr` market scene names the Red Lantern, the envoy's table and the miners' row.
- **Hessel:** `city.kyr` observe `bridge_foot_banns` (the bill: Wenlock and Cherrett, third time of asking, "If any of you know cause") until the banns are done or withdrawn.
- **Quiet in the Sky:** `larum.kyr` street scene gives the job (nobody goes up but the guide; somebody should find out why it stopped); `fellowship.kyr` cloth observe trimmed to two sentences.
- **Half the Hive:** `country.kyr` scene for the Smoker's Rest (the SEARCHERS slate).
- **Book of Faces:** `warrant.kyr` the sergeant points down the Cuff to where the Registrar lies.

## Still weak

- Lone novices still cannot reach the headline calling beats (Book of Faces thief, Carth cleric, Gosse's cleric, Hive cleric); the lone-company routes exist but are not signposted.
- Hospice, Hark, Counting Row: more asks than difference.
- Patient Star's sellers say nothing; the Lull's streets are still talk-and-buy.
- Gosse's wagon examine is a dead action; left as it is.
- These notes cover the first fifteen choices of each, not the whole game.
