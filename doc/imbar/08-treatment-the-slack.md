# 8. The Hours: a creative treatment, and what of it is playable

*The ambition is a protagonist who begins with a provincial problem, finds themselves aboard a ketch seeking an
island, and later discovers that the same experience of departure, danger, fellowship and the unknown can occur in
a ship that sails the pause between counts. This document is the design of that ship and her place, followed by an
honest account of how much exists in play. Status labels as in `02-history.md`.*

## 8.1 What the Mathematical Age may have found **[P]**

Not a technology. A **remainder.**

Halm says the makers "made things to answer questions, by number". The Reckoner counts what is bound. Let this be
the working premise, one among the commission's several and chosen because it grows from the corpus: *their
discovery was that a sufficiently exact count has a remainder*: what the count cannot divide. For a people who
counted everything, a remainder is not a philosophical matter: it is a heap of things (a river's bend that was
straightened, a language that stopped being spoken, a winter that did not end, a bird that remembered it) that
exist and are in no column. Everyone who counts finds them. The Mathematical Age's distinction was that it **found a
place for them**: the interval between one count and the next, the **slack**. A ship that carried the remainders into
the slack would keep them out of the count and therefore out of the way of the Fall.

Why a ship? Because the remainders could be carried in no other thing: a place stays in the count; a vessel is the
only thing whose location is *its own business*. The Age's habit was to make the answer to a question into an
engine; the answer to *where do the leftovers go?* was a hull.

This is a theory a person in Imbar could hold, and it is not asserted anywhere as fact. It is what the ship says of
herself, through her Captain, who has been told it by his mother. **[D]** The Reckoner of the Second Watch has
doubts. The Collegium of Lesser Instruments would regard it as a hypothesis worth a society. Nothing says why the
Age ended, or whether the ship was launched before the Fall or because of it.

## 8.2 The Plane of Time, and what it is not **[P]**

There is no Plane of Time in the canon (`data/imbar`); I did not add one. There is the Keeping in *The Patient
Star*, where every moment of Imbar's past is kept as a place and one walks inward into the past. The Slack is a
different thing, and the two are **not unified** here: the Keeping is the Virina's tally of *moments*; the Slack is
the Age's bookkeeping *interval*. A reader who wants them to be the same may; a reader who wants them to differ may
that too.

What distinguishes the Slack from ordinary planar travel:

- **Its barrier is order, not kind.** Passing between the planes needs the right door; passing into the Slack needs
  to have the right *account*: a reading (what it says), a measure (how it is divided) and a standing (who answers).
  The black square asks for all three because a count must be *set* before it can be paused.
- **It charges interest.** Time spent below is paid again, in full, on the stair back (`tariffed`). The count keeps
  its books. (Only the announcement is implemented: the world's clock runs normally; see §8.7.)
- **It binds those who vouch.** A harbour wants a surety, and a surety is a legal object (*custodia*, the Aeneans'
  gift to law): the House that stands behind the Watch answers for the keeping named, or for everything.
- **It has an edge.** The pin on the count-room chart has moved by a hair. The slack is thinning. Whatever it is
  that the count will do when it closes, it will do without malice.

## 8.3 The ship **[N]**

**The Remainder** (the Aeneans' gloss on her bow: *RELIQUA*). Long and low, the colour of old paper, one mast, sails
that are not cloth. Her deck is scoured level metal with a mast and a bell; the rail is warm; the notches on it are
in elevens. Below: the count-room (a table of rods and beads that is always being corrected, and a chart with a
single pin), the watch-room (twenty-two people, bedding patched, children's drawings of people on a shore, no one in
them with a face), and the hold of remainders.

*Why she survived:* because she was never in the count. *Who maintains her:* the **Slow Watch**, who are born
aboard and passed their duties like a coat; and the **Quartermaster**, a keeper of fired clay at the foot of the
gangway with a blank oval for a face, the kin of the Mower of the Long Man, waiting for a face to be fitted. *Who
seeks her:* the Aeneans, per the second roll (below); a Collegium; the count.

*Her people.* **Orrel Sabe**, Captain, old and patient, who counts to eleven and stops, and begins again, and means
the thing he says about a hand not being liftable off the count in the middle. **Tamsin Quaye**, Reckoner, young,
inked to the elbows, who has been up eleven nights checking a pin and has decided that harbour is not
cowardice. **The Watch**: twenty-two, who want nothing and are afraid.

*The hold.* A bend of the Lune with a heron in it (REMAINDER 4). A winter in a frosted chest, REMAINDER 7, that the
Captain is afraid of. A grey tern in a wicker cage that says one sentence in a dead tongue eleven times and a
question. A card on each in a hand that has been copied and copied.

*The two rolls.* The first is in marks no one reads. The second is in the Aenean hand, forty names, thirty-eight
struck through, and says a Prefect came aboard with his household at the Counting and was lost. The Captain thinks
it was written by someone who was there. The Reckoner thinks the Watch wrote it to explain the Aenean names on the
bulkhead. **I did not decide.** The disappearance of the Aeneans is therefore *not explained*; one of the
accounts, offered by a doubtful source, gains a physical trace.

## 8.4 The Aenean interpretation **[P]**

The Aeneans' achievement was administrative: they **wrote law about keeping what they did not make**. At Sennet
they found a black square and built a House over it ("the House of Doors was built over the Aeneans' own"); they
cut HORARIUM on it, *the hour-place*, and under it QUOD RESTAT CUSTODITUR, *what remains is kept*. They did not
understand the square and did not need to; they learned that it answered to a formula, and wrote the formula down:
*non dominium sed custodia*. The same formula is in a Prefect's writ in a dale in the north, and the same
numerals are in a keeper's tally on a headland, and the same measure on a bronze table in a lighthouse. Their
empire was an auditing of inheritance; the Slack was the largest thing in it that they audited and never looked at.

## 8.5 How earlier adventures matter, and how they need not **[N]**

The black square asks for three things; each can be had in several forms. **None is required of any adventure, and
there is a costly route round each.**

And a fourth, which is not a lock but a **term**: the Ninth Valuer's note on the law of a surety (What Tessary
Owes). Without it, standing surety for the Watch is for *everything*, and a boy brings an account every morning at
nine. With it, for the keeping named.

## 8.6 What it changes **[N]**

The protagonist learns, on a ketch, that they can lose a lamp. They learn, on a headland, that a phrase in a charter
was a keeper's column. They learn, in a dale, that a lord's card said *holds* where the writ said *keeps*. They learn,
under a floor in Sennet, that all three were the same sentence said by the same people about different things; and
they find that the people who wrote it are the people to whom the ship is a debt. The world is **larger and more
tender** than it was. It does not become simpler. A ship in the pause between counts has twenty-two people who
need bread.

## 8.7 What is playable, and what is only designed

**Playable, tested** (`test/the-slack.test.ts`, 8 tests, all in a generated world with the provinces installed by
walking to their gates):
- The black square, three locks, ten ways (reading ×3, measure ×3, standing ×4 — tested in two combinations
  and in the costly alternatives), opened to a stair.
- The Quay of Hours; the Remainder boarded by a gangway; her Captain and Reckoner; the two rolls; the Quartermaster
  waking when a grey face is fitted; the hold with its three remainders; the surety in the writ's formula and in the
  Collegium's name; declining surety; the world events `slack_opened`, `remainder_harboured`, `remainder_kept`,
  `mechanus_seen`.
- A voyage to the threshold of Mechanus (canonical `outer_mechanus_threshold`): twelve watches at the berth, the
  ship casts off, a company ashore waits forty-eight for her next call.
- The cost of a blind surety (three coin each morning) and its avoidance by the Valuer's note.

**Designed and *not* implemented** (and therefore not claimed):
- **The interest on the stair.** `tariffed` and `slackWatches` are written and announce themselves; the world clock
  does not actually jump. The engine has no way to advance the clock by a measure's value in an action's `Time`
  (I did not find one, and did not build one).
- **A second destination** (the Hour of the Prefect's boarding) and a third (a harbour of the Age as it was before the
  Fall). These are the stories the ship exists to tell, and I left them as scenes in the treatment, not in the Kyr.
- **The Watch ashore.** `harboured(remainder)` is recorded as a world event and charges the company; the Watch does
  not walk into Sennet. That is a quest, not a flag, and it is the next module.
- **The hold's remainders as persistent consequences.** The tern's sentence is a written-down Discovery; the bend
  and the winter are hazards and wonders, not items you can carry.
- **A voyage among worlds.** There is one far station, a canonical plane's threshold. The ship does not navigate; she
  has two stops.

## 8.8 What stays unknown

Why the Age ended. Whether the ship was launched against the Fall or because of it. Whether the Aeneans came
aboard. What the count will do when it closes. Whether the remainders are a people. Whether the Slack is a plane at
all, or a kind of pause that a plane can contain. Whether any other ship is out there.
