# 5. Character dossiers

*For figures revised or newly used by this commission. Each: what the text now makes of them, what they want, what
they are obliged to, what they will not say, what history does to them, and what the revision changed. Quotations are
from the Kyr on this branch.*

---

## Aldous Venn, steward of the Hall (*The Long Man*)

**Voice.** A smile that arrives "a moment before the rest of him." Cheerful, ledger-ready, euphemistic ("old metal",
"lumber from the Long Folk's mill"). Under pressure: precise and ashamed.
**Wants.** To keep Dame Isolde's suit alive and the Hall hers. Eleven crowns a month is the suit; the Hall brings in
less; the only unused asset on the estate is a hill.
**Owes.** Sixty crowns to Cass Rowell, a letter a week to the Lord's widow at Marlow, three months' wages to Dunnet.
**Will not say.** That he has never read his charter. He was given a card.
**What history does to him.** The card under the frame says *the House holds the Long Wall and all in it.* The Latin
above it says *non dominium sed custodia.* Three generations of Halls have read *custodia* as holding. When the
company reads him his own charter he does not argue: "We have been taking a wage for a job nobody told us we had."
He is not a thief by temper; he is the fourth steward in thirty years to be handed a card.
**Revision.** New topic on the Lord and her suit (`venn_lord`); two new endings to the charter conversation, one
alone (he writes that night "to say I have been a fool in her service"), one with the reeve behind him (he gives
up the key; Dame Isolde "will be ruined, and she did not know"). Unchanged: the ledger path.
**Do not.** Make him redeemed. The Hall's study at the end is quiet, with one long letter half written.

## Mother Ysolt, priest of the Chapel of the Ford

**Voice.** "Eyes of someone who has buried half the parish and christened the rest." Dry, exact; the register is her
instrument.
**Wants.** The mask of the Mower, to bury it with the words for the dead and be done with the king.
**Believes.** The font is a king's tally of sins and the parish has prayed him down for two hundred years.
**What history does to her.** She is the best reader in the dale, and she reads the charter standing, twice, the
second time with her finger under the line. She finds the pun her parish cannot ("Murus Longus… say it quickly in
the mouth of a dale that never spoke Latin, and where do you come to?"). "I have been christening children on the
Wall for twenty years and calling it a king." She recognises *Hesci* from the register's Hesks.
**Revision.** The charter topic; the register's second leaf (the bell given once before); the bell topic, in which
she weighs "a bell bought with the pennies of children" against "a parish that had decided what a bell was for"
and asks for it from the font. She will not forgive herself for a year. Previously she was the person who had to be
shown the notches; now she is also a woman who has to choose what the parish gives up.
**Her cost.** The bell. After it, the sexton ties a rag to the rope "to remember where it was."

## Gammer Coller, dyke-reeve

**Voice.** Acerbic; the cat, the pipe she does not smoke, "the way she would look at a ditch." She has a rhyme.
**Wants.** To be left to her ditches. Does not know she has a second job.
**What history does to her.** The rod "that every reeve has had since there were reeves" — black wood, notched
bronze that matches no measure she knows — turns out to be the dale's check on the Hall's keeping and the key to the
Water-House wheel. Her office is older than the Hall's title. "I thought I was to keep the ditches. Forty years I have
kept the ditches and been thanked for it with a pot of lard at Michaelmas." She is seventy-one and takes the rod to
Venn herself.
**Revision.** Two topics (`nell_office`, `nell_remedy`); a changed scene with Venn. She also supplies the line that
declines the thought of a "new House": "There is no other House. There is Dunnet."
**Dilemma left.** She is the person who can legally act and has no way to enforce. The parish choosing a man is the
only enforcement the writ has.

## Dunnet, foreman

**Voice.** Stoic, slow, loyal, owed three months. Afraid of what stood up out of the floor and took Pell.
**Wants.** To be paid; not to go back through that door.
**What history does to him.** The charter's wage — a sheaf in forty — is his to be given if the parish chooses a
warden, and the first person who suggests it to him is a stranger. He answers: "Keep it. Not own it. Not sell it."
and asks for it to be said at the font, "where I can be held to it."
**Revision.** A new topic (`dunnet_warden`) that retracts `serves(dunnet, venn)`. Previously he could be bribed or
doctored; he can now be *asked to be someone*.
**Unresolved.** He does not become a companion. That would be the natural next step and needs the follower
machinery (`Joins`) to be written for him.

## Cass Rowell, master of the *Heron* (unchanged text; new fate for her cargo)

Grim, fair, "my father was under a weir on the Lune when it went". She will not carry a weir downriver in a sack if
she has been told what it held. If she has *not* been told, the bronze goes to the factor of the Founders' Exchange
in the biggest town in the country, and from there south.

## Loveday Kitto, keeper of the Holm Lantern (*Fair Weather*)

**Voice.** A small old woman in a grey shawl who polishes by touch. "Oh, there it all is," on seeing with spectacles.
**Wants.** To keep the Lantern until she can't, and then some more. She has told no one she is going blind.
**New fact.** Her mother said you sit the third ebb on the cell's bench with whoever the sea gives back, and say
nothing of what they were. She took it for a sailor's superstition.
**What history does to her.** She is, by the oldest reading of the bench, not the Court's servant but the Court's
predecessor. She does not know this and is not told it in her own voice; the company reads it to the Court, and she
says "Third ebb," to nobody. A very old man at the back says it with her.
**Revision.** `loveday_bench`; her presence at the Court after the tally is read. Her quiet answer when Hender
puts down his pen is the new scene.

## Parson Hosken

**Voice.** Clerical, gently ironic, preaches against what nobody reads. Offers a verse for the drowned, "nobody came
for three weeks."
**What history does to him.** He lies on the cell floor in his cassock with a candle for a quarter of an hour, and sits
up with stone-dust in his hair: "A hermit does not count ships." He had preached a hermit for twenty years.
His wish ("I should like, this once, to know their names") and the tally's last line ("THE KEEPER SITS") are
the same wish.
**Revision.** `hosken_tally`; an after-court line in which he turns the church chalk-board to its back and begins
the Constancy's names with a space beside each for the day the sea gives them back.

## Hender Brask, Reeve (unchanged; new line)

His denial that the Court relieves what it did not appoint is the only Reeve line added: "The keeper keeps. The Court
notes the tally." It is what a man says who has spent forty years oiling a charter and whose ears go red when he
cannot lie.

## Teodor Halm, Collegium of Lesser Instruments (*Low Water*)

Manic, correct about almost everything, wounded by looting. The new scene (`halm_tray_ask`) gives him the opposite of
his usual joy: a tray of Hesk teeth he paid double for and wishes he had not. "Twelve. Each a voice, each pitched to
the next. They come out of a wheel, and a wheel with twelve teeth missing is a wheel that has stopped doing something."
It is the only time he asks the company for help rather than lecturing it.

## Generated figures: the factor and the carter (the Founders' Exchange)

**What they are.** Deem casts them. In five worlds the factor was Taklinn, Naillil, Arannis, Valen, Jeremiah (names of different kinds;
no pronouns in the module, because Deem does not promise one). The factor's one fixed line is "I do not ask where a thing
was, only what it weighs"; the carter is a role, "who goes south on the first fine day of the week with whatever the factor has bought".
**What they want.** The factor: stock, a lot to sell on. The carter: his run.
**What they are not.** A conspiracy. The Exchange is a trade. Its customers are foundries and, for lots that ring,
a learned society in the south "that pays double." Their ethics are the market's.
**Honesty.** These are *roles*, not characters; the proof of the handoff does not depend on their having inner
lives. A future author should give a factor one thing she is afraid of, and let Deem choose which.
