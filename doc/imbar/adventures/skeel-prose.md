# Skeel (Fair Weather): prose quality pass

Editor's pass on `data/adventures/skeel/*.kyr` (wreck, court, skeel, dog, holm, inish, skerry, craigs, reach, tide, kerensa, constance, keeper, endings, after). Text only; no ids, conditions or mechanics changed. The ledger `skeel.md` was left alone.

## What I found

The best-voiced of the four. Mother Jago (gossip as hospitality), Bartle Tonkin (walks backward; "which from Bartle Tonkin is a speech"), Cuddy (bell; legs), Jinny Rowse (apple, knife), Parson Hosken (spectacles that get worse), Hender (numbers, "I have counted") and Kerensa (the mallet) each sound like one person, and the Wreck Court scenes have physical turns (the dog's paws on the open book; Nye pointing across the table; the ears going red "from the lobes up, like a boy's"). What needed doing was small: a tide-board room and a few weather Observes that were a clause long, and the house pause "a long time" standing in for stage business when a character is silent.

## Before / after

1. **The Holm in flood.** *Before:* "The sea is round the Holm, and laps at the turf." *After:* "The sea has come round the Holm and stands level with the turf. The sheep have gone up to the tower wall in a huddle, and one of them is eating the washing."
2. **The Reach in the dark.** *Before:* "You can hear the Reach, out in the dark, and not see it." *After:* "You hear it breathe in over the Strand and breathe out, and you know where the Holm is only by where the sound changes."
3. **The Sill, running.** *Before:* "Each step is a negotiation." *After:* "The current has your shins and wants the rest of you. The stones turn under your boot like loaves."
4. **The Sill, slack.** *Before:* "The water hangs still in the Gut, for now, as if it were thinking." *After:* "A strand of weed that was streaming toward the sea hangs straight down, and for a few minutes you could cross in your sleep."
5. **Hender, silent over the book (court.kyr).** *Before:* "He looks at the book in front of him for a long time, and then he closes it." *After:* "until the candle gutters".
6. **Piran and the mother (craigs.kyr).** *Before:* "does not say anything at all for a long time." *After:* "does not say anything at all, while the gannets go on shouting over them both."
7. **Ewan Marrock and the letter (skerry.kyr).** *Before:* "looks at the writing on it for a long time, and at the thumbprint in the tallow." *After:* "runs a thumb along the lines of writing as if they were a rope he was testing, and then fits it to the thumbprint in the tallow." (He cannot read; the first draft had him reading, and was wrong.)
8. **Kerensa on the night of the wreck.** *Before:* "She does not answer for a long time." *After:* "She does not answer. She is looking at the gig's name, and the mallet hangs from her hand."

## Speaking habits already distinct

Jago, Tonkin, Cuddy, Jinny Rowse, Hosken, Hender, Kerensa, Loveday Kitto, Constance Ferrar (ledger and pencil), Jack Cawse (earnest, large), Nye Gallow (a ship's boy: name, rank, ship), Ewan Marrock (big hands, few words), Ebrel Penhallow (says it all at once).

## Still weak

- "neither man nor dog nor cat" is used 7 times (it is the running refrain; one use in Jago's mouth, one in Hosken's, one in Hender's are the best; the Keeper's three could shrink).
- The long confession in the loft ("I put the sea in Loveday Kitto's oil") is the one place a character explains the plot in a paragraph; it is also the climax, so I left it.
- Court rules use "Skeel" as a collective agent in many lines ("Skeel sits and listens"); that is a voice, but it recurs.
- The three tide-board Observes (lag 1, 2, 3) are identical apart from numerals; an opportunity for a different painted sign each time.

## What a structural fix would need

Give the Court rules a second reaction channel (one named townsperson) so the loft is not always "the loft"; a different painted warning on the tide-board for each lag.
