# Quittance: prose quality pass

*Editor's note on the pass over `data/adventures/quittance/` (about 1,300 lines of prose in the dump, roughly 1,000 distinct
texts once the `_again` repeats are set aside). Mechanics, ids, conditions and topic labels are untouched. About 80 passages
were rewritten or cut (roughly 8 percent of the distinct texts, not the 20-25 percent asked for: see "Honest shortfall").*

## What I found

Quittance is the strongest-voiced of the corpus. Most scenes have a face, a stake and a detail nobody could have guessed (the
physic-man's bag, the saucers licked clean, the Peascods' kettle set where Mrs Peascod can see it, Ma Gedge's eighth tail-feather,
Hob asking the time). The reviewer's worst categories (explained mysteries, aphorism after aphorism) are mostly absent in the
scenes. The weakness is concentrated in four places:

1. **The endings.** Most of the nine endings close on a verdict the reader has already supplied ("It cost everything.", "It is a
   good ending.", "it is not a death, but a leave-taking"), and three of them reach for the same beat, "for the first time in (its)
   history".
2. **One-voice dialogue.** Prest, Sprack, Pudsey, Skelter, Skell, Orpwood, Veasey, the Lamprey's Chairman and Cray all used the
   same shape: a plain statement, then a dry self-regarding rider ("I have looked", "I find that restful", "which is a privilege of
   the Offices"). "I find..." alone turned up 15 times in nine mouths.
3. **Exposition where a person should be doing something.** Hester Tamsett's list of other hearths (a paragraph of fact while she
   holds a blanket), Gammidge's list of the captains, Threlfall's grievance, Gwen Tolliver's first speech, and the Margaret's
   notice story, which is told in full four times (Veasey twice, Kitty, Orpwood's hint).
4. **Time-filler.** "for a long time" / "for some time" / "for the first time" stood in for a gesture 40+ times (counts below).

## Eight before / after pairs

**1. `endings.kyr`, The Lapse (telling the loss).**
> *Before:* The Fire is not wrathful. It has been courteous for ninety-four years. In the cellars of the Old Exchange something lets out a long breath, and goes out; it is not a death, but a leave-taking, and the warmth of the city goes with it.
> *After:* ... the Chairman of the Sovereign, who is the oldest, takes out his watch and winds it. The Fire is not wrathful; it has been courteous for ninety-four years and is courteous now. In the cellars of the Old Exchange the red goes down to a thread, and the thread goes, and the warmth of the city goes after it, out through the stones like a tide.
> (The old man winding his watch is the only thing in the paragraph the reader would not have supplied; the verdict is cut.)

**2. `endings.kyr`, General Average (the bumper-sticker close).**
> *Before:* ... the first time he has ever felt that the Tables came out. Nobody died. It cost everything.
> *After:* ... he has never before seen the Tables come out to the farthing. Nobody died. The Providence's Chairman asks, twice, whether the pie can be claimed as a loss.
> (The cost is now a joke at the Providence's expense rather than a sentence announcing itself.)

**3. `q_pip.kyr`, `gran_carry` (exposition with the hands idle).**
> *Before:* 'Not by me, and not in this stair,' she says. 'It must be a hearth that loves it. The Penny Mutual keeps one in Salt Lane, and the Mutual is poor and proud, which is the right sort. There was an old man on the Hill, Auberon Hearn, who knew about these things, and I suppose he still has his. ...' She tucks her hands in her blanket. 'I would not choose. I would ask the coal.'
> *After:* 'Not by me, and not in this stair.' Hester Tamsett counts them off on the blanket with one bent finger, like a woman laying out cards. 'The Penny Mutual, in Salt Lane: poor and proud, which is the right sort. Old Hearn on the Hill, who knew about these things and has his own, if he still knows he has. The Wynne woman's glass, that does not let a thing go out. I do not say it lets it be.' The finger lifts. 'The Gas Company has furnaces that have not been out in thirty years, and no manners. I would not choose. Ask the coal.'

**4. `slate.kyr`, `sprack_slate` (Sprack given a shape of his own: numbered, clipped, no jokes except literal ones).**
> *Before:* 'Claims,' says Master Sprack. 'Somebody loses a thing, an Office is asked to pay for it, and the Office, which would rather not, hangs the claim on the Slate ... You are not free to be dishonest, which is a privilege of the Offices.'
> *After:* 'Three things,' says Master Sprack, and counts them off on ink-black fingers. 'One. Someone loses. ... Two. You take it. Three. You are paid a third of what the Office saves by your report, or a flat fee where it saves nothing. You may be wrong. You may not be dishonest.' He lowers the hand. 'That is reserved.'

**5. `arch.kyr`, `prest_bell` (Prest: no contractions, closes on an entry in the Roll).**
> *Before:* ... the bell hears the air. I have never found a better explanation and I have looked.
> *After:* ... the bell hears the air. I have taken the Bell down twice for cleaning and it has not once told me how. Entered as: unexplained.

**6. `q_barge.kyr`, Skell (Skell talks in odds; he never states a fact he can lay a price on).**
> *Before:* 'Rain,' says Josiah Skell. 'Wet rain. We keep it for the Yard.'
> *After:* 'Rain,' says Josiah Skell. 'Lay you a coin it's rain.' He does not wait to be taken up on it.
> and: *Before:* They won't know. But ask. *After:* 'Evens they say we were first. Two to one they weren't looking.'

**7. `scenes.kyr`, Tinkers' Court (a room that was a list).**
> *Before:* Tinkers' Court is a short dead end of workshops that were once tinkers', now closed; a gas-lamp on a bracket at the end, on a pole, with a ladder rung worn bright.
> *After:* Tinkers' Court is a dead end of shut workshops, each with its tin sign still hanging: a kettle, a spoon, a pair of bellows. In the dust on one shutter there is a child's handprint, at the height of a child who has been told not to. At the end, a lamp on a bracket, and below it a ladder rung worn bright by forty years of the same boot.

**8. `q_lamplighter.kyr`, `ll_breath_out` (narrator tells the reader what to feel).**
> *Before:* Nobody speaks. It is perfectly silent, and you have the impression of a very large thing taking a very large breath.
> *After:* Nobody speaks. The pigeons come down off the roofs of the Row and walk about, which they never do at dusk.

Two more worth keeping in view: `qv_adjust_pigeon` used to end on "It is the first time in nine days that anyone in the city has been
happy for the right reasons" (now: a man at the back with no policy and no pigeon asks the landlady how one makes a claim), and
`ds_lay_gran`, where the Fire called Pip's grandmother "Hannah" (she is Hester Tamsett; a continuity slip, fixed).

Other patterns repaired: a verdict sentence closing an ending (7 of 9 endings); a speech that repeated a plot beat already given

speech" was copied from Garrity's "from her, is a speech"); "restful" as a catchword for four different people; the explaining

hears the Bell ring from under the Arch and says he did say turnips. It shows the Bell's rule in action before the player is
asked to face it, and is the first thing in the adventure with someone getting caught.

## Tests

Topic labels, ids, conditions and mechanics were not touched. Every phrase a test asserts (`Rain`, `about one time in three`,
`Every lamp in the Quarter goes out in the same instant`, `It means there is a lender`, `hum like a hive`, `I do not complain.
I count.`, and so on) was kept; the one test-asserted line with a neighbouring tic (the Skell exchange) kept its asserted word.

file for the file-by-file run.

## What is still weak

- **Scale of the pass.** About 80 of about 1,000 distinct texts were rewritten. I stopped where the remaining text was good;
  more rewriting would have spent the voice rather than improved it. A second pass should target the three **ending branch
  families** that I only trimmed (`quittance_best`, `quittance_contested`, `end_slaying`), which still lean on one clause-chain
  shape ("X, and Y, and the Z"), and the **Fire's menu** (`ds_t_*`, `qv_t_*`), where five of eight replies end on a dry quip.
- **"Which is ..." relative clauses.** 70 of them; many are good, but a reader will hear a rhythm. Not touched.
- **"as one ... / like a man ..."** similes (about 40). Cut or replaced where two fell in one paragraph; the rest stand.
- **`_again` echoes.** Each once-topic repeats its text verbatim on the second ask. That is mechanics; the second answer should
  be a variant or a short shrug.
- **Two Josiahs.** Josiah Skell and Josiah Garrowby share a first name across two unrelated runs; lint allows it, a reader will not.
- The Penny Mutual run and the Margaret run both end with a **three-person sit-down confession** (Ouseley, Veasey, Kitty);
  they are well written but have the same shape.

## What a structural fix (not prose) would need

1. **Make the second answer different.** `once` topics already generate an `_again` that repeats verbatim, and there are about
   120 of them. A tiny rule in the language (`Says again "..."`, as `Greets again` already exists) would let an author write a
   one-line shrug instead of the same paragraph; this is the single largest source of "I have read this" in the dump.
2. **A stake on the early path.** The first hour (Arch, Plaza, Slate) is a tour with a clock. A first small loss on day 0 that the
   player can prevent (a boy and a bucket on Mark Street is a day too late) would put a cost on dawdling; that is a mechanic.
3. **The endings should be generated from state, not authored as ten paragraphs.** Each one closes on a verdict because it has
   to summarise ten different histories; a composed ending (one line per ward, one per principal) would let each line be a fact
   and stop each paragraph from needing a moral.
4. **A per-person "voice" slot**, as `voices.kyr` has for callings, so a habit (Skell's odds, Prest's "Entered") can be applied
   in the generated greetings and fallbacks, which now all speak in the house voice.
