# The Patient Star: prose quality pass

Editor's pass over `data/adventures/patient-star/*.kyr` (campaign `the-patient-star`, about 480 distinct passages, all read). Roughly 25 rewritten: the description layer (the glass, the Undersun, the Corbel) is the best prose in this set and was mostly left; the weakness is thinner, and sits in the Ennick and Sorne rooms that are lists, in a few reflexive closers, and in the companion voices.

## Before and after

**1. Companion voices (five rules, `kept_season.kyr`).** Thief, cleric, ranger, warrior and mage each got a habit, as in the other two adventures: the thief reads the wage and the polish ("Left hand, season after season. That is a job. Somebody drew a wage for it."); the cleric tests an office ("Every rite ends. ... That is not a prayer. That is a question left standing."); the ranger counts ("Not one print inside."); the warrior wants a date ("Which means there is a day it arrives, and I can count to it."); the mage corrects herself ("I would say. One cannot dispel a masonry.").
- Before (thief): "'Every cleat is polished on the near side,' {?m} says. 'Pulled by a left hand, season after season. Somebody did this as a job.' ... 'and the second bolt is up in the lintel, where a man who is looking for a door would never look.'"
- After: "'Polished on the near side, every one. Left hand, season after season. That is a job.' ... 'And the second bolt is up in the lintel, where nobody looks. Which is where I would put it.'" (the tested sentence kept).

**2. A garbled speech.** Mirel at the cord: "'Not against them.' 'I will go to the holder's house now...'" (two quoted blocks with no narrator between). Now: "She puts the hood on. 'I am going to the holder's house, to see what you have done.'"

**3. Rooms that were lists.**
- Before: "A holder's house: tally cords hung on pegs by the door, felt hoods, sacks of clean sieved sand, and a map of the glass above the village drawn in charcoal on the wall, with knots for marks."
- After: "...and on the wall a map of the glass above the village in charcoal, with a knot of cord pinned for each of the held. The knots are grey with handling. One has been tied and untied so often that the cord has gone to fluff."
- The same treatment for the Warden's storehouse (the clerk "writes down every sack that goes in and, with less care, every sack that comes out"), the survey tower (shelf of logs "in one hand that gets smaller towards the end"), the vestry (hooks "worn bright where seven offices a day have hung seven kinds of key"), the taproom, and the watch-room (a cot "never slept in").

**4. Reflexive "looks at you a long time".** Nine instances in the speech layer are the same gesture. Amaru's are kept (he is slow on purpose). Elsewhere: "Ilis looks at you for a long time" became "Ilis puts down what she is holding"; "Ayzhe looks at you a long time" became "Ayzhe's hand stops on her knee"; Sorrel's "For a long time she says nothing" became "Behind her a clerk puts his pen down very quietly."

**5. Mance and Ruan sounding like the same exposition.** Mance (the prior) now speaks in the cadence of someone obeying a letter ("...and to be seen praying while I do."). Ruan, the Waiting's elder, lowers his voice as in a church ("What comes after is not more name. It is the thing named.").

**6. A cliché simile in a room that did not need it.** The sunk houses "lean into one another like drunks, doors hanging" became "lean on one another with their doors hanging, and nobody lives in them, and nobody has taken the doors."

**7. "for the first time" tic.** Sister Wenda's honey: "smiles for the first time" to "smiles, which she has not done since you came in."

## Pattern counts

- "a long time / a long while": 19 to 13 (the remaining are Amaru's slowness or the Remembrancer's silence).
- "as if / as though": 17 to 17 (they are mostly doing real work in the glass descriptions).
- Tested strings checked for every change: none lost (a script compares each quoted test literal that existed before with the current sources).

## Speaking habits

Dagny (numbers, chalk), Pike (blots a figure; fussy), Kerra (the deadpan list: "turnips, devout"), Bren (few words, stops), Ilis (domestic, formal), Ayzhe (counts), Amaru (vocatives, a clause per half minute), Saro (no contractions, exact), Aske (smooth, "I have it in a book"), the Remembrancer (bells, "Added", "True, and sufficient"), the Interlocutor (your own voice).

## Still weak

- The Concord scene is a table of "Show the Concord X" results of one sentence each; the six delegates have no lines of their own.
- The "this minute" shore scenes (five) share one shape on purpose; they read as a form, not as places.
- Quell and the Mutual hall are the thinnest rooms in the first act, where the player decides whether to bother. The best prose is hours in.
- Many Ask topics end on a closing dry line ("Nobody held is dying. He'll keep." works; "I tell them I keep an inn." works; the third and fourth in a row do not). Only the weakest were cut.

## What a structural fix would need

Delegates as Persons in the Concord with their own topics; a second line for repeat visits to the Undersun rooms (the stopped scenes read the same twice); text variants for Ask-again.
