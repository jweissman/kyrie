# 2. A working history of Imbar

*A working architecture, not a canon. Every claim carries one of five labels, and the label is the most important
part of the claim.*

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[E]** Established | Said in the Kyr or the catalog of an adventure as it stands in `main`, in a voice the text vouches for |
| **[I]** Intended | Stated direction in `doc/design/{ATLAS,VISION}.md` or the commission, not yet fully in play |
| **[P]** Provisional | My working hypothesis, chosen so adventures can agree; any author may revise it |
| **[D]** Disputed | Different people in Imbar say different things; both are kept |
| **[N]** New in this branch | Added to a played adventure by this commission (with the file) |

A fact in-world is also marked **(tradition)**, **(scholarship)**, **(local lore)**, or **(documentary)** when who
says it matters. *The Mathematical Age and the primordial cosmology are not assumed to be the same story.*

## 0. The reckoning problem

Adventures carry calendars 612, 1121, 1288, 1290, 1312, 1313, 2731 (with AB/BB), 3640, and "year 1144 of the
Drowning" **[E]**. No text relates them. **[P]** They are *local reckonings*: each country counts from an event of its
own (a drowning, an unlidding, a charter, a founding), as real calendars do. Authors should not reconcile them; a
traveller who compares them is the story. When a deep-time figure is needed, use years-before-now in round,
contestable numbers, as the characters do.

## 1. Primordial and cosmological inheritance

**[E/I]** Imbar's bodies, souls and workings are bound through the *prima materium*. Beyond it lie innumerable planes
or realities, separated by barriers embodied beings find hard to cross; portals sometimes connect them. Old
traditions (**tradition**) tell of a primordial conflict among divine and supernatural powers in which the world was
nearly unmade. The *Virina*, High Arbiters, were sent by the Allfather to broker an uneasy peace, separating the
contending powers among planes (**tradition**; "said by the Wise"). The *Varakiiri*, chaos-spawn beyond the planar
order, are linked to incursions, forbidden rites, monstrous constructions and ancient calamities. Nine *Kiritarun*
jewels engraved with sigils of doom are said to bind the Varakiiri hearts and to have been bound to the foundations
of the planes.

**How the adventures use these, and the epistemic status they give them:**
- *Low Water*: nine jewel-hearts lost when the Varakiiri were driven out; the ninth fell and made the Bowl
  (`citadel.kyr:108,157`, `wheels.kyr:83`); "the count is owed upward, to the Arbiters, the Virina"
  (`library.kyr:84`). Said by the Auditor and the Lector's stones: **documentary**, in a hostile voice.
- *The Patient Star*, *Let One Remember*: the Virina's Tally, Arbiters' Way, the Arbiter of the gate
  (`stairhead.kyr:94-95,174`; `act4_voyage.kyr:100`). Said by institutions.
- The Grand Tour and the planes: real doors, canonical thresholds (`data/imbar`).

**[P]** The cosmology and the Mathematical Age are *separate stories whose relation is itself undiscovered.* The one
bridge the repository already builds is Low Water's reading of the Reckoner ("IT COUNTS WHAT IS BOUND": it is a
census of what the prima materium binds). I do not extend it. The commission's rule stands: **no module may use the
Mathematical Age to explain a Virina, a Varakiiri or a Kiritarun**, and none should use a Kiritarun to explain a
Mathematical Age work. If an adventure needs both to appear, let them disagree.

## 2. The Mathematical Age

**[E]** *Before the Aeneans, who were before everybody. People who made things to answer questions, by number, and
made them so well they still run* (Halm, `halm.kyr:42`; the Collegium of Lesser Instruments, which collects "working
things"). Their marks are not letters. The Reckoner under the Mere has run "three thousand years" and counts, by
water, what is bound (`rim.kyr:167`; `halm.kyr:45`). Their works are *shaped like questions* and are cut so exactly
that Halm says no Aenean could have made the joints (`halm.kyr:36`).

**[I]** (VISION.md) A civilisation or succession of civilisations whose mathematics, computation, physics and
engineering were nearly incomprehensible, whose works the present age experiences as magic.

**[P]** Four design rules for authors, chosen so the Age is a *world* and not a prop shop:
1. **A work arises from a premise, not from a style.** Pick *one* of the commission's hypotheses per work (new
   physics; computation involving other places; contact with an intelligence that sent answers without proofs;
   dangerous manipulation of reality's constants), and let the work show its premise: the Reckoner shows *answers
   without proofs* (it sings its question in Remainders), the Long Man's wall shows *something much plainer*.
2. **Its people were people.** A guild of question-makers had rivals, taxes, a canon and a quarrel about whether the
   answers were owed to anyone (the Auditor's unfiled "court that never sat" is the surviving trace of a lawsuit).
   *An advanced civilisation need not be a wise one.* A work that was botched, patented, abandoned or sold is more
   Imbarish than one that is simply glorious.
3. **Its works are not one catastrophe.** They fall at different times and in different ways. There was not one
   Fall; there were falls.
4. **Not every strange old thing is Mathematical.** The Hesk (below) are a deliberately *undetermined* case.

## 3. The Fall (or falls), and what they did to the world

**[E]** The Age ended; why is not reliably known. **[I]** (the commission) The fall *remade the world*: climate,
ecology, geography, habitability, communication between regions and planes. **[P]** Authors should prefer
*ecological* inheritance to *treasure* inheritance:

- A contaminated valley the folk call cursed, with an empirical local custom that avoids it correctly and knows no
  reason (use the Long Man's *font* for the pattern: a parish keeps a gauge as a liturgy).
- A machine that still maintains a river, an atmosphere, a field: **the Long Man's wall** is this, and it is the
  model, because it is *dull*. It cuts saplings.
- A drowned or dried region, a salt well that went brackish "the year the pilots went" (Skeel) — local history that
  *rhymes* with the Fall without being it.
- Species that did not exist before; fisheries and fens that are an engineering side-effect.

**[D]** Different institutions offer different causes. Low Water's Auditor says a creditor was never paid. A priest
will say a punishment. A scholar will say a mistake. Do not decide; let the *evidence* disagree with the
explanation each of them prefers (see the worked examples).

## 4. The long darkness and its recoveries

**[P]** Roughly the 1500–2000 years between the last Mathematical works and the first Aenean charters. Not a blank.
In what the corpus implies:

- **Survival as craft.** Knowledge became *technical custom, taboo, liturgy or profession*: the Gauge-font in Merrow;
  the Holm keepers' tally in Skeel (a notation nobody reads, **[N]**); Gammer Coller's rod whose notches "match no
  measure I know"; the Seridane granary formula misread as prayer (Patient Star).
- **Uneven recovery.** Some regions never fell far. Hessel (canals, a Season) has *no* deep layer; that is a
  statement, not a gap.
- **Real lives.** The Hesk reeves of Merrow, buried "in the year after the water" and thinning to one **[N]**
  (`charter.kyr`): descendants, not legend. Their disappearance is not extinction; it is marriage into the
  Collers.
- **Religions made of maintenance.** A saint who carries (Wade), a keeper who sits the third ebb (Skeel), a church
  that rings a bell given once before (Merrow).

## 5. The Aenean reformation of Empire

**[E]** A Latinate language (the `languages` package's `aenean` is Latin by construction; the generator names old
works in it and the folk wear the names down: `src/world/history/wearing.ts`). Aenean inscriptions on works they did
not make (Skeel's palimpsest of numerals; the Long Man's grate, "a thousand years" newer than the arch). Aeneans
as law-hand, as engineers of a tide-engine, as temple-builders, as drowned city, as pump-starters, as theatre
builders **[E, and mutually inconsistent in emphasis]**.

**[P]** What I think they were, so as to let authors agree without agreeing about the end:

1. **Their achievement was administrative.** They did not reinvent the Age. They *found* working things, learned to
   start and stop some, fenced the dangerous ones, and — their real invention — **wrote law about keeping them**:
   *custodia*, a keeping that is not ownership. A Prefect's writ in the Long Man (**[N]**), a plaque that says
   "this wheel turns" (Grand Tour), a keeper's tally that continues in their numerals (Skeel **[N]**), a theatre ring
   that licenses speech in the mask (Gosse's). Empire was *an auditing of inheritance.*
2. **They claimed continuity and partly had it.** A charter ("the dale shall render one sheaf in forty while the wall
   stands") continued an older arrangement. Whether the older arrangement was a gift, a rent or a duty, the writ does
   not say; it *renamed* it.
3. **Their language outlived them in three channels**: place names (Merrow < *murus*; the Long Man < *murus
   longus*: **[N]**), legal formulas (*neque homo neque canis neque felis*, a keeper's count that a later clerk
   took for a charter's whim: **[N]**), and scholarly Latin (Perrin Halyard "reads Aenean like a menu").
4. **The Aeneans were not biologically or exclusively one people.** "Aenean" is a language of administration and a
   claim of descent. Gaunt's Register, Merrow's reeves and Skeel's keepers continue through the empire's absence
   without being Aenean.

## 6. The disappearance of Aenean power

**[E/I]** Sudden, or experienced as sudden; a profound problem. **[D]** Competing accounts the corpus already holds:
- *Drowned city by a broken oath* (What Tessary Owes: "eleven hundred years ago"; Aen-Vessaly emperors; Oath Stone).
- *Withdrawal of the Prefects* (implied by the Long Man: the Hall holds a writ whose issuer has no successor).
- *A failed attempt to operate something* (Grand Tour tide-engine; Low Water temple "built over" the Reckoner).
- *Dissolution compressed into legend* (Gosse's "the Aeneans" as a stone with a curse / a price list).

**[P]** Keep all four alive. Do not choose; give each a different *physical trace*: a **drowned city**, **writs without
issuers**, **fenced machines**, **inscriptions read two ways**. Traces the present world can show:

- Roads connecting places that no longer acknowledge one another.
- Fortifications whose purpose is forgotten (Wrayford's bridge-gatehouse is a candidate for a *later* proposal).
- **Imperial law surviving in successor jurisdictions** — *every* wreck-law, tithe, pontage, liberty and
  weights-and-measures office is a candidate to be the fossil of a writ.
- Cities built over infrastructure (Merrow lives under a dam it believes is a king).

## 7. The present divided world

**[I]** Kingdoms, cities, peoples, faiths, guilds. Some claim succession from the Aeneans; some remember being
subjects and repudiate the memory; a long-running struggle may eventually try to reunite former allied peoples
against a gathering adversary (VISION.md). Whether to restore Empire is *a political question and not a moral
one.* **[P]** To make it a question, give each side a true thing:

- **For restoration**: the Prefect's writ kept a wall standing; the lords who inherit its payments forget its
  duty (Dame Isolde's suit, Venn's card). Without Empire's *auditing*, inheritance rots.
- **Against**: the writ also made a dale pay one sheaf in forty for eight centuries to a House that never
  looked at the wall. Empire's law outlived its own reason.

Both are in *The Long Man* now. That is the intended scale of the question: a sheaf.

## 8. What stays unknown (see also `06-workbook.md` §10)

- What the Mathematical Age *was for*.
- Whether the Hesk were its late hands or something plainer.
- Why the Aeneans vanished, and whether anyone did.
- What the Virina decided, and whether it was a peace.
- Whether the Kiritarun are objects.
- Whether the Mathematical Age and the cosmology are one story.
