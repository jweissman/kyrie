# Kyr: an artistic charter

What Kyr should be like to play, set down so that every part of it (the generator, the language, the narrator and
the page) can be held to the same idea. It is written for whoever builds or authors Kyr.

## The idea

Kyr is a book you walk around in. A particular person comes into a particular country with a reason of their own,
and the country was there before them and goes on without them. What they find is concrete: a dam that still holds a
valley under water, a family that has stopped speaking to its neighbour, a road that goes into a wood and comes out
somewhere else. The extraordinary grows out of the ordinary and obeys it: the machine has workings, the ritual has a
purpose, the ruin was built for a reason and used for others since.

The page should be quiet enough to forget. It should look like a well-set book, not like software.

## Voice

- **Concrete before atmospheric.** Name the thing: salt-houses black with tar, not "a gloomy town". One exact detail
  does more than three adjectives.
- **Selective.** Each scene says what makes it this place, now, and stops. Nothing is listed because the generator
  happens to know it: the census, a name's etymology and the weather report do not belong in the scene.
- **True to the circumstances.** A line about cold getting in at the gaps needs walls with gaps. The rain falls on
  the road, not in the taproom. A door that has been opened is described as open.
- **Spoken in people's own voices.** What someone says is set as their speech, where they say it. People are not
  quaint; they are busy, tired, afraid, kind, wrong. They do not explain the plot.
- **Ordinary life is told with care.** The salt-boilers, the toll-reeve and the woman at the silk-frames get as much
  attention as the dead in the barrow.
- **Not solemn by default.** Wonder, dread, tenderness, humour and plain oddity all have their place. Mystery is not
  a texture to spread on everything.
- **Promises kept.** A described thing can be looked at, asked about, used or fought. If the world cannot answer for
  something, the text does not mention it.

## Rhythm and attention

Different moments are different kinds of attention, and each gets its own composition on the page:

| Moment | What the page does |
|---|---|
| Arriving | The place is told in full the first time: what it is, what stands out, who is there. |
| Being there | The place stays on the page, short; what changes is what is new. |
| Speaking | The person and their words come forward; what you could say is what you can choose. |
| Travelling | The way is told by what is passed and how long it takes. Where the country is wild, a small chart of the known ways sits beside the choices. |
| Danger | Foes and companions with their condition in words, what each can do, and what each blow did. |
| Revelation | A great discovery gets room on the page and its own heading. It does not take the controls away. |
| Returning | What has changed since the last time is told first. |
| Preparing | The company, the pack and the journal open beside the page, never instead of it. |

A small moment is small: buying bread takes a line. A large moment is large: a drained valley gets a page.

## The page

- **Four functions, kept apart.**
  1. *The present situation:* where you are and what can be perceived. It is short, and stays in place.
  2. *The immediate exchange:* what came of the last thing you did, including what anyone said. It is replaced by
     the next exchange, not added to.
  3. *Intentions:* what you might do now, grouped as speaking, looking, going and seeing to yourself. Each group is
     short, a longer list opens in place, and choices do not move under the hand.
  4. *Memory:* the journal (what you know, what is unfinished, who you have met) and the chronicle (what happened),
     kept off the page until asked for.
- **The book's form.** On a wide screen or a tablet held sideways the book lies open. The left page is the world:
  the situation and the exchange. The right page is what you might do, with the chart of the way where it helps.
  Held upright, or on a phone, the pages follow one another: the world, then the choices.
- **Type.** Alegreya for the text, its small capitals for heads, Alegreya Sans only for small marginal notes
  (durations, prices). One accent colour, for what can be chosen. Paper and ink, with a dark ink-on-night variant.
- **No machinery on show.** No dashboards, no counters for their own sake, no repeated metadata. Health is said in
  words outside a fight ("hurt", "badly hurt") and in numbers only in one.
- **Motion** only where it explains: a new exchange fades in where it stands, and a journey shows its way for a
  moment. Nothing jumps, reflows under the hand, or scrolls away from the choices.

## Geography

- **The ground is real.** Every place stands somewhere on the land. Roads run through woods, along valleys and over
  passes, and the places on them are where they are. A forest is a stretch of country with its own ways in it, not
  three rooms.
- **Two scales of chart.**
  - The regional chart: coasts, water, woods and hills as soft shapes, roads as continuous lines, settlements as
    marks. Only what the company knows is drawn, and only some of it is labelled.
  - The way chart: the known paths around where you stand, as a small diagram.
  
  Neither shows the generator's grid.
- **Lost is a state, not a bug.** Leaving the path can lose you. The page then says so, and how you might find the
  way again. An unavailable way says why: unknown, barred, dangerous, or not there.

## Discovery

- **More than one kind of evidence.** History survives in inscriptions, but also in tool-marks a mason can read,
  alignments that work at one hour of one day, songs, the habits of a village, and machinery that does what it was
  built to do when you try it.
- **Understanding changes.** A thing is first seen as it looks ("a round tower"), then known by its name, then
  understood for what it was. Different people see different things in it: a priest knows the rite, a mason the
  stone.
- **Not a ladder.** A discovery can change your plans in any direction. Not every site points to a greater one.

## People

- **One person to begin with.** You are someone with a past in this country or a reason to come to it, and the
  country knows it: a mother at the inn, a debt to a merchant, a vow at a shrine.
- **Companions stay themselves.** Someone who joins you keeps their history, their ties and their opinions. They
  speak rarely, and when it matters: they know this village, they will not rob that grave, they want to see the
  library.
- **The world has intentions.** People with interests and means act on them, within limits. What you do has a second
  consequence as well as a first.
