# Getting cold play to 8–8.5

**27 November 2026 · evidence review and mitigation programme**

## Judgment

Kyrie does not need a larger world to reach an 8–8.5 cold-play result. It needs the reference experience to waste
less of the attention it already earns.

The strongest evidence is not the numerical score by itself. In the four 25 November plays, every player found a
reason to descend again: the drowned level and the paymaster's jar; the hall larger than the town; AQUAVIGILIA's far
water; or the cleared level newly occupied by ghouls. The scores nevertheless stopped at 6, 6, 7 and 7. The repeated
deductions were for:

1. a promise broken by the machinery (an unseen lethal threat, unsafe automation, death without an ending, a lamp
   that could not be lit, treasure that could not be sold, or a row whose recipient or identity shifted);
2. attention spent on traversal, light, pickup and menu administration rather than decisions;
3. danger and reward that were unevenly legible;
4. an expedition loop whose later trips did not keep opening new play;
5. conversation that rarely acted on the player or changed the next expedition.

The work since those plays has already removed important causes. On 27 November the page went from 191 of 201 resting
pages with more than one menu to none, and from 2,447 page-invented rows to none (OR-1490). Moves with no worthwhile
way in reach fell from a median of 14 per life to 1 (OR-1502). The reference dungeon became visible and directly
reachable (OR-1501). The town acquired an economic ladder (OR-1124), the automated delver began taking actual finds
(OR-1620), and rare finds acquired a buyer (OR-1621). These are material mitigations, not yet evidence of an 8: no
fresh blind round has judged the integrated result.

The immediate programme should therefore be **measure the current build; remove the remaining trust and attention
tax; give expeditions two through five distinct preparation, danger, consequence and reward; then judge it on the
rendered page.** Breadth, distant worlds and additional surfaces should not enter the programme.

## What the available scores do and do not establish

### The scored evidence

| Date | Player and path | Result | What it establishes | Limitation |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| 24 Nov | careful reader, rendered page, Iscoed | clarity 6; continuation 7 | The sea-gate mystery, honest odds and moral choice created real pull; hidden rows, clipped prose and continuity slips broke absorption. | It played the older Iscoed route, before compactification and the 27 Nov one-menu work. |
| 25 Nov | careful explorer, runner | 6/10 | A connected mystery can carry three expeditions; unseen lethal danger and an 11-move return path make continuation feel like work. | Runner, not rendered browser; the first run also met OR-680. |
| 25 Nov | impatient fighter, runner | 6/10 | Depth and improving returns sustained four trips; unsafe automation and death without closure destroyed trust. | Not the reference settlement (OR-682 at the time). |
| 25 Nov | cautious planner, runner | 7/10 | Preparation paying off, nonviolent answers and a connected dungeon were strong; unsellable loot and skill gates without learnable skills stopped the fifth-trip case. | Not the reference settlement; predated the reference economy. |
| 25 Nov | newcomer, runner | 7/10 | Search, traps, selling, restocking and a changed dungeon were understandable to a newcomer; trivial fights and row administration dominated. | Runner; stopped during trip two at the move cap. |
| 27 Nov | owner, rendered page | unscored | Found two menus, unearned surfaces, thin fetching, a hard-to-find dungeon and pointless wandering. These produced OR-1490–1505 and the compactified one-menu direction. | Not a blind cold play and not a numerical baseline. |

The 6–7 scores are therefore the correct **diagnostic baseline**, but not a controlled numerical baseline for current
main. Two of four 25 November players did not play the reference settlement; all four used the runner; three were
replayed after a local fix; and the large 27 November presentation and world changes had not landed. Claiming a
numerical improvement from those scores would be unsound.

### The score instrument is not yet settled

`doc/cold-play/rubric.md` proposes five criteria—beginning, agency, trust, discovery and continuation—scored 0–4, but
states that it has never been accepted or used. The beta cold-play brief instead asks separately for clarity and
wanting to go on, each out of ten. The 25 November reports gave one informal overall score.

Before optimizing a number, the project should adopt one record without erasing the useful old questions:

- retain **clarity /10** and **wanting to go on /10** in every report, for continuity with beta's existing method;
- score the five rubric criteria from 0–4 with cited presses or screenshots;
- report the rubric total as a percentage, not as a faux-precise conversion to ten;
- treat “8–8.5” as achieved only when the mean of clarity and continuation is 8.0–8.5 or better **and** the rubric is
  at least 80%;
- do not average away a trust failure: any encountered P0/P1 defect, softlock, lost state, unheralded lethal choice or
  false consequence invalidates the acceptance round and becomes a regression first.

An 80% rubric result is 16 of 20. A plausible target profile is beginning 3, agency 3, trust 4, discovery 3 and
continuation 3 (16), advancing to one additional 4 for 85%. Trust is the required 4. Discovery is currently the
closest criterion; agency and continuation are where the loop must earn the increase.

## Evidence synthesis by failure family

### 1. Trust failures erase more pleasure than ordinary weakness

The careful explorer found “a real mystery I want to finish,” then lost the whole company after ordinary-looking
dogs concealed bone hounds and “Press on to the end” ran twelve rounds. The impatient fighter called the answer a
“loud yes” until one automated fight and a leading rest killed the protagonist without an ending. The cautious
planner bought the dearest useful item available and could never light it. The newcomer was offered actions that
named or used gear the company did not carry.

These are not four content-quality complaints. They are failures of the common contract between row, state and
consequence. They affect the trust score and reinterpret earlier play: after one false promise, the player can no
longer safely infer that a clue, cost, threat or reward means what it says.

Much of the observed set has since been repaired or superseded: dangerous fights are asked before they begin
(OR-322); “Press on” is restricted to decided fights (CL-59); one authoritative row set and atomic commands hold
action identity; the reference economy buys valuables and rare finds; one menu now comes from the engine. Remaining
reachable trust work should be selected from the supported reference walk, not from the global defect count. In the
current register this notably includes:

- OR-1137: every change to coin, gear, wounds, notes or companions must be told in the receipt;
- OR-1138 and OR-127: the page's start and answer latency still make a correct response feel absent;
- the current knowledge-integrity and restoration rows named by the 27 November platform retro;
- any supported-slice P1 from `planning-instruments next` that the new baseline actually encounters, including blank
  or malformed prose and questions cut off on the page.

### 2. The loop still spends most of its moves on routine

All four 25 November players named the repeated way down. Expeditions cost 40–110 moves. The newcomer also named
light rows, pickup rows, per-member trap rows and per-item buy/haggle rows. The new quantity instrument confirms that
this was not merely a player's impression:

- OR-1440 first measured traversal and administration at 79% of moves after expedition one;
- correcting the delver's failure to pick up finds moved it only from 74% to **71%** (OR-1620);
- the design direction says this share should fall toward one fifth;
- the 25 November players liked the one-move retreat, but descent still replayed cleared rooms.

This is the largest measured obstacle to an 8. It suppresses agency—most presses do not express an intention—and it
makes every later reward cost more attention than the first.

### 3. The economic ladder now exists, but the fifth trip is spent

The earlier cautious planner could not sell any treasure. That exact failure should be re-tested rather than assumed
to remain: the reference town now buys valuables and rare finds, and its shelves extend from ordinary gear through
plate and healing to wands and rings (OR-1124, OR-1621).

The new instrument finds a subtler problem:

- after repairs, the s0 purse is 12, 27, 39, 37, 285 and 309 over five returns (OR-1520);
- the first rung opens after two expeditions and the second after five, within their intended bands;
- but the fifth expedition itself brings only 24;
- the second rung's top would still take about 20 expeditions and the long haul about 42;
- newcomers occupy cleared rooms but carry nothing to take; there is no level below the grave; valuable side rooms
  are not given a reason to be revisited (OR-1622).

Thus the economy is no longer absent. Its dramatic curve is wrong at exactly the acceptance question: the fifth
expedition does not reveal a fifth expedition's worth of possibility.

### 4. Danger is best when it is legible and preparation changes it

The best-rated combats were the she-bear, which visibly prepared to strike, and the ghouls, against which Bless,
Pure Beam, taunt, guard, Gust, Second Wind and a potion all mattered. The worst were trivial repetitions or sudden
lethal bands disguised as ordinary animals.

The current game has useful foundations:

- fights capable of killing the protagonist are now asked before beginning (OR-322);
- the reference shrine-keeper tells what the next depth demands, and the store sells the answer (OR-646);
- the fight frame now communicates injury, the acting creature and available actions at a glance (OR-1133).

What has not yet been proved by a current cold player is the complete experiential chain: **see the warning → choose
preparation → recognize its use in the fight → pay a real but fair cost → understand why retreat or victory was
wise.** OR-1440 still measures only two distinct preparations across five trips.

### 5. Conversation produces less pull than exploration

The cold reports voluntarily praised places, finds, companions' brief lines and material mysteries. They said little
positive about ordinary conversation. The negative cases were topic dispensers, questions without grounds, “much as
before,” goblin talk whose only result was Attack, and aims silently handed out by asking a generic question.

The design evidence is consistent:

- OR-149 asks for one excellent, readable conversation page;
- OR-165 defines conversation as an engine-owned dialectic in which the other person asks, tests, refuses and makes
  a situation;
- OR-423 has closed the first knowledge-gating class, so specific questions wait on evidence;
- OR-370 remains open because some first-town work is still handed out rather than uncovered;
- OR-424 remains open for one reflection that joins evidence into an insight;
- compactification requires conversation checks to grant experience.

This is not a request for a general dialogue system. One recurring person, one check, one changed relationship or
piece of access, and one later dungeon consequence would be sufficient proof.

### 6. Editorial quality is high at peaks and generic between them

The lines remembered without prompting were specific: the hall larger than the town; the paymaster keeping noon in
a jar; a child's shoe among offerings; the shaft never cleared; a companion binding a wound in Father's voice; the
cold breathing out of a cleared level. The weak material was equally consistent: generic scattered coins repeated
room after room, copied townsfolk, an unnamed enchanted object, garbled ways and a “wonder” yielding only cut stone.

The mitigation is not a general prose pass. It is to protect the contrast around each expedition's decisive find:
one exact image, one useful material result, one question, and one later echo. Mechanical or generative repetition
should be repaired at its source; bespoke prose should be reserved for the beats intended to carry memory.

## Intended mitigations

The following waves are ordered by dependency and expected score effect. Each is small enough to land separately.

### Wave 0 — establish an honest current baseline

**Purpose:** distinguish already-retired complaints from failures on current main.

Run one blind rendered-page session on the reference seed after the current main is green:

- careful reader at 1280×800;
- no route, source knowledge or implementation hints;
- creation through the page;
- five expeditions or a generous cap that does not itself stop expedition five;
- the existing log, screenshots, presses and revision record;
- clarity, continuation and the five-criterion rubric, each with cited evidence.

Then run an impatient player at 390×844. Do not run all personas before triaging the first: a repeated structural
failure should be repaired and lowered to a cheap test before more cold attention is spent rediscovering it.

**Acceptance:** a reproducible baseline on the current revision, not a target score. Every encountered failure is
classified as mechanical, design or editorial and checked against the register. Kit failures remain separate.

**Trade-off and call:** this spends a cold round before more construction. The call is justified because the only
scores available predate several large repairs; without it, prioritization would be based on stale exposure.

### Wave 1 — a no-bounce reference path

**Purpose:** make trust a 4/4 and remove reasons to remember the interface.

Mitigate only failures reachable from creation through the fifth return:

1. Every act that changes company state tells the change in its receipt (OR-1137).
2. Every lethal or resource-spending automation either proves the fight decided or stops before the material state
   changes; its account agrees with the actual rounds.
3. Death, defeat and retreat are explicit engine questions with a clear ending or continuation.
4. Offered gear, clues, checks and costs agree with actual ownership, knowledge and rules.
5. No blank paragraph, template fragment, contradictory weather/place line or clipped decisive prose is allowed on
   the reference walk.
6. Start and act latency are measured in the browser; a delayed response has immediate acknowledgement while the
   engine works (OR-1138, OR-127), without inventing a client-side phase.
7. Save and restore are sampled at the town, at secured depth and during one focused activity; restored offers and
   durable narration agree with the pre-save state.

**Cheapest proofs:** setting-independent receipt tests; the existing action/Outcome parity contracts; a seeded
reference journey asserting every offered action remains legal until the next command and every company-state write
is told; save/restore equality at the three boundaries; the contact sheet at both required sizes for prose clipping.

**Cold proof:** no encountered P0/P1; no report sentence of the form “the row said X but the game did Y”; trust 4/4.

**Non-goal:** clearing every P1 in the global register. Breadth failures remain night-lane evidence unless the
reference path encounters their structural cause.

### Wave 2 — compress the secured descent

**Purpose:** turn later expeditions from replay into return.

Use changes the player caused, not a teleport menu:

- the first expedition establishes or discovers the route;
- repairing the cage, leaving a rope, opening a shaft, securing a camp or recruiting a dungeon companion creates a
  durable staging point;
- on return, one engine-owned act takes the company to that secured depth while charging the same time, light, food
  and interruption risks that the traversed legs require;
- any new obstruction, repopulation, pursuit or changed route interrupts the compression and is played normally;
- the narration recalls enough of the route to preserve place without replaying every empty room;
- “go deeper” never bypasses an unvisited room, unresolved danger or meaningful choice.

Also compress administration at its actual sources:

- collect an unambiguous pile in one act and say who carries it;
- replenish a chosen quantity in one store act where price and capacity are already known;
- promote light decisions when darkness or remaining duration makes them consequential, not after every neutral
  transition;
- ask which member leads only where the member changes odds or consequence;
- retain the one flat menu outside fights and the four promoted rows.

**Instrument:** OR-1440's traversal-and-administration share. Ratchet it from 71% first to no more than 55%, then
toward 40%; do not claim the direction's eventual one-fifth until the instrument honestly reaches it. Also record
total presses and presses per consequential decision for each trip.

**Parity proof:** compressed and leg-by-leg traversal end with the same time, consumables, threats, world changes,
history and destination under the same keyed draws; interrupted cases fall back to the ordinary legs.

**Cold proof:** by trips three to five, no player names the walk back down, light-row repetition or pickup as a main
reason not to continue.

**Trade-off and call:** a shortcut reduces the felt size of cleared space. The call is to earn it through persistent
world change and preserve all material costs, because repeating solved inputs is not depth.

### Wave 3 — design five different expeditions

**Purpose:** make continuation arise from escalation rather than unfinished inventory.

Keep the same dungeon and arrange its existing and minimally added material into five jobs:

| Trip | Dramatic job | Preparation | Decision | Durable result | Invitation onward |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find and survive the mouth | light, basic gear, entry companion | press on or withdraw | entrance and first safe route secured | evidence of the older works below |
| 2 | Learn the dungeon's rule | shrine counsel; club/pole/fire as appropriate | choose how to answer the first resistant or mechanical obstacle | a mechanism repaired or passage opened | a specific threat and reward at depth two |
| 3 | Test deliberate preparation | the tool learned on trip two | spend, negotiate, retreat or risk the prepared answer | keeper defeated, bargained with or left in a changed relation | a side chamber and the deeper threshold become meaningful |
| 4 | Meet the dungeon after absence | supplies for a changed level | deal with newcomers, aftermath or a returning consequence | prior mercy, violence or neglect is read back | a new route, person or truth appears |
| 5 | Claim a genuinely new capability or stake | chosen from the economic rung reached | take, leave, promise, recruit or awaken something consequential | the town and dungeon both answer | a sixth expedition is imaginable for a different reason |

This table is a design check, not five forced quests. A wanderer may reach the beats in another order. The invariant
is that later depth changes available decisions, not merely hit points and treasure values.

**World mitigation for OR-1622:** satisfy its existing acceptance without simply placing a larger cache. The fifth
trip on s0 must bring at least one third of the best earlier take because the world creates a reason to return: a
deeper level realized, newcomers carrying something specific, a changed side room, or knowledge that makes an old
find accessible. Prefer a side branch made newly intelligible over a sixth linear floor.

**Preparation mitigation for OR-1440:** at least four of the five trips should have distinct preparation, and the
prepared thing must be used or consciously declined. Buying two torches on another day does not count as a distinct
preparation.

**Variety mitigation:** preserve the eight answer kinds now measured by OR-1445. Across the five trips include at
least one material manipulation, one conversation or bargain, one search/listen/read inference, one dangerous fight
and one chosen retreat or refusal. Do not force each into every seed; measure the repertoire across controlled seeds.

**Cold proof:** the player can summarize why each expedition differed; continuation reaches 4/4; a fifth trip is an
unqualified yes rather than “maybe, if walking and rewards improve.”

### Wave 4 — one legible danger arc

**Purpose:** convert combat from alternating triviality and surprise into a readable expenditure of preparation.

Build one complete level-two arc:

1. evidence above names the creature or at least its material nature;
2. the shrine-keeper or another grounded source says what ordinary weapons fail against;
3. the store, dungeon or companion offers more than one answer;
4. the encounter description shows the same danger the reckoning asks about;
5. the fight telegraphs one dangerous intention;
6. the prepared answer materially changes odds, cost or available actions;
7. retreat remains visible before catastrophe;
8. the aftermath says wounds, spent uses, death, surrender and loot exactly once.

Slight fights should be safely compressed by the same simulation. Fair, hard, deadly or mortal fights remain in the
player's hands. Automation stops when the reckoning, leader state or available resources materially change.

**Proof:** seeded comparison of manual and compressed slight fights; the level-two pin for warning → preparation →
fight → aftermath; a loss and retreat path, not only victory; cold readers identify the danger and whose turn it is
without being asked to decode the frame.

**Metric:** distinct preparations rises; no unexpected lethal encounter; at least one cold report names a tactical
decision or prepared answer, not merely that combat was quick.

### Wave 5 — one conversation that changes the dungeon

**Purpose:** give the reference loop a social peak comparable to its best environmental mystery.

Choose one existing person tied to the old woods or deep place. On one scrolling engine-owned conversation page:

- the person asks why the company is going below;
- two to four grounded answers express approach or character;
- one answer invokes a real calling, background, evidence or companion;
- one check says its stakes and awards experience;
- refusal is interesting and leaves another route;
- success grants neither a generic quest nor bare exposition, but changes a later price, route, companion response,
  warning, truce or available preparation;
- on return, the person remembers the relevant event and responds once;
- specific questions remain gated by what the character has observed or heard.

Use OR-424's reflection only if the conversation's evidence genuinely needs joining. Three pieces of evidence
producing one insight and one new act is enough; do not create a repeatable “hint” verb.

**Proof:** setting-independent engine-question conformance; knowledge legality before and after evidence; one check's
success and failure; experience once; later play differs; terminal and page receive the same rows and consequence.

**Cold proof:** a player describes the person as wanting, testing or deciding something; the conversation is named
among what worked; agency rises without adding menu breadth.

### Wave 6 — editorial contrast at the five beats

**Purpose:** make the new structure memorable without a broad rewrite.

For each expedition's decisive beat, require four authored or generated products:

1. one concrete image that occurs nowhere else in the five trips;
2. one find with a name, material and use, buyer or implication;
3. one line that changes the player's question;
4. one later response from a person, place or returning inhabitant.

Remove or vary repeated filler only where the controlled walks expose it. Generic coin text should be terse and
infrequent; the valuable narrative space goes to consequential finds. Generated people in one scene need a
name-distance check so Gek/Gesk-like pairs do not stand together. Weather-aware paintings must fall back to a neutral
painting rather than contradict the text.

**Proof:** prose-economy and identity pins for the repeated templates; contact sheets at 1280 and 390; a five-trip
beat sheet showing the image, find, question and echo actually encountered.

**Cold proof:** players independently recall at least two specific finds or images and do not cite repeated filler,
garbled navigation or copied people among their principal confusions.

## Verification programme

### Lower-rung gates before each cold round

1. **Contracts:** receipt truth, command atomicity, action identity, knowledge legality, save/restore, compressed-path
   equivalence and fight automation equivalence.
2. **Seeded reference journeys:** creation → mouth → five returns, asserting invariants every turn.
3. **Cheap players:** gold-follower, wanderer and the five-expedition delver. They are instruments, not judges.
4. **Rendered cold play:** careful reader, impatient presser, wanderer, gold follower and gold ignorer, on milestone
   revisions only.
5. **Human acceptance:** still outstanding until a human judges it; never inferred from agent scores.

### Controlled cold-play matrix

Do not vary every factor at once. Use the following sequence:

| Round | Seed | Kind | Viewport | Question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | reference seed s0 | careful reader | 1280×800 | Is the complete causal chain understandable and trustworthy? |
| B | s0 | impatient presser | 390×844 | Is the loop brisk and are key rows visible without patient excavation? |
| C | fresh reference seed | wanderer | 1280×800 | Does unguided attention find the dungeon and form an intention? |
| D | fresh reference seed | gold ignorer | 390×844 | Does the place offer worthwhile play without obedience to guidance? |
| E | fresh reference seed | cautious planner/gold follower | 1280×800 | Does preparation pay through five expeditions? |

Each report records full revision, seed, viewport, presses, expedition boundaries, chosen rows and screenshots of
each decisive beat. The round also records:

- seconds from Play to first meaningful act;
- moves to the dungeon mouth;
- presses per expedition;
- traversal/administration share after trip one;
- number of distinct preparations and whether they mattered;
- number of encountered fights by reckoned danger;
- purse and meaningful purchases on each return;
- distinct answer kinds encountered;
- encountered trust failures;
- clarity /10, continuation /10 and the five rubric scores.

### Exit rule

Call the cold result 8–8.5 only after **three consecutive milestone rounds** meet all of:

- mean clarity and continuation at least 8.0;
- neither clarity nor continuation below 7.5 in any round;
- rubric at least 80%, with trust 4/4;
- no encountered P0/P1, softlock, lost state or false promise;
- at least two player kinds willingly choose or plainly desire a fifth expedition;
- one phone round passes;
- the cheap instruments remain within their ratchets.

Do not require every persona to like the game equally, and do not tune prose to an agent's personal taste. Require
them to understand it, trust it and encounter enough causal variety to make an informed continuation judgment.

## Priority and expected score effect

The effects below are hypotheses to test, not additive promises.

| Priority | Mitigation | Evidence addressed | Expected rubric movement |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | Current rendered baseline | stale and non-comparable scores | establishes truth; no claimed gain |
| 1 | No-bounce reference path | every 24–25 Nov report contained trust or continuity failures | trust 2–3 → 4; beginning +0–1 |
| 2 | Secured-depth compression and batched administration | all four 25 Nov players; routine share 71% | agency +1; continuation +1 |
| 3 | Five differentiated expeditions; fifth-trip world response | OR-1440, OR-1622; every verdict was “maybe” | discovery/continuation +1 |
| 4 | One warning–preparation–combat arc | best and worst fight evidence | agency/trust +0–1 |
| 5 | One consequential conversation | OR-149/165/370/424; conversations rarely praised | agency/discovery +0–1 |
| 6 | Editorial contrast at decisive beats | remembered peaks versus repeated filler | discovery +0–1 |

The likely transition from 6–7 to 8–8.5 is not six points of improvement added together. It is the removal of one or
two catastrophic deductions, followed by one additional positive reason to continue. Trust and compression are
therefore ahead of conversation polish and new prose even though the latter may be more visible.

## Explicit non-goals

- No additional country, settlement, province or map surface.
- No general dialogue engine before the one conversation proves its obligations.
- No wholesale SRD catalog import; only mechanics met in the reference dungeon.
- No new inventory or journal surface; the one menu and earned surfaces remain the ruling.
- No larger treasure numbers as a substitute for a new use, choice or consequence.
- No authored shortcut that waives time, light, food or interruption.
- No score gained by narrowing the declared supported slice after a failure is found.
- No claim of human acceptance from cold agents.

## Recommended immediate queue

1. Run and triage the current careful-reader rendered baseline.
2. Repair any supported-path P0/P1 it exposes, lowering each to the cheapest reliable proof.
3. Implement the secured-depth return and ratchet routine share below 55%.
4. Close OR-1622 through a world response that makes expedition five productive.
5. Raise distinct preparations from two toward four and prove one complete level-two danger arc.
6. Build the one consequential conversation, using the knowledge gates already established.
7. Run the phone impatient round; repair visibility and pace failures.
8. Run the remaining controlled matrix and require three consecutive qualifying milestone rounds.

This order burns down observed obstacles before adding surface. Its central design call is simple: **the fifth
expedition should be about what has changed and what can now be attempted, not about walking through the first four
again.**
