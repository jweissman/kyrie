# Caught Below: the intended experience

Written before implementation. No engine vocabulary.

## At the river (first installation)

The company steps off the ferry at Reedwake. Wren keeps the ferry house on the quay. She meets the company at the door
because her brother Pell has not come back. At first light Pell went up to the old foundry on Censer Hill to copy the
flood marks for the ferry board. He promised to be back by mid-morning, and it is nearly noon. The river is rising.
By the middle of the afternoon the low culvert under the foundry will be under water, and anyone inside will have to
come out the long way, over the ridge.

The company now has a reason to go, a person to find, and a clock: the short way in will close.

Two ways lead into the foundry. The low way goes from the willow causeway through the drain culvert into the
workshop: quick, but it floods. The high way goes over the chalk ridge and through the foundry yard into the
vestibule: always open, but longer.

Pell is in the hoist room at the back of the works. A timber rack came down across his legs while he worked; he
cannot pull free. When the company finds him, Pell calls out before they see him. Reaching him is its own problem.
The casting gallery in front of the hoist room is a cave hound's den, and the hound will not let strangers past. The
company can fight it, soothe it if someone knows animals, or toss it some food and slip past while it eats. Or, if
they read the survey board Pell left in the vestibule, they will know of a cramped tram crawl from the dry stores that
comes out behind the gallery.

Freeing Pell has more than one answer:

- **Lever the rack** with an iron bar. There is one in the dry stores. It is quick and gentle.
- **Dig out under him** with bare hands. It needs no tool but takes the better part of an hour.
- **Heave the rack** by main force. It is quick, but the beam drops back as they let go. The lifter wrenches
  something, and Pell's leg is broken. Pell cannot walk alone after that; he can only go somewhere with someone's
  arm under his shoulder, unless someone in the company can set and bind the leg.

Once Pell is free, he wants one thing: to get home. If the company says "come with us", he goes with them, a step
behind, wherever they can lead him. If they leave him, he does not stand there forever. If his leg holds, he starts
for home on his own, by whatever way is open to him. He will not go past a hound that is still guarding, and he will
not take a way that has flooded. If every way home is shut to him, he waits where he is. If his leg is broken, he
cannot go anywhere alone.

The rack stays as the company left it: propped on the bar, dug under, or tipped against the wall with a split beam.
The bar stays wherever it was set down.

When Pell reaches the ferry house, Wren sees it. If the company walked him home, she thanks them on the doorstep and
pays what she can. If Pell came home alone because they sent him off, she still pays, but she says what she thinks
of it. Afterwards she remembers the company, and that changes how she speaks to them.

## In the hills (second installation)

The same situation installed in a different place and time. At Skellfold, a slate-quarrying hamlet in the first week
of snow, old Marrit's grandchild went up to the quarry workings to fetch the tally-board before the snow closed the
quarry. They have not come back. Here the short way is the quarry lane up the beck. The snow is drifting in, and by
evening the lane will be choked; the long way is the shepherds' track over the fell. There is no hound. Instead,
inside the workings, a rotten ladder is the only short way down to the lower gallery. Someone has to trust it, brace
it with a line, or go round by the incline. The fallen weight is an ore tub off its rails, not a timber rack. The
tool is a crowbar in the smithy, not in the stores.

The people, the place, the danger and the timing all differ. The causes do not: someone is pinned; freeing them can
be gentle, slow or harmful; the freed person tries to get home and reacts to what is in the way; one route home
closes with time; and the relative at home reacts to how it ended.

## What this must not need

- A sentence telling the player which commands exist.
- A sentence promising that a state persists.
- A person who stands still, waiting to be moved like luggage.
- A way that is dangerous in one direction and harmless in the other.
