# Cold play 1 (careful explorer) — Iselde, elf bard (archivist), report
**Expeditions made: 3.** **Would I willingly make a fifth expedition, knowing what happened on the first three? Maybe, leaning yes — the drowned second level, the notebooks pointing at a water channel by the spring, and "the paymaster keeps noon in a jar" are a real mystery I want to finish. But the 11-move walk from town to the mine each time, and a party wipe I couldn't see coming, make a fifth trip feel like a chore.**
**Score: 6 / 10**

## My path
T0–25: Made Iselde (elf, Lantern-born, bard, Learning/Archivist). Gave priestess Ian the brothers' letter (got the shrine's rope). Heard about the opened graves/Elmspire, the two chronicles, and the boundary-stone quarrel. The castellan offered 300 coin for the first king's crown from Nightking (20 coin advance). Eogan told of the woods' old stones and the jar of day. Hired Jeremiah (bard). Found the moved stones' old holes by searching the inn garden and faced Bryce down (300 xp). T26–113, expedition 1: woods → fallen stones → a stair down into an old Delvers' mine. Hired Dunstan (warrior) on the ledge, found a cache, talked past goblins, made traps safe, read two notebooks, read SEALED with my glossary, opened a lamp/pick/wheel strong-box, killed two noble hounds, and read the sealed-stair inscription. Then I attacked three skeletal "bone hounds", picked "Press on to the end", and the whole party died. T114–171, expedition 2 (began again): ran the same route faster, broke the wall at the sealed stair, and found the second level drowned to the roof. Walked out in one move and sold gems and ingots (90 coin). T172–198, expedition 3: went back down, fought a "blasting charge" at the shaft head, freed the winding wheel (the cage now works), and walked out at the move cap.

## What I understood
It's a text RPG: you pick numbered rows, and every place has looks, searches, people and objects. The settlement (Reedmere) has a shrine, a store, an inn and small local mysteries. The old woods hold a barrow whose stair opens into an abandoned Delvers' mine. That mine is the deep place: an adit level, a haulage level, then a flooded stair down to a second level that must be drained. Traps come with several ways to deal with them (pole, lifting, rites, reading the carving). Loot is gems, ingots and rings that sell in town. "Travel" takes you straight out to the landing from deep inside, but stops when a danger is present. Death offers "begin again in this country, from its first day". The world was deterministic (the same fights played out the same). What you know lives with the character: in life 2 the path to the stones only showed up after I searched the burners' hut, because I hadn't asked Eogan.

## What I wanted
1. Read the old writing for the brothers. I read three inscriptions, one of them how the makers ended ("THEY SANG THE LAST OF THEMSELVES INTO THE STONE"), but the aim never moved. I think Musicarch is a different place in Flintcrag that I never reached.
2. Find the "jar of day/noon". I learned the paymaster keeps it, but the paymaster's strong-box held only a ring. Not found.
3. Drain the drowned level (my own goal, from the notebooks). I freed the wheel, but the water stands. Unfinished; next I'd try the dry channel by the spring.
4. Settle the boundary-stone quarrel. Done, and satisfying.
5. Bring up the first king's crown for the castellan. I found out that Nightking is somewhere else on the map, not this mine. Not attempted.
6. Survive with a weak party (two bards). Hiring Dunstan helped. I died once anyway.

## Confused, bored or stuck
1. T5: Ian names "the lone tower you heard of: Elmspire". I hadn't heard of it.
2. T9: "Ask the castellan again" gives "The castellan tells you again, much as before." The move was wasted, and he never says where Nightking is.
3. T11: Musicarch and Marblegate get the identical description, "a smooth doorway in a crag in Flintcrag".
4. T25: the places lens shows raw ids as labels: "travel:region__s1_wood_edge", "travel:region__s1_taproom".
5. T31/T33: searching the clearing and the stones prints only "you search the …" with no result, not even "nothing here".
6. T35/T42: the stair room says "your light goes out into nothing. Below you is a hall that could hold the whole of Reedmere", yet it and the adit mouth also say "Spoil heaps run down the hillside… a timbered mouth opens into the hill". I couldn't tell if I was underground or outside. The exit label "Into the old dead under the old woods by the way in" is garbled, and "Out to the stair" is used for a way down.
7. T35/T38: the beam reads LAPISORATIO in the old script. As an archivist with a glossary I expected to be able to study it there; I couldn't.
8. T46, T56, T140, T145, T162: "Samwise sets their shoulder to it and heaves." My company is Iselde, Jeremiah and Dunstan. There is no Samwise.
9. T44–45: after I talk to the goblins, a row with a talk id reads "Attack the goblins", listed twice. "The goblins are here" is never described.
10. T42/T45: the "enchanted object" is never named or described, and its options mention a pole before I knew I had one. I only learned my starting kit (glossary, pole) from a "Put in a rotted pack" list at T57. The things lens shows only room items, not what I carry.
11. T53, T79, T127, T141, T151: ids shifted between turns, so I got "not offered" errors: "Gather up the coins", "Prise the planks", "exit:…hall" vs "exit:…hall/0:Go", and the direct "To the fallen stones" disappeared in life 2.
12. T87–92: the lamp/pick/wheel strong-box had no clue I could find. I opened it by trial and error after a failed "clang".
13. T98: a trapped switch guarded an empty store. "The iron tools are stacked neatly", but I couldn't take a bar.
14. T100: the build-up "something large, that is not in any hurry" turned out to be two 8-hp hounds with "armour" that gets "torn open".
15. T110–113: "curious bone hounds… long-legged rough-coated hound, grey-brindled" described as ordinary dogs, but they were skeletal hounds with 18/14/14 hp against our 6/6/12. Nothing signalled the danger. "Press on to the end" ran 12 rounds to a party wipe without stopping when anyone fell. The ending says the last blow was "for 2"; the fight log said "for 6".
16. T114: "Begun again, the country… remembers you". I saw nothing remembered. Jeremiah now spoke Dunstan's line.
17. T151: the torch went out and the only light offered was a candle. Jeremiah held "a hooded lantern, oil still in it" and a lamp, and I held oil, but there was no row to light any of them.
18. T176: buying a lantern gave it to Jeremiah, and leather armour went to Dunstan, who already wears scale. The recipient changed silently.
19. T193: "Attack a blasting charge… A blasting charge comes at you, as it was made to." I fought a bomb with daggers. It "goes down" at 4/8 hp, then "Iselde looks away solemnly."
20. T196: the notebook said a bar under the axle would free the wheel. I bought a crowbar for that, but the wheel came free from beating the charge, and the crowbar was never used. At the sealed stair it "finds no purchase".
21. General: the town-to-mine walk is about 11 moves each way, with no shortcut, so a re-run after death and every new expedition cost a lot of moves.

## What worked
1. The prose of places and found writing: the shrine's ivy wreath, the landing's salt and tar, "Nobody in Reedmere knows it is here", the child's shoe among the shrine offerings, and the tally-board's "The shaft was never cleared."
2. The two damp notebooks set up a multi-part environmental puzzle (rusted wheel, bar under the axle, the water wheel, the dry channel by the spring). It made me want to come back.
3. My background mattered. The glossary of the old script showed up in the lore roll (19+2+2+2 = 25), and I read SEALED and the sealed-stair inscription: "IT CANNOT BEAR THE NOON. THE PAYMASTER KEEPS NOON IN A JAR." That was the high point.
4. The boundary-stone mystery was small and complete: rumour, then search ("your heel finds the old holes"), then confrontation ("Bryce goes red, then white"), then reward.
5. Traps offered several sensible approaches. Poling the life drain from a distance felt clever.
6. Turn-by-turn fights read clearly (Inspire, shoot, Sweeping Strike), with nice touches like "Jeremiah chuckles: 'That was fun!'"
7. "Out to the landing" in one move from deep inside, stopping when danger is present, is a good return.
8. Small helpful warnings: "It burns oil, and none of you carries any."
9. Brewing a minor healing potion from two herbs.

## Surprise
The barrow in the old woods opened into a vast, forgotten Delvers' mine instead of a tomb. And the second level was simply drowned: water to the roof, "Your light goes into it and does not come back". It turned the dungeon from a list of fights into a machine I had to understand. The phantom "Samwise" doing all my heavy lifting was a less welcome surprise.
