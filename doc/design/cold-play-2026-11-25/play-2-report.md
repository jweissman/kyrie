# Cold play 2 (impatient fighter) — Brokk Strakeln, dwarf warrior (sellsword), report
**Expeditions made: 4.** **Would I willingly make a fifth expedition, knowing what happened on the first four? Maybe. Until T140 the answer was a loud yes: every trip went deeper and paid better, and the drop from the vault into AQUAVIGILIA's hall felt like a real dungeon. But my character died in one "Press on to the end" move and a rest the game led me to, and the game never said my life was over. Whether I'd make a fifth trip depends on whether a new life starts cleanly, and nothing on screen told me.**
**Score: 6 / 10** (7 for the dungeon itself, minus the anticlimax of the death)

## My path
T0–1: made a dwarf warrior in about 8 answers and skipped the poacher-boy opening. Went to Fairham's ford, where about 45 rows were on offer. T2–4: the mine-head teases ("Dark, wet and low. And that's the good level") but has no way in. T6–22, **Expedition 1**: at the old quarry pit I wrenched a padlock, lit a torch, killed a wolf and then a she-bear (down to 4/14), searched the den and found a ring of letters. T23–29: in town I took the bounty (12 coin and a rope), hired Warren the ranger, slept and bought a torch. T30–51, **Expedition 2**: rats at the working face, then the undercroft. There I pressed the den's three letters on a flag, a stair opened, and below it the vault-keeper paralysed me while Warren killed it. I took a cord of rings and a moonstone, found the AQUAVIGILIA stair, and hired Ellyjobell the gnome thief on it. T52–65: back in town I slept, sold the loot at the forge for 13 coin, and bought a lantern and oil. T66–96, **Expedition 3**: walked all the way back down, killed rats and goblins, and lost 7 HP to a rune trap. I guessed a word on a casket wrong and Ellyjobell died. I carried her out, leveled to 2 at the shrine and buried her. T97–102: sold a shortsword, bought oil and a ten-foot pole. T103–142, **Expedition 4**: dragged a barricade apart and killed the goblin chief. Down the ladders at the winze foot was a treasure cache. I set off a frost trap with the pole and killed three bone hounds, then rested in the hall. A spider-and-snake ambush in the stores followed: I pressed on, bled out and was "down". I rested as the leading row suggested and died. I stopped at about 142 moves.

## What I understood
It is a text RPG with a rows-based menu. A town has an inn (beds 3 coin, torches 1), a forge that buys loot (sell rows appear only after you talk to the smith), a chandlery that only sells, and a shrine that heals. The deep place starts at the quarry pit's adit: cut gallery and den, then the working face and undercroft, then a sealed vault. A stair below the vault leads to the AQUAVIGILIA workings (adit, smithy, crossing, winze). Ladders go down to the haulage level (Delvers' hall, stores, pump-room, long haulage). Light burns down and matters. Traps sit on exits and can be looked at, prodded or set off with a pole. Puzzles want skills ("needs lore", "needs trapwork"). Companions are hired for coin and do most of the damage. Going out is one move ("Out to the old quarry pit"), but going back in means walking every room again. A downed character with no healer dies.

## What I wanted
1. **A fight, right away.** I got it at T10, about ten moves in. Satisfied.
2. **Treasure.** The early hoards were thin: rings and a moonstone from the vault boss sold for 13 coin. The cache at the winze foot was the first loot that felt like loot (ingot, rose quartz, 11 coin). I died with 37 coin and gems I never sold. Partly satisfied.
3. **Go deeper each trip.** It worked: quarry, undercroft, vault, AQUAVIGILIA, haulage level. Satisfied, and it was the main hook.
4. **A party that fights.** Warren was excellent, too good really: he killed most things while I missed. Ellyjobell lasted one trip.
5. **Not to die stupidly.** Failed: a 1-in-4 casket guess killed my thief, and "Press on to the end" followed by a rest killed me.

## Confused, bored or stuck
1. T1: the ford offers about 45 rows at once (puppies, needles, a riddle, five Talk-to rows with names like "Bimpnottin" and "Loopmottin"). To an impatient player it is a wall. The dungeon is a plain "To the old quarry pit" in the R list, with nothing to mark it.
2. T2–4: the mine-head has an adit, a lamp-trimmer and "the good level", but no way in. A teaser with no door.
3. T26–27: "Take a bed and supper" was not among the taproom's rows until I talked to Warryn again. At T55, "Talk to Warryn" went straight to bed and supper and took 3 coin without a choice.
4. T28: copy-paste townsfolk: "Fonkin is at the long table with a cup, telling a long story about a horse." / "Jebeddo is at the long table with a cup, telling a long story about a horse."
5. T33, T39: the torch I bought guttered after about half an hour of play. The "Wait until dark 40/30" numbers looked like the light's minutes left, and they weren't.
6. T48: treasure says only "+3 experience: found a cord of old rings". There is no hint of value until a shop pays 6 coin.
7. T57: Boddynock's chandlery buys nothing, and the forge's sell rows appear only after talking. I had to hunt for where to sell.
8. T71: at "It is pitch dark... do nothing that needs seeing" the exit row still worked and I walked on in the dark.
9. T90: the casket's four words ("Stamp, Bag, Watch, Cup") with "needs lore": I could guess with no clue, and the wrong guess hit Ellyjobell, whom I hadn't chosen, for 6 cold. She died. "That was fun!" was her last line.
10. T90–91: there was no row to tend or carry a downed companion. The only rows were "Take ... from Ellyjobell", that is, looting her.
11. T113 onward: "Ask Ellyjobell to stay here" was still offered after she was dead and buried, through to the end.
12. T66–72, T103–111: no quick way back to the depth I had reached. Each trip meant 8–9 moves of walking through cleared, identical rooms. Out is one move, in is nine.
13. T139–140: "Press on to the end" ran the whole ambush without stopping when I got low, and ended "The fight is won. Brokk Strakeln is down." It should stop and ask when the leader is about to drop.
14. T141–142: the leading row while I was down was "Rest six hours", and resting killed me ("they have died where they fell"). No game-over and no new-life prompt followed. The header still reads "Brokk Strakeln, dwarf warrior, 0 of 30", and Warren is offered my battleaxe.
15. Minor prose slips: "The rats come up into the working face stands its ground", "The wolf... does not look pleased", "Brokk Strakeln recovers 5 HP (14 HP)... reaches level 2 (+16 HP)".

## What worked
1. A fight within ten moves of starting, and the dungeon right next to town.
2. The she-bear fight: a telegraphed "rears up... ready to strike next turn", Defend to dodge it, a stun, and a win at 4 HP. Tense and fair.
3. The letter ring in the den opening the flag in the undercroft. It rewarded searching.
4. The reveal: "Below you is a hall that could hold the whole of Fairham, its pillars going down further than the light does." That is the line that made me want to go back down.
5. Each trip went deeper, with a boss or chief and a cache: the vault keeper, the goblin chief, then the winze-foot cache.
6. Traps you can deal with: Look closely, then "Knock it over with the pole, from well back", and the frost nova was "spent on nothing". The 1-coin pole felt like a smart purchase.
7. "Press on to the end" and "fight:auto" keep small fights quick. Good for an impatient player, until it killed me.
8. Companions with personality and real power (Warren: "Another one falls."). The companion you can only hire down in the dungeon was a nice touch.
9. Burying Ellyjobell: "Then you go on without them." Short and earned.
10. Small, honest touches: "It burns oil, and none of you carries any", and the padlock noise that "goes further than you would like".

## Surprise
That a companion could die from a puzzle guess, and that my own character could die from a rest the game led with, with no game-over afterwards. The world carried on with Warren standing over my body and an offer to take my axe. I also didn't expect a quarry under a small town to open into a buried hall "that could hold the whole of Fairham", with a goblin warband and bone hounds below it. The good surprise and the bad surprise came within a few dozen moves of each other.
