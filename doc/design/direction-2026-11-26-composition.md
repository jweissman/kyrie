# Composition by contrast (the owner, 26 November)

The owner, after the afternoon's playtest:

> You want to be sparse, so that important things feel relevant. You want to be terse, so that when you _are_ verbose,
> it stands out. You want to be resource-poor to start so that the economy feels relevant. You want to know feats and
> perks are chosen at every level as an incentive to level up. You shouldn't be inundated by quests; things should be
> extremely simple and mostly mundane, and very thin for the first town, so that plot hooks stand out as you uncover
> them (you shouldn't just start being able to ask incredibly specific questions about the world without grounding it
> somehow in your investigation or at least deduction etc)

And on the same day:

> This project lives and dies by the quality of prose… [integrate adventure material] as deeply as possible, while
> _also_ restricting the amount of content a player sees when first starting to almost nothing but a combat-windmill
> and a few traders… most of the interesting things about the world you should have to discover.

## The principle

Every quality the game wants to make felt is first held low, so that its rise is noticed. A rich world reached through
a sparse start is not a contradiction. It is the composition.

| Held low at first | So that | Today's gap |
|---|---|---|
| Things in view | What matters feels relevant | A busy first town (OR-1074, OR-1075) |
| Words | A long passage stands out | Prose ratchets hold the first hour (test/small) |
| Resources | The economy matters | A start that is not poor; nothing to spend on (OR-1081) |
| Quests | A plot hook stands out when it is uncovered | Errands handed out (OR-370, OR-1075) |
| Knowledge | A question is earned | Specific questions before any grounding (OR-1082) |
| Power | Levelling is wanted | No choice made at every level (OR-1083) |

## What follows

- **The first town is thin and mundane:** a few traders, ordinary people, ordinary talk. Its hooks are few and found by
  investigation, a check or deduction, never listed.
- **A question about the world is offered only once the character has grounds for it:**
  - something seen, read or heard;
  - a deduction from two known facts;
  - a check passed.

  The grounds are the act's own consequence (facets are inferred, never declared), so the page can say why the
  question is there.
- **A new life starts poor:** what it carries and can buy is scarce enough that its first purchases are choices.
- **Every level offers a choice of a feat or perk:** a house rule over the SRD's cadence, to be proved on the tabletop
  first, like the economy (OR-678).
- **The world itself stays maximalist.** Adventure material is integrated continuously and found by travel and
  discovery, never shown at the start.
