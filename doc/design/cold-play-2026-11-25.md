# Cold play, 25 November 2026: the fifth expedition

The owner's question, put to four blind players before the owner plays again: **"Would I willingly make a fifth
expedition into this dungeon, knowing what happened on the first four?"**

These are rung 4 of the acceptance ladder: blind cold-play agents of stated kinds. **None of this is human
acceptance.** The agents feel no wall-clock time, and each had a cap of about 200 moves.

## How it was played

- **The revision:** origin/main at 1bd39d8ba. OR-644 (every new life in the reference game), OR-646 (each depth wants
  its own preparation), OR-651 (ways to another hex only on the map) and the old-masters rule had all landed.
- **Players 1, 3 and 4 were replayed on a fix.** Players 1 and 4 died on their first act (OR-680, below), so they were
  run again on the same revision plus that fix (data/rules/services.kyr). Player 3 was started after the fix.
- **The entry point:** the runner (`bin/kyrie run --world <seed> --create`), drawing the engine's own rows as the page
  and the terminal do. The life was made through the engine's creation requests, as Play makes one.
- **The worlds:** each seed was drawn as a new life with no seed named draws one (FreshSeeds.seed(FirstChoice.seed)):
  nariguwi, diziveje, tedewane and cimicinu.
- **What the players were told:** the box's words only. You begin in a small settlement on the edge of old woods;
  somewhere near is a deep place, entered by stages; you can come back up, rest, trade and prepare; make as many trips
  as you are willing to, up to five. Each was blind to the source, the data and the documents. None asked for help.
- **The records:** each play's moves (replayable through the runner) and its report are in
  [cold-play-2026-11-25/](cold-play-2026-11-25/), as play-N-lines.txt and play-N-report.md.

## The players and their verdicts

| | Kind | World, where the life began | Expeditions | Fifth? | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | careful explorer | nariguwi, Reedmere (the reference settlement) | 3 | maybe, leaning yes | 6/10 |
| 2 | impatient fighter | diziveje, Fairham (**not** the reference settlement) | 4, then died | maybe | 6/10 |
| 3 | cautious planner | tedewane, Nochlëa (**not** the reference settlement) | 4 | maybe | 7/10 |
| 4 | newcomer to text games | cimicinu, Pardatîr (the reference settlement) | 2 (the cap) | maybe, leaning yes | 7/10 |

**Nobody said yes outright, and nobody said no.** Every player wanted to go down again for something the dungeon had
shown them:

- the drowned second level and "the paymaster keeps noon in a jar" (1);
- the hall "that could hold the whole of Fairham" (2);
- AQUAVIGILIA's far water (3);
- ghouls in a level already cleared, "whoever lives here came after you went" (4).

What held each back:

- the walk back down, room by room, on every trip (1, 2, 3 and 4);
- deaths no one could see coming, under "Press on to the end" (1, 2);
- loot that bought nothing (3);
- fights that were mostly trivial, and turns spent on light and pickup rows rather than decisions (4).

**Three of the four were stopped by the move cap, not by boredom.** An expedition cost them 40 to 110 moves. A fifth
expedition is more than an hour's play at this density, which is itself a finding (see design, D1).

**Two of the four never played the reference game** (OR-682). Both began as "Coming home", in their country's second
or third settlement, and found that town's own generated undertown: a quarry, a crypt, a vault, and the AQUAVIGILIA
workings below. That dungeon proved deep and well liked. Players 1 and 4 were in the reference settlement and found
the Delvers' mine under the old woods.

### 1. The careful explorer (Iselde, elf bard)

Gave the priestess the brothers' letter, heard the town's three troubles, hired a bard and settled the boundary-stone
quarrel (rumour, search, confrontation: "complete and satisfying"). Down through the fallen stones into the Delvers'
mine on expedition 1, reading notebooks and inscriptions with the old script's glossary ("IT CANNOT BEAR THE NOON. THE
PAYMASTER KEEPS NOON IN A JAR", the high point). Then a party wipe under "Press on to the end" against three bone hounds
described as ordinary dogs. Began again in the same country, ran the route faster, broke the sealed stair's wall,
found the second level drowned to the roof, and sold gems for 90 coin. Expedition 3 freed the winding wheel. The
verdict: "a real mystery I want to finish", but "the 11-move walk from town to the mine each time, and a party wipe I
couldn't see coming, make a fifth trip feel like a chore."

### 2. The impatient fighter (Brokk, dwarf warrior)

A fight within ten moves. The quarry pit beside Fairham led to a wolf and a she-bear ("tense and fair"), a ring of
letters that opened a flag in the undercroft, the vault-keeper, and the stair to AQUAVIGILIA. A gnome thief, hired on
that stair, died on a one-in-four casket guess. On expedition 4 came the goblin chief, a cache, three bone hounds, then
an ambush pressed to the end, and the protagonist down. The row led with was "Rest six hours", and the rest killed him.
No ending followed (OR-683). The verdict: "Until T140 the answer was a loud yes … every trip went deeper and paid
better."

### 3. The cautious planner (Hild, dwarf cleric of Life)

Took the town's three notices (rats, catacombs, spiders) and found that they were three ways into one undercroft. Laid
the crypt's dead with the rite of rest, burned a spider brood with one firebomb through a crack ("the planning paid off
directly"), and paid the vault's keeper ten coin to sit down. Reached AQUAVIGILIA, saw ghouls and withdrew, then came
back with Bless and Pure Beam prepared and killed them. The trouble: no way to sell anything anywhere, a lantern that
could never be lit, and the remaining leads all saying "needs lore". The verdict: "I can't see what a fifth trip would
pay for."

### 4. The newcomer (Brenna, dwarf warrior)

The robbers on the road, a pedlar who joined "because you stopped for a man in a ditch", five aims in Pardatîr. Asked
Uriah about the old workings, searched twice in the woods and found the stair. Expedition 1 was long: traps defused
with the pole, a secret air shaft, the Delvers' hall, a riddle casket, a wall broken onto a flooded stair, the pump set.
The player walked home in the dark when the light ran out and found a ring under a fallen oak. Rested, levelled, sold
loot (50 to 210 coin) and bought a crowbar and oil. On the second trip things had moved in: "a cold like a breath let
out … three silent ghouls". The verdict: "the mine keeps paying out … what makes me hesitate is that most fights were
trivial, and a lot of my moves went on fighting with light and pickup rows."

### Design

For the owner, not built.

- **D1. The way back down is the expedition's tax.** Out is one move, from anywhere ("a good return", 1 and 3). In is
  every cleared room again, 8 to 11 moves on each trip (1, 2, 3 and 4). A fifth expedition is mostly walking. A
  delver's way down to the depth already reached (a rope left, a cage working, a found shortcut) would make the fifth
  trip about the fifth level.
- **D2. "Press on to the end" never stops for the leader falling.** It caused both deaths (a party wipe for 1, the
  protagonist for 2), and it spent a planner's only Pure Beam on a 1-HP spider (3).
- **D3. Danger is not legible before it is met.** Bone hounds were "curious … long-legged rough-coated" dogs at 18 HP
  against a company of 6, 6 and 12 (1). "Something large, that is not in any hurry" was two 8-HP hounds (1, 4). The
  writing undersold one threat and oversold the other.
- **D4. Most fights are trivial, and the few hard ones are sudden** (4, 1). The fights rated best were the
  telegraphed she-bear ("rears up … ready to strike next turn"; 2) and the ghouls with every tool used (3).
- **D5. Selling is hidden or absent.** It was found only after talking to the smith (2), and not at all in Nochlëa (3,
  "my loot buys nothing anywhere"). It was a clear, big reward where it worked (4: a topaz for 60). Where the dungeon
  is not the reference game's, the reference town's two traders are not there.
- **D6. Light is the one real constraint, and its rows crowd out decisions.** All four players planned around light,
  which is good. The light rows, the pickup rows, a "Risk it" for each member at each trap, and Buy and Haggle per item
  bury the few rows that matter (4).
- **D7. Skill gates without a way to earn the skill.** The leads left at the end all said "needs lore" (3). The
  strong-box stamps and the casket's words had no findable clue, so they were guessed, and a wrong guess killed a
  companion (2, 4, 1).
- **D8. The order's errand points away from the dungeon.** It begins at the reference shrine, but its aim is the old
  writing at a makers' work in another country (Musicarch in Flintcrag). The castellan's crown is at Nightking,
  elsewhere too. Player 1 read three inscriptions in the mine and "the aim never moved".
- **D9. Level-ups were seen by 2 and 4 (at the shrine) but never by 3** (about 900 experience, no level, no sheet).
  Raising a quality in a shop "just happened, with no cost or explanation" (4).
- **D10. Clues that are not needed.** The notebook said a bar frees the wheel, but the way down worked without one, and
  the crowbar bought for it went unused (4, 1).

### Editorial

For the owner, not built.

- **E1. The best lines are places' and finds'.** "Below you is a hall that could hold the whole of Fairham" was the
  line all three who saw a version of it named as the reason to go down again. Others that landed: the notebooks, the
  tally-board ("The shaft was never cleared"), the child's shoe among the offerings, and Grimnar binding a wound "in
  Father's voice".
- **E2. Filler finds.** "Scattered coins … most of it old and some of it good" in nearly every deep room was "filler"
  by the fourth (3). A riddle casket held one bronze bracelet. The "wonder" gave "only cut stone now" (3).
- **E3. Copy townsfolk.** "Fonkin … telling a long story about a horse" and "Jebeddo … telling a long story about a
  horse" (2). Musicarch and Marblegate shared the description "a smooth doorway in a crag" (1).
- **E4. Garbled place words.** "Into the old dead under the old woods by the way in", and "Out to the stair" for a way
  down (1, 4). An underground stair room spoke of spoil heaps on a hillside, so neither player could tell whether they
  were underground (1, 4).
- **E5. Unnamed things.** "The enchanted object" was never named (1). The brood-chamber had no description (3). "A lamp
  burns over the door of the Green Mask" led to nothing (4).
- **E6. Letters that do not fit the character.** The brothers' letter calls a monster-hunting warrior one "who reads
  the old script", from an order creation never gave (4).

## The owner's complaints of tonight, against the cold plays

- **Starting takes long.** Not felt: agents feel no wall-clock time. Creation was 7 to 11 questions, and every player
  called it quick and evocative. On this machine, creation with every default and the first scene took about 15
  seconds through the runner. AX-04 stands as the owner saw it, and these plays neither confirm nor refute it.
- **It is unclear how to get to the dungeon.** Partly hit:
  - Every player found a deep place, in 6 to 31 moves.
  - In the reference game, the way was asking the innkeeper or Uriah, then two searches in the woods (1, 4). Player 4
    noticed a hint ("trouble under the town too") with no row to follow it.
  - The impatient player found the quarry pit among about 45 unmarked rows at the ford (2).
  - Two of the four were not in the reference game at all (OR-682).
- **A site is over in seconds.** Not hit; the reverse. Every player found a dungeon of three or more levels with
  puzzles across branches.
- **Only one combat.** Not hit: many fights each trip. Hit in spirit, though: most fights were trivial (D4).
- **No treasure worth having.** Mixed:
  - Sold well where a buyer was found: 90, 160 and 210 coin (1, 4).
  - Thin early (2: a boss's rings for 13 coin).
  - Unsellable in Nochlëa (3).
- **Nothing to spend on.** Partly. Light, a pole, a crowbar, oil, firebombs and hirelings were bought and used (2, 3,
  4). But the dearest buy, a lantern, could never be lit (OR-685). In Nochlëa there was nowhere to sell (3).
- **No constraints, upgrades or new verbs.**
  - **Constraints:** light is a real one, which every player felt.
  - **Upgrades:** level-ups were seen by two players.
  - **New verbs:** there were new verbs with depth: the pole against traps, firebombs, rites for the dead, a bribe,
    draining a flooded stair. Nobody said "nothing new".
  - **The gaps:** skill gates with no way to earn the skill (D7), and a level-up that one player never saw (D9).
- **Conversation and combat lack juice.**
  - **Combat:** juice where a threat was telegraphed (the she-bear) or every tool mattered (the ghouls). None in the
    trivial fights, and none in "Press on to the end", which killed without a pause (D2).
  - **Conversation:** little remarked on. "Ask the castellan again" gave "much as before" (1), and talk to the goblins
    led only to "Attack" (OR-698).
- **Crossing hexes without the map.** Not hit. No player left the first hex. The woods, the stones and the mine were
  all reached through the action menu, leg by leg. Player 4 asked for a "go to the mine" travel (D1).

## What this says, in experience

- **More interesting to do:** a dungeon whose branches answer each other. The letter ring from the quarry opened the
  undercroft's flag (2, 3), and notebooks set up a machine to understand (1, 4). Levels changed behind the company
  between trips (4).
- **Easier to understand:** traps (look closely, then the pole). Out is one move.
- **Harder than it should be:**
  - the way back down;
  - which rows matter;
  - what you carry;
  - when a fight will kill you.

## Fixes landed in this work

- **OR-680** (data/rules/services.kyr; test/small/two-beds). A host's work for keep and song for supper are offered for
  a bed the host lets (fee above nothing). The trade-off: hosts whose only bed is free (the Book of Faces' gate, the
  bounty kit's elders) no longer offer either.
- **OR-681** (src/narrative/telling.ts; test/small/feat-told). A feat is told as done by a member of the company, never
  by a stranger who brings the skill.
