# C. Representative player journeys

These are product acceptance journeys, not scripted walkthroughs offered as new play evidence. Each distinguishes **O — observed**, **D — desired presentation/behavior**, and **new capability/content**. Exact current outcomes are in the [additional record](../../playtests/product-2026-10-19/README.md) and original cold record.

## Journey 1 — Learn a need, prepare a company, solve a physical problem, return

**Intention:** find Pell and bring them home. **Company:** hunter-ranger, locksmith-fighter, medic-cleric. **Product loop:** settlement → preparation → route → site → rescue → escort → relationship/reward → new priority.

1. **Arrive at Reedwake.** Adventure orients quay, foundry up-river, shops/inn/shrine and Wren watching the route. Inspect Wren freely; the reply introduces Pell and estimated culvert closure. **O:** these descriptions and plea exist. **D:** Notes records source/time/task and estimated closure immediately; a brief explicit acceptance can record a promise if the player makes one. Reading a request is not compulsory acceptance.
2. **Prepare.** Open Party to see ranger/hunter approaches, lockwork provider and medic supplies. Buy or carry what seems useful; compare route durations and deadline. **O:** tools/backgrounds, wares/time and current bars exist. **D:** ability summaries and item effects are readable, money shared, service/entity inspection safe, known capability providers named. Do not promise a required fighter if a slower tool-free method is valid.
3. **Choose an entry.** Map shows upper route via ridge or culvert shortcut when known. Preview exact itinerary and time; commit named leg/journey. **O:** alternate paths/durations exist. **D:** route totals include internal legs, known/unvisited distinctions and closure uncertainty. A report of the culvert does not reveal every room.
4. **Investigate the vestibule.** Inspect board, then deliberately Read for ten minutes; discover stores-to-hoist crawl. **O:** reading unlocks route. **D:** source and connection added to map/Notes; no generic “evidence recorded” sentence.
5. **Open stores.** Inspect padlock; choose Bran's lockwork if provider is recorded, preview time, commit; then enter. **O:** lockwork and padlock state work. **D:** clicking destination does not secretly choose lockpicking; an actual result explains the route opening. Collect bar for a named companion with one explicit Take.
6. **Rescue Pell.** State explains trapped leg and warning. Compare lever, slower digging, risky heave. Choose lever; authored specific lift/free response; rack propped and Pell mobile. **O:** this is a successful current physical intervention. **D:** final state is not repeated as three narration paragraphs; provider attribution is truthful.
7. **Escort.** Ask Pell to accompany company. Show a compact following/condition entry while traveling, not a fourth combatant assumed by UI. **O:** escort moves along named passages. **D:** load/pace and hazard eligibility reflect Pell's condition; a closing route interrupts only at actual boundary. If he cannot travel, show treatment/assistance rather than teleport.
8. **Return to Wren.** Reunion, exact thanks, six coins, then NPC departure in order. **O:** authored reward/reunion exists; original UI lost thanks on departure. **D:** reply survives, shared purse changes once; rescue thread resolves with evidence and last-known destination.
9. **Choose again.** Review injuries/supplies, known Hesta/sword/remaining-route leads only if learned. **D:** no artificial quest assignment for unexplored rooms. **New content required:** a motivated continuing situation if this is to be a campaign rather than a completed rescue module.

**Acceptance:** without implementation knowledge, player can explain why bar/locksmith mattered, why chosen route was safe in time, what happened to Pell and what was earned. Save/load preserves these answers and exact world changes.

## Journey 2 — Different party, a cheaper physical approach, independent NPC agency

**Intention:** free Tamsin before snow closes the route. **Company:** mage-locksmith, miner-fighter, medic-cleric. **Product loop:** composition changes rescue → improve a route → let another actor pursue their own return → receive a specific response.

1. **Set up company.** Creation previews capabilities and valid combinations. **O:** incompatible explorer/mage was only rejected at launch; miner grants digging. **D:** prevent incompatible selection with explanation before Begin; compare roles without engine IDs.
2. **Enter quarry.** Player sees smithy crowbar/line, decides not to fetch crowbar because miner offers a plausible fallback. **O:** line picked up; crowbar deliberately left. **D:** no hidden declaration that miner can do everything a bar can; inspect actual methods first.
3. **Descend via tub incline.** Site graph distinguishes upper workings and lower gallery, incline versus rotten ladder. **O:** small multi-level loop exists. **D:** graph labels vertical connections without implying metric plan.
4. **Dig safely in twenty minutes.** **O:** miner-specific action frees Tamsin without bar. Physical hollow and mobile injured NPC remain; ability has practical time value.
5. **Let Tamsin return alone.** **O:** they depart and later reach cottage under their own pursuit. **D:** choice preview describes known condition and independence, not an omniscient guaranteed homecoming; note is “freed, heading home” until reunion/report observed. Player may follow later without forced escort bookkeeping.
6. **Secure ladder.** Inspect hazard, lash with carried line, cross separately. **O:** this changes state and safe topology. **D:** record actual line consumption/deployment and whether it can be recovered; do not invent this property from the noun “lash.”
7. **Return after exploration.** Tamsin is settled, Marrit relieved. **O:** three coins with reproach for not escorting. **D:** preserve exact reaction; do not invent a reputation meter deduction. Notes distinguishes different rescue outcome from the escorted one.
8. **Revisit route.** **O:** snow now blocks beck lane. **D:** remembered prior route versus current closure remains clear; map/planner offers known longer route. No reset to original condition.

**Acceptance:** the player can explain how party composition saved time, that Tamsin was independent rather than magically transported, and why gratitude/reward differed. This is currently a bounded rescue, not demonstrated institutional simulation.

## Journey 3 — Dangerous creature, tribute or timing, meaningful treasure return

**Intention:** recover Thrale's bell while preserving village and company. **Company:** mage, thief, cleric. **Product loop:** information → preparation/economic tradeoff → noncombat resolution → treasure → return.

1. **Hear bell-ringer and Tobin.** **O:** stolen bell, dragon demand, afternoon hunting report exist; hunting departure observed. **D:** know who said what and whether the schedule is current. Do not disclose live remote dragon location on a map.
2. **Prepare salve and rope.** **O:** 22 coin becomes 17; salve and rope bought. **D:** effects/clock and rope's known purpose visible before buying; allocation to companions deliberate.
3. **Compare tribute and waiting.** **O:** fifteen-coin tribute grants passage; source supports timed absence, but stealing during absence/raid branch was not played. **D:** present both only when learned/observed and applicable. Waiting for absence trades time and uncertainty; paying consumes recovery budget. No universal persuasion button promises success.
4. **Pay and retrieve.** **O:** dragon tail clears, bell collected without hostility. **D:** respectful passage/permission and treasure ownership are explicit; bell has its campaign-specific physical identity and carrying requirements, not only a generic signal-bell description.
5. **Return by known route.** **O:** alternate scree path usable with rope. **D:** full time/load preview and route knowledge age. Big treasure should create declared transport considerations without a physics sandbox.
6. **Handover.** **O:** simply carrying bell to ringer/chapel did not complete; code-guided ground deposit on green did. **D:** “Return chapel bell to bell-ringer” is an explicit social/item action with exact recipient and result. It satisfies the same authored outcome without frontend inference. If hanging bell requires work, author that task and who does it.
7. **Changed settlement.** **O:** bell-ringer becomes glad and thanks player under ground-location trigger. **D:** chapel/town state prose updates, bell's physical/custody location agrees, no bell remains simultaneously in inventory/on ground/in use. Notes resolves thread and retains the choice to spare/pay dragon.

**Acceptance:** both return action and changed scene are discoverable without source reading. No fight or registry coverage is required to call this a meaningful adventure outcome.

## Journey 4 — Catastrophe, withdrawal, casualties, recovery and re-entry

**Intention:** survive a failed assault, protect what can be recovered, reassess goal. **O baseline:** dragon opens with breath; salve on cleric yielded total defeat. Salve on mage left her at 1 HP; sleep resisted then death. A separate first-turn Retreat let one survivor return by pasture route after bridge burned, rest and re-enter next day.

**D intended flow:**

1. Inspect dragon without attack. Known signs and any learned warning establish deadly scale; no universal exact HP scan. Explicit attack preview distinguishes hostility from conversation.
2. Resolve opening action in causal order. If a surprise rule denies response, cause is readable; if intent is telegraphed, show real threatened area and applicable defenses. Do not invent cover to save the party.
3. Downed states name incapacity versus explicit death. Damage display distinguishes breath's resolved harm, resistance and HP lost. Last spell's failed save and fatal response remain in aftermath.
4. If still able to act, choose withdrawal. Preview actual passable exit, response window, carriers and those left. A burning bridge may remove that route; choose shoulder/pastures only if actually reachable.
5. A lone mobile companion cannot carry two incapacitated ones. Choose assistance, whom to carry or whom to leave; their location/property persists. **New mechanics:** body/casualty location, load and withdrawal policy. This replaces current silent transport ambiguity, not an observed improvement.
6. Return to refuge. Recovery screen shows who can be treated and who cannot, costs/time and current situation. Rest restores eligible living companions; actual treatment/rescue is needed for incapacity and no service silently revives the dead.
7. Choose rescue/recruitment or abandon objective. **New content/capability:** credible local assistance/costs or replacement companions; no guaranteed savior invented by UI. Dragon may still be too strong.
8. Re-enter. Bridge stays gone, threat/custody/left gear reflect real intervening events. Notes supplies last-known state and reports, not certainty that abandoned people remain safe. Re-entry is a new expedition with changed capabilities, not an encounter reset.

**Acceptance:** player knows who/what was lost, where each entity is, what recovery can do, and why return route/next approach differs. Total defeat disposition is explicit; loading an earlier save is a player branch, not fictional recovery.

## Journey 5 — A continuing campaign, advancement and competing interests

**D / new authored slice, not currently played:** use the quarry setting and established ladder/return consequences, then add a small institution-backed situation. Proposed stakeholders and obligations are design examples, not existing Hob/Marrit behavior.

1. After rescue, a quarry cooperative wants the workings reopened; a family representative asks that unsafe routes be secured first. Hob offers tools but wants them returned. Neither request is automatically accepted.
2. Notes records who offered each opportunity, terms, estimated reopening time, current knowledge and known alternatives. Player chooses a promise and can leave another unresolved.
3. Rescue/exploration achievements grant configured progression credit. At a safe point, choose an eligible ability/level gain or training route; exact new effect/cost previewed. **New progression design:** catalog-defined curves and rewards, not arbitrary frontend XP thresholds.
4. Preparation changes for second expedition: invest in line/shoring, a newly learned protective technique or transport support instead of another damage upgrade. Gold, time and obligations constrain the choice.
5. During time away, cooperative advances its declared reopening plan using resources/actors. A report or visible work reveals the development; journal does not see through walls.
6. Return: repaired ladder can remain useful; a rushed closure/reopening can change access. NPCs respond to actual promise fulfillment or breach, opening/withholding specific services/permits. Evidence and institutional membership determine response; no generic “world reacts” sentence.
7. Player may renegotiate deadline, assist, oppose or pursue another known site. A second encounter uses a different approach enabled by the first expedition's knowledge/capability. Completed rescue remains history, not the only active objective.

**Acceptance:** two expeditions have different options for causal reasons; one independent situation advances; at least one development decision and one changed relationship/access state affect play. This is the campaign-capable milestone, not an extra Journal panel standing in for missing content.
