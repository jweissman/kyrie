# Kyrie retrospective and futurespective (25 November 2026)

A read-only review of the owner's own words from 20 October to 25 November, the design corpus, the M22-beta
architecture and the commit record. It is meant to give direction, not to add a plan.

**How to read the provenance.** Every claim of intent says whose it is:

- **"The owner, <date> (<file>):"** is the owner's own message in the session transcripts. The file is the first eight
  characters of the .jsonl name in `~/.claude/projects/-home-jweissman-work-kyrie/`. The files are:

  | File | Dates |
  |---|---|
  | `6526afdc` | 20 to 29 October |
  | `4aa3b6e5` | 29 October to 4 November |
  | `8e071ed0` | 4 to 10 November |
  | `e973e1f5` | 10 to 13 November |
  | `4a454a05` | 13 to 16 November |
  | `8f0352a4` | 16 to 23 November |
  | `b6294316` | 23 to 25 November |

  `doc/design/VISION.md` (committed by the owner on 31 October) and `DOCTRINE.md` (14 November) also count as the
  owner's.
- **"A review / an analyst / the Kyr report recommended:"** is outside text that the owner relayed, often "with a grain
  of salt". It may have been adopted by the lead since. Being adopted does not make it the owner's intent.
- **"The lead adopted / built:"** is agent work recorded in the repository.
- **"This retro suggests:"** is my own judgment and nothing more.

**Method.** I streamed the user entries of all eight transcripts and kept only typed text, not tool results: 560
messages. I read every message of 300 to 3,000 characters whole. I read the long mission briefs selectively, and my
extraction cut each one at 6,000 characters, apart from the 25 November note, which I read in full.

---

## 0. The pattern in one paragraph

The owner's founding ambition has not moved in five weeks. In the owner's words of 21 October (`6526afdc`), it is
**"authored depth at procedural scale"**: a party RPG with the immediacy of a BBS game, old-school danger, abstract
JRPG combat and a world that "should remember what happened" (20 October). What has moved is the owner's patience with
the gap between systems and play.

The same sentence recurs from 22 October ("the playable experience remains profoundly unsatisfactory") to 24 November
("still getting bounced off almost immediately"). Most of November's platform investment went into one root cause,
which the analyst's fourth note named and the lead adopted: player-facing meaning was derived more than once.

Since 23 November that investment has started to pay in ways a player could feel:

- one installed context per campaign;
- one row set per player revision;
- one door to the world's knowledge, proved by noninterference;
- journey receipts.

The owner's note of 25 November turns this into a creative brief: **"make one small place worth inhabiting, then make
it possible to build a thousand more"**, judged by one question, **"Would I willingly make a fifth expedition into this
dungeon?"**

This retro suggests treating the next ninety days as the period in which the trust work starts paying creative
returns, not as more foundation.

## 1. Historical promises

### 1.1 One life, and companions who matter (characters and companies)

**What the owner wanted:**
- 21 October (`6526afdc`): party-composition perks such as Orsino's **Synod** (three clerics), as "a general mechanism",
  not a hard-coded exception.
- 22 October: the owner was dismayed that "companions are often silent, interchangeable additions to combat."
- VISION §XI (31 October): "The player begins as one person… Other people may become companions through encounters,
  affinities, practical arrangements, old obligations, and shared experience."
- 20 November (`8f0352a4`): a character-creation perk stage that offers "surprising, substantial capabilities", and a
  ladder of character standing (Commoner, Adventurer, Aristocrat, King, Vizier, perhaps Dark Lord), "not to implement
  now."
- 14 November (`4a454a05`): the *Befriender* pin, with "reunite with the relationship intact."

**What exists now:**
- Recruitment works.
- The feature map's *Companions and hirelings* capability has 2 pins and 4 defects.
- The perk stage is a P1 requirement (OR-153).
- On 24 November the owner found the party roster "a mess" and asked that a companion get "full identities, at least on
  contact".

**What is in the way:** companions have no perception or agency apart from the company. The paragon pins of 13
November record "no companion acts or perceives apart from the company". Nothing they live through is remembered as
theirs.

**The near-term demonstration** (this retro suggests): the companion the owner requires to be recruitable inside the
first dungeon (24 November) comes back up with the company. Later, unprompted, they recall something specific from the
descent, using durable narration and the knowledge boundary.

### 1.2 Combat that is abstract, legible and decisive

**What the owner wanted:**
- 20 October: "the approachable, abstract party combat of classic JRPGs."
- 20 November (directions §6): "Do not assume tactical richness requires a grid… visible intentions, intelligible
  danger, useful preparation."
- 25 November (`b6294316`): "the core is conversation and combat; and we need a thorough rethink from first principles."
  Auto-resolution only "when you're clearly overmatching… I don't want to cheapen it."
- 25 November: the dungeon is "the principal integration gauntlet for our combat and SRD work."

**What exists now:**
- A full battle engine.
- The planes' laws.
- `Engaged` narrows the frame for a threat.
- The lead's design note `conversation-and-combat.md` (25 November).
- SRD fidelity is low: 37 of 119 spells and 7 of 79 creatures are faithful (STATUS.md).

**What is in the way:**
- The fight is not yet a mode of the scene (R10-01, OR-181, both blocked by TB-09).
- No intention is telegraphed.
- The owner wants creatures built as overlays, "a skeletal dog should be a canid with a skeletal aspect overlay" (21
  November). That model does not exist, so SRD creatures arrive one at a time.

**The near-term demonstration:** the boss of dungeon level two:
- it shows a dangerous attack coming;
- a companion can hold the doorway;
- retreating up the stair is visibly the wise choice on a first descent and not on a third.

### 1.3 A world that remembers (persistent history)

**What the owner wanted:**
- 20 October: "The world should remember what happened."
- VISION §X: "later events can transform the meaning of experiences the player genuinely remembers having."
- 9 November (`8e071ed0`): a distinction between Journal, Chronicle, Reference and Narration.
- 20 November rulings: narration is durable, and "Historical narration shouldn't change when templates or engine code
  change."

**What exists now:**
- Keyed draws (approved by the owner on 20 November).
- Durable narration.
- Porthalen answers what the company did, and every town answers for its own dungeon (OR-222 and OR-223, 24 November).
- Response audits for the drove, the hall and the sea-gate.

**What is in the way:**
- Typed receipts are not yet enriched; that is step 6 of the trust thread.
- The feature map's pins "a spared enemy reappears later" and "a promise broken resurfaces" are unwritten.
- There is no Chronicle.

**The near-term demonstration:** the fifth descent (see §2A). The boss stays dead. The looted cache stays empty, or
shows that someone has been back. A kobold the company spared turns up two levels down as what being spared made of
him.

### 1.4 Material civilization

**What the owner wanted:**
- VISION §IV: "Wood burns. Stone resists ordinary fire… A small number of well-designed causal relationships may
  produce thousands of interesting circumstances." And: "Simulate the circumstances in which people act; write the
  people with the care of a novelist."
- 20 November (§7): professions as "a generative model of civilization", which recombine over generations.
- 24 November: the civilizational atlas (eight terrains, a settlement matrix, canonical cities).
- 25 November: a **five-resource economy, stated as a hypothesis** (gold, blue, red, green, and a fifth still to be
  settled). "Resources flow into one another"; "validate the game as a small tabletop system first."

**What exists now:**
- The pole that "should work against mechanisms that its physical properties could genuinely engage" (the owner, 19
  November).
- The assembler's ledger records `practises(person, craft)` and `dwells(person, settlement)` (20 November).
- The tinker kit.
- *Markets and prices* has 0 pins and 6 defects.
- WD-07: expedition gains open no new way into the world.

**What is in the way:**
- No table of production and conversion has been played.
- Items, materials and currency are the last Canon group in the port order.
- Trade does not yet open a real exchange end to end (an owner requirement, 25 November).

**The near-term demonstration:** the owner's own tabletop test. A hunter, an investigator and a gatherer each play
three days in one terrain, and each arrives at the dungeon differently prepared.

### 1.5 Other kinds of activity

**What the owner wanted:**
- 31 October (`4aa3b6e5`): "The full adventuring loop should be exploration, achievement, and *adding new verbs and
  capabilities* … no pack animals… no discovery of spells from a secret vendor behind a bookseller."
- 14 November: five pins, the Alchemist, the Befriender, the Landholder, the Commander and the Eye.
- 20 November: Fun Fridays, from the Pleasant Activities List.
- 21 November: "the implementations often fictionalize activities without modeling participation… distinguish focus,
  activity, and engagement… First, prove one excellent focused activity."

**What exists now:**
- 18 pastime pins are met: the fiddle, the cards, the fox's earth, the letter home, the pump.
- `focus-activity-engagement.md`.
- The owner found the violin and the cards to be the very fictionalization they warned about.

**What is in the way:** an engagement has no participants, moves, stakes or exit as a first-class idea. Until it does,
each activity is a chain of acts.

**The near-term demonstration:** one hand of cards with a real hand, real turns and a way to resign. This also proves
the engagement idea that riddles, haggling and conversation need.

## 2. Newly available dividends

These five were impractical a month ago and are near now with systems that exist. None of them needs a new
architecture, only the architecture that has just landed, aimed at an experience.

**A. The dungeon that remembers: restock and return.**
- It needs:
  - the definition of a deep first dungeon and its measure (measure 9, OR-311, 24 November);
  - the return-expedition work (OR-321, OR-322, OR-326: "the gold goes on into the dark with a light to hand");
  - keyed draws, durable narration, journey receipts and one frame per revision.
- What is new is that a revisit can now be compared with a fresh build cheaply and honestly.
- The demonstration: a scripted fifth descent in which each change since the first is witnessed: a fallen boss, an
  empty cache, a new occupant, a companion's remark.
- This is the owner's own acceptance question, made concrete.

**B. The innkeeper who asks (knowledge-gated conversation).**
- It needs toldMatter (OR-423), the knowledge door (OR-527) and noninterference (OR-524).
- A conversation can now offer only what the company could know. That is the precondition for what the owner asked on
  25 November: "a more active participant… someone who will ask me questions, pose skill challenges, create situations.
  Not just a topic dispenser."
- The demonstration: the tavern keeper asks the newcomer one question ("Have you been into the old woods?", the owner's
  line), with three answers and an evasion. What the company learns in the woods opens exactly one new line on the
  return, and a lie is remembered.
- It reuses the riddle's focus shape (the owner, 17 November: "the four actions can just be the four possible
  answers").

**C. The specimen under the lens.**
- It needs the knowledge boundary, module kinds namespaced per installation (OR-540), the new-item-kind experiment (19
  November), and the First Aims as a nested undertaking.
- The owner's progression, 25 November: "encounter → collect a specimen → examine with ordinary senses → acquire an
  instrument → discover a new property → unlock an investigation."
- What is new is that a property hidden from the company can be proved absent from every frame until it is examined.
- It joins the economy (green to blue), the Encyclopedia (§1.8) and the canon of the Mathematical Age in one small
  loop.
- The owner calls that canon "creative canon to develop carefully". It is not established fact.

**D. Dark to revelation, into the First Aims, with no gold breach.**
- It needs the opening's successive revelations (OR-441), the First Aims (OR-443, OR-468), Play with no long press
  (OR-160), and one row set per revision.
- The owner's beats: 19 November, "maybe even in the dark so we have to light a light"; 23 November, "light, rations,
  persuading people to join, combat… within 5-10 seconds."
- What is new is that gold continuity can be checked per revision, not hunted for by eye.
- The demonstration: cold-play agents of three kinds play the first five minutes on several seeds, with zero continuity
  breaches and a first act within 1.5 s.

**E. A walk across the old woods, hex by hex.**
- It needs journey receipts (a leg told from its receipt, with the frame built only at the end; AX-05) and itineraries
  computed only when asked.
- The owner, 21 November: "reasonably mechanical, hex-by-hex exploration, with reveal at every step, and possibly
  recomputing the live route with what you know." A leg that costs no frame makes that reveal affordable.
- The leg is still 74 ms against a 5 ms target, so this one is "nearly".

**Recommended for immediate creative prototyping (this retro suggests): A and B**, both inside the reference game.

- **A** answers the owner's acceptance question directly. It exercises combat, SRD, lazy realization and persistence
  together, and it is where "the dungeon is the main game" becomes testable.
- **B** goes to the half of the core the owner calls "so close to the heart of the game, right up there with combat",
  and it is cheap now that knowledge is gated.

C is the best third: it is the owner's own named experiment, and it tests the economy hypothesis at its smallest.

## 3. Ninety-day futurespective: late February 2027

The owner has already named the target (25 November, `b6294316`): "an extremely fast, well-isolated, genuinely
playable platform with a polished common interface, enforceable package boundaries, strong tests, substantial
SRD/Orsino coverage, and at least one satisfying complete game loop," and "the ninety-day work should make the one-year
ambitions easier, not merely accumulate prerequisites for them." What follows is this retro's reading of that target.

### Essential outcomes

1. **The reference game passes its own question.**
   - Several kinds of cold-play agent make five expeditions on several seeds.
   - Retreat is sometimes rational, deeper levels introduce new mechanics, and the town has changed in ways they can
     state.
   - Cold-agent success is not human acceptance, per the owner, 20 to 23 November.
2. **Responsiveness is met as stated:**
   - a decision under 15 ms median (26 ms now);
   - a turn under 30 ms (105 ms);
   - a leg under 5 ms (74 ms);
   - Play to the first act in 1.5 s;
   - an act settled under 300 ms p95.

   The owner, 23 November: "an ordinary act in a headless engine should take milliseconds."
3. **Trust by construction.**
   - One frame per player revision.
   - The knowledge door complete for every activity of the loop.
   - TB-09 done for travel, talk, the fight and the map.
   - The ratchets on world reads outside the projector and on independent row derivations stand at zero on product
     paths.
4. **Two domains, one lifecycle.**
   - `kyr-lang` is a package.
   - The installed context is per campaign.
   - Westernesse and a second domain alternate in one process.
   - The runtime's edges to the setting are near zero.
5. **SRD coverage in the gauntlet.** Every creature and spell the dungeon's first several levels use is faithful.
   Fidelity measured across the catalog rises from today's 37/119 spells and 7/79 creatures to a clear majority.
6. **Authoring leverage, shown on real work.** One retained Situation/Incident construct shortens a real adventure, and
   Deem sampling, inspection and critique are routine.
7. **Fast tests that are the norm:** declared horizons, a fragment harness, and no push spec over ten seconds.

### Stretch goals

- A second game playable on the page (the island or the vessel, as a small slice).
- The economy in a second terrain.
- The perk stage in creation.
- The Arcana as a real deck.
- An interior map derived from the assembler (19 November).
- Conversation as dialectic owned by the engine.
- Sparse samples of the dungeon at depth.
- The Studio's omniscient view of the Atlas (19 November: "a particularly valuable future Studio feature").

### A dependency order

Each step leans on the one before it, but in the spirit of the brief they overlap rather than queue.

1. **Fast testing: declared horizons, the fragment harness and rule packages (BX-02, OR-407).** It comes first because
   it makes every later step cheaper to verify. It is small, and partly done.
2. **The language and parser.**
   - Kyr is pure and its public entry is enforced.
   - It is parsed and compiled once per build (AX-04: about 2.2 s of a new world is the Kyr parse).
   - The language grows only one retained construct, Situation and Incident (the owner, 23 November), before more
     content is written.
3. **The privileged interpreter.** `src/interpreter` evaluates Kyr with a causal trace, cold equal to warm, and owns its
   capability ports. The owner, 24 November: "the same path that the actual engine uses."
4. **The runtime.**
   - The installed context, which exists now.
   - Canon by groups as the loop asks for them: bodies and ancestries, then creatures with the overlay model, then items
     and the economy.
   - Typed change sets and receipts.
   - Lazy realization with explicit persistence boundaries.
5. **The common player.** One frame per revision, then the knowledge door, then TB-09 activity by activity. This is the
   `kyrie-player` contract.
6. **Settings.** The fantasy-rules domain installed separately from Westernesse. `data/` is inventoried, then laid out
   as `settings/` and `scenarios/` (the owner, 24 November) once its readers are known.
7. **Clients.** The page becomes a renderer of the frame. The terminal reaches parity. 3D stays an optional adapter
   (BRIEF §8).
8. **SRD and Orsino migration.** It runs alongside from step 4, by proof slices through the dungeon. Bulk creature
   migration waits for the overlay model, or it hard-codes the very variants the owner forbade ("'frost wolf' … should
   be forbidden as a defined type").

### Decisions costly to postpone

- **The identity and persistence boundary for realized places:** site identity, local seeds, the generation version,
  and what is persisted against what is regenerated.
  - Every dungeon level, wilderness point of interest and revisit test written before it is decided will be rewritten.
  - The owner's "two traversal orders" proof depends on it.
- **Situation and Incident as a retained construct in Kyr.** Each adventure authored before it lowers into latches that
  must later be lifted again. The owner's dramatic lint and the Studio view need it.
- **The ontology of the Atlas colours against the five resources.** The owner has left the fifth colour unsettled and
  calls the mapping a hypothesis. Facet inference (AP-03), the economy's pins and the commissioned paintings will all
  encode whatever is assumed by default.
- **The creature overlay model, before bulk SRD creature migration.**
- **The player protocol's shape** (frame, question, command, receipt, checkpoint), before `web` becomes a package or any
  spatial client is begun.
- **The categories of knowledge** (perceived now, remembered, learnt, inferred, mechanically available): conversation,
  the Encyclopedia and the specimen loop all bind to them.

Two decisions are safe to defer: the physical package moves, and save compatibility. The owner, 20 November: "No
cross-version compatibility promise yet."

## 4. One-year futurespective: November 2027

The experiences to aspire to support. The owner's words anchor each one; the shaping is this retro's.

**A mature Westernesse.**
- VISION §XIII's arc made playable: "a lantern, a few coins, and an uncertain rumor… descend beneath an abandoned
  tower… return to a town where the right person knows enough to be alarmed." The discovery leads to another
  expedition, the expedition changes a relationship, and the relationship opens a city.
- By then the reference dungeon is a deep and varied ecology.
- The first town has grown only where its functions demanded.
- Dun Rìerdan, the City of Seven Towers, is a campaign setting in its own right (VISION §VIII: "The royal palace should
  become a great campaign setting").
- The House of Doors is reachable through play. The owner, 8 November: "I sort of want to wait on the actual planes
  until we can definitively *arrive* at them through normal play."

**Several distinct games.**
- The owner's three are an island simulated in depth, the dungeon expedition, and a science-fiction vessel with social
  and strategic conflict.
- Each runs on `kyr-lang`, `kyrie-runtime` and `kyrie-player` with no Westernesse exceptions.
- This retro suggests keeping the allotment society as a fourth: a comic civic game whose pounds-not-gold economy keeps
  the platform honest.

**Human authoring tools.**
- `kyrie suggest` choosing by census: under-used paintings, the least-represented genres, a combat gauntlet against a
  wonder sequence (the owner, 24 November).
- Dramatic lint.
- Sampling and inspection of Deem.
- The Studio's omniscient Atlas view of a scenario.
- Adventure authors working in branches whose friction becomes cheap tests.

**Institutions and histories that are genuinely simulated.**
- Professions as a generative model passed through households and generations (20 November, §7).
- Character standing, from Commoner to Dark Lord, gained and lost "through actual world events" (19 and 20 November).
- Unvisited places with "abstract histories, populations, pressures," and "strategic adversaries act whether or not the
  player watches" (25 November).

**Exceptional artistic presentation.**
- A painting library organized by terrain, subject, season, weather and light, with demand held below exponential by
  falling back to the painting without weather.
- Instruments as the second art form: the sky, the map, the microscope.
- Every screen an engine page in one fixed frame.
- A 3D or spatial view, if built, only as an adapter that submits the same commands.

### Creative design worth beginning early, with implementation deferred

These are documents and tabletop exercises, not code. This retro suggests the owner and the lead begin them now,
because each takes longer to get right than to build.

1. **A bible of the dungeon's ecology:** what each band of depth teaches, how the strata connect to mines, buried
   cities and the Mathematical Age, and what makes level 40 unlike level 4.
2. **The economy as a tabletop game:** the rules sheet and conversion table the owner asked for, played by hand
   before any code is written.
3. **The canon of the Mathematical Age:** its microscopic machinery and its deliberate diversification of life. The
   owner calls it creative canon "to develop carefully", so it needs prose before content.
4. **Three companions written with a novelist's care** (VISION §IV): voices, contradictions, what each would remember.
   Simulation cannot supply this.
5. **Rules sheets for the island and the vessel**, written now to put pressure on the ontology while the ports are
   still being cut.
6. **The professions graph and the terrain columns** (gamma's commission). This includes the orcs as a history of
   admixture, "never reduced to a mechanical racial essence."
7. **Conversation played as a tabletop encounter between wills:** questions, refusals, silence (20 November, §5).

## 5. Conclusions

**The three highest-leverage investments to make now**

1. **Finish the trust thread through the reference loop:** one frame per revision, the knowledge door, and TB-09 for
   travel, talk, the fight and the map.
