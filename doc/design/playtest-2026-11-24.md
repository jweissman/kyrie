# The owner's playtests, 23–24 November 2026

The owner's play of the deployed build on the night of 23 November and a cold play the next morning. Their notes are
kept here as they wrote them. The register's rows OR-300 to OR-316 come from them. Each finding is filed by its kind:
a defect is an observed violation of a claimed contract; a requirement is a commissioned outcome not yet met; a
direction is a wanted future kept whole and not commissioned.

The owner's ruling: **continue the current execution plan.** These are findings and directions to fold into the beta
tracks. They are not a request to restart discovery, to reorganise the sprints, or to begin a large UI rewrite.
"Beta converge on one consistent player interface and a genuinely enticing first environment … Keep the game simple on
the surface, truthful underneath, and generous with possibilities. Don't hide the ball."

## The morning's cold play (24 November), the owner's findings in brief

1. **One global page frame and one consistent action menu.**
   - Action labels are concise and literal ("We come into the world as we are" is far too long); literary language
     belongs to the presentation around them.
   - The global page template owns the primary action menu's placement, layout and behaviour; no screen decides it
     for itself. Bottom-left and hard-right are both candidates; a fight might switch sides, in the same interaction
     language.
   - The hypothesis that rows may extend partly beyond the safe area, with a meaningful part (perhaps 20%) inside it.
   - The number of page templates and menu placements ratcheted down with layout-consistency regression tests, and no
     big-bang rewrite.
   - The player UI relatively unintelligent: a page frame faithfully presenting the engine's authoritative player
     model.
2. **Few exceptional controls; a small library of reusable instruments.**
   - Backstage tabs are a standard navigation instrument, at a fixed hard top, cycled by left/right or R1/R2.
   - The constellation filling most of the viewport, its twelve options in a fixed right-side menu with no paging on a
     large enough screen, and perhaps a modest orbit or zoom of its stars in three.js.
   - A lock's mechanism, or a crafted object. Instruments show and change authoritative fictional state and never
     simulate on their own.
   - Ancestry told with demographic, social, cultural and physical information, not combat statistics only.
   - One bounded instrument experiment at most; the shared page frame is the mainline.
3. **Backstage, character sheet and party.**
   - The backstage a coherent folio, perhaps in the Encyclopedia's parchment.
   - The sheet shows the character, their abilities, values, condition and equipment, with calculations on demand.
   - Shared semantic presentation primitives with the Encyclopedia.
   - The roster a compact floating portrait selector (lower right), the selected person the sheet.
   - A consequential person (joining, escorted, anchoring an undertaking) has a stable identity resolved from facts
     or generated, and persisted: an intentionally mysterious person is fine, an uninitialised placeholder is not.
   - A simple in-game Options / Return to Title flow, and Continue, New Character and New World kept distinct. During
     beta, migration of obsolete saves does not constrain world changes: a compatible save stays truthful, and an
     incompatible one is recognised and said.
4. **Atlas opportunity contradictions resolved at their source.**
   - A person or place marked with an opportunity whose offered acts do not bear it is a violation of the
     opportunity contract, not a styling problem.
   - Latent (known-possible, quieter, pastel) and immediate (actionable, brighter) told apart semantically, by what
     the player knows and what is genuinely reachable.
   - A strong mark leads to its act, or says what remains to be found.
   - Conversation drawers, paging, focus changes and the act's effects are in the invariant tests.
5. **The initial quest roster is too repetitive and too boring.**
   - Similar errands and fetch quests across runs, hours of travel for trivial reasons, to destinations with nothing
     worth doing, uncontested by the world.
   - Wanted: a much richer, more variable starting-situation repertoire (a large curated Deem table, a d100 rolled
     several times), varying motive, location, participants' relationships, urgency, obstacles, rewards, competing
     interests and ways of unfolding.
   - Fewer, excellent, reusable situations varied substantially, over many shallow jobs.
   - Most quests uncovered through people and places, who have lives, occupations, motives and problems and react to
     events.
   - Generated initial worlds tested across seeds for structural variety and first-minute appeal.
6. **The opening dungeon unmistakably inviting.**
   - From the first town the Atlas leads reliably to one substantial POI, in the town's hex or within one or two.
   - Its presence plain through scenery, rumour, visible approaches or a genuine opportunity gradient.
   - A companion to recruit at its entrance, and light, provisions and basic equipment to be had nearby.
   - The loop: notice the dungeon → prepare → recruit → explore → face danger → discover → return.
   - Secrets, deeper levels, alternative approaches kept: the invitation revealed, not the answers.
   - Several POIs and entrances within one hex, eventually connected; no assumption that a hex is one encounter, one
     entrance or one destination. The starting wilderness stays explorable beyond its marks.
7. **Acceptance.**
   - The normal acceptance ladder for defects.
   - For editorial quality, several independent generated openings compared: do their situations differ in meaningful
     ways, does each invite exploration, do the first fifteen minutes give the player something they want to do?
   - In the next relevant sprint demonstrations, without enlarging the active queue indiscriminately; the feature
     atlas and the architecture roadmap keep the longer-range directions.
