# The first hour, directed (a draft beat sheet, 20 November)

The lead's mission for M22 (the owner, 20 November): design the first hour, don't only repair it. A game of beauty,
wonder, fear, awe and delight; the first fifteen minutes and the first hour scripted with clear beats, while ordinary
exploring stays the engine's own. Two questions answered definitively: **in the first thirty seconds, is there something
exciting here? In the first five minutes, is there something fascinating?** Measured by cold plays on the deployed page,
scored beat by beat; the target is 8–9 out of 10 (cold plays have sat at 5–6.5).

This is a draft for the owner's eye. The route is the golden one: Iscoed, *Down the drove road* (the warrior), with the
weir (the mage) as the alternate. Each beat names its emotion, the time it should take, what the player meets, what is
there today, and what changes. Evidence: doc/architecture/mission-22/cold/ and first-hour-evidence.md (to come).

## The shape

| When | Beat | The player should feel | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–30 s | **The image and the wrong** | arrested; a little indignant | Is there something here? |
| 30 s–2 min | **A charge in your hands** | responsible; drawn in | What do I do? |
| 2–5 min | **Something not right in this town** | curious, a prickle of unease | Is it fascinating? |
| 5–15 min | **The first find, and a choice between goods** | competent, then torn | Does what I do matter? |
| 15–35 min | **Under the town** | fear, then awe | Is it dangerous and strange? |
| 35–50 min | **The best scene: the dead raised** | dread, then wonder | Can I be clever here? |
| 50–60 min | **Coming back, and the road out** | relief, belonging, longing | Do I want to go on? |

Between the beats the engine's ordinary life stays open: the green, the dog, the pump, the sky at night, a shop, a
talk. Beats are where the hour is pointed, not a corridor.

## 0–30 seconds: the image and the wrong

**Wanted.** One painting, three sentences, one person, one thing in your hands. Nothing to configure.
*At first light the customs-men were waiting at the march-stone. They counted the drove, and they took Bran. He threw
his tally-stick at your feet and went without a word.* Owain, fourteen, at your elbow. The tally-stick on the cobbles.
Two or three acts: take up the stick; ask Owain what happened; look at the customs-house door.

**Today.** The title page, *New game*, *how will you begin*, then about ten seconds of loading (OR-144), then 27 lines (the
heading, the day, the notices, the children, a dandelion, a bay tree…) and over fifty rows on the first turn (cold
play 9).

**Changes.**
1. *Set out now* is the default path and lands in the scene; the load itself becomes the first beat: the opening's
   painting and its first sentences shown while the world is made (the prologue as the loading screen), so the first
   ten seconds are the story, not a progress list.
2. The first turn tells only the incident: the arrival, the charge, the stick, Owain. Everything else on the green is
   met on the second look or as the company moves (the opening's other Observes deferred a turn).
3. The four are the charge's: the stick, Owain, the customs-house door, and nothing else until the company acts.

## 30 seconds to 2 minutes: a charge in your hands

**Wanted.** The drove is yours to mind; the four head short is the whole of the trouble; Owain says where they went in
the dark. One short act, one consequence: the first sentence the world says back to you.

**Today.** Present and liked (cold plays 4–9: "concrete, human and solvable in a few moves").

**Changes.** Keep. Only remove what crowds it.

## 2–5 minutes: something not right in this town

**Wanted.** One uncanny thing *witnessed*, not read on a notice: the town's dogs all turning toward the hill at once, the
geese gone silent; a four-note chime from the stones on the hill that nobody admits to hearing. Not explained. A hook,
left in the air.

**Today.** "At dusk every dog in the town turns its head toward the hill" exists as a line among twenty on the opening
turn, and is never seen happen.

**Changes.** The dogs' turning and the chime made an event the company sees in its first minutes on the green, once,
told plainly; one row toward it (look at the hill) and nothing more.

## 5–15 minutes: the first find, and a choice between goods

**Wanted.** The drowned four found in the Black Rhyne (sorrow, a little fear: the water, the dark, the bodies). Then Mardle:
the clerk who counts for a toll-farmer, with three children behind the chandlery. Ruin a drover or a father: a choice
with no clean answer.

**Today.** Present and loved ("moral grey… Count for him a year, and see if you count kinder"). The find "paid off only
later, through Mardle" (cold play 9): no way to tell Owain or Bran at once.

**Changes.** Telling Owain at the Rhyne: his face, the first gratitude (a beat of warmth). Mardle's choice kept whole.

## 15–35 minutes: under the town

**Wanted.** The corn-store's cellar dropping through into older workings: fear in the dark (the light economy players
praised: "the candle has 50 minutes left"), then awe: "Those who dug did not know it was here."

**Today.** Present and praised; the rats, beetles and bats are many and small ("bats, hundreds of them" were two).

**Changes.** Fewer, stranger things below, each worth meeting; one sight of awe at the bottom (a vast old place under a
small town).

## 35–50 minutes: the dead raised

**Wanted.** The grave-robber in the ossuary: talk, creep, warn off, fight, a rite. The best scene of every cold play that
reached it ("the dead fold down into the bone-heaps like coats let fall").

**Changes.** Keep; make sure every player who goes below reaches it (signposted from the undertown, not found by luck).

## 50–60 minutes: coming back, and the road out

**Wanted.** Return to the green changed: Bran out, the drove sold, someone who remembers what you did; the night sky over
the town (the sky instrument and the night's telling), the pump giving water. Then a horizon that is *reachable*: the
road to Gatesend and the sea-gate, Reedbridge, Brynach's invitation, each one a journey that can be set out on now.

**Today.** "Go up to Brynach, the Holyking barrow, Redkeep, the dragon. All noted, none reachable" (cold play 9).

**Changes.** Every horizon the hour names is reachable within it, or named as later ("when the passes open").

## Variation

The hour must not be the same hour twice. The beats are fixed; their fillings vary by Deem and Kyr: which uncanny sign,
which find, which choice between goods, which strange thing below. Each beat wants at least three authored fillings
(the tinker kit's pattern: a kit per beat, its roles bound from the world's facts). More Kyr expressivity and easier
Deem are the means (doc/design/directions-2026-11-20.md §13).

## Measures

- Cold plays on the deployed page, two per wave, each scoring every beat 1–10 and quoting the moment that decided it.
- The first striking moment (beauty, wonder, fear, awe, delight) within 30 seconds; another by 5 minutes; never more
  than 10 minutes without one.
- The first turn: at most 6 lines and 4 led rows.
