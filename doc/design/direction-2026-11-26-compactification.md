# Compactification: unity of place (the owner, 26 November, night)

Asked how the design itself might simplify the way to an 8 or better in cold reviews, the owner answered with a
direction. It governs the slice, the specs, the page's surfaces and generation until it is revised.

## The owner's words

> I'm fine retiring specs that don't serve the core aim. I do think in general we overspec/overprepare for things in a
> way that prevents us from getting things done efficiently … we can't get through big architectural changes if heavy
> specs for unrelated experiences are weighing us down! I definitely think we could focus on a simpler reference
> experience for now and take a hard look at specs that exercise a lot more than that.

> … we should seriously think about disabling and/or hiding the world map for now. Everything should layer not just
> onto one country, but onto one location. That _should_ be okay if we can make sure there are multiple points of entry
> within a region. But in general that should be depth within one major location. We should be able to supply whatever
> an adventure needs within the hex. Does it call for a dark tower? Well, create or generate one. Placement should just
> be topological. … the ideal constraint here is complete unity of place -- everything in one city, or even everything
> in one house, with its simple daily rituals and relatively set apart from plot hooks and quests until you start
> exploring. A storytelling game mainly concerned with conserving action points and efficiently using them to build up
> resources, explore the world, gain access to more interesting things -- we need to gradually invite people in.

> … you should get experience for skill checks in conversations, which should be more prominent.

> Treasure should basically never literally be placed in the world; you should roll on a Deem table. You shouldn't
> author a name if you can help it, try to generate it with a Markov chain; or better, make sure the character template
> for Deem itself uses the Markov chain for names with the appropriate corpus for its ancestry … I'm fine with most
> content being authored -- ideally it is, and ultimately human-authored … But the generation should be seamless and be
> able to plug in and repurpose authored content in different ways -- as general scenarios or else just overlaying the
> content in the space. … a useful exercise in compactification -- all of the author content in one place, everything in
> one town; and yet most things hidden, almost all of the content 'generated' in the sense that all the character and
> monster placements, all the dungeon and manor-house structure, came from Deem templates; and we should be building the
> authoring content with variation in mind I think is the core.

> … I'm not even sure we should show the diamond/lenses at all until act II (level 4-6 at least and well into the
> game). The world map can be hidden for now as part of a compactification exercise. Encyclopedia can be hidden until
> the player visits a library. Similarly there should basically be just one backstage page at this point; your
> company, with high-level achievements and current goals. The game log and full journal is not itself part of the game
> unless you have a scholar/scribe in the party perhaps (or accessible through oracle).

## What it decides

**1. The slice is the reference experience.**
- The supported slice is re-scoped from "Iscoed's two acceptance routes" to the reference experience: creation, the
  first place and its daily round, its woods and the deep place below, return, and save, on the page.
- Rows about other worlds and the old routes leave the slice. They are not closed, but kept in the record as the
  breadth's.

**2. The specs serve the slice.**
- On 26 November, specs that exercise other worlds took 13,068 of the kept seconds and Iscoed's 3,072, against 1,873
  for the reference experience, of 30,492 in all.
- The push lane keeps the kernel's contracts, the fragments, the reference experience and the page.
- The breadth (other worlds, the old routes, adventures' long walks) moves to the night's funnel.
- What serves no aim is retired, each with its reason recorded.

**3. Unity of place.**
- Everything layers onto one location: one town, or even one house and its daily rituals.
- Entries into it are many; there is depth within it, not breadth across a country.
- An adventure's needs are supplied within the hex: a dark tower called for is generated there, its placement
  topological.
- The world map is hidden while this holds. It supersedes, for now, the 25 November ruling that ways to another hex
  appear only on the map.

**4. Surfaces are earned.**
- The lenses and their diamond appear in act II (level 4 to 6, or later).
- The world map is hidden.
- The encyclopedia appears once a library has been visited.
- The backstage is one page: the company, its high achievements and its current goals.
- The game log and the full journal are not the game's unless a scholar or scribe is in the company (and always to
  the oracle).
- Each is an engine hint, every client alike.

**5. Generation over authored kits.**
- Treasure is rolled on Deem tables, never placed.
- Names are drawn from Markov chains with a corpus by ancestry, in Deem's character template itself.
- Placements of people and monsters, and the structure of dungeons and manor houses, come from Deem templates.
- Authored content, which is welcome and ideally human, is written with variation in mind, so that generation can
  overlay it in the space or repurpose it as a general scenario. Authoring needs a friendlier interface in time (a
  Kyr studio).

**6. The game's loop.**
- Spend the day's few action points well (OR-1200) to build up resources, explore and gain access to more.
- Conversation is more prominent, and a skill check in a conversation earns experience.

## How it is staged

1. **The slice and the spec diet:** the slice re-scoped, the breadth's specs moved off the push lane, the dead ones
   retired with reasons.
2. **Surfaces earned:** the stage gates as engine hints (the lenses by level, the map hidden, the encyclopedia by a
   library visited, one backstage page, the journal by a scribe).
3. **Conversation's experience:** a skill check in conversation earns experience.
4. **Unity of place:** the reference town as the one place, its entries, its daily round, its depth, an adventure's
   needs generated within the hex.
5. **Generation over kits:**
   - treasure by table;
   - names by Markov chain in Deem's character template;
   - placements and structures by templates;
   - the compactification exercise: all the authored content in one town, most of it hidden, generated in its
     placements.
