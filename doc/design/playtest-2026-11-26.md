# The owner's playtest, 26 November 2026

The owner's notes from a play of the deployed build on 26 November, kept here as they wrote them. Each finding is
carried to the builder who owns it; the rows come with that work. A defect is an observed violation of a claimed
contract; a requirement is a commissioned outcome not yet met; a direction is a wanted future kept whole and not
commissioned.

## The notes, as written

> - "Take your leave" or 'exit-like' options always need to come at the bottom
> - The color change for the encyclopedia is too different; it either needs to completely occupy the page, and
>   certainly can't have this hard dark glass chrome around it but also we probably need to tone down the luminosity a
>   bit since it is way too sharp
> - The world map is still quite chaotic; things were unfolding that were nowhere near where I was, and I hadn't
>   explored yet (certainly I don't yet know what's there _myself_)
> - First town still feels incredibly busy and like the character knows too much; important questions are long and so
>   become elliptical with '...' at the end; it just seems overrun with nonsense and things that don't matter and
>   "haggle with helga for the price of x" three time, which can't be the most interesting things in the world to draw
>   someone into

## Carried into

- **Exit-like rows last** (Game): a standing ruling. Take your leave, put away the map, back and out come last in a
  scene's rows, by one engine rule for every client.
- **The encyclopedia's look** (Mending, with a contact sheet at 1280 and 390): it fills the page or sits in the
  frame's one folio, with no dark glass chrome, its luminosity lowered within the contrast ratchet.
- **The map draws the character's knowledge, not the world's truth** (Trust): the first slice of the knowledge door
  (OR-741). Only where the company is and has been, what it can see, and what it has been told of; nothing far away
  unfolds until learned.
- **The first town, quieter** (Game): haggling inside the trade, one row per trader; questions with short
  engine-owned labels that never truncate, offered only when the character has a reason; an audit of the reference
  town's opening rows, before and after.
