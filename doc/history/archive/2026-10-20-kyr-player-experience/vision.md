# A. Kyr Player Experience Vision

**Lead a company whose abilities change how you approach the world, and whose adventures change what the world asks of you.**

Kyr is a party-based fantasy RPG about expeditions and their aftermath. A locked store, an injured surveyor, a dragon's hunting routine, a dangerous crossing and a village's obligations belong to the same world. The player learns enough to form an intention, prepares people and equipment for it, improvises when circumstances change, and returns with treasure, injuries, knowledge and consequences. The return matters as much as the descent.

The distinctive pleasure is connection. A miner opens a careful rescue approach; the extra time saved preserves a route before snow closes it. A rope is useful on the way in and becomes crucial after dragon fire destroys the bridge. A healer's survival preserves options for a second encounter. Paying a creature may preserve lives but consume the money needed for lodging. Letting someone walk home independently frees the party to explore, but changes how their family receives the company. Character development should add new such possibilities, not only larger damage numbers.

The strongest current evidence is already of this kind: twenty-minute miner rescue, line-secured ladder and an independently returning Tamsin (`N60–73`); paid dragon passage and a hunting schedule (`N21–31`); a burned bridge changing the retreat and later visit (`N83–93`). These are bounded demonstrations, not proof of a complete RPG. Large branching dungeons, authored multi-enemy encounters, substantial advancement and institutional conflicts are not currently demonstrated in the shipped campaigns.

## What playing should feel like

- **Oriented, not omniscient.** I can understand the room, its connections and what my companions notice. Rumors, promises and discoveries have sources. I can plan with incomplete knowledge without guessing what a button does.
- **Capable, not universally equipped.** Different companies find different approaches. Missing a specialist changes cost, danger or opportunity; it rarely makes the whole adventure a composition check. Equipment, spells and relationships can complement skills.
- **Dangerous, not arbitrarily punishing.** Threats have recognizable signs and responses. A dragon can be beyond us; negotiation, timing and retreat are real decisions. The interface does not quietly initiate a lethal fight while I inspect it.
- **Tactical, not a queue of damage receipts.** Roles, target choices, protection, resources, physical features and readable enemy intentions create reasons to change plans. Killing everything is one outcome among others.
- **Situated, not a verb menu over generic rooms.** Places contain meaningful passages, objects and inhabitants. The game supports the physical interventions it invites, with explicit boundaries rather than implied universal physics.
- **Consequential, not quest-driven by necessity.** People pursue authored purposes, time changes opportunities, institutions remember obligations, and unfinished business remains legible. An opportunity need not become an accepted task. Finishing a task does not reset the place or end the campaign.
- **Readable, not overexplained.** A concrete event, a concise mechanical consequence, and updated state tell me enough. Detailed rules are available where they answer a question; implementation guarantees are never fantasy narration.

## Product boundaries

The baseline is a single-player company with **three active companions**. Classes, backgrounds, equipment and learned techniques differentiate them across battle, exploration and social access. Temporary allies and later roster changes may exist, but large armies, multiplayer coordination and unrestricted party splitting are outside the first coherent product.

The world is persistent within a campaign and advances when actions take time. Reading, inspecting, planning, switching views and closing the application do not make it advance. Deterministic resolution means reproducible causes and saved rolls, not that a player knows the result beforehand. Simulate authored people, situations, resources and commitments faithfully; do not promise an infinite world or spontaneous human motives.

The intended product includes meaningful advancement, repeat expeditions, several viable approaches to situations, encounters with multiple opponents, recoverable setbacks and explicitly lethal outcomes, and a world where actions can alter future access, trust, travel and opportunity. The current engine's missing features are work to scope, not permanent limits on this vision.

First prove a complete expedition with a changed return. Then prove a continuing campaign: a second expedition uses something learned or earned on the first, while another situation has developed independently. If those loops are not interesting, additional screens, illustrations and content volume will not make Kyr whole.
