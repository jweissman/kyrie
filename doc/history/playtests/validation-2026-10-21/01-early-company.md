# An early company prepares

*Early company preparing. Company: fellowship; seed val-early.*

**Take the Wheatsheaf's door to the Wheatsheaf's taproom** — the Wheatsheaf's taproom

> A long low taproom smelling of beer and wet wool. A board by the hearth lists the Toll in chalk, and someone has added a rude drawing.

**Ask Wenna what is said on the road** — the Wheatsheaf's taproom

'Carters coming down say there's lights at Two Barrows some nights,' says Wenna. 'Little lights, low down, going in and out of the mounds as if they owned them. And the Hobs are out again on the downs: they had the Midsummer wool off three carts last month, and Bryony Crake's sheep.'

> Little lights are seen at Two Barrows on the Varrow road some nights, going in and out of the mounds.

**Ask Wenna about the Toll** — the Wheatsheaf's taproom

'Ninefingers,' says Wenna, and spits in the fire. 'Ottilie Ninefingers and forty cut-throats, sitting in the old Lanthorn at the top of the Gap, and every cart that goes over pays her two crowns a wheel. Skerry's starving and we're drowning in unsold grain. The reeve's offering money to whoever shifts her.'

**Take the Wheatsheaf's door to Hollin market** — Hollin market

> Hollin market: carts, a wool-cross, a well with a stone roof. Wagons for the Gap stand loaded and going nowhere; their drivers argue about the Toll.

> The bell is gone from its frame over the well. 'Hobs,' people say, and spit, and nobody has gone after it.

**Take Ottar's door to Ottar's chandlery** — Ottar's chandlery

> Coils of rope, poles in a rack, lamp-oil in stone jars and sacks of hard bread.

**Take Ottar's door to Hollin market** — Hollin market


**Take the reeve's door to the reeve's hall** — the reeve's hall

> The reeve's hall is a counting-house with a dais. Ledgers lie open; most of the figures are red.

**Ask the reeve what he wants done about the Toll** — the reeve's hall

'Ottilie Ninefingers out of the Lanthorn,' says Garrow, 'dead, gone or paid off, I don't care which, and the Gap open. The town will pay fifty crowns. Her men are thieves, not soldiers, but there are a great many of them, and the Lanthorn was built to keep armies out. Nobody has been inside it in a hundred years who came back to talk about it, except Ninefingers.'

> The reeve of Hollin will pay fifty crowns to whoever ends the Toll at the Gap.

**Take the reeve's door to Hollin market** — Hollin market


**Take the temple porch to the Temple of the Kindly Lamp** — the Temple of the Kindly Lamp

> A small temple with no images, only lamps: hundreds of them, in niches, on chains, in a great iron wheel, all kept burning.

**Ask Mother Ash about the Lanthorn** — the Temple of the Kindly Lamp

'The Lamplighters built it,' says Mother Ash, 'to hold a light at the top of the Gap that could be seen from the sea. They kept light the way you keep wine: bottled, laid down, aged. Something got into their cellars that ate it, and the beacon went out, and they went with it. This temple is what is left of them down here: we keep the lamps lit, because they asked us to, and nobody remembers why it matters.' She looks at the iron wheel of lamps. 'The lamps have been guttering this autumn. All of them, a little, every night.'

> The Lamplighters who built the Lanthorn kept bottled light; something that eats light got into their cellars and put out their beacon. Hollin's temple lamps have been guttering all autumn.
