# Let One Remember (the Corrow)

*Corpus review. Read in full: `keld`, `lore`, `growth` (new); the rest by Role list, discoveries and the five-act finale.*

**Premise.** A man given out for dead is alive; the Registry's Calvessan clerks have a name they let go in 412; a mountain hangs in the sky on a chain over a well no one has plumbed. A five-act chain from the Scarp, through the Well and the Margin, to a road the star fell down.

**What works.** An epic with a mechanism: memory as rule (a Keld child cannot be forgotten, a Stray is settled by naming). Keld is the best small town in the corpus: Teshen, Saska's three modes of speech (kept, laid, let), and Webb's visit that the company hears of on the road.

**Shared forms.** "Nobody has" ending lines are rare here; the module's recurring move is the long answer ending in a short hard line ("Kept, not let."), well judged.

**Weak or thin.** 151 places and 54 people but only 90 topics; most people have one. No `Ending` and, until now, no `Milestone` and no module `experience`: a company could resolve the Strays, expose Webb and hear what the Calvessa let go without any of it counting. From their role lists alone (not read in full) Hornwake and Hightown look like the places where one person carries a whole room.

**Danger and density.** Mostly social. Unfolded kin (level 10) and an eel are the only real combat.

**Connections.** First region: rumour only; `after` wired by finale (one / given / none / followed). Others: Perrin Halyard (canon), the Finder with the Lesser Instruments letter; Bede's hammer.

**Done.** `growth.kyr`: nine milestones (25 to 200 XP) for the things the Corrow itself resolves. Three Keld additions (Saska on Teshen leaving, Bede on the nine he sent home, the dogs on the settled chair).

**Left.** A real `Ending` set (the finale is five observes); a second voice in Hornwake; Hightown's Speaker and her daughter need more than two topics apiece.
