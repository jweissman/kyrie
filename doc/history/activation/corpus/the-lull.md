# The Lull

*Read: `lore.kyr`, the Description lines, the endings, every `Greets again`, `ashbys.kyr` head and `sixways.kyr` head; the rest by structure. 317 KB.*

**Premise.** Sixways, a hamlet that lives by a valley you can walk into one day in seven; and the Lull, where it has been the holy day for fifty-seven years and whatever is done without meaning it comes apart at sunset. A week in the Lull costs a night. Five endings by what was meant.

**What works.** The central rule is a story rule, not a trick: it separates meaning from effort and makes people's small vanities visible (Aldith Mote repainting a wall, Piran Trewin trying not to mean a carving). Its people live in households (Ashbys, Pryces, Gales, Trewins), which gives the module a tone of family rather than of quest. 108 topics, 98 gated; the best gating ratio of the nine, and 24 lines about companions joining.

**Shared forms.** Several households share a shape (an elder in bed, a bustling younger, someone with a prop: a trowel, a staff, a candle). The `Greets again` family ran to "X lifts / waves / raises Y to you" seven times in twenty-one.

**Weak or flat.** Eight return greetings were a bare gesture; the Lull's own rule makes them an opportunity (the person tells you what they have meant since).

**Connections.** The rumour is excellent and `after` exists already ("I could do with that. Or maybe not: I'd mean too little of it"). `lore.kyr` links to Linger, the Glossary, a surveyor with a chain.

**Encounter density and danger.** Three bands; in a module whose theme is that one cannot be hurt by someone who does not mean it, that is the right amount.

**Changed.** Eight return greetings rewritten around the sunset rule (Wenna: "I mean this candle, so mind it"; Piran: "working very hard not to mean it"; Davy's stick is bent the same way both times).

**Not done.** No new rooms, no new interactions: this module needs the least.
