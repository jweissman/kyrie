# Mission 17: the cold evaluation

A player who had read nothing of the game, its design, its code or its documents, played the build of bdc87ba on
2026-10-28 at 820 by 1180 (an iPad held upright) by touch, through a driver that taps the page as a finger would and
reports what is on it, for about 125 taps over two and a half game days. The evaluator was an agent briefed only to
play as a first-time player for about thirty minutes of real decisions and to write, plainly, what it was like. Its
tap-by-tap log is [cold-evaluation-log.md](cold-evaluation-log.md); its 126 screenshots were kept with the session
and are named in the report (they are not in the repository).

What it found that was the game's fault, and what came of it, is in the register (M17-55 onward) and in the commit
that answered it. The report follows as it was written.

---

I played Maud, an elf ranger with a longbow, sent by her order to copy old writing at Nocsrechies. I hired a minstrel,
Mindartis, for 3 coin. I went into a barrow with no light and a rune trap put me on the floor. I lived through the
night on a roll. I got healed at the shrine, cured a sick child in the next village, killed the outlaw knight Sir
Hamo, collected the widow's price and reached level 2.

## 1. Was it enjoyable?

Yes, in patches, and more than I expected from pages of text. The writing carries it. Lines like "She doesn't cry.
She counts the coins into your hand one at a time, and then puts the ring on top, and goes inside and shuts the door"
made me care about people I had met two taps earlier. There are plenty of hooks: within twenty taps I had five places
I wanted to go. The dice bite, and they are shown honestly. A trap took my ranger from full health to the floor, and I
slept on it not knowing whether she would wake. A knight took apart my bard and my summoned deer in one round, and
then two arrows after five misses won the fight. That swing is what I play these games for.

It is also tiring. Every place is a long scroll of bordered lists. The same sentences come back from town to town.
Half the faces are the wrong face. The first dungeon I picked was pitch dark, and I spent a third of the session going
from shop to shop looking for a candle that nobody sells.

Would I keep playing tonight? Maybe another half hour. I want to know what Kendra meant by "day in a jar", I want to
go back down the barrow, and I have a new level. But it feels like reading a well-written almanac with a dice engine
bolted on, not like being pulled through an adventure. If the next dungeon is also dark with nowhere to get a light,
I would stop.

## 2. What pulled me on, and what lost me

### What pulled me on

- **The letter scene.** Kendra reads my letter: "'Brother Anselm's hand,' Kendra says. 'I did not know he was still
  living.'" Then: "People go out there and come back with their sheep missing. Go in daylight." Two lines, and the
  place had a past.
- **Hooks with weight.** "There is a door into it ... Neblastagn. The springs come up inside, and the old people held
  them there with gates of bronze. Nobody goes in now. They say something lives in the water." And the notice:
  "WHOEVER LAYS THE MOWER THAT WALKS THE LONG MAN SHALL HAVE FORTY CROWNS OF THE STEWARD."
- **The widow.** "She opens her hand: coins, and a ring. 'All I have, for Sir Hamo Ashdown. Dead or run, I don't care
  which. Just gone.'"
- **Arriving at the barrow.** "No bird sits on the mound ... the owls hunt the mound and nothing else. Whatever comes
  up out of it by night, the voles keep well away from." It was the best arrival in the session.
- **Dungeon touches.** Being asked "Maud goes first / Mindartis goes first" before a doorway. Seeing the rolls written
  out ("d20 17 + charisma 2 + persuasion 2 = 21 against 13").
- **Waking up.** "Maud passes the system shock save (d20 11 + constitution 3 = 14 against 10). Maud comes round, with
  1 HP." That was real relief.
- **Kendra and the king's door.** She threatens: "Open it, and I will not pray for you, nor heal you, nor sit up with
  your dead." My bard reads her: "It is in how Kendra says 'down there': not as doctrine, but as a place someone went."
  Then a natural 1 when I bring up her brother: "'You know nothing about it,' says Kendra, and that is the end of it."
  Then my promise: "Kendra takes your hand in both hands. 'Then I will hold you to it'", followed by the "day in a jar"
  clue. Right after that, Cade the herb-wife set me the aim of bringing the king's crown out. That is a real dilemma,
  and the best ten minutes of the session.
- **The companion going home.** "'Leafglyn,' says Mindartis ... 'Smaller than it was.'" His old friend shuts her shop
  to fuss over him, and later says "Bring Mindartis back to me, will you."
- **Small endings.** After the fever breaks: "a child's voice comes out of it, complaining." After the fight: "His men
  look at each other, and at you, and one by one they unstring their bows and go off over the hills."
- **A world that moves.** The open graves count up from 5 to 6 to 7 while I'm busy elsewhere. Someone burned out the
  spider cave without me.
- **The level-up page.** Every choice shows exactly what it changes.

### What lost me

- **The dark.** "It is pitch dark. Without a light you can see nothing here, and do nothing that needs seeing." My
  status read "Nothing lit". Nobody warned me. I asked an herb-wife, an innkeeper, a clothier, a boatwright, a
  shrine-keeper and a carpenter, and none of them sold a light.
- **The trap.** One tap, and from full health to the floor: "The magical rune brings Maud down ... Maud takes 13
  radiant damage (0 HP left). Maud falls. Maud fails to resist (death)." There was no sign of it and no chance to look
  first.
- **Lying on the floor.** My bard knows Cure Light Wounds and could do nothing for me. Meanwhile the game's suggested
  actions were to strip my body: "Take a longbow from Maud for Mindartis", "Take chain mail from Maud for Mindartis".
  The rule that explains it ("Senseless: no cure reaches them. At the next rest they come round, or die where they
  fell.") was buried in the Book.
- **The misjudged fight.** The screen said "Maud reckons you would likely beat Sir Hamo Ashdown, but Mindartis could
  fall." In round 1 my bard went down, I was at 5 of 13, and the knight was untouched at 27 of 27. Then came five
  misses in a row at "1 in 2" and "3 in 5": a dull swing-and-miss loop.
- **"A NEW AIM The brood is quiet. Garth and Odile will want to know."** It came from nowhere. I had never met Garth
  or Odile, or any brood.
- **Repetition.** The same walking lines twice ("The path runs along a river for a mile, with alders on the bank and
  the water loud over the stones"). The same notice-and-toll paragraph in both towns. The same greetings from
  different people: "'Say it, then.'" (the boatwright, then the shrine-keeper), and "looks at you for longer than is
  comfortable. 'Well?'" (the widow, then Mindartis's dear old friend).
- **Wrong faces.** The grieving widow in black is shown as a smiling woman gutting a fish. So is a different woman in
  another village.

## 3. What confused me, in the order I met it

1. **The pre-filled character.** It offered "Warrior, Str 8 ... Int 17". It looked random, not like a sensible
   default.
2. **"+1 scribe" / "+2 scribe" on my qualities** before I had chosen anything called scribe. It is only explained two
   steps later.
3. **Rolling again changed my weapon** to a scythe, and nothing said so.
4. **Weapon tags.** "A RANGER'S OWN" and "SUITS YOU BEST" are never explained. I took a longbow, which was neither, and
   never learned whether that cost me anything.
5. **Ranger oddities.** The warrior got a specialty and the ranger got none. "Set Spear" is listed for a bowman. My
   animal companion is a deer with 3 hit points.
6. **The first game screen is a painting and four paragraphs.** Nothing to tap is visible until you scroll. I didn't
   know where the game was.
7. **The scene and its picture disagree.** The text describes an overgrown fountain; the painting is a crowded fair.
8. **The tower view's distances contradict each other.** "a long way off · about 2 hours" sits next to "some way off ·
   about 5 hours".
9. **The map.** The mound I chose is marked right on top of the word "Neblastagn", so I took them for the same place.
   The tower, the cave and the mound, the places I most wanted, have no labels on the main map.
10. **"Sir Hamo will see you."** He never appears. The way in just opens.
11. **"SET OUT FOR Sanhtusrecs"** is offered while I'm standing in Sanhtusrecs.
12. **The dark.** I expected a torch in my pack, a warning, or a shop. I got none of the three.
13. **"The grave-robbers go still when your light finds them."** I had no light, and the same screen said so.
14. **"Talk to the grave-robbers"** gave one line, no conversation.
15. **Being down.** I expected my companion to heal me, or a button for it. The only explanation was in the Book's
    Company tab.
16. **A candle I couldn't take.** The camp text mentions "a cross is scratched on the wall at the turning ... with a
    candle-end stuck in the crack". I was in an open forecourt of standing stones, so which wall? A candle was exactly
    what I needed, and there was no way to take it.
17. **"A NEW AIM" for the tower again.** It was the same aim with a higher grave count. I thought it was a new quest.
18. **Finnan's two faces.** She greets Mindartis like family, then greets me coldly ("Well?"). And she sells healing
    herbs, though the child's mother had just said "the herb-wife has nothing left".
19. **"Single combat"** that my bard and my deer fought in. Afterwards the page called it "You attack Sir Hamo
    Ashdown."
20. **"The brood is quiet."** I only worked out what it meant one journey later, from town gossip.
21. **Sun in the rain.** "Rain is falling, and people keep to their doorways" and "Ginger, a cat, lies in a patch of
    sun", on the same screen.
22. **The widow after she leaves.** She "goes inside and shuts the door", yet "Ask again what she is waiting for" is
    still offered.
23. **The inn bed.** I took a bed in the morning and woke in the evening of the same day.
24. **The Story tab drops real dialogue.** It replaces it with "Kendra tells you what they have heard about the opened
    graves". It also says the widow "has gone back to work", which she never did.

## 5. Does it feel like a fantasy RPG, or like a website?

It has the bones of a real tabletop RPG, and it is more honest about them than most computer RPGs. You get rolled
qualities, callings, to-hit numbers, saving throws, a system shock roll when you go down, a marching order, hirelings
with wages, rations, rest, experience for finished quests, a level-up choice, and a quest log that records who told you
what and when. The writing is closer to a good gamebook, or to Fallen London and 80 Days, than to Baldur's Gate. At its
best (Kendra, the widow, Mindartis coming home) it is better written than almost any RPG I know.

On the tablet, though, it looks and behaves like a well-made website:

- white pages, serif headings and rows of bordered link-cards
- a "Book" button with tabs
- places that are lists, not spaces
- travel that is a timestamped log
- fights that are a form with buttons
- a map that is a subway diagram

The paintings are beautiful, but so often the wrong person or the wrong scene that they read as stock decoration. It
feels like an RPG when people talk and when the dice bite: the rune, the night on the shock roll, the fight with Hamo.
In between, which is most of the taps, it feels like browsing. The closest thing I can name is a Fighting Fantasy book
crossed with a wiki.
