The taproom of the Lucky Phoenix

Salt-boilers at the long table, still white to the elbow. The fire has been let go low. Your mother is at the hearth with her back to the room.

You ask her where he was going.

‘Meóswaeter, with the reed-cutters’ salt,’ she says, and keeps her eyes on the pot. ‘Three days there and back, the way he goes. The dog came home on the fourth, lame in the off foot.’

She wipes her hands, though they are dry. ‘Eamon says the road’s been bad past the mound since the spring. Nobody will go and look.’
