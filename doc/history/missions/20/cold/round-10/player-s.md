# Cold play, round 10, player S: Gunnvor, a dwarf warrior (once a monster hunter)

An agent's cold play, not a human's. The build was daa96430 (12 November, early morning), in a random world, Wetcarr,
by the "Dead Man's Map" origin. The brief: a fighter who likes danger and finding things. S played about 155 actions,
to the evening of day 1. The running notes are in `player-s-notes.md`. The screenshots were kept outside the repository
(/var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold10/s/shots), and numbers in brackets refer to them. The agent's tool could not write its report
file, so this is its returned report, condensed by the builder: section by section, its claims and screenshot numbers
kept.

## 1. Summary
- In Stillshaw S read the well-post notice and took the catacombs job from Aric. S killed a grave-robber and his ghoul
  and took his book of the dead.
- S then went deeper: the charnel passage, the undercroft, a lettered flag levered up, a sealed vault, and an old mine
  with spiders and a goblin chief, beaten at 2 of 16 HP.
- S climbed back out, was paid by Aric, and travelled to Murkweard. There S solved the riddle door (River), killed
  wolves and a zombie, and read the castle roll.
- The map aim ("a cross at its door") was still open when S stopped.

## 2. The first five minutes
- Creation was quick and attractive, and each step's Details panel explains what the choice does.
- The road scene (a poacher's boy, the keeper's whistle, "an even chance · one try") was immediately interesting.
- The first town showed 11 portraits, two talk choices and a notice. The notice gave three jobs, each with "Follow
  this". After that S "always knew what I could do".

## 3. What S pursued
- **The catacombs:** chosen from three notices; S felt it was a real choice.
- **Going deeper:** S's own drive, and it paid off best.
- **Murkweard:** S's origin goal.

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
- **Talk:** clear and beautiful. Hepzibah's portrait does not match her description, and few people had anything to say.
- **Fights:** clear and pleasant, with intents, odds and "Let Gunnvor fight". Most were too easy; the goblin chief was
  the one real fight.
- **Pack:** the dead man's map, the origin's whole point, is not in it. The book has a blank icon. Light is invisible:
  S learned the candle was running out only from the Wait panel.
- **Map:** pretty, and planning the trip was clear. The request hung and S arrived without seeing the journey.
- **Notes:** good. A few aims picked up underground never appeared.

## 7. Writing
- **Memorable:**
  - the bear asleep "with a thin man for a pillow";
  - the undercroft "dug down into it, from three sides";
  - "something is turning";
  - the riddle door and the castle roll cut in two.
- **Repetitive:**
  - "will give you N minutes, no more" on every monster;
  - "looks away solemnly";
  - every gem "princess-cut, a little clouded".
- **Paintings against the text:**
  - sunny springs for "evening, stormy";
  - an intact castle for a broken keep;
  - a statue gallery for the charnel shelves;
  - one mine painting reused three times.

## 8. Danger
The goblin chief was tense and real. The odds went from "should be won" to "could be lost" in two rounds, with no
chance to prepare or sneak. Flee failed despite "sure, now". Running out of light was a real danger that S noticed only
by chance.

## 9. Keep playing?
"Yes, moderately": for the mysteries (something turning below the spring, the barricaded winze, the walled stair). The
fights are pushovers, walking room to room through Go… is tedious, and S "stopped trusting" the paintings.

## 10. Boredom and interest

- **Best:** levering up the lettered flag (64), which S found unaided, and the goblin chief at 2 HP.
