# Westernesse, cold play: report (Dagny, dwarf cleric of Love)

## 1. Summary
- I made Dagny, a dwarf cleric of Love who used to be a prospector. Her origin was "Gone Under": her sister went into Ísencepan and never came back. Play started at Lowmouth.
- I talked to almost everyone on the landing and in the inn. I hired Cyril, a pedlar and bard, for 6 coin, and took the spider job from the well-post notice.
- I cleared the quarry: a cave spider, the spiders' mother, her eggs, and three beetles. Then I found the hidden undercroft under the town and opened its lettered flag. Below it I killed a ghoul in a sealed vault and took a cord of rings and a moonstone.
- Eamon paid me for the spiders, and I paid the players' bar bill so they could leave. Gosse offered me a season with his company, and I said no.
- I reloaded once and my progress was kept. I travelled to Ísencepan, slipped past wolves, and reached the lord's chamber, where I read the castle roll. I found nothing about my sister. I stopped there after about 155 actions.

## 2. The first five minutes
The origin cards were excellent: seven short, personal reasons to have come, each one a hook. Creation was quick. Callings that don't suit your ancestry are greyed out with "Not for dwarves", which explains itself. Clerics have twelve domains, which was a pleasure to choose from.
- **Who I was:** The road scene (the wagoner, 20-after) and the letter card (18-setout) told me clearly who I was and why I had come.
- **Where I was:** I arrived at the causeway (22-arrive) with a painting, one paragraph and a way on. The landing (27-landing) was rich and full of people right away.
- **What was interesting:** A bear asleep with a clown for a pillow, and an actor-manager squinting at the sky. I wanted to talk to all of them at once.
- **What I could do:** Talk, look, go. That was clear from the portraits and choices.

The toolbar icons had no labels and showed no hover hints (26-hover-route). I learned them by clicking each one.

## 3. What I decided to pursue, and why
1. **The people first.** That was my temperament, and the players' troupe was plainly the heart of the town.
2. **Cyril.** The Go list marked the taproom "someone who might join you". She turned out to come from Mistshaw and to know Ísencepan, which tied straight back to my sister.
3. **The spiders.** I took this job because Eamon handed me the key and an antidote when I asked about the quarry. The boy Dannel was right there to be asked, and the reward would let me pay the players' bill. That chain felt like my own choice.
4. **The undercroft.** I found it myself, and that felt like discovery.
5. **My sister.** After the town I followed my sister. The map took me there in one click, but the trail went cold once I was inside.

I always felt I had a choice: three notices, the players, my sister, and Gosse's offer.

## 4. Moments of confusion
- **Hepzibah, 27-landing / 28.** The choice "ask hepzibah about lem" is all lowercase, and I didn't know who Lem was. I only learned he had run off with the takings after talking to Gosse. Hepzibah's "If you find him" came before I knew he was missing.
- **Ending a talk, 31-absalom.** Pressing 2 for "Take your leave" emptied the talk panel instead of leaving. Only "ASK AGAIN…" and "2 Take your leave" were left, and I had to click again.
- **Portraits move, 61-eamon and 134-gosse2.** The portrait row reorders when a companion joins. I tapped where Eamon had been and got Cyril ("Nothing to say to you now"). Later I tapped where Gosse had been and got Nicol.
- **Eamon's topics, 64-eamon-sister.** Pressing 1 then 3 gave me an answer about Saltgrave and Thrume that I hadn't meant to ask for, and the 14-item list renumbered itself.
- **The adit gate, 80-candle and 129.** I was already inside the adit, which I had entered with my key, and it still offered "Push and pull at the padlock on the adit gate" and "Listen at the old quarry pit". The pit kept offering the padlock after I had been through the gate twice.
- **The undercroft clue, 107-search.** Searching the brood-chamber gave me "The ring of letters on the undercroft's flag is cut here too" before I had ever seen an undercroft. It paid off later, but at the time it referred to something I didn't know.
- **The book button, 140-notes2.** The first tap on it from the map did nothing; the second worked.
- **Fen or fields, 142 / 143.** Ísencepan is called "the fen" in the header and "Open fields north-west of Mistshaw" on the card.

## 5. Misleading or useless controls and text
- **Lambert, 38-mistshaw:** "Ask for news of Mistshaw" got "The bull's out again". Filler.
- **Moll, 43:** "Nobody looks at a pauper, so I see everything." No follow-up was possible: I couldn't ask what she had seen or give her a coin.
- **The wagoner, 71-sit:** "talks while you eat", but nothing he says is shown.
- **Ask again (30, 35):** it only repeats the answer you already had, marked "· asked". It looks like an option but isn't one.
- **Retreat in gold (81-gallery, 118, 148):** "the way you came" was edged gold in danger rooms, the same as my aim.
- **The wolves, 148-bailey:** "The wolves will not wait for it" warned me off moving, but walking past them to the great hall had no consequence at all.
- **"STILL TO DO" after the mother fight (100-r5):** it still said "Deal with the spiders' mother" after she was dead.
- **Damage numbers in the fight log:** the headline and the small print disagree. Examples: "hits for 5" against "takes 1 piercing damage (roll 5)" (87), and "hits for 6" against "takes 2 bludgeoning (roll 6, bonus -1)" (100).
- **Duplicate trade:** "Draftsman" and "Draughtsman" are both offered (14-next).
- **Spell picker (09–13):** three spells come pre-ticked. Nothing shows which one is "prepared first", and there is no Done button, only CLOSE.

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
- **Conversation.** The portrait-led talk page is beautiful, and the best lines are very good (Gosse, Jem Pudding, Eamon on my sister). But most townsfolk have one canned "Ask about his work" line and then nothing. Hooks like Moll's go nowhere. I could never ask anyone about my sister except Eamon, and I found that only by digging through his 14 topics.
- **Fights.** Clear and quick. The intent arrows ("→ Dagny · bite"), the "2 in 3" odds when picking a target, the pre-fight reckoning ("A hard fight… Dagny could fall") and Let-X-fight (T) all worked well. Bleeding, infection and festering cluttered the log, and the log is hard to read over bright paintings (95-club). All four fights were won comfortably.
- **Inventory.** I barely used it. Loot comes from links in the Look panel. A text click on "Take" didn't work and I had to tap the button. Buying a candle at Lambert's was easy. The sheet (75-company) is readable.
- **Map.** Gorgeous little 3D hex diorama, almost all fog. "Ísencepan, on the map" from the notes worked. No route was drawn ("THE WAY +" was collapsed), and the trip was instant.
- **Notes.** Well organised: what you mean to do, the jobs, what you were told, what you saw. But the single most important clue to my main aim, Eamon saying "look just inside, where the light gives out", was never written down. Once I followed the spider job, my sister's aim was filed under "TO HAND IN LOWMOUTH", which is wrong.

## 7. Writing
- **Memorable:**
  - Gosse: "I do not ask for money. I never ask for money. I mention that it exists." and "Forty-one years I have played under the sky."
  - Jem Pudding on Patience the bear: "young for a duchess… never hurt anyone but a goose, and that was a misunderstanding."
  - Eamon on my sister.
  - The undercroft that the corn-store, church and quarry "were all dug down into… by people who did not know it was here."
  - The castle roll: "ships in sight. Then a day with no entry. Then no more days."
  - Dagny "does not look pleased" after killing a beetle.
- **Repetitive:**
  - The wagoner resolution restates "the cart is out of the ditch" three times (21).
  - "The spiders in the old workings is here" (grammar).
  - "Things click and scuttle in the spoil" after the beetles were dead.
- **Mismatched descriptions:** Hepzibah is "a small grey woman in spectacles", but her portrait is a young dark-haired woman with a mandolin (29). Baldric is a "chocolatier" in a salt port who mends hinges, and his mother is a pauper.

## 8. Danger
The danger boxes were clear and arrived before contact, with a time limit ("will give you 9 minutes") and an honest reckoning of the odds. The wolves were the only "Beyond you" threat, and I could walk straight past them. I never came close to dying.
- **Healing makes harm meaningless.** The Love at-will cantrip Kindle heals 1d3+10. It sits under Wait → Tend and costs nothing, so I was back to full after every fight.
- **I couldn't really prepare.** The boy said the eggs must be burned, so I worried about fire. But burning them was a single click, with no oil, torch or check needed.

## 9. Did I want to keep playing?
Yes, but mostly because of the town. Lowmouth's people, the undercroft secret, and Gosse's offer of a season on the road pulled me in. Ísencepan let me down. I came for my sister, followed the gold line, and got kingdom history and a list of feudal dues. Nothing in the keep acknowledged her, and the place Eamon described ("just inside, where the light gives out") never appeared. If the next room still had nothing of her, I would lose the thread.

## 10. Boredom and interest
- **Bored:** going portrait to portrait through Moll, Baldric, Nicol, Lambert and Marina, who each had one line or nothing. Also the spell-picker fiddling, and status-effect spam in fights.
- **Most interesting:** opening the undercroft (116, 117). An odd clue I had picked up by searching suddenly made sense. It joined three separate jobs into one buried place and opened a stair under my feet. It felt like I had found it, not been handed it.
- **Second:** paying the players' bill and coming back to find Gosse "inspecting its wheels as if he had just bought it" (133).

## 11. Decisions
- **Hiring Cyril for 6 of my 10 coin** meant I couldn't pay the players' 8-coin bill until I earned more. A real trade-off, and Cyril paid for herself: she read the glyph at Ísencepan with her lore.
- **Paying the players' bill** (8 of 16 coin). It changed the landing scene and opened Gosse's offer to take service with the company for the season.
- **Gosse's offer itself** (declined for now) felt like a fork in the story.
- **Dashing past the wolves** felt risky, but nothing came of it.
- **Tactics:** Cyril's Inspire at the start of each fight, Dark Bolt on the first spider, picking beetle targets by their odds, and letting Cyril auto-fight.

## 12. Ratings
- **Clarity 3/5.** Choices and danger are clear. Unlabelled toolbar icons, shifting portraits and the hidden heal under Wait are not.
- **Beauty 4/5.** Paintings and type are lovely. Several paintings contradict the text: a sunny temple for the ruined keep (144), an intact candle-lit gatehouse (147), a flag-flying castle (148), and egg sacs still shown after burning (103).
- **Interest 4/5.** Lowmouth is dense with real hooks, and the undercroft is a gem.
- **Challenge 2/5.** Every fight was comfortable, healing is free, and the wolves didn't matter.
- **Wish to continue 3/5.** The town pulls me on, but the main aim's trail went cold.

## 13. Motivation
- **About 10 minutes:** meet the players and find out about Lem and the hat. My own curiosity.
- **About 30 minutes:** see what is under the undercroft flag, and earn enough to free the players. Mine, helped by the game.
- **End:** find my sister. That is the game's aim, but it gave me nothing to go on inside Ísencepan, so by the end it was more a feeling than a plan.

## 14. Marks and promises
- **Wagoner, teal dot (22):** the reward was there ("Let him thank you · for you": 2 coin and a bowl). Kept.
- **Hepzibah, blue dot (27):** got a "YOU KNOW NOW" fact about Lem's wages. Kept, but out of order.
- **Cyril, rose dot and "might come along" (54):** kept.
- **Eamon, blue dot and "another aim" (54):** kept (the spider job and the bill).
- **Corn-store padlock, blue dot (50):** I never found out what it led to.
- **Gold "your aim" lines:** they led truthfully through the quarry and back. At Ísencepan the gold led to the lord's chamber and the castle roll, not to my sister. The promise of the aim wasn't there.
- **Teal dot on loot (125):** the cord of rings was real.

## 15. What was offered
- **Worth doing:** the spider job, the bill, Cyril, and Eamon's sister answer.
- **Led nowhere:** "Push and pull at the corn-store padlock" and "Listen at the corn-store" were offered at every visit to the landing and never explained. The players' wagon could be looked at but not acted on.
- **Not put in front of me:** the undercroft through the broken floor of the working face, which was the best thing I found. Also Eamon's answer about my sister, buried at item 13 of his "Something else…" list.

## 16. Colours
- **Gold:** my followed aim (but it is also used for retreat).
- **Red/orange:** danger and attacking.
- **Blue:** something to learn or another aim.
- **Rose:** a possible companion.
- **Teal:** something for me (a reward, loot, "Noted").
- **White dot or filled white diamond:** never worked out. Examples are "To the shrine of Nodens" (54) and "Into the gate-passage" (144).
- **The surprise:** gold on "the way you came" in danger rooms.

## 17. Who I made
Recognisable: a dwarf woman, Love cleric, ex-prospector looking for her sister.
- **What carried through:** the origin set the main aim. The prospector trade gave me a shovel and Digging +6, which I never used. Love domain gave me Kindle and Rejuvenate, and Kindle made healing trivial. Dark Bolt was useful once.
- **What didn't:** Dagny's stats (Dex 4, Cha 7) were never shown or chosen at creation. A Love cleric with Charisma 7 feels off. Ancestry was mentioned nowhere in play.
- **What did matter:** Cyril's lore reading the glyph.

## 18. Getting about
- **In town:** the Go list worked. Its symbols (· → ⊘ ⇢ ←) are not explained.
- **Map travel:** no route was drawn. Travel from Lowmouth to Ísencepan was instant: no road, no event, and the clock barely moved (arrived 1:35).
- **Entering a place:** I could tell how to go in: an ENTER button on the map card, and "Into the bailey / gate-passage" choices.
- **Inside the dungeon:** the gold "toward your aim" thread through the quarry levels was reliable.

## 19. Waiting
There was essentially no waiting. Set Out went straight into prose cards, and the world was ready within a few seconds of clicking. Reloading and Continue took about 5 seconds. The page never seemed frozen. The very first load request returned an empty reply, but the page was there anyway.

## 20. Your aim
- **Spider aim:** followed perfectly. Each step was shown, the "STILL TO DO" summary was mostly right, and it handed off to "Tell Eamon".
- **Sister aim:**
  - Eamon's key clue was never recorded in Notes.
  - After I followed the spider job, my sister was filed under "TO HAND IN LOWMOUTH".
  - At Ísencepan the gate entry wasn't marked as my aim (144). Later the gold appeared through the bailey to the great hall and the lord's chamber, which held only a feudal roll and kingdom lore.
  - The aim lost track of what it was about: a missing person.

## 21. Top five problems
1. **The main aim's payoff is missing.** My sister's trail (Eamon's "just inside, where the light gives out") is not in Notes, and nothing of her appears at Ísencepan. The gold line leads to unrelated lore.
2. **Most townsfolk have one dead-end line.** I could not ask the people around me about my own goal: Moll's "I see everything", the wagoner who "talks while you eat", the two men from Lowmouth never present. A curious player runs out of people fast.
3. **No real danger and no real cost.** The at-will Kindle heal (1d3+10) makes HP meaningless. Warned-of threats (the wolves, "will not wait for it") do nothing when ignored, and burning the eggs needed no preparation.
4. **Paintings and portraits contradict the text.** Ísencepan is shown as a sunny temple, an intact lit gatehouse and a flag-flying castle. Egg sacs are still there after burning, Hepzibah is not grey, and there's a lantern in the "pitch dark" adit.
5. **Small interface traps.**
   - The portrait row reorders, which causes mis-taps.
   - Ending a conversation with the number key misbehaves.
   - Toolbar icons have no labels or hints, and healing is hidden under Wait.
   - Stale options persist (the padlock on a gate I'd already passed, "Still to do" after the kill).
   - Fight-log numbers contradict each other.
   - On the domain screen the "Later:" line runs under the Continue button (08-love).
