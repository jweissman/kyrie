# Cold play report — Westernesse, round 7, player "l" (world kahitude)

*Build: main at fa374a83, served locally (GitHub Pages was queued behind a GitHub Actions backlog). `?world=kahitude&opening`; asked to make a non-fighter. Notes and screenshots: /var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold7/l/.*

## 1. Summary
Merle, a birdfolk bard (once an apothecary; origin Feverwort). About 150 actions. I carried feverwort to Fearnmaere, helped a wagoner on the road, gave the herbs to the sick child's mother and sat up with the child until the fever broke; Edith (her elder sister, a warrior) joined me out of gratitude. I read the notice board (three jobs), hired Roscoe (a halfling thief) with almost all my coin, slept on a bench at the alehouse; killed the rats with ratsbane overnight (as the smith had hinted), Edith killed the old buck rat in one blow, I found grave rings in the nest and was paid. I firebombed the spiders' brood-chamber (as the boy's warning suggested), fought the remaining spider, Edith was hurt, I healed her, and was paid. Then I travelled the map to Reodcepan (a broken keep), solved the riddle door, set off a pressure plate with a pole, picked a strongbox for a royal scepter, and backed away from the undead castellan. Stopped Day 2 afternoon.

## 2. The first five minutes
Character creation was quick and handsome (origin → ancestry → calling → trade → name/sex), each step with a clear "Details" panel. I knew exactly who I was: a birdfolk bard carrying medicine for a sick child, told not to dawdle. The road scene with the wagoner in the ditch was a good first choice set against the "don't dawdle" instruction. One confusion: the first screen asks "Why have you come to Fernholt?" but my story sent me to "Fearnmaere" — it took a while to see Fernholt is the region. The first real scene (the old stones, nine portraits around them) was immediately interesting; Aldith's portrait and the fever scene were moving.

## 3. What I pursued, and why
1) The fever — the game set the aim and was right to. 2) The notice board's jobs — offered, but I chose the order myself (rats first because I'm fragile; spiders because the boy's story hooked me). 3) Reodcepan — Isla named three ruins; I picked it myself as the closest and because "what its lords left there is there still". I felt I had a choice; there were almost too many aims (about 9 open by the first evening).

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
Conversation: the big portrait on the left with choices on the right looks wonderful and the townsfolk have character (the boy Dannel's "I dropped Dad's knife. Don't tell him" was the best line); but each conversation scrolls into an ever-growing log, some lines report talk without content ("he talks while you eat"), and some people (Joan) have nothing but trade. Fights: clean and legible — company left, foe right with what it intends next, abilities with save numbers, a predicted outcome before committing. Tactically I used Inspire, Dirty Trick, an antidote. Both fights were decided by Edith's axe within two rounds; Inspire never visibly changed anything; Edith's "Got 'em!" came twice. Inventory: the Pack tab works; buying a dagger doesn't equip it ("striking with fist 1d2" until I found Equip); healing between fights is buried under Pack › "Tend the company". Map: a beautiful 3D hex island in fog; the journey card (time, arrival, how the place is known, the way step by step) is clear; I saw no route line or journey, I just arrived. Notes: excellent — aims sorted (what you mean to do / to hand in / further off), every fact with its source and time, FOLLOW THIS per aim; button placement inconsistent.

## 7. Writing
Memorable: the fever night ("her hand goes cool in yours"), the boy's puddings and knife, the riddle door that "turns over, like a sleeper", "the stone breathes out cold". Repetitive: "The wheel is out of the ditch, and the wagoner's meal is on the road again because of you" about five times, including on reload; lighting the lantern narrated three times (97); the rat-runs and nest reuse the same painting. Text and painting contradict several times: a working mine with lit lanterns for a gated, webbed adit (95); intact glowing egg-sacs after I burned them (107); an opulent palace hall with a roaring fire for a forced, ruined gate-passage (135); a pristine castle with its flag flying for "grass and nettles" (138); bright sun in the corn-store "evening" (42). Reodcepan is "open heath" on the map card but "in the woods" in the heading and in Isla's account.

## 8. Danger
Each danger was announced in a red DANGER box with a countdown ("will give you 9 minutes") and a frank forecast ("Edith could fall"); I could see everything coming. Retreating from the beetles and the castellan was safe; nothing chased me. I could prepare (ratsbane, firebomb, light, healing). Darkness was the real hazard — torches dying without warning was the only nasty surprise. I never felt Merle herself was in danger; every enemy targeted Edith.

## 9. Did you want to keep playing?
Yes, moderately. The riddle door, the scepter, and the castellan who "would not give up the keep, and has not" made me want to come back with fire. The undercroft mystery (rings of letters) is a good thread. What blunts it: navigation hidden in the Go menu, gold marks I can't always trust, and fights that Edith settles alone — I barely played my bard.

## 10. Boredom and interest
Bored: walking the town through the 13-item "20 minutes" Go list, trading, repeated lines. Most interesting: throwing the firebomb into the brood-chamber because a frightened boy had told me about the "puddings" — information from a conversation paid off in a way that felt like my own plan. The riddle door a close second.

## 11. Decisions
1) Hiring Roscoe for 7 of my 8 coin (exactly enough left for the bench). 2) Saving my only firebomb for the spider eggs rather than the rat nest. 3) Ratsbane (wait a night) instead of fighting the rats. 4) Leaving the castellan unfought. The ratsbane did kill the rats and the firebomb did burn the brood, and both were acknowledged. In fights: Dirty Trick, Inspire, an antidote on Edith (wasted). Helping the wagoner later gave a free meal at the inn and 2 coin.

## 12. Ratings
- Clarity 3 — scenes and fights clear, but ways in and out hidden in Go and gold marks sometimes wrong.
- Beauty 5 — portraits, paintings, typography and the 3D map are lovely, even when some paintings contradict the text.
- Interest 4 — good threads (undercroft, riddle, drover, castellan).
- Challenge 3 — real resource choices, but fights trivial for the warrior and forecasts take the tension away.
- Wish to continue 3.5 — I want to return to Reodcepan, but I'm weary of hunting for exits.

## 13. Motivation
~10 min: save the child (the game's aim, shared). ~30 min: burn the spiders and find what the boy saw (offered, but by then my own). End: return to Reodcepan with fire or radiant magic for the castellan, and find what the scepter is (my own).

## 16. Colours
Gold = my aim / next step. Blue edge or pip = something to talk about / a lead. Pink/rose pip = could join me (Edith, Roscoe) — consistent. Teal = something good for me (noted, "for you", gains). Green pip = a reward waiting. Red = danger or "cannot be undone". Violet = "since you were last here". Surprises: gold on "Give healing herbs", gold pointing past the buck rat; the blue edges on corn-store options didn't seem to mean anything special.

Recognisable: yes (the bird portrait in finery is charming). The origin mattered a great deal at the start (feverwort, Aldith, Edith). Being an apothecary didn't come up beyond starting items. Being a bard mattered once (singing, which I failed) plus Inspire and Cure. On the map the journey card listed the way step by step, but no line was drawn; the way went north up the road and back south to a keep that looked next door. I just arrived; I never saw the journey. Going into the keep was obvious (a big ENTER button).

## 19. Waiting
The very first request took >2 minutes and the connection dropped, but the page was ready when I checked again. After "Set out" from creation, "Enter to go on" appeared after a few seconds. The map journey request hung for a while before showing the arrival. Several clicks returned an error and then happened late. After the reload, Continue loaded in <8 seconds with everything kept.

## 20. Your aim
For the fever the game kept to the aim perfectly. Rats: after I followed the buck-rat aim, gold pointed past it to the drain; the overnight swap to the "old ways… undercroft" aim lost track of the rats until I went back. Spiders: no gold led me to Isla to hand in. "Your aim" and "another aim" labels sat on different links in the same scene without explaining which aim was which.

## 21. Top five problems
1. Navigation hides in the Go menu: ways up/down, newly opened rooms, and sometimes every exit missing from the scene's choices (47, 114, 140, 148).
2. Gold/aim marks sometimes wrong or missing (gold on "give herbs", gold past the buck rat, no gold for the spider reward, an aim appearing from nowhere overnight).
3. Paintings contradicting the text (palace hall for a ruin, intact egg-sacs after burning, a working mine, a pristine castle).
4. The bard barely matters: Edith wins every fight; no way to talk to the castellan who spoke to me; lore/Charm/gliding never came up.
5. Small trust breakers: instant sell with no confirmation and the ability to sell companions' gear; antidote "to no effect" leaving the poison; torches dying silently mid-action; repeated lines (wagoner ×5, lantern ×3); toolbar icons that move and have no tooltips.
