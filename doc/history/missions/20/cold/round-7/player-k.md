## 1. Summary
Made Wisp, a catfolk thief (origin "A Dead Man's Map", once a drifter), and played ~152 actions over one in-game day (8 a.m. → ~7 p.m., 1 Leaffall). In Merinmasto I picked the corn-store padlock, cleared the rats and their buck rat, nearly died to a centipede in the old drain (fled at 2/7 HP), got the rat bounty from Tegan, sold grave rings, healed, hired Tomas the scholar-mage, received a sling from the poacher's boy I'd covered for on the road. Paid a deserters' toll on the road, reached Heahheald, slipped past the gate hobs, freed a shepherd's boy, learned hobs flee bells, found a handbell, rang it and routed the whole hold (+600 xp), picked a chest, solved the riddle door ("River"), dug up the dead man's box, killed the Gaffer, and stopped at a portcullis that needs "brawn" nobody in my company had.

## 2. The first five minutes
Very clear who and where: the origin cards ("Why have you come to Willowweald?") plus one short painted prologue told me I carried a dead drover's map to Heahheald with a cross at its door; the first goal was stated outright. Immediately interesting: the prologue line "In the morning he was dead in the straw, and nobody in the inn would say of what", and the poacher's boy scene (00-start → 17-enter2), a real moral choice in the first minute with odds shown ("an even chance · one try"). The market (21-market) was rich (11 portraits, rumours, a bear asleep with a man for a pillow) but a lot at once; I understood portraits = people I can talk to, but not what the blue pips on two of them meant.

## 3. What I pursued, and why
The rats: the well-post notice offered three jobs; I picked the rats only because the corn-store padlock was right in front of me and I was a thief — my own choice, the game pushed only gently (gold "your aim" line). Heahheald: because it was my origin goal and the market rumour pointed the same way. At Heahheald the shepherd's boy's clue made my own plan (find a bell, ring it) — that felt like a real choice and reward for listening. Overall I always had at least three directions open (catacombs, quarry spiders, Tenebrerecs crown, Glachesfons, taking the boy home).

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
Conversation: the best part — full-height painted portrait (61-tegantalk) is gorgeous; 3–4 topics; replies in character and lead somewhere (king-barrow → Tenebrerecs; bells → plan). Trade inside conversation is neat. One tonal miss: the tied, crying shepherd's boy greets me "Now then! A new face. What can I do for you?" loudly inside the hobs' hold (93-shepboy).
Fights: clean layout, readable cards, portraits, "Let Wisp fight this turn" for speed; abilities have clear effects (Dirty Trick, Smoke Screen); Flee shows odds. Detail lines are dense (one line holding several saves: "fails the magic save… fails the poison save… passes the fortitude save"). Rats "need 24" to hit me. "The Gaffer is under frozen." "Breathing hard, does not look pleased" after an unhurt win.
Inventory: Pack has nice item icons, clear coin/ration lines; using an item works but closes the whole book each time. Only one dart, thrown forever.
Map: beautiful 3D hex island; the journey card (time, arrival, "known only by report", the way) is excellent. No route line drawn before setting out; the trip went east past Heahheald and doubled back west (dotted line in 83-travel2). Heahheald looked ~2 hexes away but took 6 hours.
Notes/journal: the Notes page ("what you mean to do / to hand / told you / what you know", with sources and times) is genuinely useful. Story tab replays my prose — lovely.
