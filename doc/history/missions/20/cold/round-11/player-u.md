# Westernesse, played cold: Saffron, catfolk bard (cold11 / u)

## 1. Summary
I made Saffron, a catfolk bard and former drifter who had won a dead man's map. On the road I stood up to two men robbing a pedlar and lost. In Dimnin I hired Fathom, a scholar and mage, with 6 of my 7 coin. I walked to Loewmasto to find Gosse's stranded players and could not afford their 8-coin reckoning. So I went back, burned and shot the quarry spiders for Rolen's 12 coin, and spent an evening at Wapshott's circus. I walked back at night, paid the players' reckoning, sang badly for a coin and slept. In the morning their wagon had gone. Day 2 of game time; one fight; about 180 requests. I never went near the map's own goal, Carmenarc.

## 2. The first five minutes
Creation was clear and handsome: an origin, a people, a calling, an earlier trade and a name, each on cards over a painting, with a "Details" panel that says what each choice does in play. The opening line ("A catfolk bard. A dead man's map. Once a drifter. A light crossbow in hand.") told me who I was. The map prologue and the pedlar scene (four choices, the first marked "an even chance · one try") were immediately interesting. When I failed, the pedlar said "Brave, and a fool. I'll say so at the inn, both." Then "Your first goal: search at Carmenarc." The first place, the causeway, was less clear: a small toolbar of icons with no hover hints, and the "Go" list headed "IN THE MARKET-PLACE" although I was on the causeway. The market (24) looked very good, but talking there was flat (see 6).

## 3. What I pursued, and why
I followed my own curiosity, not the gold aim. Fathom's talk gave me five threads; I chose Gosse's stranded players because I'm a bard and they were the warmest thing in the world. The spider job I took only to pay for that, so money pushed me there. The circus I chose freely, and it was the best part. I felt I had choices throughout. The map's Carmenarc goal was always there, but nothing pulled me to it.

## 4. Moments of confusion
- 21: I clicked the pedlar I had just stood up for: "Nothing to say to you now." I expected thanks or a recruit.
- 26–38: Ivellios says "Ask me which. Go on." and Erevan says "Ask me about anything but the weather", but each has only one or two fixed topics. I couldn't ask anyone about my map or Carmenarc.
- 66: In Rolen's talk the number keys were renumbered after a reply (6, then 1, 2). I pressed 2 meaning "country round" and instead fired "Tell Rolen the factor buys bronze that was not hers to sell", betraying Heian by accident. The aftermath says "By morning the factor's chair is bare… nobody in the Anchor can say". It was still morning, and the inn is the Brave Griffin.
- 97, 148: The portrait row reorders after each conversation. Twice I clicked a face and got a different person, once an empty Gosse conversation.
- 61: Clicking Wade did nothing at all, not even "nothing to say".
- 88: The first tap on the Eye button only highlighted it; a second tap opened Look.
- 82–83: Siren's button promised "ask about the forty crowns in merrowdale". It was hidden inside "Ask about the lands beyond", which opens a list of 27 far-off topics.
- 104: Following the players' aim drew no gold step anywhere; the game didn't know how to point me at money.
- 105–107: A lockup door "barred on the outside" could only be listened at, pried or pushed, each with the same canned failure. I couldn't just lift the bar.
- 121: At the quarry gate I carried Rolen's key, but was offered only pry and push at the padlock.
- 140–142: "Talk to Dannel Hobb — ask what he saw in the old quarry" appeared only after I had finished the quarry, then vanished. His portrait went back to "Nothing to say."
- 101: My own deed with the pedlar is filed under "Told you, not yet seen for yourself — the pedlar told you". Carmenarc is filed under "To hand in Loewmasto".

- A crowbar choice that never works (43, 106).

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
**Conversation.** It is uneven. Townsfolk have one or two fixed topics and repeat a template ("'Motlôx? I was only passing through,' says… 'Well.'" from both Ivellios and Mialee). The people with real stories are excellent: Fathom, Heian, Gosse, Hepzibah, Jem Pudding, Fipps, Quirk, Nance, Pip and the Quinceys. You can never steer: no asking about your own map, no offering to find Lem, no making peace between the brothers.

**Fights.** The one fight was the best-built screen. Company sits on the left, the foe on the right with its intent ("→ Fathom · venomous bite"), and the menu reads Abilities / Spells / Items. The roll log is readable, though some lines repeat.

**Inventory.** A nice icon grid. "Tend the company" (Cure Light Wounds) lives there, which is handy. Purchases happen silently.

**Map.** Beautiful 3D hexes, but the fog is crowded with about 40 floating labels, most of them "way into…" far countries. The map pans every time you click, so labels jump, and the card covers your own token. The journey card is clear (time, arrival, food, the way, a red "after dark" warning).

**Notes.** These are good: a clear "What you mean to do" block with a gold "Following" tag, and every rumour dated and attributed.

## 8. Danger
The spiders were signposted well. A red DANGER card, "will give you 7 minutes", and "a hard fight: Fathom reckons you would likely beat it, but Saffron could fall." I could prepare: burn the brood-chamber with Fathom's fire first, use Light rather than my candle, retreat. In the fight I taunted the spider off fragile Fathom. Two natural 1s on saves left me at 3 of 6 and poisoned, then I shot it dead. It felt tense and fair. With 6 HP and a 4-HP companion I felt fragile all day, which suited a bard. The night road was flagged ("You would come there after dark", a "!") but nothing happened.

## 9. Did I want to keep playing?
Yes, but because of the people, not the systems. The circus folk and the players made me want to see who else lives in this country. The mechanics around them often blocked or misled me: one-topic villagers, wrong-person clicks, keys renumbering, verbs that always fail, money as the only way to help anyone. I'd come back to meet more characters. I wouldn't come back to explore the map or the dungeons.

## 10. Boredom and interest
Bored:
- Clicking through the market villagers (33–38).
- The Look lists of far-off notices (90).
- Pry, push and listen at locks (43–44, 105–107).
- Both 3-hour journeys, where nothing happened.

Most interesting: the 7 o'clock show at the circus (163). I was practising tumbling with the Quinceys when the band struck up and the whole company came in. It arrived because I happened to be there at seven, as the bill said. Close second: the next morning's "wheel-ruts in the dust … a ring of flattened ground where a bear lay" (180).

## 11. Decisions
- Spending 6 of my 7 coin on Fathom (56). Real cost: it left me unable to pay the players or hire Wyn. It paid off, because Fathom's fire and Light carried the quarry.
- Paying the players' reckoning (8 coin) rather than hiring Wyn (7 coin) (176). A real trade-off, and it visibly changed the world next morning.
- In the fight: burning the brood first, then Taunt to pull the spider off Fathom.
- Setting out at night despite the warning.
- An accidental "decision", informing on Heian (66), also changed the world: the factor vanished.

## 12. Ratings
- **Clarity 3/5.** Creation, fights, notes and the journey card are clear. The toolbar, the Go headings, the renumbering keys and the reordering portraits are not.
- **Beauty 4/5.** The paintings and type are lovely. It loses a point for the wrong paintings (big top, alehouse, sunny nights).
- **Interest 4/5.** The circus and the players are rich, and Fathom opens many threads.
- **Challenge 3/5.** One fair, tense fight. Most obstacles are money or a flat failed verb.
- **Wish to continue 3/5.** I want to meet more of these people. I'm less keen on the clicking in between.

## 13. Motivation
- **About 10 minutes:** find company, and find out what Motlôx and my map are (my own wish). Then the notice and Fathom offered a pile of aims.
- **About 30 minutes:** get 8 coin to free the players (my own). I took the spider job (the game's) only as a means.
- **End:** see whether the players got away (yes), then perhaps Muscivigilia's green light or the hobs' bell. Still my own, never the map's Carmenarc.

## 15. What was offered
The notice (three jobs) and Fathom's topics were very worth doing. The circus scenes were the best of all, though the offered "Learn a part" and "Practise tumbling" had no visible effect beyond prose. Dead ends:
- The corn-store padlock: pry, push and listen all fail with no roll shown.
- The lockup at Loewmasto.
- Lem Coyle, whom everyone mentions and no one can point to.
- The dog: "Make much of" twice gives the same line.

Things I found that weren't put in front of me:
- The camp at Loewmasto, via the Go list.
- Heian's fence business.
- Siren's "Sing for your supper", hidden under Trade. It was what I'd been looking for all day as a bard.

## 16. Colours
- **Rose:** someone might join you. Consistent.
- **Blue:** something to ask or look at, an aim-giver. Mostly consistent.
- **Gold:** the step toward the followed aim. It surprised me on the retreat button and on the map dot.
- **White dot:** something new, I think, but it was unclear (Ivellios, Bucephalus).
- **Red:** danger or attack. Clear.

## 17. Who I made
Recognisable, yes. The portrait of a catfolk woman with a lute is lovely, and the opening summary line fixed her. The dead man's map drove the first scene and the first goal, but I never pursued it. "Drifter" gave +2 Dex and wayfinding, which I didn't feel. Catfolk Dex made my crossbow land.

Being a bard mattered barely at all, and late. "Learn a part with the band" made its check, to no visible effect. "Sing for your supper" was buried under Trade and rolled persuasion, not my +6 Performance. Taunt and Cure Light Wounds were useful. Charm Person never came up. Nobody's talk changed because I'm a bard or catfolk. Rolling the qualities first gave me Con 2 and Str 4; I re-rolled.

## 18. Getting about
Routes drawn on the map (dotted lines) looked sensible, and journeys went as drawn. Both 3-hour trips were eventless, and the night trip paused once for "Night has come on". Arriving at a town gave a clear "Enter Loewmasto/Dimnin" card.

Inside towns the Go list does the work. It is long (16+ places in Dimnin), its headings are odd, and the best line ("Taproom… someone who might join you") sat below the fold. Clicking a place on the map selects a sub-hex ("lanes behind", "home fields") rather than the town. Carmenarc showed as the hex next door yet 4 hours away.

## 19. Waiting
The world was ready a few seconds after "Set out". My first start request and both long journeys timed out on my side (empty reply), but the next look showed the game had already moved on. I never saw a frozen screen. Reloading went back to the title with "Continue — Wapshott's ground · Day 1, 6:20 p.m.", and continuing restored everything.

## 20. My aim
Following "Burn the spiders out", the game led well: market → quarry pit → adit → gallery → back to Rolen, each step marked "your aim". It even reminded me what was still to do after the fight ("Tell Rolen… twelve coin, salve and rope"). Following "Gosse's Company stuck" it showed nothing at all, since the only step was "have 8 coin". It didn't suggest how to get money. Rolen's advice "Talk to the boy first" couldn't be followed: the boy had nothing to say until afterwards.

## 21. Top five problems
1. **Conversation can't be steered and promises go unkept.** Villagers have one stock topic. Characters invite questions you can't ask ("Ask me which. Go on."). There's no way to ask about your own map, a missing man (Lem) or a feud. The Dannel Hobb option vanishes.
2. **Misclicks with real consequences.** Number keys renumber mid-conversation (that's how I betrayed Heian), and portraits reorder after each talk (I opened the wrong person twice).
3. **Your calling barely matters.** A bard gets no performing, persuading or reconciling choices where they'd fit (the players, the Quinceys, the bear who "dances if you hum"). The one singing choice is hidden under Trade and rolls the wrong skill.
4. **Text and paintings that don't match what you saw.**
   - Wrong paintings: the big top as a kitchen, the same taproom for two inns, sunny paintings at night.
   - Text about things you never saw: the undercroft, the spiders' mother, the "Anchor" inn.
   - Repeated sentences.
5. **Dead-end verbs and silent controls.**
   - Pry, push and listen at locks always fail, with no roll shown.
   - The key you carry isn't offered at its own gate.
   - Wade's portrait does nothing.
   - The Eye button needed two taps.
   - Toolbar icons have no hints.
   - The night "!" leads to nothing.
