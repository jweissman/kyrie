*Build: main at da99bf2c, served locally from its own worktree (/var/tmp/kyrie/wt-cold8), started as the public does
(`?opening`, no world named). Asked to play cautiously, to judge whether the game's guidance can be trusted, and to

## 1. Summary
I played Siskin, a birdfolk bard who used to be an apothecary (origin "Feverwort"), for about 147 actions, one in-game
day and a half. I took the feverwort to the sick child at Calennand and sat with her through the fever (seen through,
+300 XP). Then I took the rats job: I cleared the corn-store cellar, the rat-runs, the nest and a centipede in the
drain. That led into an ancient undercroft, where I read a lettered flag, opened it, killed the ghoul "keeper of the
vault", and went on down into "the old dead under Calennand" (Delver mine galleries). There I solved a riddle casket
for a star-iron ingot, beat a bat swarm, and turned back from cave spiders. Along the way I hired Jessica the pedlar,
sold loot, crafted healing potions, got healed at the shrine, and reloaded once (progress was kept). I never found a
bed, so I ended weary at 2:30 a.m., four storeys down.

## 2. The first five minutes
It was clear and handsome. Creation asked "Why have you come to Birdsholt?" and gave eight origins, each with an icon
and a one-line situation. Feverwort gave me a person (an apothecary), an errand (a sick child, "don't dawdle") and a
place. Ancestry, calling, trade and name followed quickly, and each one had a DETAILS panel with real numbers. In the
reed-yard I knew who I was and why I was there. I did not know how to deliver the herbs. The scene's three choices
were all about a corn-store padlock and the lord's lockup, and nothing mentioned the mother. I had to open Notes and
press "Follow this" before the mother's portrait was marked and I clicked it (22-caelynn). After that it was
immediately interesting: the mother scene, the 8-hour vigil, and the prose about the child's hand going cool.

## 3. What I pursued and why
- **Feverwort:** my character's reason for being there. I followed it from Notes because the scene gave no way to
  complete it (19-book, 20-follow).
- **The rats:** found myself on the well-post notice. I chose it as the safest of three jobs for a 6-HP bard. I did
  not follow it at first, just walked to Thamior.
- **The sealed vault and the old dead:** found myself by reading the ring of letters. I followed the vault aim on
  purpose once I had a lantern and Jessica, because it was my own discovery. Later I followed "heart of the vein"
  because I was already inside the mine.
- **Not pursued:** Rosaturris (green light, dead walking), the crypt (Erevan) and the spider quarry (Nala).

I always felt I had a choice. The notice board and the aims list gave me several leads, and I was never forced down
one.

## 6. Systems
**Conversation:** This is the best part. A big portrait on the left, short and characterful lines, 3–4 topics, and
topics that open new aims and lore. Thamior's grandfather and the Delvers, and Jessica on Rosaturris, both pulled me
in. Hiring was clear: Jessica's card showed her full stats, and "It costs 7 coin, and you have 6" was honest.

**Fights:** The layout is clean: company on the left, foes on the right with intent arrows, a hit chance on each
target ("2 in 3"), and a "What it cost" line at the end. "Let Siskin fight this turn" (T) is a good fast-forward.
Spells were filtered sensibly (Charm Person was hidden against rats). The pre-fight estimates were badly calibrated,
though. Every fight was a "hard fight: you could fall", and most died to one or two bolts. The flavour repeats:
"stands over what is left, breathing hard, and does not look pleased" after one crossbow bolt into a centipede, and
"looks away solemnly" over bats.

**Inventory:** The pack has nice icon tiles and is clear. Crafting potions from the Wait panel was a pleasant
surprise: 2 herbs at 1 coin each make a potion that sells for 8, with a real chance to spoil. The poultice healed only
1 HP.

**Map:** A beautiful 3D hex diorama (114-map). I used it once. It opened pre-set to a journey to the sealed vault,
which was an odd default. Inside the town and underground I moved entirely by scene choices and the Go list.

**Notes:** Very good structure: "What you mean to do", "To hand in", "Further off", "Told you, not yet seen", and "What
you know", with who told you and when. Follow buttons live here. Things vanished from it without being marked as done.

## 8. Danger
The game signals danger well: a red DANGER box, a red pip on the foe's portrait, and red-bordered attack choices with
a one-line estimate. Retreating was always offered and cost nothing (twice). The real threats were the first-round
crit by a rat (4 of my 6 HP before I could act) and the ghoul, which shredded Jessica's armour and took me to 2/6.
Running out of light mattered too: the candle burned out mid-dungeon (63-read), and I could prepare by fetching a
lantern. The estimates cried wolf so often that "could be lost" at the spiders only felt real because it was worded
differently.

## 9. Did I want to keep playing?
Yes, mostly. The dungeon under Calennand is good: layered, connected, with riddles, traps, lore that pays off
(star-iron, which Thamior buys) and foreshadowing. What pushed me away was friction:
- I had no bed to rest in.
- I had to walk out step by step through the Go list.
- Aims vanished.
- Every fight was over-warned, so the danger stopped feeling real.

## 10. Boredom and interest
- **Bored:** walking back up from the vault (drain, runs, cellar, corn-store, one Go list per step), hunting for an
  inn, and auto-fighting rats.
- **Most interesting:** reading the ring of letters, pressing the three deep-cut letters, and the flag sliding aside
  onto a stair, followed later by the riddle casket. Both were puzzles my character's skills (lore) helped with, and
  they changed the world.

## 11. Decisions
- **Buying a yarrow poultice (2 coin) before hiring:** it left me with 6 coin when Jessica cost 7, so I went into the
  rats alone. That is a real consequence, and it later turned out the poultice heals only 1 HP.
- **Sitting with the child for 8 hours:** this cost most of the day; I did it because it was right for an apothecary.
  It finished the aim and made two people "not forget".
- **Fighting the ghoul instead of walking past it**, which is what gold suggested. It cost me 4 HP and won loot.
- **Fight tactics:** at 2/6 against three rats I cast Cure Light Wounds rather than take the 1-in-2 flee. I learned
  that rats need a 20 to hit me, then shot one target at a time.
- **Retreating from the charnel dead and the cave spiders.**

Choices did carry forward: the lantern from the rats job let me do the vault, and the lore roll opened the vault.

## 12. Ratings
- **Clarity 3/5:** characters, fights and notes are clear, but scene choices hide the obvious (the mother, the
  trapdoor, the way out), and there was no findable bed.
- **Beauty 5/5:** gorgeous paintings, portraits and type. The fight and talk screens are handsome.
- **Interest 4/5:** the dungeon and its lore really pulled me on.
- **Challenge 2/5:** warnings were constant and inflated, and fights were short. The real danger came from crits and
  from light running out.
- **Wish to continue 4/5:** I want to see the heart of the vein and Rosaturris, if I could only sleep.

planned 36-minute "journey" to the vault, which is in my own town, so I did not take it. Going into places was fine

## 21. Top five problems
1. **There is no findable place to sleep.** The inn is named but cannot be entered, "a bed for the night" leads
   nowhere, and the shrine only heals. I ended weary with no spells, which blocks the core rest loop.
2. **Scene choices hide obvious actions and exits.** You cannot give the herbs until the aim is followed, the trapdoor
   vanishes, and following an aim prunes every other exit, including the way up. Navigation falls back to the Go list
   one step at a time.
3. **Gold sometimes points wrong:** at the wrong gift (healing herbs instead of feverwort), past the very foe the aim
   is about, and into a signposted ambush. Aims also vanish from Notes without "Seen through", and the Go panel's aim
   goes stale.
4. **Fight estimates are badly calibrated.** Every fight is "hard, you could fall" and most end in one or two shots, so
   warnings lose meaning. Fight flavour lines repeat absurdly.
5. **Text and scene incoherence:** hillside prose four storeys down, a duplicated drill-yard paragraph, a woodland
   painting for a yard, a sunset painting at night, acting in total darkness, a tooltip saying "nothing more to say" to
   a stranger, and Calennand having "no herbs left" while an herb-wife sells herbs there.
