# Cold play, round 9, player P: Gunnvor, a dwarf warrior (once a drifter)

An agent's cold play, not a human's. Build 505b002a (12 November, night), a random world (Merscfaenn), the "Dead Man's
Map" origin. Brief: a fighter. About 155 actions and 1½ game days. Running notes in `player-p-notes.md`; screenshots
were kept outside the repository (/var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold9/p/shots). Numbers in brackets are those screenshots. The
report is the agent's own, as returned.

## 1. Summary
I made Gunnvor, a dwarf warrior (once a drifter), who came for "A Dead Man's Map". I helped a wagoner on the causeway,
followed my reason to the ruined keep Ísencepan, and went room by room after the gold: a portcullis, a glyph set off
with a pole, an undercroft, a ghoul and a wolf pack, all killed. The aim was only "seen through" when I searched
*outside* the keep at its door, which nothing pointed me to. In Mistshaw I took a new aim from a widow (drive off the
outlaw knight Sir Hamo), sold loot, camped on the road and buried a dead drover. I reached the barrow Umbrarechies and
the outlaws' ring-fort, called Sir Hamo out and lost badly. I fled twice and finished penned inside the fort at 7/14
HP. That was about 155 actions and 1½ game days, and I was still level 1 (908/1000 xp).

## 2. The first five minutes
The start was very clear and inviting. "Why have you come to Merscfaenn?" offers seven illustrated reasons, each with a
one-line hook and a Details panel. Ancestry, calling, way, background and name follow quickly, with a one-sentence
summary at the end ("A dwarf warrior, afraid of nothing. A dead man's map. Once a drifter. A battleaxe in hand."). The
intro cards over the windmill painting were atmospheric. The wagoner in the ditch was an immediate small moral choice.
After that I landed on a busy quay with ten portraits and one odd option (wrench a padlock). I knew who I was and why
I'd come, but not how to start on it. "Follow this" was hidden on the Notes page, and I had to go and look for it.

## 3. What I decided to pursue, and why
- **The dead man's map.** It was my chosen reason and the game's only real push. I followed it as soon as I found
  "Follow this" in Notes.
- **The widow's bounty on Sir Hamo.** I found this myself by talking to the woman in black, an interesting figure on
  the green. It felt like a genuine choice: as a fighter, a bounty on an outlaw knight appealed.
- **Unasked offers.** Two more "A NEW AIM… Follow this" appeared on their own: the dead drover's wife at the inn, and
  the barrow's grave goods. That was more than I could follow.

## 4. Moments of confusion
- **20 / 23-back:** I expected the quay to offer a way to start my aim. After I clicked Follow this, the padlock option
  silently vanished, and the only gold was a pip on the map icon.
- **29-route:** In the Go panel both "Bailey" (barred ⊘) and "Gate-passage" had gold borders. The scene's own "Into the
  gate-passage" option was not gold.
- **43-back / 47-go:** After the gold led me to the lord's chamber and I read the roll, the gold vanished. Notes still
  said FOLLOWING / "Search there", with no next step. I searched the chamber and found nothing.
- **54 / 62 / 76:** The bailey's exits kept changing. Sometimes there was no way to the gate-passage, then only "Attack
  the wolves" or "Undercroft", so I had to fight to leave. The wolves said "a few minutes, no more" for hours and
  never attacked.
- **66 / 68:** A locked strongbox in the undercroft needed lockwork or a crowbar. It looked like the "cross" target
  but was a dead end.
- **101-searchout:** The real answer was to search outside Ísencepan "where the map put its cross". I only found it by
  taking the text literally. On my first arrival (28-enter) the gold sent me inside instead.
- **91 / 93 / 96:** On the map, clicking Mistshaw and clicking Lowmouth both produced "Journey to The ring of
  living-wagons". "Enter Lowmouth" put me in a circus field (Wapshott's ground) outside the town. Later, Wapshott's
  ground was also listed under Mistshaw.
- **109:** The text said "Mistshaw is close by: you can hear it", it was the next hex, and the trip took 2½ hours.
  Lowmouth to Ísencepan took 1 hour.
- **122:** Clicking Mrs Aske gave "Nothing more to say to you now." I had never spoken to her.
- **123:** The Go panel said "Mistshaw green — someone who might join you". I never found out who.
- **160:** Sir Hamo's man invited me in, then "Hoy! Hands where I can see them! Bows come up all round you". The only
  option offered was to smash the padlock on Hamo's own chest, in front of him.
- **174 / 181:** After fleeing I was in the long hall with only "further in" listed, and the yard was full of his men.
  I was penned in.

## 5. Misleading or useless controls / text
- **"Something is going on here" (142):** the scene showed only a milestone and asters. The real event was hidden
  behind a small "THE FRESH RUTS…" link.
- **"Since you were last here: the portcullis is wedged up" (54):** I did that myself.
- **"The wolves will not wait for it" on the exits (54):** they did wait.
- **Damage numbers disagree:** "hits the ghoul… for 8" alongside "takes 4 slashing damage (roll 8)" (59); the same in
  the wolf fight (81).
- **Status contradicts the log:** "Gunnvor shrugs off the armor shred" while my status showed Armor Shred (166).
  "Vigorous for 5 turns" vanished after one round (168).
- **Odd rolls:** "rolled 18, needed 11" counted as an evade (168). A bite that did 0 damage still left me Bleeding (78).
- **Wrong pronoun:** the prose says "their" for Gunnvor, whom I made a woman (85b).
- **Small text slips:** "FOOD one rations of 2" (140); "1 minutes" (93); the Wait panel offered "Make camp and sleep
  until 3:00 p.m." at night, though I woke in the morning (149).
- **Click target:** clicking the words "BROKEN SCEPTER" hit the "You know now" line instead of the link (51).

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes/journal
**Conversation:** short and characterful: the wagoner, Jos the cobbler ("a goose that's been told it's a swan"), the
widow. There were few topics. Sir Hamo had no talk options at all, only Attack / Call out / Leave, which undercut the
widow's "dead or run off". Trade with the herb-wife was clear and useful.

**Fights:** the layout is good. Abilities show odds ("3 in 5"), and you pick the target with per-enemy odds. Sweeping
Strike and Cleave felt tactical against the ghoul and the wolves. Against Hamo, "Let Gunnvor fight this turn" ran
several rounds at once: I went from 14 to 2 HP before I could react. The log is noisy, with a prose line and a dice
line for every action, and the numbers sometimes contradict each other.

**Inventory:** the Pack page was plain and clear. Using an item threw me back to the scene.

**Map:** a beautiful little 3D hex diorama in fog, but the labels are crowded with "way into X". Clicking a town picked
an odd sub-destination. Journeys were instant, apart from interruptions, which appear as a gold "!".

**Notes:** genuinely useful: aims, "what you know" with timestamps, hirelings at the inn. But it is where "Follow this"
hid at the start, and it never showed a next step for an aim.

## 7. Writing
Often excellent:
- The castle roll: "ships in sight. Then a day with no entry. Then no more days."
- The circus sign: "PATRONIZED BY THE NOBILITY… WHICH NOBILITY."
- The buried drover's knife: "nobody could bring themselves to bury it, or to take it".
- Hamo "mending a bridle he has no horse for".

Repetitive or mechanical:
- The bailey's "Since you were last here" block repeated on every visit.
- The roll's text was printed again in jumbled order in the chamber (43).
- Odd flavour after kills ("does not look pleased", "looks away solemnly").

Paintings often don't match the text:
- A sunny, intact castle with a market awning for a ruined, nettled bailey.
- A candle-lit hall with a sunny street for the gate-passage.
- A white fairy-tale palace for the wall-walk.
- Sunny rocks for the "low smoky" end of the hall.
- The Ísencepan star banner at the outlaws' fort (174).

The burned hall and the barrow paintings were perfect.

## 8. Danger
The DANGER box and the "should be won / could be lost / beyond you" labels made threats easy to read, and "Choose it
again to do it" confirmed risky choices. The wolves' countdown was hollow: they never acted. Sir Hamo was honestly
flagged "beyond you"; I fought him anyway and nearly died. Flee at 1 in 2 worked, but afterwards the fort's exits
closed: his men attacked in the yard, and the only way listed from the hall was further in. Getting away from a fight
was possible; getting out of the place was not.

## 9. Did I want to keep playing?
Partly. The world's texture and writing make me curious about the barrow, the drover's wife and Aergodríce. But the
hour ended with me trapped in the fort at 7 HP with no exit offered. It also involved a lot of guessing where the game
wanted me to click, while the gold guide kept switching off. If I could walk out of the fort I'd go on, warily.

## 10. Boredom and interest
- **Bored:** going back and forth through the bailey; clicking strangers in Mistshaw's portrait row to find the one who
  mattered; the long dice log in the Hamo fight.
- **Most interesting:** the glyph puzzle (look closely, then hold the pole across it looking away), the castle roll,
  and finding and burying the dead drover. All were small, readable and rewarded attention.

## 11. Decisions
- **Calling out Sir Hamo though marked "beyond you":** it cost 12 HP and left me trapped.
- **Fleeing at 2 HP (1 in 2)** rather than using Second Wind.
- **Burying the drover:** it cost two hours and a ration, with no visible consequence yet.
- **Fighting the wolves while inspired** after touching the crystal.

In fights I used Sweeping Strike to knock the ghoul prone and then the axe, and Cleave on the most likely wolf so the
kill carried on to another. Helping the wagoner earned +2 coin and a promised meal at the inn, which I never collected.
Nothing else I chose visibly changed later.

## 12. Ratings
- **Clarity 2:** creation and the fight screen are clear, but where to go next and which exits exist kept shifting.
- **Beauty 4:** gorgeous paintings, type and map, though some paintings contradict the text.
- **Interest 4:** the world, lore and characters made me want more.
- **Challenge 3:** the labels were fair. The wolves were a hollow threat and Hamo a real one; being trapped after
  fleeing was unfair.
- **Wish to continue 3:** curious, but tired of guessing.

## 13. Motivation
- **About 10 minutes:** get to Ísencepan and find the cross. That was the game's aim and mine.
- **About 30 minutes:** work out why "Search there" wouldn't finish. That was my own aim, forced on me when the game
  lost track.
- **End:** escape the fort, heal, and perhaps find a companion before facing Hamo again. My own aim, in service of the

The wagoner, the glyph, the ghoul, the crystal, the ruts and the drover were worth doing. The quay's padlock (20)
vanished before I could try it. The strongbox led nowhere. The bailey wolves' threat was empty. The great hall's stair,
mentioned in the prose, was never offered. Things I found myself: searching outside the keep (the actual aim),
searching the great hall (a broken scepter, sold for 15), and the widow's bounty.

## 16. Colours
- **Gold:** "your aim's next step", shown on options, toolbar pips, map pips and fight targets. Mostly consistent, but
  missing at crucial moments, and twice doubled: the Go list at 29, the two glyph options at 62, and the "!" plus flag
  on the map at 141.
- **Red:** danger or a fight.
- **Blue:** something worth doing here, or a person to talk to. Consistent.
- **White or silver:** ordinary ways back.
- **Surprises:** a red pip on the Go icon at Mrs Aske's (123) turned out to mean "someone who might join you", not
  danger. The map's ENTER button was sometimes cream and sometimes brighter, and I couldn't tell whether that meant
  gold.

## 17. Who I made
She was recognisable: a dwarf warrior woman with a battleaxe and a fine portrait. Choosing dwarf excluded five of the
seven callings, which was clearly stated. Bravery and drifter/wayfinding never visibly mattered. Warrior abilities
mattered a lot in fights, and the ten-foot pole mattered for the glyph. As a warrior with no lockwork I couldn't open
the strongbox, which is a fair consequence.

## 18. Getting about
Routes were listed as legs with minutes. A dotted line was drawn once (93); the first route (24) was hard to see.
Journeys happened instantly, and interruptions appeared as a gold "!" that stopped me sensibly. When a town was beside
me, "Enter X" was clear, but Lowmouth's Enter put me in a circus field, and clicking a town targeted a sub-place.
Travel times were inconsistent: 2½ hours to the adjacent Mistshaw.

## 19. Waiting
World creation after SET OUT took about 3 to 5 seconds while the intro text showed. Two requests that triggered travel
came back empty, but the game carried on. The page never looked frozen.

## 20. Your aim
- **Ísencepan:** the game led me well to the keep, then took me inside room by room while the target was under the
  door-stone outside. It lost track after the chamber (43–47) and after the ghoul (61). Later it sent me from Lowmouth
  back to Ísencepan (97), out and back again, with no hint when I arrived.
- **The widow's aim:** no gold or route was ever shown for it (113–154). I found the barrow and the fort myself.

## 21. Top five problems
1. **The gold guide doesn't lead to the real objective.** It walked me through the keep's interior while the "cross at
   its door" needed a search outside. It then vanished with the aim unfinished, and later sent me back with no cue.
2. **Exits appear and disappear.** In the bailey and the outlaw fort the only ways listed were "fight" or "further
   in". That penned me in, even after a successful flee.
3. **Paintings often contradict the scene text.** Sunny intact castles stand in for ruins and smoky halls, and the
   star banner appears at the outlaws' fort.
4. **Fight feedback is unreliable.** Damage numbers disagree, status effects contradict the log, and "Let fight this
   turn" skips many rounds at once.
5. **Map and town navigation is muddled.** Clicking a town picks a circus wagon-ring, "Enter Lowmouth" lands in a
   field, and travel times are inconsistent. There are also social dead ends: "nothing more to say" from people never
   met, a joiner I couldn't find, and a Sir Hamo with no talk at all.
