# Cold play, round 3, player C

Build: `09664cf0` (main), served locally from a pinned copy, with the real opening. Screenshots
`/var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold3/c/shots` (00–152), notes alongside. About 152 actions. Character: Valanthe, elf enchanter
(5 HP), *Coming home*; hired Beren the scholar for 7 of 8 coin. Intention given: curious, cautious, fragile; talk and
prepare; postpone what the game wants at least once.

## 1. Summary
Found brother Carric and mother in Brockminster, petted Father's lame dog, hired Beren. **Put off the search for Father
on purpose**: the scrivener's shop, a hidden spell teacher behind its curtain, Mage Armor prepared. In the old quarry
burned the spider brood through a crack (the mother dead without a fight), then two easy fights. A ring of letters found
by searching the brood-chamber opened a sealed vault under the undercroft: a ghoul beaten, loot. After a reload
(progress kept) went to Father's road: Carric knew the left-wheel ruts, Father found dead in the ditch, buried. Stopped
Day 1, afternoon.

## 2. The first five minutes
Creation clear and handsome; detail panels explain rules well; but a lot of it (origin, ancestry, calling, school,
three spells, trade — 14 cards and ~30 sub-trades incl. "Badger" — sex, name), and 4 HP to learn how fragile you are.
The road intro and the wagoner lovely. Understood at once: home, Father missing, can talk, look, go. Immediately
interesting: the brother with Father's spear; the drover's dog "caked in black ditch-mud, the off forefoot cut as if on
iron."

## 3. What I pursued and why
The father's road was clearly the game's ("Following", on the map). Chose to postpone: the scrivener's curtain, then the
quarry, because the guest-master said "talk to the boy first" — and preparing paid: the boy's account of the egg-sacs
and "burn the brood" killed the mother without a fight; searching the chamber found the letter-ring that opened the
vault. Felt a choice; but the game pushed jobs hard: ~9 aims in ~15 minutes (three from the inn's "country round", two
from the notice, two from Cadmus).

The wagoner's subtitle still "up to his knees in the ditch" after the cart was freed; he gave 2 coin, then the note says
I "would take nothing for it but his thanks". Notes list my own deeds (the cart, the spiders I burned) under "told you,
not yet seen", credited to others. "Listen at it" → "put an ear to the padlock". "Carric binds the hurt" with nobody
hurt; "Your brother is waiting by the rail with the spear" two storeys underground. "Easily won" on every foe (right, but
reads as stock). "The spiders in the old workings is…" for one spider. Look lists padded with 20+ plants; Father's body
and the burial in the same drawer as meadowsweet. A natural 20 "critical" for 3 damage.

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
**Conversation:** beautiful lines (the mother's stool going over, Carric's eels, "Father would have liked Beren… given
Beren the worst ox"), but thin: **nobody but family can be asked about Father or the cart** — not the wagoner who
"knows the road", not the pilgrim on that road. The inn's "lands beyond" a wall of 27 rumours. Three greetings reused
word for word across people. **Fights:** clear layout; each foe's intended target ("→ Carric · venomous bite") excellent;
but every foe went for Carric, I never took a hit; aiming then clicking the target felt clicky; let it auto-play.
**Inventory:** tidy; loot to whoever the game picks; Father's knife with stock text. **Map:** gorgeous; easy to misuse.
**Notes:** useful (who told what), cluttered, sometimes wrong.

## 7. Writing
Memorable: "It is your father. You know the coat first, and the hands, before you will let yourself know the face."; the
burial; a pilgrim's twist of grass on the milestone; Carric: "Father would never go under a roof he could not see the end
of… I think now it was because he had sense."; the boy: "Bags. Hanging up, white, like puddings." Repetitive: copied
greetings; "will give you N minutes, no more" on every foe; "Your brother is waiting by the rail" everywhere. Not
knowable: a "you know now" about "the undercroft's flag" before seeing the undercroft. Paintings against text: a gothic
castle for a village gate-yard; a lit adit called pitch dark; the burnt brood-chamber with sacs intact; the old working
face with no painting; Carric human in town, elf in fights.

## 8. Danger
Nothing threatened; every foe "easily won"; companions took every attack; ended unhurt, Mage Armor never cast. Could see
and prepare (antidote, torch, pole, Mage Armor, hireling), none needed. Only "something dragged the oxen off toward the
hills" felt menacing, and it is not an aim one can follow.

## 9. Want to keep playing?
Partly: to tell Mother, and to find what took the oxen. "The game made me care about this family, and that is its real
strength." Not for more errands or easy fights; talking to anyone outside the family gives nothing about the story.

## 10. Boredom and interest
Bored: the trade sub-list, the 27-rumour wall, plant lists, the beetle fight. Most interesting: the boy's warning →
burning the brood → the letter-ring found by searching → the ring opening the flag: "curiosity paid off three times in a
row." Most moving: Carric at the ruts: "That is the left wheel. That is his."

## 11. Decisions
Hiring Beren for 7 of 8 coin (real cost; paid off). Burning the brood instead of attacking. Fighting the ghoul at 5 HP
(no real risk). Tactics: Minor Distraction on the foe targeting Carric. Postponing Father: no consequence for the delay.
Choices that changed later events: the brood; the search that unlocked the vault; asking Carric about the road.

## 12. Ratings
Clarity 3 (dead portraits, hidden spell preparation, aim overload, the map's instant move). Beauty 4. Interest 4.
Challenge 2. Wish to continue 3 ("for the mother and the oxen, not for the errands").

## 13. Top five problems
1. You can't talk about the main story with anyone but family; greetings copied across people.
2. No danger, so caution and preparation barely matter; Mage Armor not castable before a fight.
3. Aim and list overload (9 aims in 15 minutes, a 27-rumour list, 20+ plants in Look).
4. Continuity errors (the wagoner, "nothing but thanks", own deeds as hearsay, the brother "by the rail" underground).
5. Controls that don't respond or surprise (portraits, Escape on the map, spell preparation below the fold, Take, the
   map's instant move from underground).
