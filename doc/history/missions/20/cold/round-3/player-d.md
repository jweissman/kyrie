Build: `09664cf0` (main), served locally from a pinned copy, with the real opening. (A first attempt was spoiled when
two agents' requests crossed onto one page; this is the clean replay, through a per-agent wrapper.) Screenshots

## 1. Summary
Went straight for the most valuable thing heard of: the first king's crown in Lapisrecs. Tore the corn-store padlock
off without asking for the key; killed the rats, the buck rat, a centipede, a ghoul (the vault's keeper) under a lifted
flag, and three goblins in a mine level, most of the way with no light. Back up, paid for the rats (10 coin, a lantern,
oil), sold a ring (7). Reloaded. Travelled five hours to a barrow — Dolorechies, not Lapisrecs. Solved its riddle door
(the wrong word first, on purpose), disarmed a humming trap, took an iron torc off the dead, killed three ghouls. Ended
Day 1 ~8 p.m. in the dark in the barrow's east recess with 25 coin; never hurt worth noting; never found Lapisrecs.

## 2. The first five minutes
Creation clear and handsome ("Not for dwarves" marked; stats rolled with reasons). The opening's "patient until market
day, ten days from now, and not after" gave a debt, a deadline and a creditor at once: "immediately motivating". Tuck's
"Pay what you owe: it costs 50 coin, and you have 11" said exactly where I stood; under Something else nine rumours,
including a barrow with a gold crown and a man who "came out with … his hair gone white": hooked within a minute. The
landing rich but crowded (eleven portraits, four paragraphs).

## 3. What I pursued, and why
The crown, the most valuable thing mentioned, found myself in Tuck's talk. The game kept steering to "ask Aiden at the
Stormy Horn" (the map defaulted to it; it stayed Following); ignored. **Lapisrecs was never placed on the map; the crown
aim has no "on the map".** Guessed "a long mound north of Thornhaven", found Dolorechies instead, with a good clue: a bone
flute answered from "the greatest of the barrows" in Asholt. Real choice, but no way to know where my target was.

## 8. Danger
Nothing threatened (lowest 14 of 18). Warnings plentiful to the point of overkill and often wrong in tone. The only real
pressure was light and food. Never needed to flee, bargain or retreat ("Ask the goblins to let you by · an even chance"
existed; fighting was always better). **Unscrupulous acts had no consequence**: the padlock wrenched off, Tuck paid anyway
and said nothing; grave-robbing, the "dead who keep the bounds" kept letting me by; killing the keepers, nothing.

## 9. Want to keep playing?
"Somewhat, yes, for the world" (the crown, the strongroom, the green-light tower); "as a fighter, nothing pushes back."
The unusable lantern was the moment I felt like quitting.

## 10. Boredom and interest
Bored: auto-fighting rats, centipedes and goblins; walking nine rooms back up by Go. Most interesting: Dolorechies (the
seal-stone, the riddle door with a cold breath for a wrong answer, the humming trap with several approaches, the flute
pointing to the real barrow): "the one place where thinking mattered".

## 11. Decisions
Declining Tuck's five days (a real offer). The wrong word on the riddle door (flagged, mild). Going into the vault and
mine with no light (no penalty: undercut the light economy). Cleave on the ghoul targeting me. Consequences: the rats
paid off; nothing dishonest or bloodthirsty changed anything.
