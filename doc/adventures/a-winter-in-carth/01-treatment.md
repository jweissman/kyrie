# A Winter in Carth — Stage 1: the original treatment

*Written before the engine was read for this purpose, as the commission asks: ambitious first, accommodated later.
Spoilers throughout. Names have been checked against every adventure in `data/adventures` (see §0).*

## 0. The commission, and what is already taken

The commission names two seeds: *A Winter in Carth* (horror in a snowbound farmhouse) and *The Road to Orm* (a
two-person philosophical walk, escorting a heretic to trial), inside a metropolitan region with many small,
interlocking adventures and an overarching mystery, satire, intrigue or romance, tonally distinct from what Kyrie
already has.

Two facts shape everything below.

1. **The seeds have been planted once already.** *The Banns at Hessel* took both. Its "Winter in Carth" is a shuttered
   house in a spa town where every clock says two; its "Road to Orm" is a morning's walk with a physician going before
   his College. Neither is a snowbound farmhouse, and neither is a heretic. Both proper names are now Hessel's: Carth
   is a family and a house there, and Orm a town with a gate. This work keeps **Carth** as the name of a farm on a fell
   (a place, which Hessel does not declare, and no surname), and gives up **Orm**: the heretic walks to a city of this
   work's own, **Gaunt**. The seed's title is kept for the strand, *the road to Gaunt*, and for the whole.
2. **The collection is crowded in exactly the neighbourhoods the seeds point at.** Pellam (*The Liberty of the Ice*) is
   a winter city with a frost fair and a con; Pentrice (*Gosse's Company*) is a city that licenses speech; Wrayford
   (*The Third Night*) is a civic tragedy over the dead. A winter city and a trial will be measured against all three.

The repertoire survey (`doc/adventures/repertoire-2026-10-26.md`) says what the collection still has not done after
twelve works. Of its list, the ones this commission can reach are: **satire as a structure** (still none: "a society
made visible by exaggerating its law"); **fear that lasts**, in one bounded place, where the danger is partly the people
you are shut in with; **philosophical dialogue as the central action**, with positions the protagonist takes held and
turned; **fault** (the protagonist as the cause); and **anger the work takes the side of**.

So: a satire with a horror inside it and an argument walking through it.

## 1. The premise in a paragraph

**Gaunt** is a walled cathedral city on a white plain, three days from the Fast. Its law is the Commerce of Merit:
*every fault has its price in penance, and any penance may be borne by another.* The rich of Gaunt sin, are assessed,
and hire the poor of the Row to fast, kneel, walk barefoot and keep cold vigils in their names; the Penitentiary keeps
the tally; the Minster has been building its north tower with the fees for two hundred and eleven years. **Ninian
Hern**, called Barefoot Hern, was for thirty years the most sought-after proxy in Gaunt: he has done the penance of
half the Guildhall. Last winter he refused one. Then he began to say on the Minster steps that he had repented for a
hundred men and been sorry for none of them, and neither had they, so who had been forgiven? He is summoned before the
Consistory on Ash Day. You meet him on the north road, where the beadle sent to fetch him has frozen his foot, and you
are asked to bring him in. On the way the snow closes the fell, and the only house on it is **Carth**, where a family
is keeping, for wages, the vigil Hern refused: no fire in the house for forty nights. It is the twenty-third.

## 2. Tone

The collection has one narrator: plain, dry, humane, exact, consoling. This work wants three registers, each the
consequence of where the company is.

- **The city is satire.** The narrator has opinions and keeps a straight face: "The Ladder of Merit is a board of
  black oak in the Minster porch, on which the most penitent men and women of Gaunt are listed in gold, in order of
  how much they have paid other people to suffer." Swift's deadpan, Fielding's asides, Gogol's grotesques, Bruegel's
  *Fight between Carnival and Lent* for the streets. The satire's object is not religion but **moral outsourcing**:
  a society that has found how to be sorry without being changed, and has made the poor's suffering a commodity the
  rich buy as virtue. It is funny, and it is angry, and the work takes the Row's side without making the Row
  simple.
- **The farmhouse is horror.** No jokes at Carth. Dark (a flame is a fire; the vigil forbids it), cold, small
  sentences, a dead man in the byre waiting for the thaw because the ground will not take a spade, a child coughing,
  and a man who comes in the night without knocking to kneel at the hearth and put his bare hand in the ash. Nothing
  supernatural is confirmed. The grandmother believes in the Cold Guest, who sits down at a hearth that has been let
  go out for a debt, and the work never says she is wrong.
- **The road is argument.** Hern is funny, vulgar, vain of his feet, generous, ill, and sometimes wrong. The talk is a
  game: what the protagonist says is held, and turned on them later.

## 3. The region

### 3.1 Shape

A walled city in concentric rings on a low rise in a flat white country, the **Levels**, with the **north road** coming
down off **Carth Fell**, and **the Bishop's Wood** on the south. The rings are the satire made spatial: the nearer the
Minster, the warmer, the cleaner, the more forgiven.

### 3.2 Districts

**The Close.** Inside its own wall, warm. The **Minster of Saint Oswy the Bearer**, whose north tower has stood
unfinished since the year the Commerce began, scaffolded like a man in splints. In the porch, **the Ladder of Merit**.
In the nave, Saint Oswy's shrine: a stone man with another man on his back. (The legend: Oswy carried a dying sinner up
the hill so he could confess before the Minster doors. The Commerce of Merit is founded on him. The legend does not say
whether the sinner was sorry.) **The Penitentiary**, where the **Register** of every fault and its penance is kept in
forty books, and where **Dame Hawise Strake, the Lady Penitentiary**, assesses. **The Chapter House**, where the
Consistory sits on Ash Day. **The Schools**, where the Doctors of the Faculty dispute and sell Determinations.
**The masons' lodge** at the tower's foot. **The Consistory gaol**, a warm cell, because the Consistory is merciful.

**The Middle Town.** Merchants, the Guildhall, inns. **The Stand**, in the old sheep-market, is where proxies are hired,
standing in pens with chalked boards: FASTS · 3d A DAY. VIGIL (INDOORS) 6d. VIGIL (OUT) 1s. BAREFOOT 1s A MILE.
STRIPES BY ARRANGEMENT. **The Wood Exchange**, where firewood is sold by the Bishop's licence, and **Alderman Hugh
Thackray**'s yard behind it. **The Fat Lamb**, an inn, at the height of Shrovetide. Houses of the burgesses who compete
for the Ladder. The **Shrove baker**, who has three days to sell what the Fast will forbid.

**The Row.** Between the old wall and the new, where the ditch is. Tenements of the proxy families, cold. **The
Barefoot Hall**, the proxies' fellowship, where their Prior, **Bess Ackroyd**, keeps their rates and their dead.
**Heb's cellar**, where tallies are forged. **The ditch**, where the children slide and where a proxy who died on the
ice last winter was found kneeling. Hern's old room, kept by nobody.

**The Gate of Ash.** The west gate, opened once a year: on the evening of Ash Day, for whoever the Consistory has put
out. To be put out is to be struck from the Register: no penance owed, and none to be had; no door in Gaunt opened to
you; walked into the Levels at dusk in what you stand in. It is the city's only capital sentence, and it never says
"death". It does not have to. It is February.

**Outside.** The north road and **Carth**; the Levels; **the Bishop's Wood** to the south, where the Row has the old
right to dead wood and Thackray's woodmen say there is no dead wood this year.

## 5. The law, as a game

The Commerce of Merit is the region's physics, and the protagonist is inside it from the first night.

- Every fault in Gaunt is assessed in **pains** by the Penitentiary: a brawl, a lie to an officer, trespass in the
  Close, feasting after midnight on Fastens' Eve, breaking a vigil. The company carries a **shrift**: the pains it
  owes.
- **Unshriven** (owing anything), you cannot pass the Close Gate, cannot be heard as a witness at the Consistory, and
  are served last or not at all in the Middle Town.
- A debt of pains can be paid: **by doing it** (a fast, a vigil out in the cold, a barefoot mile, stripes: hours, hunger,
  cold, wounds); **by hiring a proxy** at the Stand (coin; and you can watch them do it); **by earning**, as a proxy
  yourself, the coin to hire one (so that a woman fasts for you while you kneel for a brewster); **by a forged tally**
  from Heb (cheap, and a crime with a price of its own if the Close Gate's porter has the eye); **by remission** from the
  Lady Penitentiary (who remits for reasons, and for people); **by a Pardon of the Fabric** (a gift to the tower,
  expensive).
- The law has a shape the satire can be played through. **A sentence is a penance. Any penance may be borne by
  another.** The Consistory's own law therefore allows Hern's sentence to be borne by a proxy, which no one has ever
  tried, because no one has ever been put out who had friends.

## 6. The small adventures

Each is meant to have a person with a motive, a place or object that matters, something misunderstood, more than one
approach, a turn, a consequence, and a trace in what comes later.

1. **The Beadle's Foot** (the north road). Clem Sutcliffe offers two shillings now and eight at the North Gate to bring
   Hern in. Accept, refuse, or bargain. Refused, Hern walks in alone and may not arrive. Accepted, you are his custody,
   and the city will hold you to it.
2. **The Road to Gaunt** (the walk). Three of Hern's questions, a station each: at the Cross, *can one man be sorry for
   another?*; at the Proxy's Stone, where a penitent froze kneeling last winter for someone's adultery, *what do we owe
   the dead?*; at the Drove Fork, *are you the one who promised the beadle this morning?*, and he walks off down the
   drovers' road to see what you will do. Your answers are kept.
3. **A Winter in Carth** (the night). The snow closes the fell. The Scaifes take you in under the vigil's terms: put
   out your light at the door. A dark, freezing house; a child's cough; the grandmother in the byre; the Ash-reeve at
   three. Light a fire and ruin them, or let the child freeze, or find the vigil's letter: the beasts brought in, one
   bed for everyone, a fire in the byre where Raistrick never puts his hand, the hearth packed with snow before he
   comes. Whoever lights a fire takes the vigil's debt: seventeen nights.
4. **The Stand** (the Middle Town). Hire on as a proxy: a day's fast for Mistress Dinsdale, a barefoot mile round the
   walls for Master Sowerby, a night's vigil on the Minster steps for an alderman's son. Or hire one. The proxies talk
   while they work.
5. **The Ladder** (comic escalation). Dinsdale and Sowerby, each a rung below the other by turns, outbid each other in
   penance before the Ladder is gilded on Ash Day: a thousand Aves, a pilgrimage on the knees round the walls, forty
   stripes. The proxies are being worked to collapse. Broker, inflame (and profit), expose, or get one of them to do
   their own penance, once, in public, which would end the race and the Ladder's dignity together.
6. **The Widow's Thoughts** (comic romance). Joan Pickersgill pays Abel Lumb to fast for her impure thoughts, which are
   about Abel. Abel is starving. Neither can say it. Carry a word, set them to the same penance, feed Abel at the Fat
   Lamb, or tell her what her thoughts are costing him. Married by Ash Day, or not.
7. **The Unfinished Tower** (satire). Joby Thwaite's last course goes up by day and comes down by night. Watch the
   scaffold after dark and see Canon Pellow's men unbuilding it. Help Joby set the stone and guard it, tell the
   Chapter, or use it on Pellow, who is Carth's landlord and the trial's loudest voice.
8. **The Determination** (satire of the learned). The Consistory will ask the Faculty. Ploughwright has been paid to
   find Hern's proposition heresy. Buy Quist's contrary opinion, or set the Doctors to dispute it in the Schools on
   Fastens' Eve, where they forget which side they were paid for.
9. **The Barefoot** (labour). Bess Ackroyd will call the Row out on Ash Day, the year's best day for wages, if the
   Wood Exchange will not open the dead-wood right. A strike means the rich must do their own penance. Help her
   organise, break it, or broker the wood.
10. **Whose Forty Nights** (the mystery). Who bought the Cold Vigil, and for what? The Register's sealed page; Raistrick
    followed down the fell; the vigil's coin, stamped in the Wood Exchange; Thackray's own confession, if he is
    brought to it. A human culprit who chose harm, and is sorry, and has not changed.
11. **Hal's Ladder** (intrigue). Hal Metcalfe has a ladder and a plan for Fastens' Eve. Prudence Garth has been offered
    the informer's fee to tell the gaoler when. Either can be found out; either can be stopped; one can be turned.
12. **Fastens' Eve** (the carnival). The last night before the Fast: masks, the Fat King's effigy, licence until
    midnight. Masked, people say what they would not. The Lady Penitentiary walks out masked, every year, to the Row.
13. **Ash Day** (the trial). Hern before the Consistory: the charge, the Faculty's answer, witnesses heard (if shriven),
    the people in the nave, and Hern's own words. Dame Strake pronounces.

Purely local, and kept so:

- **The Shrove Baker.** Mother Tunstall has two days of pancakes, fat-cakes and a pig's worth of crackling to sell
  before the Fast forbids them; her oven is cold, because wood is dear. A bundle of wood, a strong arm at the trough,
  or the trade of the Stand's proxies, who can eat. Ash Day morning, whoever helped finds a cake left on the step.
- **The Bell.** The Minster's tenor bell, which rings the Fast in at midnight on Fastens' Eve, has a ringer whose hands
  shake. Someone must ring it, or the Fast does not begin, which is a question the Faculty will enjoy.
- **The Dog.** The Scaifes' dog follows the company down the fell, and will not go home.

## 7. The overarching conflict

What each wants from Ash Day, and what each does if the company does nothing:

- **Dame Strake** wants Hern to recant, which saves him and the Commerce. Failing that, she will put him out, because
  she is exact.
- **Canon Pellow** wants him put out, loudly. The tower's fees depend on the Commerce.
- **Thackray** wants it over. He sits on the Consistory as the Guildhall's man and will vote as Pellow does, sick.
- **Bess Ackroyd** wants the Row's winter wage, and has nothing against Hern but his argument.
- **The Faculty** wants to be paid.
- **Hal** wants a rescue, **Prudence** the fee.
- **Hern** wants to say it in the Minster and then, he thinks, to be put out. He is not sure he wants to die. The road
  and Carth will decide that.

Left alone: Hern is tried, will not recant, the bought Determination condemns him, and he is walked out of the Gate of
Ash at dusk. Hal's rescue on Fastens' Eve fails and Hal is taken. The Carth vigil runs on; the grandmother dies in the
byre; the Scaifes are paid, and evicted at Lady Day anyway. Thackray is gilded at the top of the Ladder.

## 8. Principal arcs

- **Hern**: from wanting to be a martyr to choosing between being one, being useful, and being alive. What moves him:
  the Carth child (whose cold is his refusal's price), Bess (whose bread his truth would take), Hawise (whom he has not
  spoken to in thirty years), and the protagonist's own answers on the road.
- **Hawise Strake**: from exactness to a choice. She can be moved by Hern, by the Row, by the protagonist's account of
  Carth, by being masked on Fastens' Eve.
- **Thackray**: from being sorry to changing. A mind changed by a person: by Annis Scaife, by being made to keep one
  night of his own vigil, by his daughter, by the widow.
- **The protagonist**: from custodian to something else: witness, proxy, accomplice, executioner. The work keeps a
  fault for them from the first night: whatever happened at Carth, they were there.

## 9. Possible resolutions

1. **Put Out.** Hern condemned and walked into the Levels. Followed, he can be brought to Carth.
2. **The Recantation.** He recants; lives; the Commerce stands. Bitter, or sly ("I recant, and I'll do the penance for
   it myself").
3. **The Proxy.** Someone bears his sentence: the protagonist (who walks out of the Gate of Ash and winters at Carth: *a
   winter in Carth*); Thackray (his own penance at last); the Barefoot, forty of them, a night each, which the Consistory
   cannot accept without becoming absurd, nor refuse without conceding Hern's heresy; or Hawise.
4. **The Remission.** Hawise, moved, remits; the Faculty's paid opinion is set aside. Hern free; nothing else changes.
5. **The Fast.** The Barefoot strike on Ash Day; the rich of Gaunt walk barefoot in the snow for their own sins in
   front of the city; the Consistory adjourns. The comic ending.
6. **The Flight.** Hern slips away at the Drove Fork, or Hal's ladder works, or the company breaks him out.
7. **Dead before trial.** At Carth, or in the gaol, of the cough. The Consistory tries him dead, and must decide whether
   a dead man can recant by proxy.

Carth's own fates run alongside: the vigil kept, broken, cheated, released by Thackray; the grandmother saved or not;
the rent paid, forgiven, or the family evicted.

## 10. What it asks for, before reading the engine

- People who change their minds because of people, with no visible meter.
- Positions the protagonist takes held as facts, readable by others and turned.
- A debt measure the region reads everywhere (shrift).
- Darkness and cold as a physical condition of a place; a light that counts as a fire.
- A night of hours in which things happen without the company asking.
- A public occasion: the trial, with witnesses, a crowd, and pronouncements.
- Placement in a generated world: found beyond a march, entered by the north road, where a beadle waits.
- About 25 rooms in the city, 10 on the road and at Carth, 30 people.
