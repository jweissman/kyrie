# A Winter in Carth: the second pass

*The adventure re-read as literature, once more and more harshly: whether it has depth or only the look of it; where
it was pulp; what its themes are and whether the whole thing carries them; tone, foreshadowing, dramaturgy; and what
was changed in answer. 12-critique.md was the first verdict. This document supersedes it where they disagree.*

## The diagnosis

**The ideas were better than the history.** The first build had a strong premise: in a city where sorrow is bought,
a farm is paid to keep a rich man's penance in the cold. But nothing in Gaunt had happened before last winter. The
forty-one who froze in the Row were a number on a wall. The Gate of Ash "last opened nineteen years ago", and nobody
had been put out of it. The Bishop's Wood had no bishop, and the Register's two hundred and eleven years had no first
page. Every strand pointed at the present. Depth in a setting is the sense that the present is the latest layer of
something. Gaunt had one layer.

**The strands touched only at the trial.** Carth, the Row, the tower and the Lady met in the Chapter House and nowhere
before it. A player could learn everything about the vigil without its meaning anything to the Row, and everything
about the tower without its meaning anything to the Lady. A satire can survive that. A tragedy cannot, and the
material wanted to be one.

**Everybody was witty.** Hern, Hawise, Joan, Joby, Annis, Bess and Thackray all spoke in the same dry rural cadence,
in the same rhythm of "and… and… and", and each had a line that knew it was a good line. A reader stops trusting
the people when each of them is performing for him. The most distinct people in the module were Raistrick and Will,
who barely spoke. That is a verdict on the rest.

**Some of it was pulp.** It was not the OSR kind, a weird intensity aimed at something. It was the kind that is
pleased with itself:

- Hern, getting into a dying child's bed: "I'm a famous man. You can tell them in Gaunt you slept with Barefoot Hern."
  It is a joke at the moment the scene most needs him to mean it.
- The Doctors' pulpits "like ships about to fire"; Doctor Quist who "once, famously" argued "with a chair"; "a fire you
  could roast an ox in, and an ox in it". These are cartoon similes, three in one district, and they flatten the
  district into a sketch.
- "As if you had pulled out a drowned king"; the Lady's "wonderful smile" (the narrator telling us to like her);
  Hern's "three teeth" grinning across the Chapter House.

Not everything comic is pulp. A man selling hot chestnuts and indulgences from the same brazier is the satire in one
image, and stays. Joan Pickersgill's "laugh like a dropped tray" is a character, and stays. The test applied was this:
does the extravagance show the thing more sharply, or only show the writer?

**The mystery was handed over.** Annis gave the company a shilling with the Wood Exchange's mark on the first night
and named the Exchange. The intrigue was over before the city was entered.

**The Lady's arc had no past.** She would put out the man she loved, "fairly". But fairness had cost her nothing yet,
so her moving at the copper had nothing to move against.

**There was no "after".** Hern's deepest line, "There's never been an after, for a proxy", was a statement the world
did not answer. The gate led to a fade-out.

## What was done

### The forty-one have a face

Annis Scaife's sister, **Ellen Wray**, went down to the Row to proxy for the winter and froze with the others. Her
peg is the thirtieth. Annis knew whose shillings paid for the vigil, because the mark on them told her, and **she
took the vigil for that**: "I want him forgiven by somebody who knows what it cost. A night at a time. I've not
decided yet whether I'll finish." So the cold vigil is no longer only the rent. The poor are keeping the rich man's
penance for their own dead, and one of them is choosing, each night, whether to forgive. Will's one line in the whole
night ("And Ellen's for nothing") now carries weight. The thirtieth peg in the Barefoot Hall carries Ellen's name and,
in another hand, a little cut log that someone tried to rub out. At the cob, Annis says the name to Thackray's face,
and in the city a company can carry it to him: "So that it's kept by somebody who knows." The motto on the Cross, FOR
THE FAULTS OF ANOTHER, now cuts both ways at Carth.

### The last man put out, and where he went

Nineteen years ago the Gate of Ash was opened for **Cuthbert Thwaite**, journeyman mason, Joby's son. He went back for
his level one night, saw the Fabric's men taking the day's course down, and said so: drunk in the Fat Lamb, then
sober at the Penitentiary. Laverock prosecuted. It was **Dame Hawise's first sitting in the stone chair**, and she put
him out, fairly; she has put nobody out since. **Hern was hired by the Fabric** to fast three days in thanksgiving for
a slander stopped. "Best-paid fast I ever kept."

One buried event now holds four strands: the tower, the Lady, Hern and Joby. It is found from four sides:

- Hern on the road ("Ask Hern what becomes of a man put out");
- Joby at the Fat Lamb ("I'd a lad once who said it wasn't frost");
- the Lady at her desk ("Ninian will be the second"), and her silence when the unbuilding is told to her: she walks
  to the last of the forty books, turns it back nineteen years, and keeps her finger on a line;
- Cuthbert himself, in **the put-outs' fold**: a ruined sheepfold on the Levels, roofed with turf, where seven or
  eight of the people the gate was opened for live within sight of the city, owing nobody, "the first thing in my life
  that was mine".

The fold answers Hern. A company that takes him there on the way in gets the scene the first build lacked:

> 'Barefoot Hern,' says Cuthbert Thwaite at last. 'You fasted for my going.' 'I did,' says Hern. 'Three days. A
> shilling a day. The Fabric paid.' 'Was it a good fast?' 'It was the best I ever kept,' says Hern, 'and I never meant
> a minute of it.' … 'There's room,' he says, 'if they put you out.'

An "after" is now one of Hern's reasons to want to live. If he is put out alone, the closing account has the fold
make room for him by the fire. Joby, told that his son is alive, takes him a mason's lantern on Ash Day. Both lines are
in the account, and the fold is the lyric counterweight to Carth: elegy where Carth is dread.

### Raistrick has a childhood

Asked whether he has ever found a hearth warm, the Ash-reeve tells it. His mother kept forty nights for a miller's lie
when he was nine; his sister had the croup; he lit it, and the reeve put his hand in and said "Broken", and they were
off the farm by Lady Day. "I thought the hand in the ash should be a hand that knows." It explains why a lie about a
fever can hold with him, and it says the module's argument from the other side. The rule is kept by someone who once
broke it for love, and keeping it is his penance.

### The coin is a clue, not an answer

Annis now gives only a mark, "a little raised oblong, like a cut log", and "I know whose mark that is. I've known
since Candlemas". The match is made in the city, by the player, at the Exchange door: "You know that shape." Asking
Thackray directly now needs the match, or Raistrick's ten o'clock, or the weeping rider on the fell together with his
cousin's word that he has been strange all winter. In each case a
person is seen doing something.

### Danger, with ways round

The **Fabric's night-men** now hold the scaffold-yard from nine till four: four men with crowbars and a horn lantern.
They warn a climber once, and then they fight: "in the morning they'll find you at the foot of it, and say it was
the frost." There are five ways round them, each a different kind of play:

1. watch the unbuilding over the wall from Gaol Lane;
2. come over the wall or through the lodge at dusk, before them;
3. go back over the wall when warned;
4. sit up with Joby's masons on Fastens' Eve, when they do not come;
5. have their canon silenced by the Lady, after which they stop coming.

With the woodmen (pay, or fight, or come when the right is open), the wolves at dusk (come by day) and the serjeants
in Gaol Lane (pay Prudence, or fight), the city has four fights, all avoidable and all dangerous.

### The prose

The lines above were cut or rewritten. Hern in the box-bed is now "I've done this before. I'm the best there is at it,
ask anybody in the Row", which is vanity in the service of tenderness, as his vanity always is. The Schools' pulpits
face each other "like a quarrel built in oak". The Fat Lamb has "half a sheep turning over it, and a boy turning the
sheep". The Lady's smile is "a Row smile, all at once and gone, that has not been taught any manners by forty books".
The Lady's farewell, the masked woman's, and Hawise's manner were given voices that fit them (acerbic, grim).

## Tone, by place

Each district now holds one register, so that walking is a change of key:

- **the fell road**: open air, lyric, philosophical (Hern's questions);
- **Carth**: horror, dark, almost wordless;
- **the Levels and the fold**: elegy, distance, the city as a shape on the skyline;
- **the Row**: plain and bitter, "Down here we eat";
- **the Middle Town**: comedy of manners, where the jokes are allowed (the Doctors, Joan, the Fat Lamb, the Ladder);
- **the Close**: hushed and grand, the Register, the thirty-one names, the warm lawn;
- **Ash Day**: ceremony, then the verdict as news coming down the Steps.

## Dramaturgy

Five acts, each with its place and its faces, each ending on a turn:

| Act | Place | Faces | It turns on |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. The road (Saturday morning) | the Cross, the Stone, the Fork | the beadle, Raistrick, Hern | "Are you the one who promised?" |
| II. The first night | Carth in the dark | the Scaifes, Fly, the Ash-reeve | what is done for the child, and whose rule is kept |
| III. Gaunt (Shrove Monday) | the Fell Gate, the Row, the Stand, the Exchange, the Close | the Assessor, Bess, Thackray, the Lady, Joby | the coin's mark matched; the lantern on the tower |
| IV. Fastens' Eve | the Doctors' Schools, the copper, the scaffold, the Fat Lamb, Gaol Lane | Quist, the masked woman, the masons, Joan and Abel, Hal | what is said under a mask before midnight |
| V. Ash Day | the Minster, the Chapter House, the Gate of Ash, the fold | the Consistory | who bears it, and where he walks to |

The arcs: **Hern** from a man who has never thought about after to one who has seen it. **The Lady** from fairness
that cost her nothing to fairness that cost a boy nineteen years, and a decision about whether the law means what it
says. **Thackray** from carried to walking. **Annis** from keeping the vigil as a sentence on him to deciding,
perhaps, to stop. **Joby** from frost to his son. **Raistrick** has no arc: he is the fixed point the others turn on,
and now we know why.

## What is still not right

- **The middle still waits for its clocks.** There are six city-wide beats in three days. The fold, the peg and the
  coin give a player more to do between appointments, but Gaunt does not yet overhear itself by the hour.
- **The trial is attended, not argued.** The witness speeches are set pieces chosen from a list. A Consistory that
  questioned the company about what it knows (the billet, the tower, the fold), with each answer moving one canon,
  would be the module's real climax.
- **Returning to Carth** is built but not played: the oak arriving, the twenty-sixth morning, Annis deciding.
- **Hern talks too well.** Of all of them he most needed the pruning and got the least, because he is the one the
  pruner liked.
- **The engine shows through** in the dark (the room's visual list of people), in the weariness notice, and in Hern
  existing twice to speak through bars (friction.md).

## Where ten times the ambition would start

1. **Annis's decision as the hinge of the account.** Make the vigil's last fourteen nights a playable chapter after Ash
   Day. Whether she finishes them, forgives, or burns the oak Thackray sends, and whether the company or Hern or
   Thackray is in the house when she decides, would be an ending of its own.
2. **The Consistory as a duel.** A sitting played in rounds: the Determinations read, the prosecutor's questions, the
   witnesses cross-examined from what the company actually knows, Hern's answer, the offer. Each canon on the bench
   would be a person with a price or a conscience.
3. **The fold as a second city.** The put-out have their own economy, own penances and own heresies, and a drovers'
   road out to a world that has never heard of Gaunt. A company struck from the Register should be able to live there,
   and come back through the Gate of Ash in nineteen years.
4. **The Register as a dungeon.** Forty books on forty lecterns: every fault in the city for two hundred and eleven
   years, readable at night by someone who gets in. Cuthbert's line, Thackray's confession, Ellen's death recorded as
   "exposure, no fault", the first line about the Bishop. It is a library that remembers everyone's sins, and finding
   one would be a lever on anyone in Gaunt.
5. **The Bishop.** Who went away two hundred and eleven years ago, and why, and whether the keys the Penitentiary
   took up were ever his to give.
