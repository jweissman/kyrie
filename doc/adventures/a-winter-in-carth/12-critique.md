# A Winter in Carth: a final critique

*Written after the last reading of the transcripts, as an editor would write it to the author: what the adventure
is, where it succeeds, and where it does not.*

## What it is

A satire about bought penance with a horror story in the middle of it and a trial at the end. The seed *A Winter in
Carth* became the night at the farm. *The Road to Orm* became the walk with Hern and his three questions. The
metropolitan region is the city they lead to. The parts are joined by one question (who paid for Carth?) and one
room (the Chapter House at noon on Ash Day). Tonally it is unlike the existing adventures. It has no monster, almost no
fighting, and no treasure worth anything outside the walls. Its danger is cold, and its antagonist is an economy.

## Where it succeeds

**The night at Carth is the best thing in it.** It is a real moral problem with eleven tested ways through. Each one
is a different answer to what a rule is for: the fire for the child and the debt; the fire in the byre and the letter
kept; the beasts brought in from the old days; everyone in one bed; the nursing; the lie; Hern's confession, which
makes the household break its own vigil and own the debt. The Ash-reeve's hand in the ash is a precise, repeatable
horror. The house's darkness is real on the page ("It is pitch dark"), and the door refuses a lantern. When nobody
does anything, Hern does it, and pays: the default is a sacrifice, not a fail state. That is the structure the whole
module should have had.

**The trial gathers the city.** Witnesses, a contrary Determination, a moved Lady, a man who will walk out for
another: everything done in three days is either in that room or not. Hern quotes the company's own answers from the
road back to the Consistory, so the walk on the first morning is paid off on the last afternoon.

**The two approaches are genuinely different politics.** From above is reform: use the law exactly until it says the
truth, and have it written down. From below is the strike and the window: stop the machine, or leave it. They meet at
the Lady, whose mind every good ending needs, and who can be reached at her desk or under a mask in the Row.

**The romance is the right size.** Joan and Abel are a comedy of a hired fast for impure thoughts about the man hired.
Hern and Hawise are a forty-five-year quarrel about salt in a soup. Neither is played for more than it is.

## Where it does not

**The middle is a city of appointments, and between them it is still.** The transcripts show long runs of waits in
which nothing is told: nineteen and twenty-three in *from below*, twenty and seventeen in *the letter*. Gaunt happens
at fixed hours: ten, two, seven, eleven, midnight, noon. A player who is not where the hour is hears nothing. Stage 6
added the masks, the bell and the verdict as news to the whole city. Shrove Monday afternoon and Ash Day morning are
still thin. A metropolis should overhear itself more.

**The mystery is solved too soon.** Annis puts the billet-marked shilling into the company's hand on the first night,
and the Wood Exchange's sign is on the city's every woodpile. The intrigue becomes what to do about Thackray, which
is the better question, but there is little detection in it. A version where the billet means nothing until the
Exchange is seen, and Raistrick's ten o'clock is the confirmation, would ask more of the player.

**Thackray is moved too cheaply.** Annis at his stirrup and a children's rhyme are true and well placed, but two of
them undo eleven years at the top of the Ladder. The design's fourth way (his cousin's laughter, after the widow's
betrothal) is the best one, and the least likely to be found.

**Nobody opposes the company.** Laverock is the only person with an interest in the Commerce who acts, and he acts by
night on a tower. The Stand, the Guildhall and the Penitentiary's clerks all let the company do what it likes. The
satire is clearer for it, but there is no pressure. A Gaunt that noticed a stranger making Bess Ackroyd call a
strike, and sent a beadle round, would be a better city.

**The Row's approach earns less than the Close's.** In experience (1,200 against 2,900 for the played journeys), and
in prose: the Close has the Penitentiary, the Schools, the Lady at her desk, the Chapter House; the Row has the
Barefoot Hall, Heb, and the copper. *The Fast* is the boldest ending and has the shortest closing text.

**One voice.** Hern, Hawise, Joan, Joby, Annis and Bess all speak the same dry, rural, rhythmic English: "'Did you.'
'Did you.'" It is good English, but they are not different enough from one another. Raistrick and Will, who barely
speak, are the most distinct people in the module.

**Returning is built and not felt.** The vigil counts on, the old woman dies on the twenty-sixth morning if nobody
stops her, the fire is lit when the vigil is released, and Fly goes home. All of it is in the closing account. None of
it is played by a journey that walks back up the fell, which is three hours, and which most players will not think to
do. The account is doing work the world should be doing.

**What Kyrie made hard shows.** The dark room lists its people by sight. Weariness is announced in the middle of the
Ash-reeve's visit. Simultaneous texts land in their own order. Hern exists twice so he can speak through bars. These
are worked round (friction.md), not solved.

## Where ten times the ambition would start

- **The city should move between appointments.** It needs ambient life by hour and quarter: the Stand's hiring at
  eight, the ash at nine, the clerks at noon, the proxies home at dusk. Each should be told to whoever is in the
  street, in the way the Fastens' Eve masks now are.
- **Make the second night at Carth playable.** A company should be able to go back up with Thackray's oak, or with
  Hern put out, and keep the last fourteen nights in a house it has changed.
- **Give the Commerce a defender who acts.** Dame Hawise's deputy, perhaps: a man who believes in it more than she
  does, and who reads the Register.
- **Let the trial be argued, not only attended.** Questions put to the company as a witness, answered from what it
  knows (the billet, the tower, the Ladder), each moving one canon on the bench.

## Verdict

As a piece of interactive fiction it has one great scene, a good last act, and a middle that is a well-furnished city
waiting for its clocks to strike. As a Kyrie region it is complete and placed. An ordinary wanderer can hear of it two
countries away and walk into it. Every principal ending is reached by a different build and is tested. A player can
be wrong in it, and the world remembers. It is better than the design's middle deserved, and the night at Carth is as
good as anything the engine has been asked to tell.
