# A Winter in Carth: the small adventures

*Fourteen strands and three purely local stories, what each is on its own, and what it gives the others. Every strand
can be played alone and has its own end; every strand also hands something to the trial on Ash Day or to the closing
account.*

## The strands

| # | Strand | Where, when | On its own | What it gives elsewhere | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The beadle's foot** | the Cross, Saturday morning | take Hern into custody for two shillings and eight more at the Fell Gate; or refuse; bind the beadle's frozen foot (mending) | custody opens the road questions and the Close Gate on delivery; refusing sends Hern ahead to Carth; the bound foot is a fate line and teaches that a sentence can be borne | `road.kyr` |
| 2 | **The road to Gaunt** | the fell road, Saturday | Hern's three questions: can one man be sorry for another; what do we owe the dead; are you the one who promised (asked by walking away down the drovers' road) | the answers are quoted back by Hern in his cell and in the Chapter House; letting him go at the Drove Fork is *the Flight* before the city is reached; Tobias Kell's board, taken, costs two pains at the Fell Gate and is a milestone | `road.kyr` |
| 3 | **A winter in Carth** | the house at Carth, the first night | a farm keeping a forty-night cold vigil for the rent, a coughing child, a dead grandfather in the byre and a grandmother who lies down with him; nine ways through the night (below) | the vigil kept, broken, cheated or released is the first line of the account; Raistrick's report at ten tells the payer; the night is the company's testimony at the Consistory; whether Hern gave his coat decides whether he is dying | `carth.kyr` |
| 4 | **The Fell Gate** | the Fell Gate, on arrival | the Assessor's tally: association with Hern (1), the board (2), the vigil broken (17) | the tally is the city's whole economy for the company: it is what keeps it out of the Close | `gate.kyr` |

| 6 | **The Ladder** | the Stand, Dinsdale's brewhouse, Sowerby's drapery | the two burgesses racing for second place on the Ladder of Merit by buying penances in advance; bear for them, and tell each what the other is paying | raised far enough, they buy out the Stand, and on Ash Day there is nobody left to hire: one half of *the Fast*; Sowerby, walking his own mile, is changed | `stand.kyr` |
| 7 | **The widow's thoughts** | Tanners' Row, the Stand, the Fat Lamb | Joan Pickersgill pays Abel Lumb to fast for her impure thoughts, which are about Abel Lumb | betrothed, Joan laughs at her cousin Thackray, which moves him | `widow.kyr` |
| 8 | **The unfinished tower** | the north tower, Gaol Lane, the Fat Lamb | by night the Master of the Fabric's men take down what Joby's masons set by day | told, Joby opens the lodge (a way into the Close) and guards the course on Fastens' Eve; Laverock offers to forgive the Scaifes' rent for silence; told to Hawise, Laverock is silenced and does not prosecute | `tower.kyr` |
| 9 | **Whose forty nights** | Carth, the Wood Exchange, the Barefoot Hall | a coin with a cut-log mark, matched over the Exchange door; Raistrick's ten o'clock report: the vigil was bought by Hugh Thackray, the Wood-reeve, for the forty-one in the Row he froze by holding back the oak; and Annis took it knowing, because her sister Ellen is the thirtieth peg | moved (by Annis to his face, by Ellen's name, by the children's rhyme, by a night of his own at Carth, by his cousin's laughter), he releases the vigil, opens the wood, and offers to bear Hern's sentence | `thackray.kyr` |

And for the grandmother: sit with her in the byre, or bring her in, or lay Ralph out with the words for the dead
(rites), or she is found on the twenty-sixth morning with her arms round him.

## Purely local

- **Tib's skate-bones**, under the black ice by the culvert grating (a cold-water hazard). Returned, the children's
  rhyme is sung to its end: *NOW WALK, said Oswy*. The verse is a key that moves Thackray and the Lady.
- **Mother Sugden's oven**, cold for want of wood two days before the Fast. A faggot from the Exchange or the wood.
- **Fly**, the Scaifes' collie, who follows a company that was good to the house and goes home when it next passes
  Carth with the child alive.

## How they hold together

Everything turns on one fact, which the company carries about the city: *who paid for Carth*. The coin's billet,
Raistrick's report, Joan's "he's been strange all winter": three ways to the same door. And every strand ends in the
same room at the same hour: the Chapter House at noon on Ash Day, where whatever the company has done is either in the
room (a witness, a Determination, a moved Lady, a man who will bear it) or not.

The two approaches use different strands. From above: 4–5 (a clean tally), 8 (the lodge), 9 (Thackray), 13 (witness).
From below: 5 (Heb), 6 (the Ladder bought out), 10 (the Row out), 11 (the window), 12 (the Lady, unmasked only in
the Row). They meet at the Lady, who belongs to both.
