# A Winter in Carth: environment and resolutions

*How the place itself carries the story, and every resolution, where the player sees it again.*

## The environment as argument

- **Cold and dark are the antagonist.** There are no monsters at Carth. The engine's darkness (`unlit`) is put on
  the house-place and the byre at nightfall unless there is a fire, so "It is pitch dark. Without a light you can see
  nothing here" replaces the room's description, and the choices are what can be done blind. The door will not admit
  flame. The hearth is a Thing with three states (*cold*, *ablaze*, *snowed*), and every way through the night is a
  change of that one thing or a refusal to change it.
- **Night is the module's, not the engine's.** The engine's dark is fixed at nine to six; February dark is five to
  seven, so the module asks its own `nightfall()` everywhere (friction 1).
- **Snow as a door.** The snow on the fell is scenery with states; while it falls both fell roads are shut, so the
  night at Carth is an unwalkable room, and "Nobody could go down that tonight and arrive."
- **The tally as a key.** The city is a set of doors with prices: the Close Gate opens to the shriven; the Fell Gate
  shuts for ever on the put-out; the gaol admits visitors ten to four; the Chapter House opens on Ash Day; the Gate
  of Ash opens once in nineteen years at dusk. The satire is that every one of these is a canon of the Penitentiary,
  and the company has to live by them or find a way round (the lodge, the wall, a forgery, a ring).
- **Bodies pay.** Penance done in person is a hazard: a barefoot mile on frozen setts, stripes at the Stand's post, a
  night on the Bearer's Steps, the cold side of the box-bed, the black water under the ditch ice. They are physical
  damage, small, with constitution saves; they are what the rich pay to avoid. (The engine's cold damage paralyses,
  "frozen", and killed a bard in the first draft: all the cold hazards are physical: friction 4.)
- **Time pushes.** A clock of chapters (Saturday, Shrove Monday, Fastens' Eve, Ash Day), and fixed appointments:
  Raistrick at three, his report at ten, Thackray in Carth's yard at nine, the dispute at two, the informer at eight,
  the ladder at eleven, the Fast at midnight, the Consistory at noon, the Gate at dusk. Missed, they are gone, and the
  account says so.
- **Things that speak.** The board round Tobias Kell's neck; the billet on the coin; the Ladder of Merit, which is
  gilded fresh each morning by whoever is ahead; the price board at the Wood Exchange, which Thackray rubs out with
  his fur sleeve; the forty-one pegs in the Barefoot Hall.

## The endings

| Ending | Reached when | Its closing text, in brief |
|---|---|---|
| **The Fast** | the Row struck on Ash Day and the Stand bought out, so nobody is clean | "Adjourned. Until the Commerce of Merit resumes." |
| **The Flight** | Hern escaped through the window, or let go on the road | the trial held to an empty floor |
| **The Recantation** | Hern willing to live and given the Lady's or Bess's words | he goes home to the Row; "It's still too salt" |
| **The Bearer** | someone shriven (the company, or Thackray) offers after Hern has answered, and the Lady is moved | an innocent put out for a guilty one, written down |
| **Put Out** | otherwise | alone, followed, or in a coffin |

The account that precedes each has forty-two fate rules in eleven families: Carth, the child, the grandmother,
Thackray, the widow, the Ladder, the tower, the Row, the rescue, the company's tally, and the dog. Each family says
exactly one line.
