# Left Alone at the Cross

Played by Wren, a bard; seed `g1`, no steps but waiting; 83 steps; ended at watch 493, at the ending "Put Out". Written by `bun scripts/winter-in-carth-transcripts.ts` from the journey as the page told it.

*(The first page)*

The road comes up out of the march between snow-fields and stops being a road and starts being a fell road at a cross of grey stone, man-high, with a ledge round its foot worn hollow by knees. Someone has carved on its shaft, very neatly: FOR THE FAULTS OF ANOTHER. A low stone house stands beside it with smoke coming out of it, and on the step of the Cross a barefoot old man is mending a sandal.

*(17 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

An hour passes. It is a long time since you slept. You are weary, and will fight the worse for it until you sleep.

*(3 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

SHROVE MONDAY. In Gaunt the bells of the Minster ring the hour, and the Stand opens at eight, and the Ladder of Merit is read by more people than read anything else in the city. Today the bakers have two days left to sell what the Fast will forbid, and the Consistory has two days to make up its mind about a man whose mind is made up.

*(23 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

FASTENS’ EVE. The last day before the Fast. At two the Doctors dispute in the Schools; at seven the masks come out, and the Fat King is carried round the Middle Town on a door, to be burnt at midnight outside the Wood Exchange, where wood is dearest; and at midnight the tenor bell of the Minster rings the Fast in, and whatever was said under a mask was never said.

*(5 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Hern gets up and looks at the sun. ‘Noon on Fastens’ Eve,’ he says. ‘I’ve an appointment in Gaunt, and I’ll keep it on my own feet.’ He goes off north, barefoot, at a walk, and does not look back.

*(17 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

ASH DAY. At nine every forehead in Gaunt is marked with ash at the Minster door, and the Ladder of Merit is gilded for the year. At noon the Consistory sits in the Chapter House on the matter of Ninian Hern. At dusk, for the first time in nineteen years, the Gate of Ash may be opened.

*(10 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At five the serjeants walk Ninian Hern out of the Gate of Ash into the Levels, and come back in without him, and the gate is shut and chained and sealed; and through the grating, for a while, the Row watches a barefoot man in a grey coat walking away south across the snow toward the fell, not fast, not looking back, until the dark has him.

**Wait › An hour**

Hal Metcalfe is in the Consistory gaol, in the warm room, for Fastens’ Eve and a ladder, and will be heard after the Fast. Prudence Garth’s mother has paid her pains and can buy in the Shambles, and Prudence has not been seen on the Steps since. The Carth vigil goes on: night twenty-six tonight, fourteen to go. Raistrick will come at three and kneel and put his hand in the ash, and say ‘Kept’, and go. At the thaw the Scaifes will be paid, and pay the Fabric, and be square, and it will be spring. The Ladder of Merit is gilded for the year with HUGH THACKRAY, WOOD-REEVE at the top of it, and the price of oak is thirty-one shillings the cord. Abel Lumb fasts for Mistress Pickersgill’s thoughts, eleven a week, and she goes down on Mondays to watch him not eat; and neither of them has said anything to anyone. Under the top of the Ladder, DINSDALE is gilded second and SOWERBY third, or the other way about; the city has stopped being able to tell. On the north tower Joby Thwaite’s masons set a course of white stone today, and tonight the frost will get into it. In the Row the Barefoot sit in their doorways with their feet in bowls, and the stove in the Hall is not lit.

**Wait › An hour**

Ninian Hern was put out of Gaunt at dusk on Ash Day, through the Gate of Ash, into the Levels, in what he stood in, struck from the Register; and the gate was shut and chained and sealed, and the city went in to its supper, which was fish. The next morning the snow had covered whatever footprints there were.
