**Talk to Beadle Clem Sutcliffe**

**Help Hal Metcalfe up the ladder and get the bars out of the gaol window**

Up the ladder with the file, the lane below full of masks going by who see nothing because it is Fastens’ Eve: lead dug out of the stone, a bar bent back, and another, until there is a hole a thin old man could go through. Inside, by the fire, Hern is sitting on the bed with his hands on his knees, looking at the hole. Hern looks at the hole for a long time. Then he gets up, and buttons the Penitentiary’s grey coat up to the neck, and comes to the window, and puts one bare foot through it, and then the other. ‘I’d thought I’d go out of the Gate of Ash,’ he says, on the ladder, ‘like a man in a story.’ He comes down into Gaol Lane among the masks. ‘This is a better story. Nobody’ll believe it.’ Hal Metcalfe takes one arm and you take the other, and the three of you go down to Bow Gate and out through the Row, and nobody stops anybody, because it is Fastens’ Eve; and at the Fell Gate, which is never shut, he stops, and shakes your hand for the last time, and goes off into the Levels on his bare feet in the dark, toward the fell and the drovers’ road, with Hal beside him carrying the ladder for no reason. Dick gains 150 experience: got the bars out of the gaol window. At nine the Minster’s west door is opened and the city goes past it in a line that reaches down the Bearer’s Steps and into the Shambles, and a canon with a bowl of ash puts his thumb to every forehead, the brewster’s and the barefoot man’s alike, and says the same four words to each. **Wait › An hour**

At noon the Consistory sits in the Chapter House on the matter of Ninian Hern, and the little square of different stone in the middle of the floor is empty. The charge is read to it. The Determination is read to it. The Lady Penitentiary asks it whether it recants, and waits, with great courtesy, the proper time for an answer. Then she pronounces him put out, in his absence, from a city he is no longer in; and the precentor, who believes in nothing, has to turn away to the wall.

Your tally is still notched. In the Register of Gaunt you owe what you owe, and it will be there when you come back, doubled. The Carth vigil goes on: night twenty-six tonight, fourteen to go. Raistrick will come at three and kneel and put his hand in the ash, and say ‘Kept’, and go. At the thaw the Scaifes will be paid, and pay the Fabric, and be square, and it will be spring. Dowsie Scaife is up and about in her mother’s shawl, with a cough she is proud of, telling the hens about the stone man in the Minster, who she has never seen. On the twenty-sixth morning of the vigil Old Mother Scaife did not come in from the byre. She had gone through to Ralph in the night, as she did every night, and lain down under the sheet with him to keep him warm; and this time nobody brought her back. There are two hurdles in the byre at Carth now. The Ladder of Merit is gilded for the year with HUGH THACKRAY, WOOD-REEVE at the top of it, and the price of oak is thirty-one shillings the cord. Abel Lumb fasts for Mistress Pickersgill’s thoughts, eleven a week, and she goes down on Mondays to watch him not eat; and neither of them has said anything to anyone. Under the top of the Ladder, DINSDALE is gilded second and SOWERBY third, or the other way about; the city has stopped being able to tell. On the north tower Joby Thwaite’s masons set a course of white stone today, and tonight the frost will get into it. In the Row the Barefoot sit in their doorways with their feet in bowls, and the stove in the Hall is not lit. Hal Metcalfe came back through the Fell Gate on Ash Day morning without his ladder and has not said where he left it.

Ninian Hern was put out of Gaunt in his absence, from a city he had already left. Nobody knows where he went. In the spring a drover coming in by the Fell Gate said there was an old man barefoot on the drovers’ road west of Carth Fell, preaching to cattle; but drovers will say anything. The next morning the Stand was full, and the Ladder was gilded, and on the Minster steps, where Hern used to stand, nobody stood.
