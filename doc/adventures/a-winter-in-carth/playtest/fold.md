The road comes up out of the march between snow-fields and stops being a road and starts being a fell road at a cross of grey stone, man-high, with a ledge round its foot worn hollow by knees. Someone has carved on its shaft, very neatly: FOR THE FAULTS OF ANOTHER. **Talk to Beadle Clem Sutcliffe**

The man in the beadle’s gown does not get up. ‘You’ll be going north,’ he says. ‘Everybody’s going north. It’s the Fast.’ He looks at his foot, which is the colour of a candle. ‘I’m not. I’ve a commission for whoever is.’

**Ask the beadle what the commission is**

‘Him,’ says the beadle, and nods at the old man on the step. ‘Ninian Hern. Barefoot Hern, they call him in the Row. The Consistory sits on him on Ash Day, for heresy, and I was sent to fetch him in, and I’ve fetched him this far, and here’s my foot.’ He takes it out of the hat and looks at it and puts it back. ‘He’s not chained. **To the Bearer's Steps, before the Close Gate**

The Bearer’s Steps: thirty broad stone steps up to the Close Gate, every one worn into a dip in the middle by knees, and the Close Gate at the top with a porter behind a grille.

*(5 waits in which nothing is told)*

**To Gaol Lane, under the gaol window**

Gaol Lane: a narrow way between houses and the Close wall, and in the wall, at the height of a tall man’s head, one barred window. Further on, the wall goes up past the north tower’s scaffold, poles and ropes and ladders lashed against the sky. Over the Close wall you can see the top of the north tower’s scaffold, and on the top platform, by the light of a horn lantern, men in brown taking the day’s new stone down, block by block, very carefully, and lowering it on a rope; a canon in a fur tippet watching from below with his hands in his sleeves. Here are Ninian Hern, a voice and two hands and a bit of a face at the barred window over Gaol Lane, the hands holding the bars; and Hal Metcalfe, a printer’s apprentice of eighteen with ink to the elbow, loitering under the gaol window with his hands in his pockets, very obviously not loitering.

**Into the Fat Lamb**

The Fat Lamb: a big warm inn, the warmest room you have stood in since the march, full of people eating as if somebody were going to take the plates away, which on Wednesday somebody is. Here are Matthias Dent, the landlord of the Fat Lamb, in a leather apron, carving; and Old Joby Thwaite, a master mason of seventy-one with stone-dust in his eyebrows and hands like two mallets, eating a pie with great concentration.

**Talk to Old Joby Thwaite**

Joby Thwaite waves his pie at you.

**Tell Joby Thwaite what you have seen on the scaffold at night**

Joby Thwaite puts down his pie. He sits for a long time with his big hands flat on the table. ‘Frost,’ he says at last. ‘Fifty-three years.’ And then, much more quietly: ‘Cuthbert.’ He does not shout; masons do not. He stands up, very slowly, as a wall stands up. ‘Fastens’ Eve,’ he says. ‘We’ll set it in the morning, and we’ll sit up with it at night, the lodge, all of us, with our mallets. Let them come with their crowbar.’ He looks at you. ‘You’ll come and sit with us. The lodge door’s open to you, now, the yard door off the Steps. Any time. Tell the lads Joby said.’

**Tell Joby Thwaite that his son is alive, in the put-outs' fold on the Levels**

Joby Thwaite holds the last of his pie and does not eat it. ‘Alive,’ he says. ‘Out there.’ He turns his head as if he could see through the wall of the Fat Lamb and both the walls of the city to the Levels. ‘Nineteen years. I could have walked it in an hour.’ He puts the pie down. ‘Ash Day,’ he says. ‘When the course is set and stood. I’ll take him a lantern. He’ll know what it means.’

**Wait › An hour**

FASTENS’ EVE. The last day before the Fast. At two the Doctors dispute in the Schools; at seven the masks come out, and the Fat King is carried round the Middle Town on a door, to be burnt at midnight outside the Wood Exchange, where wood is dearest; and at midnight the tenor bell of the Minster rings the Fast in, and whatever was said under a mask was never said.

**Wait › An hour**

The Fat Lamb on Fastens’ Eve: masks, music, a fiddle and a drum, the fire, the ox, couples at every table who would not be at one together on any other night, and the landlord going round with a jug saying ‘Midnight, mind. Midnight.’ An hour passes.

**Wait › An hour**

ASH DAY. At nine every forehead in Gaunt is marked with ash at the Minster door, and the Ladder of Merit is gilded for the year. At noon the Consistory sits in the Chapter House on the matter of Ninian Hern. At dusk, for the first time in nineteen years, the Gate of Ash may be opened. Wren gains 200 experience: saw the north tower’s new course stand.

*(2 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At nine the Minster’s west door is opened and the city goes past it in a line that reaches down the Bearer’s Steps and into the Shambles, and a canon with a bowl of ash puts his thumb to every forehead, the brewster’s and the barefoot man’s alike, and says the same four words to each. For an hour everyone in Gaunt has the same mark on them. Then they go and look at the Ladder.

*(4 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At two the news comes down from the Close and goes through the city like cold through a coat: Barefoot Hern would not recant. He said it in the Chapter House, all of it, and he is to be put out at the Gate of Ash at dusk. In the Row people come out of their doors and stand in the snow, and do not go back in.

*(2 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At five the serjeants walk Ninian Hern out of the Gate of Ash into the Levels, and come back in without him, and the gate is shut and chained and sealed; and through the grating, for a while, the Row watches a barefoot man in a grey coat walking away south across the snow toward the fell, not fast, not looking back, until the dark has him.

**Wait › An hour**

At the put-outs’ fold on the Levels they made room by the fire for an old man in a grey coat, who arrived after dark with his feet bleeding and his cough worse, and has been telling them every evening since, at length, that not one of them is sorry for anything. They agree with him, and give him the warm side. The Carth vigil goes on: night twenty-six tonight, fourteen to go. Raistrick will come at three and kneel and put his hand in the ash, and say ‘Kept’, and go. At the thaw the Scaifes will be paid, and pay the Fabric, and be square, and it will be spring. Dowsie Scaife is up and about in her mother’s shawl, with a cough she is proud of, telling the hens about the stone man in the Minster, who she has never seen. The Ladder of Merit is gilded for the year with HUGH THACKRAY, WOOD-REEVE at the top of it, and the price of oak is thirty-one shillings the cord. Abel Lumb fasts for Mistress Pickersgill’s thoughts, eleven a week, and she goes down on Mondays to watch him not eat; and neither of them has said anything to anyone. Under the top of the Ladder, DINSDALE is gilded second and SOWERBY third, or the other way about; the city has stopped being able to tell. The north tower of the Minster of Gaunt has a new course of white stone on it, the first in two hundred and eleven years that stayed, and Joby Thwaite goes up the scaffold every morning to look at it, and comes down, and sets another. In the Row the Barefoot sit in their doorways with their feet in bowls, and the stove in the Hall is not lit. Hal Metcalfe is in the Consistory gaol, in the warm room, for Fastens’ Eve and a ladder, and will be heard after the Fast. Prudence Garth’s mother has paid her pains and can buy in the Shambles, and Prudence has not been seen on the Steps since. Your tally is still notched. In the Register of Gaunt you owe what you owe, and it will be there when you come back, doubled. On Ash Day, when the course had stood a night and a day, Joby Thwaite walked out of Gaunt across the Levels with a mason’s lantern, to a sheepfold with a thread of smoke going up from it, and sat down by the fire there beside his son; and neither of them said much, and neither of them needed to.

**Wait › An hour**

Ninian Hern was put out of Gaunt at dusk on Ash Day, through the Gate of Ash, into the Levels, in what he stood in, struck from the Register; and the gate was shut and chained and sealed, and the city went in to its supper, which was fish. The next morning the snow had covered whatever footprints there were.
