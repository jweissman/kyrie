The road comes up out of the march between snow-fields and stops being a road and starts being a fell road at a cross of grey stone, man-high, with a ledge round its foot worn hollow by knees. Someone has carved on its shaft, very neatly: FOR THE FAULTS OF ANOTHER. A low stone house stands beside it with smoke coming out of it, and on the step of the Cross a barefoot old man is mending a sandal.

**Talk to Beadle Clem Sutcliffe**

The man in the beadle’s gown does not get up. ‘You’ll be going north,’ he says. ‘Everybody’s going north. It’s the Fast.’ He looks at his foot, which is the colour of a candle. ‘I’m not. I’ve a commission for whoever is.’

**Ask the beadle what the commission is**

‘Him,’ says the beadle, and nods at the old man on the step. ‘Ninian Hern. Barefoot Hern, they call him in the Row. The Consistory sits on him on Ash Day, for heresy, and I was sent to fetch him in, and I’ve fetched him this far, and here’s my foot.’ He takes it out of the hat and looks at it and puts it back. ‘He’s not chained. He’d go by himself, he says. But the law says he’s to be brought, and brought means somebody brings him, and the somebody’s paid. Two shillings now. Eight at the Fell Gate, from the Assessor, when you hand him over.’ He sniffs. ‘It’s three days’ walk for a beadle. It’s one for anybody with two feet.’

**Take Ninian Hern into your custody, for the beadle's two shillings now and eight at the Fell Gate**

**Ask the masked woman for remission of your pains**

She laughs, and holds the ladle out to the next bowl, and while she fills it she works a ring off her finger with her thumb and drops it on the step beside the copper. ‘Remission,’ she says. ‘Show that at the Close Gate. It’s no good anywhere else in the world, but in Gaunt it’s the Lady Penitentiary’s ring, and you owe nothing.’ She does not look at you. ‘Nobody gave it to you. It’s Fastens’ Eve.’

**Take**

You pick the ring up off the step by the copper. It is still warm from her hand.

**Take your leave**

‘Mind how you go in the dark,’ says the masked woman at the copper.

**To the Long Shambles**

The Shambles on Fastens’ Eve: lit end to end with torches, crowded, masked.

**To the Bearer's Steps, before the Close Gate**

The Bearer’s Steps: thirty broad stone steps up to the Close Gate, every one worn into a dip in the middle by knees, and the Close Gate at the top with a porter behind a grille.

**To Gaol Lane, under the gaol window**

Gaol Lane: a narrow way between houses and the Close wall, and in the wall, at the height of a tall man’s head, one barred window. Further on, the wall goes up past the north tower’s scaffold, poles and ropes and ladders lashed against the sky. Over the Close wall you can see the top of the north tower’s scaffold, and on the top platform, by the light of a horn lantern, men in brown taking the day’s new stone down, block by block, very carefully, and lowering it on a rope; a canon in a fur tippet watching from below with his hands in his sleeves. Here are Ninian Hern, a voice and two hands and a bit of a face at the barred window over Gaol Lane, the hands holding the bars; and Hal Metcalfe, a printer’s apprentice of eighteen with ink to the elbow, loitering under the gaol window with his hands in his pockets, very obviously not loitering.

**Talk to Ninian Hern**

‘Is that my custodian?’ says the face at the bars. ‘Or a stranger? Either is welcome; I’ve had the precentor in all day, explaining my heresy to me. He’s very good at it. I’m thinking of taking it up.’ A cough. ‘Stand where I can see you. I’m on the bed on tiptoe.’

**Give Hern the Lady Penitentiary's words: that he was sorry once, really, for one of them**

The hands go still on the bars. ‘She said that,’ says Hern’s voice. Then, a long while after: ‘Once. Aye. A fishmonger’s wife, and a week’s fast, when I was fifteen. I was sorry all week. For her.’ A cough. ‘She was in the next pen. She’d remember.’ Then, very quietly: ‘If it’s true, it’s not giving in. Is it.’

**Tell Hern you would rather he lived, because one man can be sorry for another, and you are**

‘You said on the road that one man being sorry for another is what love is,’ says the voice at the bars. ‘I’ve had that in my head ever since, and I can’t get it out, and I’ve tried.’ Then: ‘You’re a dangerous person to walk with.’ A long cough. ‘I’d thought the Gate of Ash was the end of the story. Proxies don’t get an after. I’ll think about it. I’ll think about it on the bed, on tiptoe.’

**Take your leave**

‘Off you go, then,’ says Ninian Hern, ‘and don’t do anything I’d be ashamed to hear about. Or do, and tell me.’

**To the Bearer's Steps, before the Close Gate**

The Bearer’s Steps: thirty broad stone steps up to the Close Gate, every one worn into a dip in the middle by knees, and the Close Gate at the top with a porter behind a grille.

**Show the porter of the Close Gate the Lady Penitentiary's ring**

The porter looks at the ring through the grille for a long moment. Then he takes your tally, and with his knife he shaves every notch off it, slowly, one by one, until it is a smooth white stick, and gives it back. ‘Remitted,’ he says. He does not ask how. Wren gains 100 experience: made your shrift clean in Gaunt.

*(8 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

ASH DAY. At nine every forehead in Gaunt is marked with ash at the Minster door, and the Ladder of Merit is gilded for the year. At noon the Consistory sits in the Chapter House on the matter of Ninian Hern. At dusk, for the first time in nineteen years, the Gate of Ash may be opened.

*(2 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At nine the Minster’s west door is opened and the city goes past it in a line that reaches down the Bearer’s Steps and into the Shambles, and a canon with a bowl of ash puts his thumb to every forehead, the brewster’s and the barefoot man’s alike, and says the same four words to each. For an hour everyone in Gaunt has the same mark on them. Then they go and look at the Ladder.

*(4 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At two the news comes down the Bearer’s Steps faster than anyone can walk it: Barefoot Hern has recanted, in the Chapter House, in front of everybody. Nobody in the Row believes it, and then everybody does. By three he is walking down Bow Gate in a grey coat, slowly, coughing, with a crowd behind him that does not know whether to cheer, and so does not; and at the door of the Barefoot Hall somebody puts the copper on again, at three in the afternoon on Ash Day, which is not allowed. Wren gains 400 experience: gave Ninian Hern the words to live by.

*(4 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Your tally is clean. Gaunt has nothing against you, which in Gaunt is the most anybody ever has. The Carth vigil goes on: night twenty-six tonight, fourteen to go. Raistrick will come at three and kneel and put his hand in the ash, and say ‘Kept’, and go. At the thaw the Scaifes will be paid, and pay the Fabric, and be square, and it will be spring. Dowsie Scaife is up and about in her mother’s shawl, with a cough she is proud of, telling the hens about the stone man in the Minster, who she has never seen. The Ladder of Merit is gilded for the year with HUGH THACKRAY, WOOD-REEVE at the top of it, and the price of oak is thirty-one shillings the cord. Abel Lumb fasts for Mistress Pickersgill’s thoughts, eleven a week, and she goes down on Mondays to watch him not eat; and neither of them has said anything to anyone. Under the top of the Ladder, DINSDALE is gilded second and SOWERBY third, or the other way about; the city has stopped being able to tell. On the north tower Joby Thwaite’s masons set a course of white stone today, and tonight the frost will get into it. In the Row the Barefoot sit in their doorways with their feet in bowls, and the stove in the Hall is not lit. Hal Metcalfe is in the Consistory gaol, in the warm room, for Fastens’ Eve and a ladder, and will be heard after the Fast. Prudence Garth’s mother has paid her pains and can buy in the Shambles, and Prudence has not been seen on the Steps since.

**Wait › An hour**

Ninian Hern recanted in the Chapter House on Ash Day, and went home to the Row that evening, and slept in his own room with somebody’s coat over his feet. The Commerce of Merit stands; the Row eats; the Ladder is gilded. On the morning after, very early, someone saw the Lady Penitentiary in the Row without a mask, at the Barefoot copper, tasting the soup, and adding water.
