# A Winter in Carth — Stage 2: a severe reading of the treatment

*The treatment ([01-treatment.md](01-treatment.md)) read as an editor who has to publish it beside twelve other works,
against the commission's own questions and the repertoire survey's warnings. Verdicts first, then the reasons, then
what changes. The revised design is [03-revised-design.md](03-revised-design.md).*

## Verdicts

| Question | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Is the city alive apart from its central story? | **Not yet.** Every street is about penance. A one-law city is a one-joke city. |
| Are the small adventures substantial and varied? | **Too many, unevenly built.** Sixteen named; five are one scene each. |
| Does the plot grow from the setting? | **Yes**, and that is the treatment's best quality. The Commerce makes the vigil, the vigil makes Carth, Carth makes the fault, the fault makes the protagonist a party to the city. |
| Is the satire a game, or a long passage of dialogue? | **Half a game.** The shrift is a real law. The Ladder race and the Determination are described, not played. |
| Is the horror a game? | **One night is not "fear that lasts".** And its danger is cold, which is a hazard, not a dread. |
| Is the philosophy a game? | **Promising, unproved.** Three questions are a quiz unless the answers bite later. |
| Do minds change for reasons other than evidence? | **Mostly.** But the mystery's answer is a sealed page in a register, which is the collection's ledger again. |
| Are there real alternatives? | **Too many endings, chosen too late.** Seven trial outcomes is a menu, not a narrowing. |
| Does it reward different builds? | **On paper.** Carth does; the city is weighted to talkers. |
| Too much exposition? | **The premise is a lecture.** The Commerce is explained before it is seen. |
| Are the comic moments earned? | **Set up, not paid off.** The widow is a premise; the Ladder race has no beats. |
| Does a character change? | **Yes, three can**, and the protagonist is given a fault. Hawise is in danger of being a type. |

## 1. A one-law city

Every person in the treatment is defined by their relation to penance: buyer, seller, assessor, forger, proxy,
theologian of it. The repertoire's complaint about earlier works, that a city exists to serve its mystery, applies
here to a satire: Gaunt exists to serve its joke.

A city is alive when people in it want things that have nothing to do with the plot. **Changes:**

- Keep the three purely local stories and make one of them nothing to do with penance at all. The Shrove baker's
  oven is about wood, which is the Commerce again. Keep her, but give the city one story with no theology in it:
  **the children's slide** on the frozen ditch, and the boy who has lost his skate-bones, which his dead father cut.
- Let the satire be the city's weather, not its only subject. Most people in the Middle Town do not think about the
  Commerce any more than a modern town thinks about its tax code. The jokes should arise from people going about
  their business inside an absurd law, not from people explaining it.

## 2. Too many strands, and five that are scenes

Sixteen strands, of which the Bell, the Determination, Hal's Ladder, Fastens' Eve and the Ladder race are each one
scene with an option or two. A strand that is one scene is a *beat*, not a small adventure.

**Changes:**

- **The Determination** is folded into the trial and the Schools: the Doctors are a place and a scene the company may
  use, not a quest.
- **Hal and Prudence** become one strand, *the Rescue*, with both young people in it, because the turn is between them.
- **The Bell** is cut. It was a joke about the Faculty and nothing else.
- **Fastens' Eve** is not a strand but the occasion on which three strands come to a head: the widow, the rescue,
  the Lady Penitentiary in the Row.
- **The Ladder race** must be played in beats or cut. It stays, with three escalations the company can cause and an
  end (see §6).

## 3. One night is not lasting fear

The treatment's horror is a single night, and its danger is cold, which is damage. Lasting fear needs return,
anticipation, and a danger that is partly the people you are with.

**Changes:**

- **Carth recurs.** The vigil is the frame of the whole work: forty nights, of which the company sees the
  twenty-third and may come back for others. What has changed at Carth each time the company returns is told, and some
  of it is worse.
- **The danger is partly the household.** Will Scaife has a billhook and the family's ruin to lose; Annis hates Hern
  and has reason; the grandmother is not sane tonight. A company that lights a fire in that house is in danger from
  its hosts, not only from the cold.
- **The Ash-reeve is anticipated.** The household tells of him before he comes, so that every sound after midnight is
  him. He comes when the company does not expect it, and he does not knock (Hessel's Carth has a knock; this one must
  not).
- **The Cold Guest stays ambiguous.** The grandmother lays a place at the dead hearth. The dog will not go into the
  byre. In the morning the dead man has moved. The explanation (she has been lying beside him) is findable, by
  watching, but not given.

## 4. The mystery is a ledger again

"Who bought the Cold Vigil" answered by "the Register's sealed page" is the decisive ledger the repertoire asks to
rest, and a clerk who keeps an honest register (Hawise as first drafted, "exact") is the grey-coated clerk it calls
the collection's most important accidental convergence.

**Changes:**

- **The Register is sealed and stays sealed.** No one opens it in this work. The patron is found through **people**:
  Raistrick, who reports to the man who pays, and can be followed down off the fell or asked; Annis, who was paid in
  coin with a woodyard's mark on it; and Thackray himself, who cannot keep away from Carth, and is on the road on the
  second morning, alone, looking at the house.
- **Knowing is not enough.** Knowing who paid opens a door to Thackray; it does not move him. He has to be moved by a
  person (§7).
- **Hawise is not a clerk.** She is warm, worldly, funny, partial, and a politician; she bends small rules for the
  Row every day and is proud of it. Her exactness is about one thing only: the sentence. She is the most likeable
  person in the city, and she is going to put out the man she loved.

## 5. The trial is a hearing, and the endings are a menu

The repertoire asks that votes and hearings rest for two commissions. The seed requires a trial, so the trial stays;
but it must not be a credence vote fed with exhibits, and it must not offer seven doors at the end.

**Changes:**

- **The trial is short and is not where the work is decided.** What Hern will say, who will stand with him, who will
  bear what for him, are settled before he walks into the Chapter House: on the road, at Carth, and on Fastens' Eve.
  The trial plays out what has been made possible, and the company has one or two acts in it, not a debate.
- **The endings narrow.** Each is reached only by what has already happened; at the trial at most two are open to a
  given company. Seven become five, plus Carth's fates as an account:
  - *Put out* (the default; and, if the company follows, a night at Carth);
  - *The recantation* (Hern moved by Bess or by Hawise to save himself);
  - *The proxy* (someone the company has moved, or the company itself, bears the sentence);
  - *The Fast* (the Barefoot out on Ash Day, the rich in the snow, the Consistory adjourned);
  - *The flight* (on the road, or from the gaol).
  - *Remission* is cut as a separate ending: Hawise's change of mind is the engine of *the proxy* (she accepts it, which
    is the scandal) and of *the recantation* (she gives him the words), not a third thing.
  - *Dead before trial* is kept as a fate, not an ending: if Hern dies, the book still closes on Ash Day, on a
    Consistory trying a dead man, which is the satire's last word.

## 6. Comedy described, not built

The widow is a premise, and a premise is not a joke. The Ladder race is a list of escalations. A comedy has setup,
complication and payoff, in that order, with the company causing the complication.

**Changes:**

- **The widow.** Setup: Abel at the Stand, fainting. Complication: the company can make it worse before it is better
  (offer to do Abel's fasts for him, and Joan, told her proxy has changed, thinks harder). Payoff: Fastens' Eve, when
  licence lets people say what they think, and Joan says it; or Ash Day, when they are found doing the same penance in
  the same pew.
- **The Ladder.** Three beats. Dinsdale hires the company to fast; Sowerby learns of it and hires two proxies to fast
  *and* walk; the company, carrying news between them, raises the stakes (or not) until one of them hires the whole
  Stand for Ash Day, which leaves no proxies for anyone else, which is the joke and the Barefoot's leverage at once.
  End: one of them, beaten, does a penance in person, in public, and is laughed at, and discovers it is better.
- **The narrator's irony is rationed.** The satirical voice is for the city's institutions, not its people. It never
  sneers at the poor. It is silent at Carth.

## 7. Minds changed by people

The treatment names who can move whom. It must also say *by what*, and the answer must not be a fact shown.

| Mind | Moved by | Not by |
|---|---|---|
| Hern (to live) | Dowsie Scaife alive or dead; Bess telling him what the Row eats if he wins; the protagonist's road answers; Hawise | Argument alone: he has won every argument for a year |
| Hawise (to accept a proxy, or give him the words to recant) | Being met masked in the Row on Fastens' Eve; Hern himself, if the company brings him a message; an account of Carth from someone who was there | The Register, which she wrote |
| Thackray (to bear his own penance, or open the wood) | One night of his own vigil, in the cold, which the company can talk him into at Carth or on the Minster steps; Annis Scaife to his face; the widow, who is his cousin and laughs at him | Being told who he is: he knows |
| Bess (to strike, or not) | The dead-wood right refused or given; Hern asking her; the company doing a day's proxy work in the Row | Hern's argument, which she thinks is a rich man's luxury |
| Will Scaife (to break the vigil) | His daughter's breathing; Hern confessing that the vigil was his first | Being told it is foolish |

## 8. Builds

The city as drafted rewards persuasion everywhere. Each strand should have at least one non-social way through.

- **Carth**: woodcraft (the beasts into the house, a fire that the Ash-reeve will not find), mending (Dowsie's fever),
  rites (the dead man laid out, which lets the grandmother come in), brawn (digging out at dawn), stealth (following
  Raistrick), a light-bringing spell is a fire and counts.
- **The Stand**: constitution and brawn do proxy work that a talker cannot afford to.
- **The rescue**: lockwork, climbing, stealth, or a fight with the gaol's two serjeants.
- **The wood**: the Bishop's Wood is the work's honest danger: wolves at dusk, and Thackray's woodmen. A company that
  brings a sledge of dead wood down to the Row or to Carth has done the strike's work by hand.
- **The tower**: climbing and brawn to set and guard the stone at night.
- **Lore** reads the Faculty, and the founding legend of Saint Oswy, which has a second verse nobody quotes.

## 9. Exposition

The premise paragraph explains the Commerce. In play, the company must **see** it before it is told: the frozen
penitent at the Proxy's Stone with a board round his neck in another man's name; Abel fainting at the Stand; the price
board. Hern's speeches are cut to exchanges of two or three lines, with the company's answer between.

## 10. Against the collection

- **Pellam** has winter and a con: Gaunt has no fair, no ice-street, no con at its centre. Its winter is a condition,
  not a festival, and its comedy is satire, not caper.
- **Pentrice** has a city that licenses speech: Gaunt licenses guilt. The two satires rhyme; this one should not have
  an office of censorship, a stage, or a play.
- **Wrayford** has the dead unburied by decree: at Carth the dead man is unburied by frost, which is no one's decree,
  and nobody is asking to bury him; the grandmother is asking to keep him.
- **Hessel** has a household in a shut house and a walk to a hearing. The Scaifes are shut in by snow and law, not
  by a man's jealousy; the walk is a custody, not a witness's errand; and there is no knock.
- **No inherited mechanism, no other plane, nothing older than Saint Oswy, and no ledger that decides.** The
  misunderstood thing is a legend, and nobody has misread it: they have chosen not to read its second verse.
