# A Winter in Carth: the story

## Synopsis

On the Saturday before the Fast a beadle sits at the Penitent's Cross with his frozen foot in his hat, and offers the
company two shillings to take his prisoner the last forty miles to Gaunt. The prisoner is Ninian Hern, barefoot, old,
ill, funny and vain of his feet, for thirty years the best paid penitent-for-hire in Gaunt, where any penance may be
done by proxy at the Stand by the day. A year ago he began to say on the Minster steps that he had been sorry for a
hundred men and never once meant it, and neither had they, so who had been forgiven? He is walking to his own trial
because he wants to say it inside the Minster, where nobody can walk past.

On the road he asks three questions. Then the snow comes in over Carth Fell, and the only roof is a farm with no
smoke. Carth is keeping a cold vigil, forty nights without fire, for the rent; the penance was bought for a sin in
the city and sold on by the Penitentiary, and Hern was the man who refused it. Inside, in the dark, are a woman who
reads out her losses like a list, a man who will not look at anyone, an old woman who lays a place for the Cold Guest,
a child with a cough that is changing, and, in the byre, the grandfather who died on the fourteenth night and cannot be
buried till the thaw. At three the Ash-reeve comes in without knocking and puts his bare hand in the ash.

Morning, and Gaunt across the Levels, walled three times like a target. At the Fell Gate everyone is assessed for the
faults of their road and given a tally; with pains on it, the Close and its Consistory are shut. The city is a
commerce of merit: the Stand where the poor are hired to fast, the Ladder of Merit in the Minster porch where the rich
are listed in gold by how much they have paid other people to suffer, a north tower that has stood unfinished for two
hundred and eleven years. Three days to Ash Day. Somebody in this city paid for Carth.

## Acts

| Act | Days | What happens | Where it turns |
|---|---|---|---|

| **II. Carth** | Saturday night | the dark house, the child's cough, the old woman and the byre, Raistrick's hand in the ash, morning | what the company does for the child, and whose rule it keeps |
| **III. Gaunt** | Shrove Monday | the Fell Gate tally; shrift or a forgery; the Close or the Row; Thackray's grey cob in Carth's yard; the Stand; the tower by night | finding out who paid for Carth |
| **IV. Fastens' Eve** | Shrove Tuesday | the Doctors dispute; the masks come out; the Lady at the copper; the masons on the scaffold; Joan and Abel; the ladder at Gaol Lane at eleven; the Fast rung in at midnight | what is said under a mask, and what is done before midnight |
| **V. Ash Day** | Wednesday | the ash at nine; the Row out or at work; the Consistory at noon; Hern asked at one; the verdict; the Gate of Ash at dusk; the account | who bears it |

## Characters

- **Ninian Hern**, proxy, heretic. Thirty years of other men's sorrow. Generous, coarse, vain, ill, sometimes wrong,
  and not in a hurry to be saved. He has never thought about after: "There's never been an after, for a proxy."
- **Dame Hawise Strake**, the Lady Penitentiary. Came out of the Row with Hern at twelve. Thirty years of turning the
  rich's vanity into the Row's bread, and proud of it. She will put him out, fairly. She has had a coat made for him
  and told nobody. On Fastens' Eve she ladles soup in the Row in a mask, as she has for forty-five years.
- **Alderman Hugh Thackray**, Wood-reeve, top of the Ladder for eleven years. Held back the oak last winter and
  forty-one in the Row froze. Bought forty nights cold for it, which Hern refused and Carth took. Rides up the fell to
  look at a chimney with no smoke and weeps. Kind, soft, carried all his life.
- **Annis Scaife**, who reads her losses aloud like a list; **Will**, who will not look at anyone; **Old Mother
  Scaife**, who lays a place for the Cold Guest and lies down with her dead husband in the byre to keep him warm;
  **Dowsie**, with a cough that is changing; **Fly** the collie.
- **Raistrick**, the Ash-reeve. Exact, polite, never knocks. "A vigil isn't a thing you're told about before." At nine
  he lit his own mother's vigil for his sister's croup, and lost them the farm; he took the post so that "the hand in
  the ash should be a hand that knows".
- **Ellen Wray**, dead, the thirtieth peg in the Barefoot Hall: Annis's sister, who went down to the Row to proxy for
  the winter. Annis took the vigil knowing whose penance it was, to be the one who forgives him, or does not.
- **Cuthbert Thwaite**, Joby's son, the last man put out of Gaunt, nineteen years ago, for saying the tower was
  unbuilt by night: the Lady's first putting-out, and Hern's best-paid fast. He lives in the put-outs' fold on the
  Levels, within sight of the tower, owing nobody.
- **Canon Hamo Laverock**, Master of the Fabric, prosecutor, the Scaifes' landlord: unbuilds the tower by night so the
  Fabric's tithe never ends. Very clean hands.
- **Joby Thwaite**, master mason of seventy-one, who has set the last course fifty-three years running.
- **Joan Pickersgill and Abel Lumb**: the widow who pays the young man to fast for her impure thoughts, which are
  about him, and the young man who would sooner starve than let anyone else do it.
- **Grizel Dinsdale and Pentecost Sowerby**, brewer and draper, racing for second place on the Ladder.
- **Bess Ackroyd** of the Barefoot Hall, and her grandson **Tib**, whose skate-bones are under the ice.
- **Doctors Ploughwright** (Irrefragable, never refuted because never understood) **and Quist** (Seraphic, argues
  with chairs).
- **Hal Metcalfe and Prudence Garth**, the apprentice with the ladder and the student who means to sell the night.
- **Heb**, who cuts tallies in a cellar; **Matthias Dent** of the Fat Lamb; **Beadle Clem Sutcliffe**; **Mother
  Sugden** the baker; **Tobias Kell**, dead, kneeling, for someone else's adultery.

## Conflicts

1. **Hern against the Commerce**: can one person's sorrow be bought by another? The whole city is the argument for
   yes, Carth the argument for no.
2. **Hern against Hawise**: two children of the Row, one who went up and fed it, one who stayed down and told the
   truth. The trial is their quarrel in public. The soup was always too salt.
3. **The letter against the spirit**: at Carth, the vigil kept by the rule (a fire in the byre, the hearth wet) or
   broken for the child; in Gaunt, the tally made clean by a forgery, a proxy or your own bare feet.
4. **Thackray against himself**: a kind man who caused deaths by a price, and keeps paying not to know it; and
   **Annis against Thackray**, keeping his penance for her sister's death, night by night, undecided.
5. **The company against its own place in the story**: Hern's third question. Bringing him in is a promise made by
   someone else; the city's whole proposition is that the one who pays need not be the one who does.
6. **The city against its own history**: the Bishop who went away, the gift of the fallen wood cut in a post, the
   verse planed off Oswy's plinth, the mason put out for telling the truth. Gaunt has been unbuilding itself by
   night for two hundred years.

## The ending, in five ways

- **The Fast**: the Row will not work on Ash Day, and the rich, who bought out the Stand to climb the Ladder, have
  nobody to hire; nobody is clean by the canons in time; the Consistory adjourns "until the Commerce of Merit resumes".
- **The Flight**: the bars out of the gaol window, or Hern let go at the Drove Fork; the trial held to an empty floor.
- **The Recantation**: Hern, given a reason to live (Bess's word, or the Lady's), recants, with the truth or bitterly,
  and goes home to the Row, and the soup is still too salt.
- **The Bearer**: someone offers to bear the sentence, the company itself or Thackray, and a moved Lady accepts; an
  innocent person is put out for a guilty one in front of the city, "which is what Ninian has been saying for a year.
  Write that down too."
- **Put Out**: he says it all and is put out at dusk, in a new grey coat, alone or followed; or, if nobody tended
  him after he gave the child his coat, he is tried dead and put out in a coffin.

Then a closing account of what became of Carth, the child, the grandmother, Thackray, the widow, the Ladder, the tower,
the Row, the rescue, the company's tally, and the dog.
