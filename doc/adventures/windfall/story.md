# Windfall: the story first

*Written before any Kyr. The adventure follows it; where play changed it, friction.md says how.*

## The place

Hythe is forty houses and an alehouse on the last dry ground of the Saltings, where the Iscoed fen gives up and the sea
begins. For two hundred years the Water-Wardens kept the shore with an orchard. They grafted three hundred apple and
pear trees so close that their roots wove into a dike a mile long, and let the trees hold the sea. The sea-gate
at the end of the causeway kept the fresh water coming down through the roots. The gate has failed. Salt has got into
the roots, and the orchard has started to walk.

It goes a few yards a night, all together, in the dark, the way a flock moves when no one is watching. It has been
walking since harvest. On the fourth morning its front rank will stand at the wall of Hythe's churchyard, and an orchard
does not know what a grave is.

## People

**Tamsin Soames** is eighty-three, the last grafter the Wardens trained, and she has followed the orchard since harvest
in a hut on skids drawn by a goat. She is not sentimental about trees. She talks to them as to difficult cousins and
swears at the pears. She knows how to stop the walk. She will not ask anyone to cross the Gault for it, because the
last person she asked was her husband.

**Ellery Maddern** is the sexton. His wife Nance is under the east wall, and over her grows a pear that Tamsin gave
Nance as a cutting forty years ago, a cutting of the orchard's own Mother Tree. He wants the orchard stopped. He does not
yet see that it is not coming for the graves. It is coming for the pear.

**Bryony Gorse** burns charcoal on the ridge. Iscoed is short of fuel, her children are cold, and three hundred
seasoned fruit-trees walking toward her ride in the dark are the best fire she will ever be offered. She is not
cruel. She has counted the cords.

**Odile Harrowby** is the Reeve's bailiff, who has come from Porthalen with a writ to clear the drove road. The
orchard has been across it for a week and carts are turning back. She would sooner not fell anything. She has been told
how many days she may wait.

**Hobin Lunt** is nine, the alehouse keeper's son, and has given every tree in the front rank a name. He knows where the
Gault will hold a person and where it will not, because he has been told not to go there.

**Verity Lunt** keeps the Spare Bucket and a fire, and will sell a bed to anyone who is not loud about the orchard.

## Scenes

**The road.** Apples in the road, hundreds of them, wet and unbruised, and a goat looking at you across a hedge it has
eaten the top off. A hand-painted sign has been nailed to a post and then, by someone else, nailed to a different post:
THE ORCHARD IS NOT HERE.

**The Walk, at dusk.** The orchard stands in the Ash Field in rows too even for an accident. Nothing moves. Tamsin sits
on the hut's step with a knife and a pear, and does not look up. 'You'll want to be gone before dark,' she says.
'It's rude to watch them go.' If you stay: the first sound is like a held breath being let out, and then roots drawing
out of the ground all along the rows with a noise like a crowd taking off its boots. They walk. You see the trunks
lean, and step, and settle. Hobin, from the hedge, whispers the names as they pass.

**The pear.** In the churchyard, against the east wall, one pear tree leans toward the Ash Field. Its leaves are in
the wrong season. Ellery has been tying it upright with strips of Nance's old apron. Every morning the ties are
loose.

**The Spare Bucket.** Bryony: 'I'm not going to fell your orchard, I'm going to buy it. Ask her what she wants for it.'
Harrowby, at the next table, does not drink: 'I may wait until the third night. I'd be glad to be given a reason.'

**The Gault.** The causeway is the Wardens' last road: a quarter mile of dressed stone, going under at every flood.
The tide leaves it for five hours, and the Gault beside it will hold a hurdle or a child but not a heavy boot. At the
end the old sea-gate stands on its landing with the sluice-house behind it, and a Warden's wheel the size of a
cartwheel that nobody has turned in nine years. Fresh water is held behind it, a mere's worth, a whole winter's rain.
Open it and the roots are washed.

**The grafting.** Tamsin can stop the walking another way. The orchard is following the pear. A tree cut from the
Mother is calling it home. Graft a scion of Nance's pear back onto the Mother Tree and the orchard has what it wants,
and stops where it stands, whatever the salt does. It needs the sexton's leave. It needs two hands that do not shake.

## What can happen

- **Salt out of the roots.** The sluice raised, the fresh water washing down through the dike. The orchard roots where
  it stands, in the Sunk Lane, and the Saltings keep a dike a quarter mile shorter than before.
- **Nance's pear.** The graft taken. The orchard comes to the churchyard wall and stops there, and stays, and every tree in
  the front rank leans over the wall at the pear. Whatever else was done, Ellery has someone to stand with.
- **The Green.** A trail of windfalls laid through the lane by a boy and a sack lures the orchard off its line and onto
  Hythe's own common. It roots there. Hythe has an orchard, and a dike that no longer holds the sea.
- **The burning.** Harrowby's writ, Bryony's burners, three nights of fire on the ride. Iscoed has coal for winter. The
  dike goes slack behind where the orchard stood.
- **Midwinter blossom.** Nothing done. The orchard walks through the churchyard on the fourth night and does not
  touch a stone. It lifts nothing. It roots between the graves, and on the fifth morning every grave has a branch. Ellery
  sits there all night. It is not clear to anyone whether this is a catastrophe.

## Why it is not a ledger

Nobody is owed anything. The orchard does not want an offering. A pear that was a gift forty years ago is calling a
Mother Tree, a widower is tying it down, and a woman of eighty-three will not ask for help. The question is whether
anyone can be asked to cut something from the one living thing of the person they lost.
