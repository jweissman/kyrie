# What Tessary Owes — first design treatment

*Written before reading Kyrie's engine, Kyr syntax, or the other adventures. Only the Living Design Atlas and the
Aesthetic Doctrine were consulted, for Imbar's orientation. This is the draft the editorial review in
`02-critique.md` attacks; `03-revised-design.md` is what was built.*

## Premises considered and set aside

Before settling, I sketched five kernels and asked of each: *could this only happen here? does it start small and
grow by cause rather than by itinerary? who could change?*

1. **The Census of the Dead.** A city that taxes its dead, a ministry of ghosts below. Rich in bureaucratic comedy,
   but its escalation was arbitrary: the dead are below because the premise says so. No strategic power with a
   plan, only a system.
2. **The Moving Mountain.** A city on the back of a slow beast whose drift is being steered. Marvellous image,
   weak human drama — nobody's loyalties are in conflict.
3. **The Long Step.** A city's legions return from war unable to stop hearing their marching hymn, which was a
   fragment of an Aenean binding-music; its composer, now Minister of Concord, wants to play the whole score and
   tune the city into permanent harmony. Strong (the Atlas's interest in demobilization and civic music), but the
   antagonist is a thesis — Plato's *Republic* in a robe — and the play would be listening and being told.
4. **The Lost Ledger.** A debt collector's apprentice whose small collection turns out to be one link in a chain
   of obligations running down through the city to a creditor below and, at the bottom, to the oldest debt in the
   world. Funny, practical, full of documents, loopholes and negotiation; the risk is allegory without bodies.
5. **The merge.** Take the *returning legions* of (3) and the *chain of debt* of (4) and make each answer the
   other's weakness: the soldiers give the debt bodies, faces and a deadline; the debt gives the homecoming a cause,
   a mechanism and a villain who is not an idea.

The fifth is the adventure.

## The kernel

**A city won its war on borrowed strength, and pledged its soldiers as the collateral. The soldiers have come
home. The note falls due on the Feast of Settlement. The only repayment the creditors accept is a kept promise of
equal weight — and the only promise of that weight the city has ever made is the one it made to those same
soldiers: land and a pension for every one who came back. The city cannot afford to keep it. Someone is making
very sure it won't.**

Every layer of the adventure is a layer of that sentence: the vanishings (the collateral being called), the
underworld (the creditors), the political crisis (the vote on the pensions), the antagonist (the buyer of the
note), the marvel (where pledged things are held), the cosmology (what the creditors are actually balancing).

## Setting

### Tessary on the Mere

A tidal city on the delta of the river Mere, built on pilings and fill over the drowned streets of an Aenean city
the sea took eleven hundred years ago. It lives on salt, eels, reed-paper, rope and shipping. Because it makes
paper it is a city of documents: notaries, scriveners, seals, contracts, a famous Office of Seals whose queue is a
civic institution and a standing joke. Its people swear casually and litigate constantly. Its new year is the
**Feast of Settlement**, when debts are paid, quarrels are closed and the year's contracts are burned in braziers
on the Weir — a custom whose origin nobody remembers.

**Wards** (each with a material and social identity):

- **The Causeway and Landgate** — the road in across the salt-marsh; toll-house, carters, a shrine to the saint of
  fair weights.
- **The Sallows** — willow-carr tenements on stilts over the backwaters; reed-cutters, eel-wives, the families of
  the rank and file. Where most of the vanished lived.
- **Saltgate and the Pans** — evaporation pans, salt-sheds, Brack's warehouses, a stink of brine and smoke.
- **The Lettered Quarter** — reed-paper mills, scriveners' stalls, the Office of Seals, the Exchequer, the
  Notaries' Hall. Ink, pumice, argument.
- **The Weir and the Legion Yard** — the great tide-barrier on Aenean foundations; the yard where soldiers swear on
  the **Oath Stone**, a black slab salvaged from the drowned city two centuries ago.
- **The Terraces** — the old houses' gardens above the flood line; the council chamber (the Board of Moieties);
  the house where the defeated enemy king is kept, politely, as a hostage.
- **Lowtide** — at spring ebb the sea draws back through the Throat and the drowned Aenean streets emerge in the
  outer basin for a few hours: colonnades furred with weed, anemones in doorways, mudlarks, smugglers, and worse.

### The war

For eleven years Tessary fought the hill-kingdom of **Harrow** over the upper Mere's salt springs. It was losing.
Then, at **Harrow Ford**, King **Aldric of Harrow** dismounted in the middle of the battle, knelt in the river and
surrendered his sword to a Tessarine captain. Tessary calls it *the Kneeling*; there are songs, a saint's day, a
bad statue. The legion — **the Long Muster** — has come home. Eight thousand of them, into a city with an empty
treasury.

### The truth beneath

Under Lowtide, under the Aenean **Hall of Oaths**, is a sealed stair into the bedrock, and at its foot the
**Strongrooms**: a vault-complex of the Mathematical Age, white stone that hums, rooms whose floors weigh whoever
stands on them. At its heart is **the Balance**, an engine that makes sworn promises physically binding by
fastening them in the prima materium — a notary for the world. The Aenean city used it as its court (hence the
legend that an Aenean oath could not be broken). The Oath Stone in the Legion Yard is one of its terminals: every
soldier of the Long Muster who swore on it is registered.

The Balance is kept by **the Kept**, descendants of its Aenean clerks altered by a thousand years of proximity:
pale, long-lived, unable to lie, their skin faintly ruled like ledger paper. Each pledged their own name to the
Balance at birth as surety for a lifetime's exactness; they go by offices. A Kept who breaks a promise unravels
into one of the **Forsworn** — the hollow, promise-hungry things that haunt Lowtide, which were once Aenean
oath-breakers.

The Kept are not greedy. Their purpose is to keep the Balance's ledger even, because (in their tradition) the
Balance is one of the instruments by which the planes were fastened apart, and an uneven ledger loosens the
fastenings.

### The loan

Twelve years ago, losing, Tessary's Treasurer **Absalom Fenn** went down to the Strongrooms with a delegation of
the council. In the Balance's records was an oath sworn nine centuries before by Aldric's ancestor to the Aenean
emperors, whom Tessary claims to succeed: *my line shall kneel to yours.* It had never been called. The Kept lent
Tessary the right to call it. At Harrow Ford they did; Aldric felt his ancestor's oath close on him like a hand.

Calling an oath consumes weight from the ledger, which must be restored. The terms:

- **Collateral:** the registered oaths of the Long Muster.
- **Interest:** silver each month, carried below by sea — by Brack's ships, through Lowtide.
- **Principal, due at the Feast of Settlement in the twelfth year:** a kept promise of equal weight. In the Kept's
  accounting only a promise *of a whole people to a whole people* weighs as much. Tessary made exactly one: the
  enlistment promise, cried in every square, of **land and pension for every soldier who came home** — the
  Veterans' Settlement.

The treasury ran dry three months ago. Since then the Balance has taken interest in collateral: at each new moon a
dozen veterans, in roll order, hear the Muster's drum, set their boots neatly together at the tideline as soldiers
do, and walk into the sea toward the Hall of Oaths. They are not dead. They are held.

## The strategic power: Factor Ottilie Brack

Mistress of the Salt Moiety, owner of the pans, the ships that carried the interest silver, and the only person
outside the council and the Kept who understands the whole arrangement.

- **What she wants.** To hold the war note when Tessary defaults, and with it the oaths of the Long Muster.
  Commanding eight thousand bound soldiers, she would govern the city.
- **Why it is defensible.** She believes — with evidence — that the old houses started a stupid war, borrowed
  against their own soldiers, and will now abandon them to save money. Under her, she says, the veterans would be
  paid, fed and housed. She runs a hiring hall, *Brack's Muster*, that already gives neglected veterans work.
  Many love her.
- **Resources.** Salt money; warehouses; the Lowtide smuggling network (the *lamplighters*); a bought Watch
  captain; a scrivener-forger; one councillor in her pocket; a Kept broker who accepts bids at foreclosure;
  grateful veterans.
- **Who serves her and why.** Veterans (gratitude, wages), smugglers (pay), Captain Orrin Gale of the Watch
  (gambling debts), Councillor Dauntry (blackmail), her nephew and heir Lucan (family; doubts).
- **What stops her now.** The note is not due until the Feast. If the council votes the pensions, the promise is
  kept and the debt is paid. To bid at foreclosure she must pledge her own house to the Balance. She must be
  present in the Weighing Chamber.
- **What she does if nobody interferes.** Spreads that the vanished veterans are deserters and thieves; on the
  eve of the vote, provokes a veterans' riot at the Exchequer; the council votes the pensions down; the Feast
  passes; the Kept foreclose; she bids; the Muster's remaining oaths are hers; she calls them; they take the
  Weir; the council capitulates. Tessary is orderly, prosperous and owned.
- **What she misunderstands.** That binding is command. The soldiers swore *to keep the city*. The Balance
  reads oaths literally: the holder of the note can call them only to service as sworn. She can make them guard
  Tessary; she cannot make them take it — unless she *is* Tessary, which is why her plan needs the council's
  Seal as well as the note.

## Principal characters

- **The Ninth Assessor** ("Tally"). A Kept official sent up to inventory the collateral before foreclosure. Grey
  coat, ruled skin, perfect manners, appalled by surface speech ("You said you would be back *in a minute*. It
  has been forty-one. I have noted it."). The mudlark child Pim names them Tally. **The adventure's principal
  arc** — see below.
- **Sergeant Agathe Rook.** Long Muster, nineteen years' service, buried most of her company. Next in roll order:
  she will be called at the new moon. She believes in the oath — she swore it — and intends to go. Dry, profane,
  a terrible singer, the person who knows every veteran in the Sallows by name.
- **Absalom Fenn, Treasurer.** The man who signed. Kind, frightened, insomniac, drinking. Keeps a private list of
  every veteran called, with their families' addresses, and sends them money he does not have.
- **King Aldric of Harrow.** The Kneeling King, hostage in the Terraces. Witty, ruined, curious; he alone knows
  from inside what happened at the ford.
- **Pim Coyle.** Mudlark, ten, entrepreneur (genuine Aenean coins, lightly used). Brother of Private Tam Coyle,
  called last moon. Saw the soldiers walk into the sea in step.
- **Ama Coyle.** Eel-wife of the Sallows, Pim and Tam's mother; posted the notice that brings the protagonist.
- **Councillor Wend.** One-armed veteran, the council's pension advocate; Brack's obstacle.
- **The Comptroller.** Eldest of the Kept. Exact, ancient, ashamed of something.
- **The Registrar.** The Balance's own intelligence. Not a character so much as a weather.

## Movements

### I. The Homecoming (the city)

The protagonist arrives at the Landgate on the notice of a mother whose son walked into the sea. Tessary is in
festival mood for the returning Muster and in a sour mood about paying for it. Investigation in the wards:
Ama and Pim in the Sallows; the paired boots at the tideline; Rook at the Legion Yard; Brack's hiring hall;
Fenn at the Exchequer; a scrivener who copied a document he should not have; the queue at the Office of Seals;
a strange grey official taking an inventory of veterans by name. The first reversal: this is not crime. It is a
lawful collection, and the council knows.

### II. Lowtide (the drowned city)

A tidal dungeon open for a few hours at each spring ebb. Aenean streets, a flooded forum, the lamplighters'
smuggling stair, Forsworn in the deep doorways, the Hall of Oaths and its sealed stair. The tide is the clock: be
out, be up on a high place, or drown. Second reversal: Brack's lamplighters carried the interest silver down; she
is in correspondence with the Kept broker; she intends to buy the note.

### III. The Strongrooms (the creditors)

An alien society under the sea. Weighing floors that register every promise made on them; the Kept's archives;
the Comptroller; the Weighing Chamber and its balance the size of a nave. The protagonist learns the terms, can
seek an extension, can make registered promises (dangerous), and discovers that the called veterans are not dead
but held in **the Holding**. A deeper discovery waits in the archives for those who read: the drowning of Old
Tessary was itself a default — the Kept promised the Aenean city that the sea would not have it, and failed.

### IV. The Vote (the city in crisis)

Return to a city that has moved on without the protagonist. The council will vote on the Veterans' Settlement.
Brack's undertakings are in motion: rumour, bribery, a riot, an attempt on Wend. The protagonist can gather
evidence, persuade councillors, protect Wend, rally or calm veterans, expose the loan publicly (risking panic),
or steal the Seal. The vote and the Feast night branch the world.

### V. The Holding (where pledged things are kept)

A plane beyond the Balance: an endless white floor under no sky, where everything ever pledged and forfeit is held
— a kingdom's harvest in sheaves, a lake folded like linen, a woman's singing voice in a jar, the names of the
Kept on ten thousand tags, rank on rank of sleeping soldiers, and, far off, nine points of dark light which are
(the Registrar says) the hearts of the Varakiiri, held against the peace of the planes. Whatever happened at the
vote, the final scene is here: redeem the held, contest Brack's claim, read the clauses, bargain with the
Registrar.

## Endings (first sketch)

1. **The kept promise.** The pensions pass and are sealed before the Feast; the debt is discharged; the held come
   home; Brack is exposed. Tessary is poorer, and the old estates are broken up for veterans' land.
2. **The offset.** The Kept's own ancient default is proved and set against the city's debt. The Comptroller is
   shamed; the Kept's world is shaken.
3. **The Commons.** The veterans swear a new oath — to each other — on the Oath Stone, re-registering their oaths
   to a new oathee; the collateral the city pledged no longer belongs to the city. Tessary is left with a debt it
   cannot pay and a new power in its streets.
4. **The pledge.** The protagonist pledges something of their own to redeem particular people.
5. **The note sold.** Brack wins. The player may still contest her in the Holding.
6. **Foreclosure.** Nobody pays; Brack fails too; the held stay held; the city declines.

## The Assessor's arc

Begins exact, contemptuous of surface promise-breakers, curious despite itself. Watches the protagonist keep or
break small promises; watches Pim, Rook, Fenn. Confronts the contradiction in its own law: the city's debt to the
Kept is a promise, but so is the city's promise to its soldiers, and Brack is using the first to break the second.
Possible ends: returns below unchanged and collects; argues before the Comptroller for the precedence of the prior
promise (becomes a reformer); tears the note — breaks a promise — and unravels to save the held; or, if the
protagonist redeems its name in the Holding, chooses a surface life.

## Wonder, terror, comedy, quiet

- *Wonder:* Lowtide emerging; the weighing floors; the nave-sized balance; the Holding.
- *Terror:* the drum heard at night; Forsworn in the doorways as the tide turns; the riot.
- *Comedy:* the Office of Seals; Pim's coins; the Assessor's literalism; the Kneeling King's commentary; Rook's
  singing.
- *Quiet:* a supper in the Sallows; dawn at the Oath Stone; the ranks of the held asleep.
