# What Tessary Owes — revised design

*The design after the editorial review (`02-critique.md`), still written before reading the engine. Sections marked
**spine** must work end to end in every resolution; **depth** enriches; the implementation record
(`04-implementation.md`) says what was adapted when the design met Kyr.*

## The kernel, restated

A tidal city of paper and contracts won its war by calling in an enemy king's nine-hundred-year-old oath, which it
borrowed from the keepers of an ancient engine beneath the sea. It pledged its soldiers' oaths as collateral. The
principal falls due at the Feast of Settlement and can only be repaid by a kept promise of equal weight — and the
only such promise Tessary ever made is the one it made to those same soldiers. It cannot afford to keep it. The
salt-factor who carried the interest down wants the city to default so she can buy the note and own the soldiers.
The engine's eldest keeper wants the city to default so the soldiers stay forfeit and fill a hole he made in the
ledger eleven hundred years ago, when the sea took the city he had sworn to protect.

The protagonist comes because a mother posted a notice about her son.

## Shape: five movements, one clock

The adventure runs over a fortnight measured by **spring ebbs**, the very low tides when the drowned city emerges
and the engine takes its interest: the first night (the protagonist arrives in time to see it), the eighth
(Sergeant Rook's turn), and the fifteenth — **the Feast of Settlement**, when the note matures.

| Movement | Register | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| I. The Homecoming | city comedy, unease, investigation | The vanishings are a lawful collection and the council knows. |
| II. Lowtide | tidal exploration, danger, marvel | Brack carried the interest; Brack means to buy the note. |
| III. The Strongrooms | alien society, negotiation, reading | The held are alive. The Comptroller is an ally — then the author of the trap. |
| IV. The Feast Approaching | political crisis, action, loyalty | The city chooses (or fails to choose) whether to keep its promise; Brack moves. |
| V. The Holding | wonder, quiet, the final bargain | The promise is delivered, released, voided, sold or forfeited. |

Movements are not gates. The protagonist may reach Lowtide on the first day if Pim takes them, or never enter the
Strongrooms and still win the Release in the city. The *spine* is the set of facts any route must be able to
establish.

## Movement I — The Homecoming (spine)

**Arrival.** From the protagonist's own country a march road leads east to the delta; an innkeeper speaks of
Tessary on the Mere, where the legions have come home and soldiers walk into the sea, and of an eel-wife's notice
offering her savings for news of her son. The road ends at the **Causeway** over the salt-marsh and the
**Landgate**, where the notice is pinned among bills for the Feast.

**The wards.**

- **The Sallows** — stilt-tenements; **Ama Coyle**'s house; the **Drowned Drummer**, where veterans drink and
  Rook holds court; the **Tideline Steps**, where called veterans leave their boots paired.
- **Saltgate** — the **Pans**; **Brack's Countinghouse**; her **warehouse** (whose cistern hides the lamplighters'
  dry stair into Lowtide); **Brack's Muster**, the hiring hall.
- **The Lettered Quarter** — **Scriveners' Row**; the **Office of Seals** and its legendary queue; the
  **Exchequer**, where **Fenn** sits up all night.
- **The Weir** — the tide-barrier walk; the **Legion Yard** and the **Oath Stone**; the **Throat Stair** down to
  Lowtide.
- **The Terraces** — the **Council House** of the Board of Moieties; **Aldric's** courteous prison; **Wend's**
  house.

**First night: the calling.** Pim, if the protagonist has found the Coyles, takes them to the Tideline Steps at
the spring ebb. Twelve veterans come down through the Sallows in step, barefoot, eyes open, set their boots
together on the top step and walk into the sea. One of them is someone the protagonist spoke to that afternoon. A
grey figure stands on the steps with a ledger, ticking names. This is the Assessor.

**Investigation threads** (any order, each yielding a fact):

- *Who goes, and in what order?* — Rook knows the names; the **Assessor's inventory** is in roll order.
- *Where do they go?* — Pim's account; the boots; the Throat; the Hall of Oaths in Lowtide.
- *Who knows?* — Fenn, whose private list of the called sits on his desk with their families' addresses.
- *Why now?* — the treasury ran dry three months ago; the silver stopped; the engine began taking interest in
  people. A scrivener on Scriveners' Row copied the **Instrument of the War Note** for the council and kept his
  draft.
- *Who benefits?* — Brack, who hires the protagonist on day one to find out "who is taking my veterans".

**The reversal.** By the end of the movement the protagonist can know that the vanishings are lawful, that the
council knows, and that the Grey Clerk is collecting a debt.

## Movement II — Lowtide (spine)

**Access.** The **Throat Stair** at the Weir, open at low water; the lamplighters' **cistern stair** under Brack's
warehouse, always open and guarded.

**The tide.** Every Lowtide place states the water. Low water gives some hours; the turn is announced; the flood
is fatal only to the stubborn: someone caught is swept out through the Throat and washed up on the Pans, hurt and
lighter. Pim's tide-tables (bought, or earned) make the timing exact. High places are refuges.

**Places.** The **Weed Colonnade**; the **Drowned Forum** (the Aenean market-square, anemones in the doorways,
Pim's diggings); the **Street of Scales**; the **Bath-house** whose dome is a refuge and whose plunge-room is
Forsworn ground; the **Aqueduct Arch**; the **Lamplighters' Landing**, where Mother Ness's crew moves boxes; the
**Hall of Oaths**, an Aenean court, its walls carved with the city's compact with the Kept — *the sea shall not
have this city while the ledger keeps* — and its **sealed stair** down.

**The Forsworn.** Hollow people, Aenean oath-breakers whose bindings unravelled; drawn to anyone who has broken a
registered promise, and to the sound of swearing. Avoidable, fightable, dreadful.

**The reversal.** The lamplighters' tally-boards record the silver they carried down for years; a letter from
Brack to a Kept broker names a bid at foreclosure. Brack is not looking for who takes her veterans. She is waiting
to buy them.

## Movement III — The Strongrooms (spine)

**The way in.** The sealed stair opens to a Kept token (the Assessor's), to the lamplighters' key, or to someone
who knocks and states their business in a form the Kept accept.

**Places.** The **Long Stair**; the **Antechamber of Weights**, the first weighing floor, which registers what is
said on it; the **Hall of Clerks**; the **Archive of Instruments** and its locked **Room of Defaults**; the
**Dormitory of the Kept**, where they sleep standing; the **Comptroller's Office**; the **Counterweight
Gallery**, whose doors open when the pans are balanced (a mechanism the protagonist can work out); the **Weighing
Chamber**, where the Balance hangs like a ship in a nave; the **Door of the Holding**.

**The Weights.** Stone bailiffs that seize the forsworn and defaulters. They do not attack the honest.

**What the protagonist can learn and do.**

- The terms of the note, from the Instrument itself.
- That the held are not dead.
- From the Comptroller: an extension for Rook, at the price of a registered promise; and his "alliance" against
  Brack, whose broker he would suspend if given evidence.
- In the Room of Defaults: the drowned-city compact, its failure recorded in the Comptroller's own hand, and a
  marginal calculation — eight thousand oaths, in his hand, against the hole. **The loan was designed to fail.**

**The Assessor's turn.** Shown the Room of Defaults, the Assessor understands that their Comptroller drafted a
promise in order that it be broken. They can file an indictment before the Assembly of the Kept, which is the
gravest thing a Kept can do. Whether they do depends on what they have witnessed and whether the protagonist has
kept faith with them.

## Movement IV — The Feast Approaching (spine)

The city has moved while the protagonist was below.

**Brack's undertakings**, each running on its own days and each with consequences:

1. *The whisper* (from day 2): the vanished are deserters and thieves. Councillors harden.
2. *Rook's silver* (day 5): Brack offers to pay Rook's interest for her service.
3. *The second councillor* (day 9): Councillor Pell is bought unless warned.
4. *The attempt on Wend* (night 11), if the vote looks likely to pass.
5. *The riot* (night 12): her men among the veterans at the Exchequer; if it happens, the vote fails.
6. *The vote* (day 13).
7. *The Seal* (night 13), if the vote passed: stolen from the Office of Seals so the deeds cannot be sealed.
8. *The bid* (day 14): her house pledged to the Balance.
9. *The Feast* (night 15): Brack in the Weighing Chamber to take the note at foreclosure.

**The Comptroller's undertakings.** He holds the city to the letter: deeds must reach every living veteran,
including the held, by the Feast's end. If he learns of Brack's bid, he suspends her broker (she then uses the
cistern stair and goes armed). If exposed, he tries to buy silence with the release of one held person.

**What the protagonist can do.** Bring Aldric to testify. Bring Fenn to confess (or fail to, and find him at the
Throat). Guard Wend. Warn Pell. Turn the riot or calm it. Guard the Seal. Rally the veterans at the Oath Stone for
the Release. Put evidence before the council, before Brack, before the Comptroller — each with a different reaction.

## Movement V — The Holding (spine)

Beyond the Door: an endless pale floor under no sky, every forfeit thing in rows with its tag — a kingdom's harvest
in sheaves, a lake folded like linen, a singer's voice in a jar, the Kept's names on ten thousand tags, a
three-hundred-year-old wagered goose who has opinions, the **Ranks** of the held Muster asleep on their feet, and,
very far off, nine points of dark light which the Kept say are what the Kiritarun tally. The **Registrar** keeps a
desk here.

Whatever brought the protagonist (deeds to deliver, Brack to contest, Rook or Tam to redeem), the movement ends at
the Registrar's desk or in the Ranks.

## Resolutions (spine)

| Resolution | Prepared by | Tessary afterward |
|---|---|---|
| **The Kept Promise** | Vote passed; Seal kept; deeds carried into the Holding and delivered | The held come home. The old estates are broken for veterans' land; the city is poorer and keeps faith. |
| **The Release** | Rook free; veterans gathered at the Oath Stone on the Feast night; Brack's men countered | The Muster releases the city from its promise; the ledger balances; the held return. The soldiers saved the city again and got nothing; they now owe it nothing either. |
| **The Offset** | Both halves of the compact found; the Assessor files; the Assembly convenes | The note is void as a promise made to be broken. The held return. The Comptroller unravels or is deposed; the Kept, led by the Assessor, must face the old hole honestly. |
| **Brack's Tessary** | Default; Brack's bid lodged | Brack holds the note and calls the Muster. If she has the Seal she governs; if not, she owns soldiers who will only guard the city — a stalemate. A compromise is possible: a registered promise from Brack, extracted in the Holding. |
| **Foreclosure** | Default; no buyer | The held stay held; the ledger is uneven; the fastening at Tessary thins and the sea in Lowtide sometimes shows other stars. |

**The pledge** (depth): in the Holding the protagonist may pledge something of their own to redeem one person.

## Characters and their possible ends (spine unless marked)

- **The Ninth Assessor ("Tally").** *Reformer* (filed the indictment) · *Unravelled* (tore the note to save the
  held) · *Freed* (their name redeemed in the Holding; a notary's stall in the Lettered Quarter) · *Unchanged*.
- **Sergeant Agathe Rook.** *Held* (answered the drum; can be woken) · *Leader of the Release* · *Brack's
  sergeant* · *Against both* (having seen the fraud).
- **Factor Ottilie Brack.** *Triumphant* · *Bound by her own pledge* · *Exposed and ruined* · *Compromised*.
- **Absalom Fenn.** *Confessed* · *Fled* · *Drowned* (if abandoned) · *Pledged* (depth).
- **King Aldric.** *Testified, sent home* · *Still a hostage*.
- **Pim and Ama Coyle.** Tam comes home, or doesn't.
- **The Comptroller.** *Unravelled* · *Deposed* · *Unchallenged*.

## Contributions for canon review

These are statements the adventure's people make, not narrator facts for every adventure:

- **The Balance**: a Mathematical Age engine that makes sworn promises binding in the prima materium; the Kept say
  it is one of the instruments by which the planes were fastened apart.
- **The Kept**: Aenean clerks' descendants, unable to lie, their names pledged at birth.
- **The Holding**: a plane where pledged and forfeit things are held against their promises.
- **The Kiritarun as tallies**: the Kept tradition that the nine jewels are tally-sticks of a pledge whose
  security is held in the Holding.
- **Forsworn**: what a registered oath-breaker becomes.

## What is cut from the first treatment

The Commons (replaced by the Release); the pledge as an ending; most named councillors (Dauntry and Pell remain as
numbers on Brack's ledger and one scene each); Lucan Brack (depth only); the Settlement Fields upriver (described
in endings, not visited).
