# What Tessary Owes, played: release (seed tessary)

Each step as the page tells it: the choice taken, then what the page said. Waits that told nothing are left out.

## day1

**To the Landgate** — You set out from the Causeway early, with your shadow long in front of you on the road. Inside the gate is a three-cornered square of trodden shell, loud with carts. A board on the gatehouse wall is shingled with bills, three deep: the Feast of Settlement, a fortnight hence; a reward for a stolen goose; rooms; a hiring hall in Panhythe that pays veterans by the day. In the corner a little chapel to Saint Evenhand has a pair of brass scales over its door. Somebody has polished the scales. Red bunting for the Muster’s homecoming is still up over the gate, a week old and sagging. Under it a man with a bucket is pasting a new bill over the old: THE BOARD OF MOIETIES WILL HEAR THE QUESTION OF THE VETERANS’ SETTLEMENT ON THE THIRTEENTH DAY.

**To the Withies** — The Withies stand on piles over a backwater of the Ossel: tarred houses on stilts, joined by boardwalks that sway, every one with a ladder down to a boat and a row of eel-traps drying on the rail. Washing hangs between the houses. So do red coats, a good many of them, airing. A girl is mending a net with a soldier’s bayonet. Here are Wick Teazle, a mudlark, about ten, with a tray on a string; and the Ninth Valuer, a clerk in a grey coat with a ledger, whose skin is faintly ruled like paper.

**Into the Muffled Drum** — A low taproom built out over the backwater, so that the floor moves a little when the tide is running. Soldiers’ coats on every peg, soldiers’ boots under every bench. Over the bar hangs a regimental drum with a black cloth tied over its head. It is loud in here, and the noise is the noise of men who have nothing to do: dice, an argument about pay, someone telling the story of Sedge Ford for the hundredth time and getting it wrong. Here are Hannelore Rackham, who keeps the Muffled Drum; Thomasin Durrance, a sergeant of the Long Muster, out of uniform; and Benno Fisk, a corporal of the Long Muster, in a new coat.

**Ask Durrance about the soldiers who have gone into the sea** — ‘Third Company,’ says Durrance. ‘All of them. Pargeter, Haskett, Moll Bryer, the Lucey twins, Davy Teazle. And in order. You know what order? Roll order. Alphabet by platoon, the way the paymaster calls it.’ She drinks. ‘I’ve had nineteen years of roll calls. Somebody is calling the roll, and it isn’t anybody in this city. Next spring ebb it’s Ruddock, Saye, Tamsett, Oddie, Amsel. And then it’s a page turn, and the top of the next page is me.’

**Ask Durrance what the oath on the Stone says** — Durrance doesn’t have to think. ‘’I swear to keep the city of Tessary, its walls and its people, while I live or until it release me.’’ She shrugs. ‘Hand on the Stone, say it once, the recruiting sergeant slaps your back and you’re the city’s. Nobody listens to the words. I listened. I was that kind of recruit.’ A short laugh. ‘Until it release me. It never has. I’ve never heard of anyone it did.’

**Ask Hannelore Rackham about the drum over the bar** — ‘The Third Company’s,’ says Hannelore. ‘Thomasin brought it home. She tied the cloth over it herself. Nobody plays it till the last of the Third comes home, she says, and since the summer that’s been looking like never.’ She wipes the bar. ‘Don’t touch it. I mean that kindly.’

**Out to the Withies** — The Withies stand on piles over a backwater of the Ossel: tarred houses on stilts, joined by boardwalks that sway, every one with a ladder down to a boat and a row of eel-traps drying on the rail. Washing hangs between the houses. So do red coats, a good many of them, airing. A girl is mending a net with a soldier’s bayonet. Here are Wick Teazle, a mudlark, about ten, with a tray on a string; and the Ninth Valuer, a clerk in a grey coat with a ledger, whose skin is faintly ruled like paper.

**To the Watersteps** — Broad stone steps go down from the end of the boardwalk into the sea, twenty of them above the high-water mark and nobody knows how many below. They are older than everything around them. The stone is cut with something like letters, worn to dimples. The water stands high on the steps, slopping and green.

**Wait › An hour** — They come down through the Withies a little after two, twelve of them, in step, though nobody is calling the step. Barefoot. Their eyes are open. At the top of the Watersteps each of them stops, and stoops, and sets his boots side by side on the stone, toes to the water, and straightens, and walks down into the sea. The water is very low. They walk out over the sand toward the walls in the basin, getting smaller, keeping step, until the dark has them. It is a long time since anyone slept. The company is weary, and will fight the worse for it until it sleeps.

**Ask the Valuer where the soldiers you saw walk into the sea have gone** — The Valuer considers whether this is the affair of a debtor, and decides, visibly, that what you saw with your own eyes is your own affair. ‘Into the Holding,’ they say, ‘where pledged things are kept. They are not harmed. They are held. They stand asleep with the rest of what has been pledged and forfeit, and they do not age.’ A pause. ‘You are distressed. I am sorry. It is a lawful collection.’ Ivo gains 250 experience: found out where the called go.

**Ask the Valuer what would bring the called home** — ‘The discharge of the debt they stand surety for,’ says the Valuer, as if reading it. ‘A debt is discharged in two ways. It is kept, or it is released: if the one to whom a promise was made says freely to the one who made it, I hold you quit, that weighs in the Balance exactly as the keeping does.’ A pause. ‘People forget the second. Nobody releases anybody. I have often wondered why.’

## day2

**To the head of the Swatch Stair** — At the west end of the Tidewall the wall-walk breaks off at a stair that goes straight down the face of the wall into the basin. A chain hangs beside it for a handrail. Below, the Swatch: the one deep channel through which the outer basin fills and empties twice a day, with a noise like a slow mill. The basin is out. Below the stair lies a city of wet stone: roofs, a colonnade, the dome of something, streets with weed lying flat in them like combed hair. Figures are moving down there, small, bent over, looking for things.

**To the foot of the Swatch Stair** — The stair comes down the face of the Tidewall into a gully of wet stone and kelp. The noise of the Swatch is very loud here: the basin draining past you through the one deep channel, brown and quick. Ahead, through a gap in the old sea-wall, a street begins.

**To the Weed Colonnade** — A street of columns, their tops lost, their shafts furred with weed that lies flat in the direction the water went. Between the columns, doorways, full of sand to the lintel. Anemones the colour of raw meat have opened in the wet, and close if you go near.

**To the Drowned Forum** — The old city’s market square, a hundred paces across, paved in great slabs, most of them still there. The fountain in the middle is a heap of stone with a crab on it. On every side the fronts of buildings stand roofless, their windows full of sky. At the south end, broad steps go down to a building with no windows at all. Mudlarks are working the square with buckets and rakes, children mostly, and a few old men who have been children here for sixty years. They lift their heads to look at you, and go back to work. Wick is up to his knees in a drain at the edge of the square with a rake, a bucket and an expression of total professional absorption.

**To the Street of Scales** — A street of shops, by the look of it: every doorway has a stone counter, and every counter a pair of carved scales. Eleven hundred years have not worn the scales off. At the far end, a quay stands up out of the sand, higher than the street, with steps.

**To the ebb-runners' landing** — A woman in a smock stands up off a crate with a cutlass in her hand and puts her other hand up, palm out. ‘That’s far enough,’ she says. ‘This is a working quay.’ Mistress Plum complains about you to the others. A stone quay at the end of the Street of Scales, high enough that the flood laps its top and goes no further. Crates, coils of rope, a brazier, a long boat drawn up on rollers. A low tunnel goes back into the quay wall, toward the city, with lamps on hooks along it. On a post by the tunnel mouth hangs a board scored with notches. In a crate by the brazier a big white goose is hissing steadily at the ebb-runners, who are ignoring it. A ribbon round its neck says PLUM in gold thread. An iron-bound lockbox is chained to the boat’s thwart. Here are Bettany Slake, who runs the ebb-runners, in a fisherman’s smock with a cutlass through the belt; and the ebb-runners, three of Bettany Slake’s people, with hooks and short clubs.

**Knock the peg out of the goose's crate** — The peg comes out and the crate door falls open and Mistress Plum comes out of it like a cannonball, wings out, neck out, screaming, and goes straight for the nearest ebb-runner’s ankles. In a moment the whole quay is chasing a goose round the brazier and the goose is winning. Nobody is looking at you at all. Mistress Plum stands on a bollard with her wings half open, daring anyone to try it.

**Pick the lockbox** — The lockbox stands open.

**Take from the ebb-runners' lockbox** — Ivo takes a letter in the Factor’s hand from the ebb-runners’ lockbox.

**Break the wax and read the Factor's letter** — It is addressed to the Broker of the Fourth Pan, the Strongrooms, by hand of B. Slake. ‘Sir. You will have had my earlier. I repeat my offer: when the city’s note falls in at the Feast and is offered at foreclosure, I bid for it, and I will pledge House Sault entire as surety, which your Comptroller will find to weigh what it needs to. The Board will not pay; I have seen to that, and if I had not, the treasury would. I will take up the note and with it the Muster. They will be fed and paid and housed. Tessary will be governed by someone who can count. Burn this. I. Sault, Factor.’ Ivo gains 300 experience: read the Factor’s letter.

**To the Watersteps** — Broad stone steps go down from the end of the boardwalk into the sea, twenty of them above the high-water mark and nobody knows how many below. They are older than everything around them. The stone is cut with something like letters, worn to dimples. The sea has drawn back a long way, and the steps go down and down into weed and wet sand, and out there in the grey, very low, the tops of walls.

**To the Withies** — The Withies stand on piles over a backwater of the Ossel: tarred houses on stilts, joined by boardwalks that sway, every one with a ladder down to a boat and a row of eel-traps drying on the rail. Washing hangs between the houses. So do red coats, a good many of them, airing. A girl is mending a net with a soldier’s bayonet. The Ninth Valuer, a clerk in a grey coat with a ledger, whose skin is faintly ruled like paper, is here.

**Into the Muffled Drum** — A low taproom built out over the backwater, so that the floor moves a little when the tide is running. Soldiers’ coats on every peg, soldiers’ boots under every bench. Over the bar hangs a regimental drum with a black cloth tied over its head. It is loud in here, and the noise is the noise of men who have nothing to do: dice, an argument about pay, someone telling the story of Sedge Ford for the hundredth time and getting it wrong. Here are Hannelore Rackham, who keeps the Muffled Drum; Thomasin Durrance, a sergeant of the Long Muster, out of uniform; and Benno Fisk, a corporal of the Long Muster, in a new coat.

**Tell Fisk what the Factor means to do with the Muster** — Fisk’s grin goes. He reads it twice in your face. ‘Own us,’ he says. ‘With a note. Like a debt.’ He takes the new coat off and looks at it. ‘She knew my name before I knew hers.’ He drops the coat on the floor. ‘There’s a thing she wants done the night before the Board sits. Lads up the Terrace Steps with torches, shouting. She said it’d show the Board what eight thousand angry soldiers look like.’ He shakes his head. ‘Not me. Not now. And I’ll tell the others why.’

**Tell Durrance what the Factor means to do with the note** — ‘Govern,’ says Durrance. ‘With us.’ She puts her pot down carefully. ‘Not pay us. Own us. Call us out of wherever we are and stand us on the Tidewall in her coats.’ Her face does not change. ‘She’d feed us. She’d be better than the Board. I’d still be a cow.’

## night7

**Wait › An hour** — An hour passes. ‘The Factor sent for me,’ says Durrance. ‘Offered to pay the silver for the next ebb out of her own pocket, all twelve of us, if I’d sergeant her Muster after.’ She turns her pot. ‘I told her I’d sworn already. Once is enough for anyone.’

**Wait › An hour** — Durrance stands up. She does it all at once, the way a soldier stands when an officer comes in, and her pot goes over and she does not look at it. Her eyes are open. She is listening to something. Then she bends and begins, very neatly, to unlace her boots.

**Pull the cloth off the Third Company's drum and beat the stand-down** — The drum is very loud in the low room. Rap, rap, and the long roll, and rap: the stand-down, that every soldier of the Muster has heard at the end of every day for eleven years. Durrance stops with one boot off. All over the Withies, in the stilt-houses, you hear other things stop: a door, a ladder, someone on the boardwalk. Durrance sits down, hard, on the floor, and looks at the boot in her hand as if she has never seen it. The Third Company’s drum hangs over the bar with its head bare.

**Tell Durrance that a promise can be discharged by release** — ‘Released,’ says Durrance slowly. ‘If the one it was made to says, I hold you quit.’ She is looking at the drum. ‘The city promised us land and pension. That’s the promise in their scales. And it’s ours. It was made to us.’ Her hand closes. ‘So if we let them off it. If the Muster stood on the Stone and said, we hold you quit, keep your land...’ She stops. ‘Then the debt’s paid. With our pensions. And the lads come home.’ She laughs, not happily. ‘Eleven years, and we save the city twice, and the second time we pay for it. Let me think. Don’t ask me today. Let me think.’

## feast

**Wait › An hour** — In the morning there are twelve pairs of boots on the Watersteps. The eleventh pair is small, and very new, and belongs to Robin Elcock, who was a drummer, and was nineteen. Ivo gains 400 experience: kept Sergeant Durrance from the sea.

**Wait › An hour** — Word goes along the Tidewall in the morning: the Treasurer went down the Swatch Stair before dawn, into the full basin, with his coat buttoned. The fishermen brought him up at the ebb. In his coat was a list of a hundred and thirty-eight names.

**Wait › An hour** — Word comes up from the Terraces in the morning: Councillor Marrable was beaten in her own house in the night, by men nobody saw the faces of. She is alive. She will not be at the Board on the thirteenth day.

**Wait › An hour** — An hour passes. Ivo gains 300 experience: kept the Muster off the Terraces.

**Wait › An hour** — At two in the afternoon the Board of Moieties votes on the Veterans’ Settlement, and the Council House bell does not ring. The question is lost. There is no money, says the Speaker’s notice on the Landgate board, and the city will not make promises it cannot keep. Under it, by evening, someone has pasted the old recruiting bill: LAND AND A PENSION FOR EVERY MAN WHO COMES HOME.

**Ask Durrance to call the Muster to the Oath Stone on the night of the Feast, to release the city** — Durrance has had her days to think, and she has thought. ‘Eleven years,’ she says. ‘And the Board votes no, or yes and then lets the Factor walk off with the Seal, and either way the lads stay under the sea.’ She stands up. ‘So we do it. The Muster on the Stone at the Feast, as many as I can get, and we say it: we hold you quit. Keep your land. Keep your pension. Give us back our own.’ She laughs, short. ‘I’ll have to tell them it was my idea. They’ll never stand for it if it was a stranger’s.’ She looks at you. ‘The Factor will send her lightermen. Be there.’

**Out to the Withies** — Wick Teazle, a mudlark, about ten, with a tray on a string, is here. Lamps in the stilt-houses, and the backwater black under the boardwalks. Somebody is singing a marching song very slowly in one of the houses, and stops, and starts again.

**To the Landgate** — On the board, the Speaker’s notice: the question of the Veterans’ Settlement is lost, the city having no money. Pasted over half of it, the old recruiting bill: LAND AND A PENSION FOR EVERY MAN WHO COMES HOME. The square is empty but for a watchman with a lantern and a cat on the bill-board. The bills move a little in the wind off the sea.

**To Scriveners' Row** — The stalls are shuttered and the paper taken in. The Office of Seals is dark. In the Exchequer one window is lit, as it is every night.

**To the Tidewall** — The Tidewall runs the whole length of the city between the houses and the sea, broad enough on top for two carts. Its lower courses are not the city’s: great blocks, tight-fitted, with no mortar, carved at intervals with a pair of scales. The city built its wall on top of someone else’s. On the landward side a stair goes down into the Lettered Quarter.

**To the Legion Yard** — A parade ground of beaten shell inside the Tidewall, ringed with empty barracks. In the middle of it, on a plinth, stands the Oath Stone: a slab of black stone as tall as a man, salvaged from the drowned city two hundred years ago, smooth as a hearthstone where eleven thousand hands have been laid on it. Cut into its face, very small and very fine, are lines that are not writing and not ornament: ruled lines, like a ledger’s.

**Stand with the Muster at the Oath Stone while they say it** — Durrance says it first, alone, with her hand on the Stone, in a voice that carries to the back of the Yard without being raised: ‘The city of Tessary promised me land and a pension if I came home. I came home. I hold it quit.’ Then the Third Company says it, ragged, and then the Yard, all of it, eight thousand voices or near enough, not together, a long rolling noise like surf: I hold it quit. And under your feet, under the Yard, under the Tidewall and the drowned streets, something very large and very far down settles by a hair’s breadth, like a pan of a scale coming level. Ivo gains 800 experience: stood with the Muster at the Oath Stone.

**Wait › An hour** — At two in the morning, all over the Withies, people wake for no reason and go out onto the boardwalks. The basin is out, lower than anyone has ever seen it. And out in the drowned city, very small, coming up the steps of the Hall of Oaths and across the Forum in the starlight, barefoot and in their shirts and in step, a hundred and sixty soldiers of the Long Muster are walking home.

**Wait › An hour** — Word runs along the Withies faster than the tide: Davy Teazle woke up on the Watersteps in his shirt with his boots beside him, and walked home, and put his boots on the mat inside his mother’s door, side by side. Ivo gains 300 experience: brought Davy Teazle home.

**Wait › A while** — Time passes. The Ninth Valuer went below at the Settlement with the inventory complete. The children of the Withies still call the stair at the end of the boardwalk Docket’s Step, though none of them can say why. Thomasin Durrance never takes a penny of the pension she released. She runs the Muffled Drum with Hannelore now, and the Third Company’s drum hangs over the bar with its head bare, and on the night of every spring ebb she beats the stand-down on it, at two in the morning, for anyone who might be listening. Brother Ancel hangs a weight in Saint Evenhand’s for Josiah Quire: lead, for a debt. It is the only one in the chapel nobody paid for. The Muster saved the city twice. The second time it paid for the privilege with every penny the city had promised it, and the city knows it, and does not know what to do with the knowledge. The Board votes the Muster a medal. The Muster does not come to collect it. Instead, the veterans of Tessary go to work, on the pans and the boats and in the Lettered Quarter, owing nobody anything, and when the Board next wants a war it finds that nobody in the Withies will answer a drum, and has to think of something else.
