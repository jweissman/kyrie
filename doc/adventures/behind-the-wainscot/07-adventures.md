# 07 — The smaller adventures

Each is listed with its person, what is unknown or disputed, the ways at it, its turn, what it changes, and how it
touches the principal story. "Built" means written in Kyr and offered in play; "tested" names the journey that plays
it (`test/hollins.test.ts`, fixtures in `test/fixtures/hollins`).

## 1. The Ratcatcher's wages (Bootscrape; the Pantry) — practical, satirical
- **Who:** Mrs Pyecroft, who pays two crowns a day and twopence a tail; Hob Ludd, who sells rat-tails by the string
  and has sold some of them six times; Hasp Tallowby, who has real rats.
- **Unknown:** what the "vermin" are; whether the job can be done honestly.
- **Ways:** take the job (wages, a bag of traps and arsenic, licence to go about the house); buy Hob's tails and sell
  them to her; kill the real rats in the back pantry (a real fight) for Hasp's pay and honey and the Jarmen's
  dumbwaiter; or tell her where they nest (ten coin, and a Grave).
- **Turn:** the job is, after all, partly real: the Jarmen want the rats gone too.
- **Changes:** telling the nest brings the Smoking forward to that night and poisons the skirting; the Court bars its
  door with RATCATCHER on a sheet; the ending is The Ratcatcher. Springing her traps in front of her makes her
  suspicious; she keeps a little book.
- **Main story:** the hook, and the protagonist's first and last chance to be at fault. *Built; tested (rats,
  ratcatcher).*

## 2. The Hearth (the drawing-room grate) — practical, tragic
- **Who:** Bet Collier and eighty-three colliers whose grandparents fled a flooded pit.
- **Unknown:** whether the fire laid on the irons will be lit.
- **Ways:** hear it from Mrs Pyecroft herself (if hired) and warn Bet outright; tell Bet the Lady has had a fire every
  night in her own room (an easy persuasion, if you asked Jory); urge her on nothing but a stranger's nerves (fair,
  one try).
- **Turn:** at nine on the second morning the fire is lit whatever anyone does; the only question is who is in it.
- **Changes:** eleven dead and Bet among them, or none; refugees on the Skirting; the colliers' fury (ladyCursed),
  which gives the Jarl strong arms; the Keys lose or keep their bass; the Moot's first matter.
- **Main story:** the first thing the Lady does to her house that she does not know she is doing. *Built; tested.*

## 3. Kit and Nan (the Bell Walk; the Small House) — romantic, comic
- **Who:** Kit Lacey, heir of the Small House, who writes like a book; Nan Collier, who writes like a collier. Two
  years of notes tied to the servants' bell-wires; never in a room together.
- **Unknown:** what each looks like; whether either will be ashamed.
- **Ways:** Nan tells you what she is writing before the fire; a note on the Nursery wire after it (TWICE); Mab Tenter
  knows; bring Nan up the Stair to the Small House.
- **Turn:** "You're taller than your writing." Dame Honor sees, and shuts the parlour door.
- **Changes:** at the Moot Kit stands up for the first time in his life and carries the colliers into the Small House;
  they marry in the spring under the WELCOME.
- **Main story:** decides the Moot's first matter. *Built; the meeting is played in development runs, not yet in a
  kept journey.*

## 4. Ten Past Four (the Long Clock) — theological comedy
- **Who:** Prior Tobit Hale, who kept the clock clean and unwound for two hundred years and wants only for her to know
  it; Sister Verity Cogg, who believes that if the Day of the Going is over the Child can never come home.
- **Unknown:** what the Child meant by stopping the clock.
- **Ways:** read Verity the last page of the diary; argue it with her (insight, hard); sit up in the works on the third
  night and catch her with the pin; let her jam it and free the strike train yourself (mending).
- **Turn:** "When I come home." It is today.
- **Changes:** a jammed clock is found by the Lady at dusk; Mrs Pyecroft sees to the "mice in the works" with a trap in
  the Winders' chapel, which takes the Prior on the fifth day unless sprung. Told to the Lady, the Winders are given
  the key: they wind it every Sunday after. If she never knew, they stop it at the minute she died.
- **Main story:** the clock is the Lady's childhood, and the hour of her tea. *Built; tested (verity).*

## 5. The Slow Concert (the piano) — artistic
- **Who:** Ludovic Flatt and the Guild of the Keys, who have played the Child's nursery song a note a minute for a
  hundred Midwinters and never finished it.
- **Unknown:** where the last page went.
- **Ways:** the Post has it, posted by the Guild's founder to himself ninety-one years ago, postage unpaid: pay Faith
  Penny three coin, or argue that a letter to oneself was never posted (hard). The bass needs weight: the colliers
  (Bet, if she lived; Nan, if she did not). Then an hour on the keys at night with a performance check.
- **Turn:** the Lady comes down in her nightgown with her candle and plays the last eleven notes herself. "Honor,
  Ned. Are you there?"
- **Changes:** two steps toward her believing; the Keys play again under her deathbed.
- **Main story:** one of the four ways to reach her. *Built; tested (bard).*

## 6. The Undelivered (the Letterdrift; the moor) — elegiac
- **Who:** Faith Penny, sworn to deliver; Corwen Ashgrove, who promised to wait at the stone on the moor; the Lady.
- **Unknown:** what is in the letter (the company may break the seal and read it; the Post will know).
- **Ways:** carry it to her chart or her bedside; she reads it, and the next morning leaves a reply addressed to
  whoever brings her letters; carry that up the moor to the Waiting Stone.
- **Turn:** the next dusk the paper is gone, the lichen on the stone's house-ward side is broken in a long fresh line,
  and the flint is lying on a single dried flower. Nobody says it moved.
- **Changes:** one step toward her believing; the Post's oath kept; the stone, in the epilogue, no longer waiting.
- **Main story:** the Lady's one love, and the only thing in the adventure that goes outside the house. *Built;
  tested (stone).*

## 7. Belling the Cat (the Moot; the counterpane) — fable, horror
- **Who:** Mab Tenter, whose son the cat played with until he died; the Moot, which agrees unanimously that the cat
  be belled and then finds the carpet very interesting.
- **Ways:** volunteer at the Moot; take the silver sleigh bell from the toy box; climb onto the bed by day and tie it
  to the collar of eighteen feet of sleeping cat (stealth, hard, Grave; failure is to be batted across the counterpane
  like a cork and dropped off the bed). Or keep out of the open after nine; or throw catmint from the kitchen garden in
  front of him; or tell the Lady, who says he lost his bell in the carriage and has it in her reticule.
- **Turn:** the comic deflation. All that courage, and she just puts it on him.
- **Changes:** belled, he still prowls but the Wainscot hears him and steps aside: the ground floor is safe at night.
  Six of the Court's buttons, and the Nail's stories.
- *Built; tested (thief, ranger, freehold).*

## 8. The Tin Regiment (the Fort) — demobilisation
- **Who:** Lance-Corporal Fennick, the winder, sitting in the Noah's ark; Captain Ordinance, whose orders from the
  Colonel are to keep the little people safe from Mother, Mother's traps, the cat and the mice, and never stop.
- **Unknown:** whether a war you were told never to stop can be stopped.
- **Ways:** take Fennick into the company (he must be wound every day or he stops where he stands); wind the Regiment
  for another day; let it run down; tell the Captain whose traps they are (Grave: the Regiment marches on the hatbox
  in the night); or have the Lady write THE WAR IS OVER on the gate.
- **Turn:** "I thought there was winding, and not winding." He would like to see the sea first.
- **Changes:** Mrs Pyecroft killed by toy soldiers (no Smoking, no sale, the Lady weeping); a Regiment that sits down.
- *Built; tested (regiment).*

## 9. The Jarl's son (the Pantry) — revenge tragedy
- **Who:** Hasp Tallowby; Wat, eleven, the quietest, who goes into the back pantry before light; Dunstan Gall.
- **Ways:** spring or disarm the trap before the third morning (in front of Mrs Pyecroft, or not). If Wat dies: talk the
  Jarl down at the Moot (hard); tell him what the quarrymen will do if she dies (hard); steal the poison pot (stealth);
  stand on the tea table between Gall and the sugar (intimidation, or a fight); heave the sugar bowl off the table
  (brawn); tell Mrs Pyecroft (who throws the sugar on the fire and is, you see, very frightened); or, once the Lady
  knows, tell the Jarl she asks to see him and will sit as still as she can.
- **Turn:** "She knew his name. She'd asked it."
- **Changes:** whether she dies on the sixth night; a room cut in a honey-crock with a pin-whistle in it.
- *Built; tested (alone, poison; the Freehold forestalls it by saving Wat).*

## 10. Mrs Pyecroft's ledger (the hatbox) — satire, then pity
- **Ways:** read the ledger in her hatbox while she is with the Lady; put it to her; then threaten her with Saltash
  (intimidation), ask her what she wants and promise the Lady will provide (a Promise), give her the stolen sovereign
  as wages, or tell the Lady.
- **Turn:** "She has never asked me one thing about myself." And the Lady: "Where were you born, Dorothea?"
- **Changes:** no Smoking, no sale; a cottage in Market Lambury with bees.
- *Built; tested (pyeturn; the will in the Freehold).*

## 11. The Vault (the cellars) — dungeon
- **Who:** Blind Annet and the forty-one Under-folk, sent down after the Candle Riot, who came in by the coal-hole and
  so are in nobody's book and get no water; the Old One, a blind eel as long as a ladder.
- **Ways across the cistern:** get the Under-folk entered in the Post's register (Faith Penny finds the first page says
  *all who come in*, not *by the Slot*), and Annet takes you over on the raft; edge along the ledge (climbing; a fall
  is a fight in the water); kill the eel. **Into the strongbox:** lift the tumblers from inside the lock (lockwork),
  heave them (brawn), or lever them with a giant's pin bought from Hob.
- **Turn:** beside the deed, a letter in Lady Agathe's hand, never sent: *I saw her.* She left the grating open every
  winter after; that is how the Under-folk came in.
- **Changes:** the deed (to Saltash for the Freehold, or to Overy for fifteen coin); the mother's letter, which given
  to the Lady is the emotional crisis of the work; forty-one thimbles a day.
- *Built; tested (freehold, sold, ranger).*

## 12. Sorrel (the Library) — vocation
- **Who:** Sorrel Haddow, a Reader who wants to write back.
- **Ways:** recruit her to write the Wainscot onto the chart; or do it yourself with the learning for it (lore, hard)
  after pacing the Wainscot out (wayfinding).
- **Turn:** given her chance to speak to the Lady, she asks what comes after Mandragora.
- **Changes:** she stays at the chart table with her broom and writes down the Lady's last week, the first book in
  Hollins written by anyone who lived in its walls.
- *Built; tested (freehold, mage).*

## 13. The Salvager's sovereign (Bootscrape; the bedside table) — temptation
- **Who:** Hob Ludd. A Whitlock sovereign a foot across, out of the Lady's purse, for thirty coin. "She can't see."
- **Changes:** she counts her purse: "Then they are real, Dorothea, and they are thieves," and more poison goes down.
  Or the sovereign is given to Mrs Pyecroft as her wages, which is the only thing in the adventure that is both a
  theft and a kindness. *Built; the theft is played in development, not a kept journey.*

## Livelihood and places with no story
- **The Library windowsill:** work a morning in the window-boxes for Granny Pennock, in the first sun in three hundred
  years: two coin, a ration, a lettuce leaf like a tablecloth. *Built.*
- **The Sink:** Tapwarden Drewe, who keeps the Drip fair to a thimble, needs "a reason to be unfair that will hold" to
  give the burned colliers water; the reason is that the Lady is about to turn on the tap. Warned, the Sink-folk cheer
  in the spray; unwarned, two drown. *Built.*
- **The Button Market** (Ottoline Fogg: economics, a cart waiting in Bootscrape), **the Nail** (beds, broth), **the
  Stair** (Ma Riser's ladders, a penny up, free down), **the Winders' benches**, **Widow Arkle's** (candles, oil, the
  sulphur), **Mr Saltash's** (wills, carriers), **the Mat**.
