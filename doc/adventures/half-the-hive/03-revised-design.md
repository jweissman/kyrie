# Half the Hive — Stage 2b: the revised design

*What survived the editorial ([02-editorial.md](02-editorial.md)), and what replaced what did not. This is the design
that was implemented; where implementation changed it, [10-changes.md](10-changes.md) says how.*

## The premise, revised

The Wold of Hume lies east over a march of the first country. In its gorge, under the Brow, four hundred Humefolk live
in a honeycomb built by bees as big as foxhounds, and belong to them by smell. The old queen is about to swarm. Half the
town will walk out behind her. **Every place she might go costs someone**, and the bees have their own opinion.

The protagonist arrives as anyone does, hears the Searchers want hands, and can walk the Wold, learn the dance, and
help choose. They may also ignore all of it, earn a living cutting wild comb, drink at the Smoker's Rest, sit with the
dead, and watch half a town walk away.

## The four sites, revised: every one costs someone

| Site | Its gift | Its cost | How the cost can be paid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Gallowtree** (2 h) | Close: everyone can walk it | Rotten at the heart; the seventh Leaving died there its first winter | It cannot. Learn it, and tell those who dance for it |
| **The Chalk Dell** (3 h) | Dry, south-facing, a cave in the chalk | The brocks' sett | Kill them (very dangerous); drive them off with fire (they come back); or keep the old truce cut in dance-marks on the chalk, with a gift of dark comb |
| **Larkhill Chantry** (3 h) | Close, sheltered, near the lowland markets | A squire's land: under lowland law the swarm is his | Change the squire: the scent granted to him, he gives the land |
| **The Far Brow** (a day) | Perfect, and free | Over the moor past the black hive: the old and lame cannot walk it | Ivo's old drove round the moor by the shepherd's bothy, and a litter for Hannet |

## The dance, revised: bees and people

- The **Sisters dance too**: two for the Far Brow, one for the Dell; one more for the Dell once the brocks are no longer
  a danger. They choose for bees.
- The **Searchers**: Kestrel for the Far Brow; Hannet for the Gallowtree; Moss for the Dell.
- **The protagonist**, once taught the dance and having stood at a site, may dance for it. **Ivo**, once he has walked
  a site with the company, may be asked to dance for it.
- **Quorum is five.** When five dance for one place, the Mother goes at the next noon.
- **Squire Corve uncorks his flask at Larkhill at dawn on the fourth day.** If the Mother has not gone by noon that
  day, she goes to Larkhill, whatever the dancing says.
- If the flask is dealt with and noon on the fourth day comes without quorum, she goes where most dance. If no place
  leads, she hangs in the lindens and builds in the open: the **bivouac**, a slow death.

Minds that change the dancing are changed by people: Hannet by being told what became of her aunt, and promised a
litter; Moss by the brocks (or by surviving them); Kestrel by Ivo knowing a road she does not.

## The structure, revised

| Chapter | When | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| **I. The Hum Drops a Note** | arrival | Smoke End, the scenting, the Comb; everybody deciding |
| **II. The Searching** | the second morning | The Wold opens: four sites, the moor, the wood, the Toot |
| **III. The Calling** | the second evening | Households say aloud who goes; Huw's mead; Linnet and Eben in one room |
| **IV. The Leaving** | the noon the Mother goes | The swarm, the procession, the founding night |
| **V. The Daughters** | the same night, at the Brow | The first princess pipes; the others answer |
| **VI. The Turning-out** | two days after | The new queen's flight to the Toot; the Escort turned out at dusk; the account |

The protagonist cannot be in two places on the night of the Leaving: at the founding, or at the Brow with the
Daughters. The other half happens without them and is learned on return.

## The people, revised

- **Linnet Amory**, Hum-keeper, and **Eben Candlish**, Waxwright: each has secretly decided to give up their calling for
  the other. Eben will go, leaving his Stair unfinished; Linnet will stay, leaving the Mother to a boy. Left alone, they
  cross. Told, they choose otherwise. The Stair can be finished in a night with white wax from the Gallowtree, and a
  hand to hold the mould or a voice to sing the hum's key while the wax sets.
- **Kestrel Dunn**, Wardmistress: never wrong in public; not wrong about the Far Brow; wrong about who can walk it. Her
  change comes from a Drone.
- **Ivo Gildersleeve**, of the Escort: a companion; warm, funny, vain, frightened. Knows the Wold by idleness. Can
  dance, can be turned out, can follow the company into the world.
- **Hannet Coke**: dances for the Gallowtree because her legs will carry her that far. Her aunt Merrit went with the
  seventh Leaving.
- **Moss Ashdown**: dances for the Dell, and means to go into it with a spear on the third morning.
- **Agnes Mellor**: her husband Tobit, the Capper, died in the Capped Gallery. She wants someone to sit the dark watch
  with him, without a light, as custom asks; and she wants to carry him with her, as custom forbids.
- **Elspet Garnock**, Scent-warden, who decides who belongs; **Pim Garnock**, the novice who will be left in charge.
- **Nell Quarrie**, perfumer of Smoke End, who forged the scent to visit her dead husband, and made Corve's flask.
- **Squire Lucian Corve** of Larkhill, a naturalist refused the scent three times, who stole belonging for love of bees.
- **Dame Brenna Breck**, whose household has tended the losing princess for two generations, and who feeds her princess
  extra jelly at night; **Tibby**, her grandson, whose pet drone Duke has flown to the Toot.
- **Huw Penry**, mead-maker, a great artist and a vain one, whose mead for the Calling has stuck.
- **Martin Sayle**, who keeps the Smoker's Rest; **Hild Thorle**, smith of smokers and veils; **Crispin Penhallow**,
  the Chandlers' factor running the Leavers' Market at a fifth of value.

## The smaller stories

1. **The Lantern Stair** (Eben; making; white wax).
2. **Crossed** (Linnet and Eben; romance; secrets carried or not).
3. **A Drone's Week** (Ivo; comedy; companion; the Turning-out).
4. **The Dark Watch** (Agnes; horror; the thing that smells like the Mother).
5. **What Agnes Carries** (Agnes and Tobit; grief; custom).
6. **Nell's Oil** (Nell and Elspet; who may belong; a culprit with a grievance).
7. **The Squire's Flask** (Corve; a theft for love; land and law).
8. **The Brocks' Dell** (danger; the truce in the chalk; Moss).
9. **The Seventh Leaving** (the Gallowtree; history; white wax; Hannet's aunt).
10. **Over the Moor** (the Far Brow; the black hive; the shepherds' drove).
11. **Duke** (Tibby; the Toot; a child's dance read; the Brecks' spoon).
12. **The Spoon and the Cages** (the Daughters; the Brecks; Pim).
13. **Stuck** (Huw's mead; a comedy of making).
14. **The Leavers' Market** (Penhallow; a trade, honest or not).
15. Livelihood: cutting wild comb in the linden wood; singing at the Rest; Searcher's pay.

## Persistent consequences

Where the Mother went; whether the Dell's brocks live; whether the Chantry is a squire's or a gift; whether Nell may
visit Aldo; whether the Stair is lit; whether the lovers are together; which princess reigns and whether a cast went;
whether Ivo walks the world with the company; whether the protagonist is **named to the hive**, scented for life, and
what the new town calls them. Told in the account, kept in the world, recognised on return and in the first country.
