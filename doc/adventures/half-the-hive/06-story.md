# Half the Hive — the principal story

## Synopsis

The old queen of Hume Brow is about to swarm. When she goes, half the town will walk out behind her and found a new
comb wherever the bees choose, and never come back. The bees choose by dancing; so do the town's human Searchers;
and the Searchers are short of hands. A traveller who comes up the drove road can be scented at the gate, taken on,
taught the dance, and sent out over the Wold to stand in the places the swarm might go: a thousand-year lime on a
knoll, a chalk dell with a cave, a squire's chantry tower, a twin overhang a day across the moor. Every one costs
someone. Meanwhile the Comb is full of people deciding: a keeper and a carver each secretly giving up their calling
for the other; a widow who wants to carry her dead husband with her, and who will not leave him alone with whatever
is in the dark; a Drone of the hereditary Escort who will be turned out of the Wold when this is over and can do
nothing but walk and talk and sing; an old Searcher who has stayed six times. A lowland squire who loves bees has a
flask of the queen's own scent, made stronger than the queen, and will uncork it on his tower at dawn on the fourth
day. Whatever the protagonist does, the Mother goes. Half the hive leaves; the other half wakes that night to the
piping of a new queen; two days later the Escort is turned out at dusk; and the book closes on where the Leaving went
and what each half became.

## The acts

| Chapter | Opens | Its movement |
|---|---|---|
| **I. The Hum Drops a Note** | The first step into the Wold | Arrival through smoke; the scenting; the Comb's warmth and dangers; everybody deciding; the notice, Kestrel, the dance |
| **II. The Searching** | The second morning | The Wold opens: the Gallowtree's secret, the brocks' truce, the squire's tower, the moor and the black hive; the dances shift; Moss means to go into the Dell on the third morning |
| **III. The Calling** | Six on the second evening, in the Dancing Hall | Every household says aloud who goes; what the week has done is heard in public; Huw's mead, good or sour; the Mother may go from now |
| **IV. The Leaving** | The noon she goes | The swarm pours out of the Brow; two hundred walk behind it; the founding night (joy, at last), or the Brow left behind |
| **V. The Daughters** | That night, in the Comb | The first princess pipes; the others answer from inside their cells; at two she goes to them |
| **VI. The Turning-out** | Two days after | The new queen flies from the Toot with the Escort walking behind; at dusk they are given a cloak and a loaf; the account |

The fourth and fifth chapters happen on the same night, a day's walk apart: the protagonist can be at the founding or
at the Daughters, not both. The other half's night happens without them and is learned on return.

## The people who carry it

- **Kestrel Dunn**, Wardmistress of the Searchers: has danced for the Far Brow for forty years; right about the place,
  wrong about who can walk it; changed by a Drone who knows a road she does not.
- **Ivo Gildersleeve**, of the Escort: ornament by birth; knows the Wold by idleness; frightened under the charm; can
  dance, can be made a Searcher, can sing a stair into being, can be turned out and walk into the world with you.
- **Linnet Amory** and **Eben Candlish**: each sacrificing for the other in secret; crossed, or told, or reunited.
- **Hannet Coke**: stayed six times; dances for the close tree because her legs will carry her there; changed by her
  aunt's wax bee, and by a promise.
- **Agnes Mellor**: the Capper's widow; the dark watch; what she carries.
- **Nell Quarrie** and **Squire Lucian Corve**: two outsiders refused the scent, who forged it, one for love of a dead
  man and one for love of bees; one of them witnessed the horror; the other made the week's one real crime.
- **Moss Ashdown**: nineteen, with a spear, going into the Dell for a song.
- **Dame Brenna Breck**: a hundred years of being the household that loses, and a spoon.
- **Elspet Garnock**, the Scent-warden: the door; **Pim Garnock**, her son, the novice who is left holding a queen.

## Themes, as they are acted rather than said

- **Belonging is a smell, and smells can be counterfeited**: by grief (Nell), by love (Corve), by nature (the moth).
  The protagonist is lent belonging for a day at a time, and may be given it for life, by walking with them or by
  staying.
- **A community that must break in half to live.** Every household chooses; every site costs someone; the best
  outcomes are made (a road found, a truce read, a gift given), not found.
- **Usefulness and ornament**: the Escort fed on fried drone brood; Ivo's one office; the Turning-out.
- **What is made outlasts its maker, or does not**: the Lantern Stair; the mead; the seventh Leaving's white comb.

## Resolutions

| Ending | When |
|---|---|
| **Under the Far Brow** | The Far Brow, and the old came by the shepherds' drove: both halves alive |
| **The Long Way** | The Far Brow by the crag: the young and strong; the old left behind |
| **The Brocks' Peace** | The Dell, with the old truce kept |
| **The Dell** | The Dell, with the brocks killed |
| **The Brocks' Cave** | The Dell, with nothing done about the brocks: held, nightly, at a cost |
| **The Gift at Larkhill** | Larkhill, with the squire named and the land given to nobody |
| **The Squire's Bees** | Larkhill, by the flask: the Leaving as a lord's property (what happens if nobody does anything) |
| **The Eighth Leaving** | The Gallowtree: rotten at the heart; the thaw will tell |
| **In the Open** | No site led: the swarm hangs in the lindens and builds in the wind |

Each is preceded by an account of the strands: you (named, forced, promise broken); the lovers and the Stair; Hannet;
Agnes and the Gallery; Nell; Corve; Moss; the Daughters (one queen; a cast; a pierced cell); Ivo (founder; companion;
turned out; kept); the mead; the Drewes.
