# Half the Hive — Stage 1a: five competing concepts

*Written 30 October 2026, before re-reading the engine for this purpose. Each concept is given enough thought to show
what it would be like to play, what it would say, and why it might fail. The choice and its reasons are at the end.*

## What I read first, and what it made me want to avoid

The collection's own survey (`doc/adventures/repertoire-2026-10-26.md`) is unusually frank. Seven of its works tell one
story: *an outsider reads a misread inheritance and descends to the mechanism before a deadline.* Since that survey,
six more works (Hessel, Carth, Wrayford, Gosse's Company, the Liberty of the Ice, the Third Night, the circus) have
answered it with **city** works: penance markets, theatre patents, frost-fair law, a bridge hanging. They are good,
and the neighbourhood is now crowded: human institutions, satire of commerce, a clerk, a court, a city.

So, besides the commission's own wishes, I set myself these:

- **Not a city of human institutions.** Somewhere whose institutions grow out of a different *material* law.
- **Not a descent, not a ledger, not a vote of humans, not a drowned Aenean layer, no Kiritarun.**
- **Exploration for its own sake**: the survey found no cave or coast "with no machine at the bottom".
- **The future, not the past.** Something is about to be *founded*, not restored.
- **Minds changed by people, places and performance**, not exhibits.
- **A person as an end**, a companion who is wrong, warm, frightened, funny, and possibly in love.
- **A real culprit who chose harm**, small and human, beside harms that are nobody's fault.

## Concept A — *Half the Hive* (the Swarming at Hume Brow)

**The image.** A limestone overhang the size of a cathedral nave, high on the side of a gorge, and hanging under it a
honeycomb a quarter of a mile long, built over four centuries by bees as big as foxhounds, and lived in by people.
Light comes through the wax walls the colour of beer. The floor is warm. Everything hums.

**The law.** The bees (the Humefolk call them *the Sisters*, which is exactly right: every worker bee is a sister)
know their own by smell. Belonging is a scent. A child of the Brow is anointed with the Mother's oil at birth and
renewed at every feast; a stranger must be *scented* at the gate, or the Sisters will treat them as a robber. Smoke
calms them. Wax holds the dead: the Humefolk seal their dead in clear comb, upright in the upper galleries, and visit
them.

**The occasion.** Every generation or so a strong hive swarms: the old queen (*the Mother*) leaves with half the
workers to found a new nest, and a new queen is raised at home. The Humefolk have always gone with her. **Half the
town walks out behind the swarm and never comes back**, to found a daughter town wherever the bees choose. Every town
of the Wold was founded by such a Leaving. Families split, marriages break or are made, apprentices choose. It is the
great sorrow and the great adventure of a Humefolk life, and it is a week away.

**What the protagonist does.** The bees choose their new home the way real bees do: scouts go out, find cavities, come
back and *dance* for them, and the swarm goes where the dancing reaches a quorum. The Humefolk have their own human
scouts, the *Searchers*, who walk the Wold, find sites, and dance on the tilted floor of the Dancing Hall, where the
angle of the dance is the bearing from the sun and its length is the distance. This year half the young Searchers are
staying behind, and the Wardmistress is hiring anyone with legs and eyes. The protagonist can walk the Wold, find
places, learn the dance, and **choose where half a town will live** — and be at fault if they choose badly.

**Its people.** A young Hum-keeper who must go with the Mother and is in love with a Waxwright who cannot leave the
stair he has spent his life building. A magnificent, useless Drone of the hereditary Escort, who will be turned out
of the town after the new queen's wedding flight and has never worked a day. A Wardmistress missing two fingers. An
Imbar widow in the outsiders' quarter who was refused the scent and forged it so that she could visit her husband's
body. A border squire who has bought stolen queen-scent to lure the swarm onto his land. Three households, each
tending one of the princess cells, each hoping its princess kills the others.

**Its dangers.** Honey badgers the size of ponies in the chalk dells. A wild black hive on the moor that robs. A death's-
head moth as long as a man's arm that *smells like home* and walks in among the sleeping dead to drink.

**Its shape.** Arrival and ordinary life; the scouting; the Leaving, a whole town walking across a moor under a cloud
of bees; and after, the princesses waking in an emptied, half-mourning city.

**Why it might fail.** Giant bees are near the edge of cute. "The swarm chooses by quorum" is a vote, which the
collection has been told to rest. Five strands (scouting, romance, culprit, princesses, moth) may sprawl.

## Concept B — *The Tax on Words* (the Hushland)

**The image.** A mountain abbey-state where speech is metered. Every adult is issued a year's words at Candlemas;
bell-ringers in the streets count them; poets are rich and the poor talk with their hands. A murder in the
Scriptorium: a brother found with a fortune in unspent words in his mouth, sewn shut.

**What the protagonist does.** Investigates a human murder in a society where every conversation costs. Talk would
cost the player too: a purse of words, earned, bought, stolen, spent.

**Why it might succeed.** A human culprit; a satire that exposes something recognizable (the price of speech, who can
afford to be heard); a mechanism that changes every conversation.

**Why I rejected it.** It is a ledger again: a tally, metered, counted, the collection's most rested habit. Metering
conversation would make the game's best faculty (talk) miserable to use. Gaunt already sells penance by the day;
a second commodity-satire city is the crowded neighbourhood I meant to leave.

## Concept C — *The Long Drove*

**The image.** A people who live on the backs of a herd of grazing giants, behemoths as big as hills, crossing a salt
steppe on a migration older than their language. Towns are howdahs; the market moves at a walking pace; when a
behemoth dies, a town dies.

**What the protagonist does.** Joins the drove for a season: picaresque episodes on the move, scarcity of water, a
herd turning toward a country that cannot feed it.

**Why I rejected it.** Places that move are faked in Kyr (rooms that teleport), and a season on the move would pace
badly in a game of ten-minute watches. Thrume already has a titan under the fells. I keep from it the idea of a whole
people walking somewhere, which survives in Concept A as the Leaving.

## Concept D — *The Ebb Court* (on the Plane of Water)

**The image.** On the threshold of the Plane of Water, where the drowned things of every sea wash up, a market where
the merrow sell lost things back to the people who lost them: rings, letters, sailors.

**Why I rejected it.** The commission says to extend a canonical plane, not make a private one. The canonical planes
are designed (`doc/architecture/mission-18/one-universe.md`) but **not on main**: no `data/imbar`, no `canon` binding.
Writing it now would make a seventh private plane, which is precisely the defect OWN-31 exists to remove. And it is
drowned things, again. I record the question in `friction.md` rather than decide it by convenience.

## Concept E — *The Nightingale Assize*

**The image.** A southern court where suits of love are tried by a bench of troubadours; the protagonist argues a
broken engagement before them.

**Why I rejected it.** Romance is missing from the collection, but this is the city again (Hessel, Gosse's Company,
the Liberty of the Ice are all within a few leagues of it in tone), and it makes romance a *case*, which is the vote
habit in a nicer coat.

## The choice: A, *Half the Hive*

It is the only concept that answers the commission's first question, *somewhere whose material laws are profoundly
unlike Imbar's*, without leaving Imbar or inventing a private plane. Its strangeness is biological and exact rather
than cosmological: almost every marvel in it is a true fact about honeybees made large and taken seriously as the
basis of a culture.

- **Belonging by smell** is a law with consequences everywhere: at the gate, in a marriage, in a crime, in a horror.
- **The Leaving** is a *future*: a town will be founded, by the player's walking and dancing, where nothing was.
- **Scouting** is exploration that matters without a machine at the bottom of it.
- **The dance** is a performance that changes minds: a scout persuades the swarm by showing it a place with her body.
- It is **domestic** (households splitting), **romantic** (a keeper who must go, a builder who must stay), **comic**
  (a hereditary leisure class kept for one ceremony and then turned out), **horrific** (a thing that smells like
  home), and **political** (who is allowed the scent).

It risks sweetness. The editorial pass must make it sting.
