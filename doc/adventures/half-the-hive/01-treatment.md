# Half the Hive — Stage 1b: the treatment

*The work as it wants to be, before the engine is consulted. Spoilers throughout. The severe critique of it is

## In a sentence

In the Wold of Hume, where a town of four hundred souls lives inside a honeycomb under a limestone brow and belongs to
its bees by smell, the old queen is about to swarm; half the town will walk out behind her and never come back; and
the scouts who must find the place they will live are short of hands.

## Where it is

Beyond a march of the first country, east, where the farmland rises into **the Wold**: high, dry limestone country of
linden woods, heather moor and chalk dells, cut by one deep gorge, the Hask. The folk of the lowlands know the Wold for
three things: its candles, which burn three nights and smell of summer; its honey, which keeps a body sweet for a
year and is sent for when a lord dies far from home; and its bees, which nobody from outside goes near.

The drove road climbs out of the farmland to **Smoke End**, a straggle of turf houses and timber yards at the mouth of
the gorge, where outsiders live: wax-buyers, smiths, smokers-for-hire, a perfumer, an inn. It is called Smoke End
because the smoke never stops; smoke is what makes the Sisters drowsy, and nobody in Smoke End is scented.

Up the gorge, under **the Brow**, a limestone overhang a quarter of a mile long, hangs **the Comb**.

## The Comb

Four hundred years of wax. The Sisters (bees as big as foxhounds, gold and black, furred, with eyes like wet coal)
built it; the Humefolk cut doors in it, shored it, carved it, and live in it. It is warm, always, from the bees'
bodies; the light through the wax walls is the colour of beer; the floors are faintly tacky underfoot; and the hum is
everywhere, in the walls, in your teeth, in your sleep. The Humefolk can hear moods in it. When they say *the hum has
dropped a note* they mean the hive is preparing to swarm.

- **The Apron**: the gorge floor under the Comb, the bees' landing board. The Sisters come and go overhead in brown
  streams. The scented market, the water troughs, and the **Scenting House** at the foot of the stair, where Sabeth
  Loame anoints strangers with the guest scent, good for a day.
- **The Brood**, warm, the lowest galleries: nurseries for bees and for children. The Waxwrights' gallery and
  **the Lantern Stair**. The **Royal Cells**: three great acorn-shaped cells hanging from the gallery ceiling, each
  as big as a cradle, each with a princess in it, each tended by a household. **The Dancing Hall**, with its floor
  tilted like a roof, where the Searchers dance.
- **The Stores**: honey vaults, gold behind thin wax; and **the Capped Gallery**, where the Humefolk dead stand
  upright in clear wax, eyes closed, in their best clothes, row on row back four hundred years, and their families
  come and sit with them.
- **The Crown**: **the Mother's Chamber**, where the queen lives, long as a pony, attended by the Hum-keepers; and
  the **Drones' Gallery**, where the Escort feasts.

## Laws of the place

1. **Belonging is a scent.** The Sisters know their own by the Mother's smell. A child is anointed at birth; adults are
   renewed at every feast; a stranger is scented at the gate for a day. The unscented in the Comb are treated as
   robbers, and robbers are stung. Smoke makes the Sisters slow and stupid; a veil keeps the worst off.
2. **The dead are kept.** Sealed in wax, they do not rot. A family's dead are part of its household.
3. **The swarm decides by dancing.** Bee-scouts and human Searchers alike go out, find a cavity, come back and dance
   for it on a sloping floor: the angle from straight up is the bearing from the sun, the length of the waggle is the
   distance, the vigour is the worth. When enough scouts dance for one place, the Mother goes.
4. **Every generation, half the town leaves.** By custom older than the Comb, those who go with the Mother are chosen
   by themselves, at the **Calling**: each household says, aloud, in the Dancing Hall, who goes and who stays. Those
   who go take nothing but what they can carry, and do not come back. Hum-keepers and Searchers always go. The dead
   always stay.
5. **The Escort.** Certain families are born to the Escort: the Drones. They are fed by the town, do no work, and
   wear yellow. Their one office is to walk with a new queen to the Toot, the hill where she flies to mate. After the
   flight, by custom, they are turned out of the Wold with a cloak and a loaf. Every Drone knows it; every Drone
   believes it will not happen to him.

## History, glimpsed

The Humefolk came to the Brow behind a swarm four hundred years ago, from somewhere east called only **the Old Brow**.
Every town of the Wold is a daughter of a Leaving: Sallowcomb, Ketter, the Nine Holes. Hume Brow has sent out six. The
seventh, eighty-one years ago, went to **the Gallowtree**, a vast hollow linden on a knoll two hours' walk away, and
was never heard of again; the Brow says they went on east. The Wold sells wax and honey to the lowland through Smoke
End and has done since the lowland kings were Aenean; the Chandlers' Company buys it; a lowland squire's family has
lately bought land at Larkhill on the Wold's edge, with a ruined chantry on it.

## Why the protagonist is there

Any reason a traveller has: the rumour of honey that keeps the dead sweet; the wax-buyers' road; the notice the
Searchers' Wardmistress has sent down to Smoke End: **SEARCHERS WANTED. ANY WHO CAN WALK AND LOOK. PAID IN WAX.**
Half her young scouts are staying behind this year, and she must find a home for two hundred people in five days.

## The people

**Linnet Abery**, twenty-four, Hum-keeper. Made the Mother's keeper last spring when the old keeper died. She must go
with the Mother; keepers always go. She is in love with **Eben Tench**, a Waxwright who has spent nineteen years on
the Lantern Stair, a spiral of carved wax that climbs the inside of the Comb from the Brood to the Crown, lit from
within by the sun. It wants one course. He cannot leave it unfinished, and he cannot finish it in five days without
white wax, which nobody has made since the old days. They have not spoken of it to each other. Everybody else has.

**Kestrel Dunn**, Wardmistress of the Searchers, forty-six, sun-dark, two fingers short on the left hand (a brock).
She hires the protagonist. She has danced for the **Far Brow** across the moor every Leaving of her life because her
grandmother told her the founders came from there. She is a better scout than anyone and has never been wrong in
public.

**Hannet Coke**, seventy, the oldest Searcher, who dances for the **Gallowtree** because it is close: her legs will
carry her two hours and not a day. She wants to go with the Mother more than she has wanted anything.

**Moss Pollard**, nineteen, Searcher, who dances for the **Chalk Dell** because he means to kill the brocks that live in
it and be sung about. His household tends one of the princess cells.

**Ivo Fairweather**, thirty-four, of the Escort. Tall, gold-haired, in yellow, with a voice like a cello and the work
ethic of a cat. He has walked every inch of the Wold, out of idleness, and knows it better than any Searcher. He has
never been asked. After the new queen's flight he will be turned out, and he is beginning, under the charm, to be
frightened. He can join the company.

**Sabeth Loame**, the Scent-warden, who decides who may be anointed; and her son **Pim**, seventeen, the keeper's
novice, who will be left to keep the new queen when Linnet goes, and is terrified.

**Agnes Garrow**, whose husband Tobit, the Capper who seals the dead, was found dead in the Capped Gallery four days
ago with his face slack and the honey cells beside him empty. He is to be capped tomorrow night. Agnes is going with
the Mother. She wants to take Tobit with her, which is forbidden.

**Dame Ursel Marl**, the richest honey-merchant in the Brow, whose household tends the first princess cell, and who
has been feeding it extra royal jelly at night so that her princess wakes first. Her grandson **Tibby**, eight, has a
pet drone called Duke that has flown.

**Huw Bellamy**, the Comb's mead-maker, a vain and genuinely great artist, who is going with the Mother and means to
leave behind the finest mead the Brow has tasted, to be drunk at the Calling.

In Smoke End: **Martin Sayle**, ex-soldier, keeps the Smoker's Rest, deaf in one ear from a sting. **Nell Quarrie**,
perfumer, an Imbar woman who married a Humeman, Aldo, and was refused the scent; when Aldo died she could not visit
him in the Capped Gallery, so she learned to make the scent herself, and has visited him every month for six years.
**Dagny Thorle**, dwarf smith, who makes smokers and veils. **Josiah Polglaze**, factor of the Chandlers' Company.

At Larkhill: **Squire Gervase Corve**, genial, improving, in debt, who has paid Nell Quarrie for a flask of queen-scent
strong enough to lead a swarm to his chantry tower, and two Searchers, the **Teague brothers**, to dance for it.

## The sites

| Site | What it seems | What it is | Who dances for it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Gallowtree**, two hours | Close, vast, sheltered | Rotten at the heart; where the seventh Leaving died its first winter, sealed in its own wax | Hannet Coke |
| **The Chalk Dell**, three hours | Dry, south-facing, a cave in the chalk | The brocks' sett | Moss Pollard |
| **Larkhill Chantry**, three hours | A tower, safe, near the lowland markets | A squire's land; under lowland law the swarm is his | The Teague brothers, and the flask |
| **The Far Brow**, a day over the moor | The founders' home | Perfect; the way to it passes the black hive on the moor | Kestrel Dunn |

## The story

**I. The hum drops a note.** The protagonist comes up the drove road through a passing cloud of wild bees, into Smoke
End, is scented at the gate, and goes up into the Comb. Ordinary life: the market on the Apron, the Waxwrights, the
dead in their wax, the Drones' feast. Everyone is deciding. Kestrel hires; Agnes asks someone to sit Tobit's last night
with her; Ivo attaches himself.

**II. The Searching.** Days two to four. The Wold: four sites, each a small adventure (the Lost Leaving inside the
Gallowtree; the brocks; the squire; the moor and the black hive). The dances shift. The protagonist learns the dance.
The Calling: households say aloud who goes. Eben and Linnet. The night in the Capped Gallery, and the thing that
smells like the Mother.

**III. The Leaving.** When the dancing reaches quorum, the Mother goes. A cloud of bees a mile long pours out of the Brow
and hangs, roaring, on the lindens, and then moves; and two hundred people with bundles and veils and bells walk after
it across the Wold. The protagonist can walk with them. At the chosen site, the founding: the first night of a town.

**IV. The Daughters.** In the emptied Comb, the princesses wake. The first pipes; the others answer from their cells.
By nature the first kills the rest. The households guard their cells. Pim must decide. A second, smaller Leaving (a
cast) is possible if the cells are caged. Then the new queen's flight to the Toot, with the Escort walking behind her
in yellow; and the Turning-out of the Drones.

## Endings

By where the Mother went (the Gallowtree: dead by spring; the Dell: raided unless the brocks are dealt with; Larkhill: a
squire's apiary; the Far Brow: a free town), by what the Daughters did (one queen; a cast; no queen), and by what the
protagonist is now (scented for life and a founder; a guest; a stranger who did harm).

## Why someone would want to go there

Because it is warm, golden, humming, sticky, and strange in a way that is entirely physical. Because the people are
in the middle of the biggest week of their lives. Because you can walk out onto a moor and find a place where two
hundred people will live, and dance for it, and be right or wrong.
