# Half the Hive — the map

## Where the Wold lies

South over a march from a mountain country of the first region's world (never on the first ring), the Wold of Hume is
the high limestone country at the mountains' feet: long dry swells of turf and linden wood, a heather moor, chalk
dells, and one deep gorge, the Hask. Its rumour reaches the first country's inns ("a town lives inside a honeycomb
under a cliff"); its candles reach every lowland church; its outcome comes back down the drove road as news.

Times are the passages' own. From the Brow, the Gallowtree is about two hours; the Dell and Larkhill three; the Far
Brow six by the crag or nine by the drove (gentle; the bothy halfway).

## The places, and why each is there

| Place | What it is | What happens there |
|---|---|---|
| Drove road, Wold's edge | Thyme turf; the first sight of the gorge's smoke | Entry; where the turned-out Escort sit on milestones |
| Smoke End | The outsiders' street, under smoke because smoke calms bees | Inn (Martin), smithy (Hild: smokers, veils, stings), Nell's perfumery, the Leavers' Market (Polglaze, the Drewes), the Searchers' notice |
| The Apron | The gorge floor under the Comb; the bees' landing board; market | Food (Doll), the Scenting House (Elspet), the Leaving seen pouring out |
| The Brood | The warm lowest galleries | Eben and the Lantern Stair; the princess cells; the Dancing Hall; the Mellors' cell |
| The Stores | Honey vaults and the Capped Gallery | Huw's mead; the dead; the dark watch; Aldo, fourth row on the left |
| The Crown | The Mother's Chamber and the Drones' Gallery | Linnet and Pim; a night beside the Mother; Ivo and the Escort |
| Linden wood | Forage; the crossroads of the Wold | Paths everywhere; the honey linden (wild comb, a living) |
| The Toot | Hill with a standing stone above the gorge | Drones gather; Duke; the new queen's wedding flight |
| The Gallowtree | A thousand-year lime on a knoll | The seventh Leaving, capped by each other; the last white comb; Merrit's bee |
| The Chalk Dell | A south-facing chalk bowl with a cave | The brocks; the old truce in dance-marks |
| Larkhill | A squire's chantry on the Wold's edge | Corve's drawings, his oak case, his tower; Halse |
| The heather moor | Purple, enormous, humming | The black robber-bees on the crag; the shepherds' bothy with IVO G. HIS BOTHY / NOT HIS. K.D. on the lintel |
| The Far Brow | The twin overhang, empty | Child handprints in old wax; nobody knows why it was left |

## History glimpsed, never wholly told

Four hundred years ago the Humefolk came behind a swarm from somewhere called the Old Brow. Every comb of the Wold is
a daughter of a Leaving: Sallowcomb (starves every third winter), Ketter (damp), the Nine Holes (they have the moth).
Hume Brow has sent out six. The seventh went to the Gallowtree eighty-one years ago and "went on east". The Far Brow
was lived in once, by someone with children, and left; why is not said anywhere in the work.
