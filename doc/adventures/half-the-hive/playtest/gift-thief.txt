You chose for the bees. The Searchers know it; the Sisters do not, and never will. Kestrel Dunn has not said your name since, and will not, either to curse it or to thank you. Eben Candlish walked out after the bees without a word to anyone, and is in the new comb, carving nothing; and Linnet Amory, who stayed for him so that he could finish the Stair, keeps the Daughters in the Brow, and goes every evening to the Waxwrights’ gallery and stands at the foot of the Lantern Stair, three turns up and stopping at nothing, and nobody has had the heart yet to tell her he meant to do the same for her. Hannet Coke stayed, for the seventh time. She sits on the slope of the empty Dancing Hall in the afternoons and runs, very short, very slow, the line of a dance for somewhere, to nobody. Agnes Mellor stayed. She sits in the Capped Gallery every night in the dark with Tobit, on her stool, talking about the goat. Something still comes into the Capped Gallery in the small hours, soft and dry, smelling of the Mother, and drinks from the seals of the dead; and the children of the Brow have a name for it, and a rhyme, and will not go up the honey stair after dark. Once a month Nell Quarrie goes up the stair in the dark smelling of her own oil, and sits an hour with Aldo, and comes down, and nobody notices. Squire Lucian Corve of Larkhill is of the Comb, named by the keeper’s own hand, and goes up the stair in the Brow every week with his drawing-board, and has redrawn the Mother twice, and is still getting her wrong, and does not mind. Moss Ashdown can walk again, with a stick, and will be a Searcher still, they say, in a year. There is a song about him in the Brow after all; the children made it up. It is not the song he wanted. It is quite a good song. The Breck princess woke first and stung her sisters in their cells, and reigns, and the Brecks are the Mother’s household at last after a hundred years, and everyone says what a strong queen she is, and how lucky. Ivo Gildersleeve was turned out with the Escort, with a cloak and a loaf, and is sitting on a milestone at the edge of the Wold looking down the drove road at the lowland, which he has never seen, deciding. The chantry tower at Larkhill hums like a struck bell from dawn to dusk, and belongs to nobody, by a deed of gift in a neat hand that the Wold has framed. The lowland markets are a morning’s walk away. The squire comes up from the farmhouse every evening and sits in the yard with his drawing-board, scented, and the Sisters walk across his paper.
