You set out from the drove road at the Wold’s edge early, with your shadow long in front of you on the road. In the Wold of Hume the limes are in flower, the queen-cells in the Comb were capped six days ago, and every household under the Brow is deciding who goes. The hum has dropped a note. Anyone in the Wold can hear it, if they know what it sounded like before. Smoke End is one street of turf-roofed houses and timber yards strung along a stream at the mouth of the gorge, and all of it smokes. Smudge-fires of damp bracken and dung burn in iron baskets at every door and at the street’s ends, and the smoke goes up the gorge on the draught, grey and sweetish, and makes your eyes run. People here walk with a hand over their mouths, out of habit. Somewhere a hammer. Up the gorge, through the smoke, a glow like a lamp behind a curtain. On the post of the Smoker’s Rest a notice is nailed up, in a square hand, in lowland letters: SEARCHERS WANTED. ANY WHO CAN WALK AND LOOK. PAID IN WAX. ASK FOR K. DUNN, THE DANCING HALL, UP THE COMB. And under it, smaller: GET SCENTED FIRST. Handcarts go up the gorge empty and come down loaded: chests, chairs, a cradle, a loom in pieces, all with that faint golden tackiness of things that have lived a long time in the Comb.

> >hume__apron
The gorge opens into a bowl under a lip of grey rock a quarter of a mile long, and hanging from the underside of the rock is a honeycomb the size of a town, with people going in and out of it, and bees as big as hounds. It is gold and amber and brown and in places nearly black; it hangs in great curved sheets, like curtains frozen in a wind, the oldest of them four hundred years old; and it is cut through with doors, and stairs, and windows. The light through the wax is the colour of beer. The hum is everywhere: in the walls of the gorge, in the ground, in your breastbone. Overhead the Sisters come and go in brown streams the width of a road, gold-and-black, furred like a good coat, with eyes like wet coal. They land on the comb’s lip and walk in. When one passes low the wind of her wings lifts your hair. The people on the Apron do not look up, the way people in a town do not look up at the weather. Stalls along the troughs sell honey-cake, comb in squares on vine leaves, bee-bread in brown loaves, mead by the horn, and a fried thing in paper cones that smells of nuts and butter, which the stallholder says is brood: the drone brood, a delicacy, fried in its own wax. Doll Rowntree, a stallholder by the troughs, with a griddle, a fly-whisk, and forearms like hams, is here.

Eben Candlish walked out after the bees without a word to anyone, and is in the new comb, carving nothing; and Linnet Amory, who stayed for him so that he could finish the Stair, keeps the Daughters in the Brow, and goes every evening to the Waxwrights’ gallery and stands at the foot of the Lantern Stair, three turns up and stopping at nothing, and nobody has had the heart yet to tell her he meant to do the same for her. Hannet Coke stayed, for the seventh time. She sits on the slope of the empty Dancing Hall in the afternoons and runs, very short, very slow, the line of a dance for somewhere, to nobody. Agnes Mellor stayed. She sits in the Capped Gallery every night in the dark with Tobit, on her stool, talking about the goat. Something still comes into the Capped Gallery in the small hours, soft and dry, smelling of the Mother, and drinks from the seals of the dead; and the children of the Brow have a name for it, and a rhyme, and will not go up the honey stair after dark. Nell Quarrie goes up the stair in the afternoons now, in daylight, scented by Elspet Garnock’s own hand, and sits with Aldo, fourth row on the left, with the light coming down the arch; and has shut her shop, because she finds she has no more use for the oil. Moss Ashdown can walk again, with a stick, and will be a Searcher still, they say, in a year. There is a song about him in the Brow after all; the children made it up. It is not the song he wanted. It is quite a good song. The Breck princess woke first and stung her sisters in their cells, and reigns, and the Brecks are the Mother’s household at last after a hundred years, and everyone says what a strong queen she is, and how lucky. Ivo Gildersleeve was turned out with the Escort, with a cloak and a loaf, and is sitting on a milestone at the edge of the Wold looking down the drove road at the lowland, which he has never seen, deciding. A cask of Huw Penry’s last mead is laid down in the honey vaults of the Brow to be broached at every Calling for thirty years, chalked PENRY · THE YEAR HE WENT, and Martin Sayle in Smoke End says it is the second-best mead in the Wold, and will not say which is the first. The Drewes bought a goat with what you paid them, at the last farm before the moor, and walked it all the way, and it is the only goat in the new comb, and the younger girl has named it after you. The Leaving lives in the chantry tower at Larkhill, safe and dry and a morning from market; and the bees, and the comb, and the honey, are the property of L. Corve, Esquire, by the law of the lowland, which the Humefolk have never had to know before and are learning. He has not taken a single pot. He has not been in among them. He cannot bring himself to, and they cannot bring themselves to ask him.
