# Half the Hive — Stage 6: the second, more severe reading

*Written after the work could be played from the drove road to its endings, as a reader and player rather than as its
builder. What was changed in answer is in [12-changes.md](12-changes.md); what was not is said at the end.*

## What it is now, honestly

A country of thirty-six rooms, twenty-eight named people, fifteen smaller stories, a week with six chapters and nine
endings, about 1,800 lines of Kyr (most of them long). It can be found from a generated world, entered, explored, and played to an ending
by an ordinary wanderer, and it persists after. The tests prove that the paths I wrote exist. They do not prove that a
stranger would enjoy them, and I have not watched a stranger play it.

## What works, as played

- **The dark watch.** It is the best scene in the work: Agnes talking to her dead husband about the goat in total
  darkness, the soft pattering, the smell of the Mother that makes your eyes water, a wing touching the back of your
  neck, her hand on your wrist. The choice it puts (keep still; reach out with a stolen sting; break the custom with a
  light) is a real one, and the light costs something with Agnes.
- **The Calling as the week made public.** What the protagonist has done is heard aloud in the order of a ceremony:
  Hannet "carried, like a cheese"; Agnes going "and Tobit Mellor with her, by leave of the keeper"; Eben keeping his
  face still while his father declares the household stays; Ivo standing up in yellow and saying *Searcher. Goes.*
  It is the one place the collection's habit of public assembly earns itself, because nothing is decided there.
- **Ivo.** Warm, vain, frightened, funny, and of real use. His dance, his singing the Stair in, his handprint at the Far
  Brow that Kestrel thought was a ghost, the bothy lintel (IVO G. HIS BOTHY / NOT HIS. K.D.) are the work's best
  connected details. The lintel was not planned; it came from writing the bothy.
- **The default ending is a satire that needs no one.** Left alone, the flask works, and the Leaving becomes a lowland
  gentleman's property, and he cannot bring himself to go in among them. Nobody is wicked in it.
- **The crossed lovers' fate** is the one ending line that refuses consolation: Linnet at the foot of the unfinished
  Stair every evening, and nobody has told her he meant to do the same for her.

## What does not, and why

1. **The account is a list.** Fourteen strands told one after another in the iterative present, most ending on a small
   custom: a rhyme, a stick, a scratched post, a goat named after you. The repertoire survey named exactly this cadence
   as the collection's consolation reflex, and I wrote it fourteen times. *Partly answered*: the account is kept (it is
   how the world tells what it did), but several fates were cut back to the fact, and the endings themselves are short.
2. **The fourth and fifth acts are a structure more than an experience.** On paper the protagonist chooses which half
   of the hive to belong to that night. In play nothing *asks* the question: the Leaving goes, the walk is offered,
   and a player who does not take it is simply in the Brow. *Answered*: the walk is the obvious offer at the Apron on
   the day; staying is now a felt choice only if the player has caged cells or learned of the spoon. Not fully solved.
3. **The Daughters were underwritten.** The two watchers under the other cells were "an old Waxwright" and "a freckled
   woman". They are Eben's father and Moss's mother; the stinging happens to *them*. *Answered*: named, and present in
   the stinging scene.
4. **The return visits were empty.** After the Leaving, everyone at the camp and in the Brow had only their old topics,
   some of them stale ("Ask Eben why it doesn't matter now"). *Answered*: each new comb is described; the Brow's empty
   bed, Tobit's empty trestle, Eben's bare bench; twenty after-topics for those who went and those who stayed; stale
   topics closed.
5. **The vigil asked the player to know to wait.** Arriving in the Gallery at ten, a player had "Wait" and nothing
   else. *Answered*: "Sit down beside Agnes in the dark, and keep the watch", broken off when the thing comes.
6. **Kestrel is thinner than her place in the story.** She hires, teaches, explains, pays, is moved once by Ivo, and
   sits by the handprints. Her "never wrong in public" is said, not seen. *Not answered*: there is no scene in which
   she is publicly wrong before the Leaving shows it.
7. **Moss is a function.** He exists to be mauled or saved. *Not answered.*
8. **The Sisters' own preference is a number.** The bees "have opinions" (two dances for the Far Brow, one for the Dell)
   but the player never sees a Sister prefer anything except by count. *Partly answered*: the Sisters' scouts dance
   for the Dell once the brocks are no danger, and go mad for the flask; still a number.
9. **The prose is the house voice where it is tired.** Absence-sentences ("Nobody...") ran to fifty-four; "the hum" to
   eighteen; "white as milk" four times. *Answered in part*: the narrator's absences were cut where they were only
   cadence (dialogue keeps them, where they are the characters' own), and the duplicated descriptions on the Apron and in
   the Gallowtree were cut.
10. **The glance hid the bees.** The Apron's first observation, the only one shown at a glance, did not mention bees as
    big as hounds. *Answered*: the first sentence now carries the honeycomb the size of a town and the bees.
11. **Brocks and moth were mis-weighted.** At first both killed a company of three in one exchange; the moth is now a
    fight a fellowship can win with a light, the brocks still are not, by intent.
12. **Rhyme versus crossword.** Nell, Corve and the moth all counterfeit the scent. The editorial pass said let one
    be simply monstrous; the moth is. Nell's and Corve's are left unresolved in feeling: the account gives each a
    plain fact, not a moral.

## A tenfold ambition, considered

Not ten times the rooms. What would most deepen it:

- **The Sisters as a mind.** A scene in the Dancing Hall where a Sister reads *your* dance and rejects it, and you
  understand why (the cavity faces north; the bees know what you did not). The bees as a non-human intelligence that
  disagrees with you for reasons is the work's least-used idea.
- **A second Leaving, years later.** The new comb's own first swarm, in a later visit: the protagonist, named, now
  dancing *as* a Humeman, and the people they founded with deciding who goes. The engine can hold it (the world
  persists); the work would need a second week.
- **The Old Brow.** Somewhere east is where the Humefolk came from. It should stay unwritten.

## What remains weak

The account's length and cadence; Kestrel and Moss; the bees as a count; the one-night-two-places structure is more
elegant in the treatment than in play; and the founding night, the work's one scene of joy, is a paragraph, not a
scene you can do anything in.
