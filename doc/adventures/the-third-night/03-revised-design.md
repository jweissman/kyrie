# The Third Night — the revised design

*Stage 2's result: the treatment ([01](01-treatment.md)) revised against the review ([02](02-critique.md)). This is the
design the implementation is judged against. Where the engine later forced a change, [04](04-adaptation.md) and the
final report say so.*

## 1. The premise, kept

Wrayford is a bridge-city where the drove roads cross the Wray. The lord put a toll on the bridge and sold the farm of
it to the city's own aldermen, the Bridge Company. Four nights ago the carters rose and broke the chain; the
toll-house burned; the toll-keeper's boy, Ned Hobday, went under a cart. The lord's riders took three carters on the
back road and hanged them from the gatehouse rail. The reeve, Simon Hulme, has decreed that they stay until the lord's
captain comes, at dusk on the third day, so that she finds order and not rebellion. The decree is nailed to the
gatehouse door: *whoever touches them shall hang beside them.* Maudie Pardoe will bury her husband before the third
night is out.

**Nothing discovered changes what must be decided.** The mystery of the night of the rising has three answers, and
none of them takes a body off the rail. Each changes what one person will do.

## 2. Two entrances

| | The homecoming | The traveller |

| How it begins | The standalone campaign. The protagonist is Wat Pardoe's elder child, home on their mother's letter, three years and a week late | A protagonist of any origin, in a generated world, hears at an inn on the drove road that three hang on Wrayford bridge, and comes |
| Who they are to the Pardoes | Kin: Maudie's child, Kit's elder sibling, Hal Hulme's oldest friend | A stranger, whom Maudie will not ask for anything, and whom Gault's men do not watch |

## 3. The city

Six districts and the bridge between them. The spatial account is in the final report; in brief:

- **Northgate**: the drove road comes in; Wain Row and the Pardoe house; the carters' yards; the Wainwrights' hall;
  the Nine Wheels.
- **The Market**: the Cross; the Guildhall; the Shambles and its pens; the Company's counting-house; Quoin's print
  shop in Cutler's Lane; the pawnshop.
- **Mercer Hill**: the reeve's house; Alderman Gidley's house and ice-house.
- **The Wharf**: the ferry-stage; the fish-stair; the old ford below the bridge, used by drovers' beasts at low water.
- **The Bridge**: Bridge Street on the arches; Jennet Hobday's house; the pie-sellers; the drawbridge; Saint Wade's
  chapel in the middle pier, and the starling ledge under it; the gatehouse and its rail.
- **Overbridge**, the Liberty: Saint Wade's church and Wade's Acre; the Abbess's lodging and her court; the
  tan-yards; the bear-garden; the Ferry Inn.

**The city's hours.** Market in the morning, the pens filling and emptying. Saint Wade's bell at dawn, noon and dusk.
The Bench at supper in the Guildhall. At dusk every day Dorcas Mudd and Corbin quarrel across the water, Dorcas on her
stage and Corbin on the Acre wall. The crowd at the gatehouse grows through each day, and its temper follows what has
been printed, said and done.

## 4. The jurisdiction of the dead

The satire's ground, and the law's: the dead are on a boundary.

| Place | Whose | What that means |
|---|---|---|
| The gatehouse and its rail | The lord's | The decree; Gault's four men; the captain |
| The bridge's north half and the city | The reeve's and the Bench's | Whoever is seen carrying the dead is arrested |
| The south bank | The Liberty's | The reeve's men may not arrest; the Abbess may, and will claim what arrives |
| The river between the stairs | The ferry's, by its charter | Dorcas will tell anyone: "What's in the Wray between the stairs is mine till it's landed." |

## 6. The mystery, found before it is told

| Truth | Found in the world | Then told, if the teller chooses |
|---|---|---|
| **Pinch set the fire**, to burn the tally of the penny in five he had been taking | An oil flask of the Company's kind in the ash of the toll-house. And Pinch could not burn his own day-book: it is under his mattress in the room behind the counting-house, its last pages in his small, exact hand | Pinch, cornered, recites the schedule, then confesses. Shown to the Bench, it is a scandal; shown to the captain, it means there was a riot and a thief, not a rising |
| **Kit was driving**; Wat took the reins back, lifted the boy in, drove for a bone-setter, and said he had been driving | Wat's cart in the reeve's yard: the wheel clean, blood on the tailboard where a boy was *lifted in*. Kit's account of where he was does not survive a walk round the yards. Bramble finds him at night under the arches | Kit tells his sibling, or his mother, or Jennet. Only if he trusts the asker, and only after something has been done for him |
| **Hal wrote to the riders**, believing they would only take the ringleaders for the lord's court | The message in Gault's coat, in Hal's hand. Gault will show it to someone who asks him plainly, soldier to soldier; a thief can take it | Hal confesses to the protagonist on the starling ledge (the homecoming), or to Maudie, or publicly, or runs |

## 8. The two principal approaches

**By day.** Make it lawful, or make it shameful to stop. The instruments are the toll schedule, the Remonstrance, the
Abbess's claim, Jennet, and Pinch's fire. Two endings: **the Liberty's Charity** (lawful release) and **the Two
Mothers** (a daylight funeral nobody will stop).

**By night.** Take them down and bury them. The instruments are the gatehouse (the stair, the rail, the rope, the
moon, Gault's men's sleep), the crossings (Dorcas's boat under the rail, Ham's ford, the butcher's cart), and the
graves (Corbin, or a spade). One ending: **Earth on Him**, with an arrest at dawn.

They differ in what they cost and in who carries it. By day costs time, concessions to the Company and the Abbess,
and one person's office. By night costs someone's freedom, and the captain sees an act of defiance.

## 9. Endings, and what makes each reachable

| Ending | Needs (at least two small adventures) | Afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| **The Liberty's Charity** | The Remonstrance clause (7) and the Abbess's claim (2 earns her ear; or a cleric's rites), with Pinch's ruling (6) or Pinch exposed (M) | The reeve releases the dead to the Liberty on day 3, before the captain. Hulme keeps his office; the Company keeps its farm; everyone is a little ashamed |
| **The Two Mothers** | Jennet's trust (10) and Kit's confession reaching her (M), with graves ready (11, or dug yourself) | Both mothers carry their dead across the bridge in daylight with the city behind them. The reeve's men will not stop the toll-keeper's widow. The captain finds a funeral. Hulme resigns. The toll stands |
| **Earth on Him** | The dead taken down at night, carried across (11, 12 or 13), and buried | At dawn someone is arrested: the protagonist, Kit, or Dorcas, who says it was her boat; or Hal, who says it was him |
| **Too Late** | The reeve moved (by Hal's confession, Jennet, or shame) only on night 3 | He goes to cut them down himself and finds the captain's riders have Maudie at the rail |
| **The Fire** | Kit's rising joined, or Kit enraged by Hal's letter and free on night 3 | The counting-house burns; the captain comes in the night; more on the rail |
| **The Company's Bargain** | Gidley's purse taken, and Kit given up to the captain | The dead released as a reward; Kit hanged on day 4 |
| **The Third Night** | Nothing | Maudie goes alone; she is taken; in the morning the captain hangs her beside him, under the decree |
| **Away** | Leaving the city before night 3 | The city goes on; coming back, the protagonist finds what it did |

## 10. Arcs

- **Kit**: fire, then confession, if someone earns it.
- **Hulme**: the decree, then resignation, or too late, or vindication (which is worse).
- **Hal**: the secret, then confession, flight, or Kit's knife.
- **Jennet**: the crows, then carrying both.
- **Corbin and Dorcas**: forty years, then a question asked first.
- **Annis**: selling pies at the gate, then walking at the funeral.
- **Gidley**: unchanged. That is the satire.

Each principal approach can be finished with no high charisma and no scholarly training. A warrior can go by night
(climb with a rope, carry, dig) or by day (Jennet's ice, Gault's letter, the two mothers). A thief can go by night
alone (climb, pass the watch, the butcher's cart), or by day through the mystery.
