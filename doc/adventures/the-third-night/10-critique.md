# The Third Night: a final critique, and what changed

*Written after the first revision. A second, more severe literary reading, and the revision it led to, are in
[`11-second-reading.md`](11-second-reading.md); several faults named below (Maudie's verbs, the stranger's lesser
version, the tedium of carrying, the thin danger) were taken up there.*

Deliverable 10, and the record of changes the commission asked for. Written after playing every route to its end and
reading the transcripts, not before.

## What it is now

A city of seventy places across a river and two jurisdictions, thirty-six people, eighty-five topics and eighty-five
actions, two hundred and fifteen rules running on the city's own clock, fourteen small adventures, a mystery in three
parts, a captain, and eleven endings. About 47,000 words of Kyr. It can be played as a homecoming or found from a
generated mountain country beyond its south march, and it leaves the world as the verdict left it.

## Where it succeeds

**It has a real problem, and the problem has several honest answers.** Three men hang where their families can see
them, and the reasons they hang are not stupid: the reeve is right that the captain will burn the carters' row if she
finds a rising. Every way of getting them down costs someone something different. A lawful release is paperwork,
done by nobody brave, and the Company enters its charity in the minutes at the Liberty's charge. A daylight funeral
costs the reeve his office and leaves the toll standing. A night burial costs whoever is taken at dawn, and the
player discovers, the first time, that it is usually their brother. The bargain buries the dead and hangs the
brother. Nobody gets everything. I think this is the strongest thing in the module, and it is the thing the
repertoire survey said Kyrie had not yet learned to do: tragedy with choices in it.

**The city is alive apart from the dead.** This was the severest note on the first draft, and it is the part that
changed most. Pinch keeps his booth and goes to bed behind the counting-house; Gidley weeps under the rail at nine and
asks for the takings; Dorcas and Corbin shout across the water at eight; Kit goes for the counting-house at midnight
whether you know or not; Maudie goes up with the bread knife. Left alone, the city produces The Third Night on its
own. Played, it reacts: the temper rises with the ring on Annis's hand, Win's claim, a broadside, a cleric's office
under the rail, and when it reaches two the city will walk behind its widows.

**The mystery is found before it is told.** The blood is on the tailboard, not the wheel; the oil flask in the ash is
the Company's kind; Pinch's shirt went to the wash stinking of lamp-oil; the letter in Gault's coat is in the hand of
the decree on the gatehouse door. The people confess only after the world has shown the player enough to ask them.
And each answer is a person: Kit, whom you came home to; Hal, who was your friend; Pinch, who is merely contemptible.

**The satire is played, not narrated.** The toll-board's categories are the mechanism of the day route: a dead man
can cross as a carcass by the hundredweight, or as the Abbess's tithe, or not at all as goods of a felon, and each
ruling changes something later. The Bench passes any clause that costs it nothing it can be seen to lose; the clause
it likes best, left alone, makes the dead its surety. Gidley is moved, sincerely, only by his own face in a portrait,
and that is enough to carry the release.

**The local stories stand on their own.** The hide, the bear-ward's boy, Win's bread, the portrait and the ring are
complete without the dead. They end with a boy home and a box on the ear, a bear in an abbess's garden, a woman who
does not thank you, a girl walking away from her pitch with her fist shut.

## Where it falls short

**The night is laborious where it should be tense.** Carrying three men over the ford is eight choices a man, three
times, through the same five rooms; the transcripts make it plain that the second and third trips are the first one
again. The weight and the time are the point (the scholar's ends one man short, and that is the best moment in that
playthrough), but the engine has no way to say "you carry Dunstan the same way" and move on. It needs a repeated
journey summarised, or a carrying action that takes a body from one place to another in one choice once the way has
been walked.

**The fights are an afterthought.** There are two: the riders on the leads if they wake, and Gault in the
counting-house. Each resolves inside a single wait. A lone warrior loses, a seasoned company wins in a blow, and
neither involves a decision. The module is right not to be about combat, but where violence is the climax of one
ending (The Fire) it should be the most fraught moment in the game, and it is the least.

**The captain is still thin.** She is the most consequential person in the story and was the least played: one
persuasion check on the road, two statements at supper, a drover's word, counted towards three. Written up here
as a fault, it was partly mended in the last pass: she now asks the company, the first time it speaks to her at
supper, whether Wrayford is in rebellion, as "the only person in this hall I have not been told what to think of".
A homecomer can answer as a rebel's child who came to supper instead of to the gate; a stranger as someone with no
reason to lie; anyone can send her to the yards, where what she counts depends on what the carters did. It is one
question. The design wanted a negotiation, and this is the start of one.

**Kit is too predictably the one who pays.** Every unlawful route takes him at dawn unless the player stands in for
him, Hal takes the blame, or the Liberty shelters the company. That is a deliberate cost and it lands the first
time; on a second playthrough it is a known tax, and the routes that avoid it are narrow. A reckoning that weighed
what the city had seen (a boat, a cart, a witness) more variously would make the dawn less foregone.

**The stranger's version is the lesser one.** Kinship is the largest single key: the answer to the reeve's question,
the coat for the dog, the promise to Maudie, Kemp's word, the hand recognised at a glance. A stranger reaches every
ending by longer ways, which is right, but they have less reason to want to. The traveller from a generated world
arrives at a funeral that is not theirs. What a stranger could have that a homecomer cannot (being nobody's, being
believed by the captain as an outsider, being unable to be blamed by Hulme) is used once, at the captain's
supper, and nowhere else.

**Some of the prose still repeats.** Short second descriptions now exist for the places walked most, but the presence
line still names everyone each time, and goodbyes come from a fifteen-line table that cannot know a sergeant is in a
tavern. A player who crosses the bridge twenty times will notice.

**Maudie has too few verbs.** She is the reason for the deadline and the most important person in the homecoming, and
the player can talk to her, sit her vigil, promise her, hold her at home, or go with her. Everything else in her story
is done by other people. The two mothers' scene is the best in the module; Maudie's part in getting there is to open
the door.

## What changed, from the treatment to the final

**From the treatment (`01`) to the revised design (`03`)**, under the editorial review (`02`): the city given its
own clock and people with errands; the verbs widened beyond "persuade"; the satire turned from description into
games (the Remonstrance's phrasing rule, the toll-board's categories, the jurisdiction of the water); the mystery made
discoverable in the world before anyone confesses; comedy made to come from consequence; a second entrance (the
stranger) designed alongside the homecoming; the ford charter cut as a misread inheritance; a builds matrix drawn.

**From the revised design to the implementation**, under play:

| Area | Designed | Final |
|---|---|---|
| Endings | Eight | Eleven: **The Reeve's Release** (the reeve moved in time cuts them down himself), **The Lime** (nothing buried, nobody hanged), **The Third Man** (some buried, not all) added; **Too Late** made to need the reeve moved only after the captain came |
| The dawn arrest after a night burial | The protagonist, Kit, Dorcas, or Hal | Kit, unless the company stands in for him, Hal takes it, or the Liberty shelters them; Dorcas is not arrested but claims the dead by charter, and Kit is taken instead; if Kit is already held, his name is written against it too |
| The Company's bargain | "The dead released as a reward" | Made real: the Company asks the captain's mercy at supper, and she grants it, before Maudie can go |
| Kit on night 3 | "Enraged by Hal's letter and free" | Both: told who sent the riders, he goes for Hal on the starling; and if his mother is taken while he is free, he leads the carters to the gate, and is taken |
| The toll | Background | Taken: a farthing a day at the chain, as a penny, three farthings owed until Michaelmas |
| Rewards | The bridge token, Dorcas's ferry, sanctuary, Kit, Bramble, the purse, the coat | All present; the token and the penny added late, when it was clear they were promised and not given |
| Consequences of local stories | Mostly stated | Wired: Ham's gratitude speaks to the captain; Win's claim raises the temper; the felon ruling binds Pinch and arms the pawnbroker; the pilgrim joke turns the crowd; the portrait carries the release; a cleric's office earns the Abbess's regard |
| The mystery's endgame | Pinch exposed is a scandal | Pinch goes south with the captain's baggage carrying his day-book; the second fire burns the proof of the first |
| The dead left lying | Not considered | Found by the reeve's men on the city side; buried by Corbin at the end of his day in the Acre |
| Placement | East of a generated country | South of a mountain country, after the east placement disturbed two existing generated-world tests |
| Hal | Confession, flight, or Kit's knife | Confession and Kit's knife; flight was not built |

## If I went on

In order: the rest of the negotiation with the captain (more than one question, and what you answer is what she
reports); a way to carry a second and third body without walking the route three times; a reckoning at dawn that
weighs what the city saw; something for a stranger that a homecomer cannot have; and two more things for Maudie to do
herself before the third night (she is a baker; the city eats her bread; there is a scene in that).
