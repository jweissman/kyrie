# The Third Night: story, people, small adventures and endings

Deliverables 3, 4 and 5: the synopsis and acts, the characters and the strategic conflict among them, the inventory
of small adventures and how they connect, and the resolutions each can reach. All of it is implemented; where a
statement depends on a rule, the rule is named so the claim can be checked.

## Synopsis

Four nights ago the carters of Wrayford broke the lord's new chain across the bridge and threw the Bridge Company's
toll-board in the river. In the press at the gate a boy was killed under a cart, the toll-house burned, and before
light the lord's riders took the three oldest carters on the fell road and hanged them from the gatehouse rail. The
reeve, Simon Hulme, has decreed that they stay there until the lord's captain comes at dusk on the third day, so that
she will find the city in order and not burn it. In Wrayford the dead are watched three nights and buried on the
third day; one who is not buried by the end of the third night goes looking. Maudie Pardoe, whose husband Wat is the
first of the three, has said that if he is still up there when it comes she will go up with the bread knife herself.

The company comes down the drove road into this: as Wat's elder child, home a week late on their mother's letter, or
as strangers from a country beyond the march. What they can do is bury the dead, or fail to; and there are a dozen
ways of trying, each owned by someone in the city who wants something else.

Underneath it is a mystery the city has got wrong. The carters did not burn the toll-house: the Company's collector
did, to burn the tally of what he had been stealing. Wat did not drive the cart that killed the boy: his son Kit had
the reins, and Wat took them back, lifted the boy in and drove for the bone-setter, and told the riders he was
driving. And the riders did not find the fell road by chance: a letter told them, in the reeve's son's hand.

Over all of it is a legend the whole city half believes. Before there was a bridge, Saint Wade kept the ford and
carried people over on his back for nothing; on the third night after the great flood he carried a drowned child
to be buried, and at the deep place the child grew heavy as all the dead of the vale, and Wade went to his chin and
did not set him down. The dead who are not carried over by the third night go looking for someone who will. The
Bridge Company's motto is WE BEAR THE CROSSING. Wat Pardoe sang the carters' hymn to Wade on the road to the rope.

## Acts

The acts are the city's, not the company's; each happens whether or not anyone intervenes (`captain.kyr`,
`fire.kyr`, `night.kyr`, `day.kyr`).

Each dawn now turns a page (THE SECOND DAY, THE THIRD DAY, THE THIRD NIGHT, THE FOURTH MORNING), with weather that
walks down the vale with the captain: heat and crows, thunder in the fells, the storm at the curfew bell on the third
night, a washed fourth morning.

1. **The first day and night.** Arrival; the decree; the confession broadside on every post; Corbin's strike; Jennet's
   shut door; Dorcas and Corbin's quarrel at eight. The first vigil at the Pardoe house, if anyone sits it.
2. **The second day.** Market; the heat on Jennet's front room; the Abbess's court on the hide (ten to noon); the
   Remonstrance at the Guildhall (noon to two), where any clause the Bench can pass for nothing will pass, and the
   clause it likes best, left to itself, keeps the dead up as the Company's surety. At midnight, Kit's fire.
3. **The third day.** Riders sighted at noon; the captain on the south road from three, at the Guildhall by dusk,
   supping with the Bench, where the Company offers her a live ringleader if it has one to offer. In the small hours,
   Maudie goes up to the gatehouse, unless held, or promised, or the dead are already down.
4. **The fourth morning.** The verdict at nine: rebellion, if fire was raised or riders beaten or the carters rose;
   order, otherwise. Whoever she holds for the decree, she hangs unless she has been given three reasons not to.
5. **Afterwards.** The captain rides out at noon. On the fifth day, the Bridge Feast, whatever the city has become.

### The strategic conflict

Four powers want the same thing for different reasons: the dead to stay up until the captain has been and gone.
**The reeve** wants it to save the city. **The Company** wants it because the dead are, by the end of the second day,
its surety. **The lord's riders** want it because it is their order. **The Abbess** would take the dead tomorrow but
will not cross the bridge to fetch them, because to fetch them is to admit the bridge is the city's road. Against
them: **Maudie**, who will go up on the third night; **Kit**, who will burn something on the second; and **the city
itself**, whose temper rises with every kindness done and every humiliation suffered in front of the crowd, and which
will walk behind its widows over the bridge in daylight if it is given a reason.

The company does not defeat any of these. It finds the seam between them: a category on the toll-board, a charter on
the river, a privilege of the offal cart, a liberty beyond the drawbridge, a widow nobody will stop.

### How they connect

Every principal ending needs at least two small adventures, and most small adventures feed more than one ending.

- **The hide** → the Abbess's regard → **her claim** → **Pinch's tithe ruling** → **the Bench's charity clause** → the
  reeve's cover → **The Liberty's Charity**. The same hide, judged for the butcher, opens **the offal cart** to a
  stranger.
- **The ice** → **Jennet's trust** → **the farrier and the cart** → **Kit's confession** → Kit's own telling →
  **The Two Mothers**, if the city's temper (raised by **the ring**, **Win's claim**, **the broadside**, **the
  portrait**, a cleric's **office under the rail**) has reached two.
- **Gault's jug** → **the letter** → **the hand** (the decree in the same round hand) → **Hal on the starling** → his
  father told → the reeve moved → **The Reeve's Release**, or, if he is moved only on the third evening, **Too Late**.
- **The pie-seller** → the pawnbroker; **Win's washing** → Pinch's shirt; **the ash** → the flask; **the collector's
  room** → the day-book → **the captain at supper** → Maudie spared even in **The Lime**.
- **Corbin and Dorcas** → the boat; **Ham's drive** → the ford; **the hide** or kinship → the cart: three crossings
  for **Earth on Him**.
- **Kit's night** decides whether the verdict is rebellion: talked down, turned, held, or walking with you, or the
  counting-house goes up. And if his mother is taken on the third night while he is free, he rises regardless.

### Purely local stories

The hide, the bear-ward's boy, Win's bread, the portrait and the ring are complete without the dead. Their
resolutions are a boy home with a box on the ear, a bear in an abbess's garden, a woman who does not thank you, a
painter's rent, a girl walking away from her pitch with her fist shut. They touch the main plot only obliquely, and
each through a person rather than a key: the Abbess's regard, Win's claim, Annis's walk, Gidley's tears, a drover's
word.

## Endings

Reached at the verdict, the first that holds, in this order (`endings.kyr`). In a world that goes on they close only
the chapter; the city stays as they left it, and shows it (`aftermath.kyr`).

| Ending | When | What it says happened |
|---|---|---|
| **The Fire** | The verdict is rebellion: the counting-house burned, riders beaten, or the carters rose on night 3 | Wain Row burned; ten hanged, the rail not long enough; the Company rebuilds in stone |
| **Too Late** | The reeve went down to cut them down on night 3, a quarter of an hour behind Maudie | Maudie hanged under his decree in his son's hand; Hulme gone in a week; four graves, Corbin silent |
| **Away** | Maudie hanged, and the company not in the city | News weeks later from a drover in another country |
| **The Third Night** | Maudie hanged | The captain finds the city in order, and one more; nobody in Northgate goes out on the third night ever after |
| **The Company's Bargain** | Kit sold for forty crowns | The Company keeps its word, such as it is: the dead given back at the captain's supper; Kit to Harbury; a ballad at a penny |
| **The Two Mothers** | The widows walked, with the city behind them | A funeral, not a rising; Hulme resigns; the toll stands; Maudie comes to Jennet's on Sundays with bread |
| **The Liberty's Charity** | Released by the Bench's clause, the Abbess's claim and a lawful crossing | Everyone a little ashamed, the rail empty, the Company's charity at the Liberty's charge; the freedom of the bridge |
| **The Reeve's Release** | Hulme cut them down himself by day | The city in order and its reeve not; he keeps a garden and stands under the gatehouse on every third night |
| **Earth on Him** | All three buried, unlawfully | Three graves nobody will admit to digging; someone taken at dawn for it; everybody knows |
| **The Third Man** | Some of the three buried, unlawfully, and not all | The one left behind put in the lord's lime, alone; the city argues ever after which of them it was |
| **The Lime** | None buried, and Maudie not hanged | The three in the lord's lime at the crossroads; Maudie in her doorway |

Around each ending, what became of the people is told by what was done: Kit's fate (hanged, freed from the cellar,
back on his father's box), Hal's, Maudie's, Pinch's (south with the captain if exposed, dismissed at dawn if Gidley
burned his book), Annis's, Robin's drum, Win at the graveside with the chisels, Corbin and Dorcas's banns. The
afterward observations show the city to a company that stays: flowers on the rail, cut ropes with black wool,
Wain Row burned to its chimneys, the reeve's empty chair, one loaf on the Pardoe table instead of twenty.

### Genuinely distinct resolutions

The brief asked for several. Played, the endings differ in what they cost and who pays:

- **By day, lawful** (The Liberty's Charity): the dead given up as paperwork; nobody brave; the Company keeps
  everything and enters its charity in the minutes.
- **By day, shameful to stop** (The Two Mothers): the city walks, and its reeve resigns, and the toll stands.
- **By the reeve's own hand** (The Reeve's Release): one man's office, given up.
- **By night** (Earth on Him, The Third Man): someone is taken at dawn for it, usually Kit; or nobody, if the company
  took his place, or Hal took the blame, or the Liberty sheltered them.
- **By betrayal** (The Company's Bargain): the dead buried, the brother hanged, forty crowns in your purse.
- **By fire** (The Fire): the city made into what the captain was afraid of.
- **By omission** (The Third Night, Away, The Lime, Too Late): four ways of not getting there in time.
