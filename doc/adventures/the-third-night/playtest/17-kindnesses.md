**› To Northgate** · *Day 1, 8:30 a.m.*

You set out from the drove road early, with your shadow long in front of you on the road. Northgate is where the drove roads come into the city: a wide street of yards and stables and inns, horse-dung and straw and the smell of hot iron from the farrier’s, carts drawn up in rows with their shafts on the ground. Nobody is loading anything. The carters are standing about in twos and threes in the yard gates with their hands in their pockets, not talking, and the drovers bringing cattle down to the Shambles go past them very politely.

**› Into Win Fenner's wash-house** · *Day 1, 8:31 a.m.*

A wash-house with a copper boiling in the corner and the windows streaming. Lines of other people’s shirts. Four children at the table doing nothing very quietly. A woman with red arms is at the copper with a paddle, her back to the door. Win Fenner, a laundress, Dunstan Fenner’s widow, is here.

**› Ask Win Fenner about Dunstan** · *Day 1, 8:31 a.m.*

‘He went to see the young ones came to no harm.’ The paddle goes round. ‘Fifty-one years old. He’d done the fells road ten thousand times and he couldn’t see a fight without going to stop it.’ Round. ‘The parish sent bread. I sent it back. The Hill’s stopped sending washing; they don’t want a felon’s widow’s hands on their shirts.’ Round. ‘We’ll manage.’ Behind her the children do not move.

**› Ask Win where her eldest is** · *Day 1, 8:31 a.m.*

The paddle stops. ‘Kester.’ She does not turn round. ‘He’s twelve. He’s been taking his father’s tools to the pawnshop in Cutler’s Lane, one at a time, the good chisels, and bringing back pennies, and telling me he found them.’ Her voice does not change. ‘I can’t stop him, because we have to eat, and I can’t let him, because they’re his father’s, and I can’t think which is worse, so I’m doing the washing.’
