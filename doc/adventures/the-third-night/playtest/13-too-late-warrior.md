**› Ask the reeve why the dead must stay on the rail** · *Day 1, 9:01 a.m.*

If she finds the city quiet and its ringleaders on the rail, she takes the toll money and goes home. If she finds it in rebellion, the lord’s law is ten hanged for every rider hurt and the carters’ row burned to the stones.’ He says it as if he had said it many times, to himself. ‘Three men on a rail, or thirty. I have kept this city twenty years.’ His hand is flat on the desk. ‘I did not hang them. I would have brought them to the lord’s court. The riders carried a warrant I was not shown.’ He looks at you for a long time. ‘You were in my house every day of your childhood,’ he says. ‘You and Hal. Tell me, then. You’ve come home and found your father on the rail, and you’re the only one in this city who can say anything to me I’d listen to.’ His voice does not rise. ‘What would you have me do?’

**› Tell Hal you have read his letter in Gault's coat** · *Day 3, 6:02 p.m.*

He goes white, and then he goes very still, the way his father does. ‘Yes,’ he says. ‘I wrote it.’ He does not look away. ‘My father said the riders would take them for the lord’s court. That’s what he believed; he hadn’t seen the warrant. The young ones were going to burn the counting-house, and the chain was already gone, and I thought: if the old ones are taken quietly on the road there’ll be no more fire, and they’ll be fined at the court, and that will be all.’ His hands are shaking and he puts them flat on his knees. ‘They hanged them on the road at sunrise. I have not slept. I have written the decree that keeps them there, in my best hand, because my father told me to and I had already done the other thing.’ He looks at you. ‘Do what you like with it. I’d tell Kit myself if I thought he’d let me finish.’ Brand gains 400 experience: found out who sent the riders.

**› Tell Hal he must tell his father** · *Day 3, 6:02 p.m.*

He nods, slowly, as if you had told him the time. ‘Yes.’ He gets up. ‘Tonight. Before supper.’ At the door he stops. ‘He’ll resign,’ he says. ‘Or he won’t, and that’ll be worse.’

**› Take your leave** · *Day 3, 6:02 p.m.*

‘Look after yourself,’ says Hal Hulme. ‘I mean it.’

## verdict

**… (waiting, to Day 4, 9:02 a.m.)**

> At dusk the captain rides in over the drawbridge with her riders behind her, and does not look up at the gatehouse as she passes under it, and goes to the Guildhall, where the Bench has laid supper.
> Word runs through Northgate in the small hours, door to door: Maudie Pardoe went up to the gatehouse with her bread knife for the cords, and the captain's riders took her at the gate-ward's door, and she is in the Guildhall cellar, and the captain has been woken.
> In the small hours Simon Hulme comes down the Cartway to the Bridge End alone, without a lantern, with a knife in his hand, to cut his cousin's husband down from the rail against the captain's word and answer for it. He is a quarter of an hour behind Maudie Pardoe. He stands under the gatehouse arch and looks at the empty step where the riders took her, and the riders look down at him from the leads, and nobody says anything at all.
> The captain hangs Maudie Pardoe from the gatehouse rail at nine in the morning, beside her husband, under the reeve's own decree, which is on the door in his son's best hand. Simon Hulme stands below with his hat in his hands and his knife still in his coat, where it was when he came down the Cartway a quarter of an hour behind her. He resigns that afternoon. In a week he is gone from Wrayford, nobody knows where. The three are taken down at noon and buried with her in Wade's Acre, four graves by the church door, and old Corbin digs them, and says nothing at all, and the reeve's house on the Hill stands empty, and nobody in the city will buy it.
> Kit Pardoe goes south with the captain, tied to a rider's saddle, for the lord's justice at Harbury. He looks back once, at the bridge.
> Hal Hulme, it is said, told his father what he did on the night of the rising, before supper, and his father sat with his head in his hands until dawn, and in the morning went to his court as usual.
