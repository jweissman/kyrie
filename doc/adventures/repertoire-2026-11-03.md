# After the inheritance: Kyrie's repertoire, and the stories it still cannot quite tell

*An independent critical survey of twenty-one completed or substantially developed adventures, read as a collective
repertoire. 3 November 2026. Spoilers throughout.*

## Scope and method

This survey treats a **work**, not a directory name, as the unit. Thus *The Third Night* is the `wrayford` module,
*Behind the Wainscot* is `hollins`, and *Fair Weather* is `skeel`. It includes the twenty adventure modules in
`data/adventures` and *Wapshott's Peregrine Circus*, a standing scenario rather than a province. Phase II of the
circus is considered only where it is implemented. It does not count *The Book of Faces*, which exists on another
branch but is not part of the checked-out collection.

I read the Kyr directly, the design and closing documents in `doc/adventures`, the available transcripts and
journeys, and the authors' own second-pass and friction reports. I also read the architecture's account of the
division among Deem, Kyr, the runtime, the assembler and the browser. I did not personally replay all twenty-one works.
Claims about cold-player experience therefore distinguish what transcripts demonstrate from what designs declare.

The collection has changed greatly since the survey of 26 October, when it contained seven adventures. That earlier
survey is evidence of what the later commissions were trying to correct. It is not an adequate description of what
exists now.

## 1. Judgment in brief

1. **The collection no longer tells one story.** *The Third Night*, *Gosse's Company*, *The Banns at Hessel*, *A
   Winter in Carth*, *The Liberty of the Ice*, *Fair Weather*, *Half the Hive*, *The Lull*, *The Quiet in the Sky*,
   *Behind the Wainscot* and the circus have substantially widened its forms. A funeral crisis, a theatrical
   competition, a marriage comedy, a heresy satire, a con, a wreck court, a communal migration, a labour dispute, an
   armistice among the dead, a giant household and a touring circus are not changes of scenery on *Low Water*.
2. **The old form nevertheless remains the collection's deep grammar.** A stranger enters a bounded community;
   notices its exceptional rule; gathers facts and favours across small stories; waits for a calendrical public
   occasion; and causes one of several named settlements, followed by a long account of fates. The inheritance may
   now be a toll, a theatre law, a holy day or a wreck custom rather than an ancient machine, but the dramaturgy is
   recognisably the same.
3. **The newer commissions successfully moved depth from cosmology toward social life.** The best recent scenes are
   not revelations below ground. They are the two mothers carrying their dead; Vane hearing a house laugh; Ninian
   asking whether anyone in Gaunt has been sorry; the lovers of Hume each surrendering a calling for the other; the
   Lady speaking to people she had been taught were imaginary; Wystan trying to write a song that is not for battle.
4. **Comedy is now a major strength, but mostly as authored event.** Pellam, Pentrice, Hessel, Sennet, Gaunt,
   Quittance and the circus contain real comic structures, not merely dry lines. Yet the player commonly prepares the
   joke and then watches prose deliver it. Kyrie can stage comedy better than it can let a player perform comedy.
5. **Romance has arrived, desire has not.** The collection now contains lovers, marriages, thwarted courtships,
   old attachments and queer possibility. Almost all belong to NPCs. The protagonist remains professionally useful,
   personally uncommitted and erotically blank.
6. **The player is still chiefly a catalytic outsider.** Even when the fiction names them kin, ratcatcher, assessor,
   player or searcher, their strongest verbs remain learn, ask, carry, show, tell, repair and choose. Other people
   possess the memorable wants. The protagonist facilitates their stories.
7. **The collection has learned to represent work, not yet to make work playable.** Water assessment, acting,
   ratcatching, wreck law, beekeeping, insurance, penance, carting, weaving and circus labour have excellent concrete
   vocabularies. Only rarely does a player acquire and exercise a practice with judgment over time. The central work
   is usually a sequence of authored actions, not a medium in which the player composes.
8. **Its prose remains unusually good and unusually uniform.** Plain present-tense observation, exact quantities,
   dry understatement, a reply followed by a small bodily action, and sentences built on absence remain the house
   music. Inserted voices—the Grand Tour handbook, plays within *Gosse's Company*, ballad, broadside, slate and
   sermon—have widened the register, but the narrator still makes nearly every place sound written by the same
   sensibility.
9. **The ending table has become the chief formal constraint.** Later works often derive endings honestly from prior
   causes and compose individualized fates. That is a genuine improvement. But twenty-one books repeatedly become a
   final civic classification: what settlement the town reached, then what happened to everyone. Tragedy is softened,
   comedy is closed, and ambiguity is itemized.
10. **Commission *The Six-Day Portrait* next.** It is a domestic chamber work in which the player paints a family
    portrait while the family cannot agree who belongs in it. Its central activity is perception and composition,
    not discovery; its stakes are one household's memory; its result is an authored object, not a verdict. It would
    force Kyrie to turn attention, interpretation and making into play without requiring another province, plane,
    antagonist or world-saving climax.

The table deliberately separates **dramatic form** from premise. “Mystery,” “comedy” or “epic” is less useful than
what the player repeatedly does and what shape the work takes under their hands.

### What the table shows

The new works are not merely more varied in subject. They have added four genuine formal capacities:

- **social complication** rather than pure revelation (*Hessel*, *Liberty*, *Gosse*);
- **collective transition** rather than restoration (*Half the Hive*, *The Lull*);
- **personal grief as the main pressure** (*The Third Night*, *Quiet*);
- **recurrence and ordinary life** rather than a one-use country (*Wapshott's*).

They have not yet displaced four old structural habits:

- a stranger as universal solvent;
- knowledge and errands as the safest verbs;
- a public deadline that gathers every thread;
- a named ending followed by an exhaustive fate ledger.

## 3. Collective literary identity

### From stewardship to social settlement

The first seven works formed a literature of **stewardship and inheritance**. Communities had misread old
arrangements; conscientious outsiders learned what the arrangement did and helped choose how to keep it. The expanded
collection is better described as a literature of **social settlement**. Imbar's people live inside customs that are
at once material and moral—tolls, oaths, wreck rights, holy days, theatrical licences, penances, insurance marks,
marriage banns. A visitor arrives when a custom can no longer contain the lives around it. The adventure asks what
new settlement can be made.

This is a meaningful broadening. The custom is no longer invariably wise, and restoration is no longer the privileged
answer. The toll can remain unjust after the funeral; the old holy day can be honoured without denying Monday; the
hive can become two communities; the ice's temporary law can expose the city's permanent one. The newer adventures
allow invention, albeit usually as one of the final settlements.

Kyrie's moral imagination is civic, pluralist and anti-villainous. It likes people who hold offices badly for good
reasons. It understands complicity better than evil. It respects labour, exact speech, promises and things maintained
by hands. It distrusts clean reform and clean revolution alike. It prefers a compromise that creates a practice over
a victory that removes a person. This is now a strong literary identity, not merely repetition.

Its danger is that every private hurt becomes public administration. Love becomes banns, grief becomes burial law,
art becomes a patent, fraud becomes a Court ruling, faith becomes Consistory, memory becomes a Moot. Kyrie gives
persons dignity by locating them in institutions; it has difficulty granting them a life that institutions cannot
exhaust.

### The house voice

The narrator remains one of the project's achievements: observant, restrained, funny without demanding laughter,
and concrete about work. Its characteristic moves are now visible enough to be mannerisms:

- second person and present tense;
- exact counts, weights, hours and prices;
- a short declaration of absence: nobody moves, nobody laughs, nothing answers;
- speech followed by a hand, cup, knife, glance or piece of clothing;
- irony produced by placing an official phrase beside a body that disproves it;
- a final image of an ordinary practice continuing after the plot.

This voice makes Imbar coherent. It is particularly suited to institutions, reticence, weather and moral comedy.
It is less able to say ecstasy, panic, erotic perception, loquacity, lyric landscape or a mind in disorder. The
inserted forms are therefore important: the Grand Tour's obsolete handbook, *The Alderman's Ghost*, the Frost Fair's
cries and libels, Wystan's attempted peace-song, Ninian's questions. They should be expanded until adventures can be
distinguished by syntax before they can be distinguished by nouns.

### Scale and the “ten times” problem

The collection has learned that ambition need not mean a deeper plane, but it still often means **more threads,
more rooms, more endings and more fates**. Quittance has thirteen cases and roughly a hundred rooms. Sennet has more
than seventy places and eleven endings. Wrayford, designed as the small corrective, grew to roughly 2,700 lines and
eleven endings. Expansion creates admirable texture but also dilutes recurrence: a person may have an arc in the
design and only two meetings in play.

The next mark of ambition should be density, not acreage: the same six people seen at breakfast, work, quarrel,
reconciliation and aftermath; the same object changing under the player's hand; a scene whose meaning changes on
repetition. Kyrie has proved it can enumerate a city. It should prove it can remain in a room.

## 4. Convergence: culture, craft and habit

Not every repetition is a defect. The collection needs a deliberate distinction among the culture of Imbar, the
editorial house style, and an overused plot machine.

| Recurrence | Critical reading | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Weights, marks, bonds, public oaths, reckonings and exact prices | **Imbar's culture.** The world understands moral relations through material accounting. | Keep, but let some stories occur where accounting is obscene or useless: the nursery, sickroom, bed, dance. |
| Work described through tools and procedures | **A signature strength.** Felt, water, stage machinery, wreck law, bee dance, insurance and penance give fantasy a lived economy. | Keep and deepen into player practice rather than exposition. |
| Bells, fairs, holy days, hearings, funerals and other public occasions | Partly culture, partly a useful presentation device that gathers distributed threads. | Keep public occasions, but stop making every book culminate in one. Write endings that happen privately or by attrition. |
| Old women as memory, authority or hinge | Sometimes cultural office; often casting habit. Abigail Cherrett is an advance because she is appetite and error, not merely key-holder. | Vary age, gender and social function; let memory reside in rivals, children, fools and hostile witnesses. |
| Conscientious clerk or exact professional companion | A beloved type: Isaura, the Valuer, Ada, claims people and assessors. Now close to self-imitation. | Rest the competent conscience. Commission companions who are needy, vain, unreliable, sensuous or wrong. |
| Exceptional local law stated as a sentence | Kyrie's strongest design technology: attention builds houses; intention persists; living hands kill; wreck law excludes survivors. | Keep selectively. A repertoire where every place has a metaphysical gimmick becomes an anthology of premises, not a world. |
| Secret history revealed through documents and tellings | The language's grain and an authorial pleasure. | Do not forbid mysteries. Require some works whose important facts are known on page one. |
| Public authority hears assembled cases | Board, Hearing, Court, Consistory, Moot, Bench, Drawing and more. This is now the most conspicuous repeated climax. | Retire the hearing for several commissions. A conversation is not improved by putting five chairs behind it. |
| Named ending matrix plus individualized fates | Technically impressive and often responsive; formally homogenizing. | Use state-composed aftermath where appropriate, but commission works that simply stop, recur, or continue without closure. |
| “More depth” answered by buried city or cosmology | Much reduced in the later corpus, though Sennet, Quiet and Quittance still descend or cross planes. | The correction is working. Protect the ordinary layers of the circus and future domestic work from escalation. |
| Dry declarative cadence and negative sentences | House style with real authority; also a tic recognized by several authors' own critiques. | Preserve an editorial baseline, then commission explicit countervoices and measure them in cold play. |

### Repetitions that now deserve a moratorium

For the next three major adventures, avoid unless the premise makes them unavoidable:

- a council, court, hearing or vote as climax;
- a buried predecessor city;
- an honest ledger-holder companion;
- a late revelation that the custom is an ancient machine;
- more than five named endings;
- an epilogue that accounts for every named person;
- a protagonist hired on arrival by the first important elder they meet.

This is not a ban on Imbar's culture. It is time for that culture to become background pressure rather than plot
furniture.

## 5. Negative space after twenty-one works

The old survey's absences must be revised. Comedy, tragedy, political procedure, satire, romance, domestic scenes,
picaresque movement, horror and creative labour all now have representatives. The question is whether their defining
experience has become playable.

### Forms and experiences

| Form or experience | Current achievement | What remains unlearned |
|---|---|---|
| **Comedy** | Strong authored comedy in Pellam, Hessel, Gosse, Sennet, Gaunt, Quittance and Wapshott's | Comic timing as a player skill; improvisation; failure that becomes a better joke; a protagonist who is ridiculous rather than competent |
| **Tragedy** | *The Third Night* and parts of *Quiet* place unavoidable cost near the centre | A work that narrows rather than branches; irreversible error by the protagonist; an ending not consoled by fates, custom or a superior settlement |
| **Political intrigue** | Excellent institutions, factions, laws and public processes | Private coalition, patronage, seduction, jealousy, compromise made in rooms; an ally who changes sides for personal rather than evidentiary reasons |
| **Philosophical dialogue** | Ideas embodied in laws; occasional disputations and strong questions | Sustained argument where positions answer each other, the player can formulate a claim, and no physical exhibit adjudicates the question |
| **Professional tradecraft** | Rich depiction of assessor, actor, con artist, wreck court, beekeeper, insurer, penitent | Practice learned and exercised over repeated cases; judgment under uncertainty; improvement visible in the quality of work rather than a skill check |
| **Satire** | Bought penance, theatrical censorship, insurance and manners are genuine social satires | Satire that implicates the protagonist's appetite; a comic narrator with partiality; institutions transformed by ridicule rather than a correct ruling |
| **Romance** | Many NPC romances and marriages; tender old attachments; possible queer relation in Wrayford | The protagonist desiring and risking rejection; intimacy not converted into a reward, companion or ending flag; erotic and bodily register |
| **Domestic drama** | Carth's farm, Cherrett rooms, Whitbread house, Pardoe and Hobday homes | Repetition of meals and care; parent-child or spouse conflict as the whole plot; dependency, illness and boredom; no public climax to validate the household |
| **Picaresque** | Roads, tours and circus circuits; episodic metropolitan cases | A morally unstable protagonist carried by appetite; episodes that do not resolve into one central civic case; change through accumulation rather than revelation |
| **Creative labour** | Gosse's play and Wapshott's acts are major advances; songs, charts, wax stairs and bread recur | Composing the thing: selecting, revising, discarding and living with an aesthetic result the game can show back differently |
| **Exploration** | Abundant authored places, dungeons and planar travel | Curiosity without plot entitlement; field observation, mapping and return; a landscape that is not secretly an argument or machine |
| **Mystery** | The collection's native form, now with more human culprits and social complicity | Mysteries without dossier assembly; ambiguity that remains; detection through character and motive rather than documents and hidden rooms |
| **Horror** | Dream houses, Reflected, cat at giant scale, cold, dead armies, creditor-fire | Sustained vulnerability in a small cast; mistrust among companions; panic, contamination and the possibility that knowledge worsens rather than masters fear |
| **Epic** | Vast cosmologies and wars in *Thrume*, *Patient Star* and *Let One Remember* | Epic through duration, recurrence and human cost; a war seen through a camp, family or supply train rather than summarized armies and planes |
| **Joy** | Fair, theatre, circus, hive and comic settlements supply real warmth | Joy as a sustained goal and activity, not relief before or after crisis; celebration the player helps make rather than attends |
| **Failure** | Bad settlements and deaths exist; retreats persist in the broader game | Productive failure as the middle of a story; damaged work, embarrassment, apology and trying again without reload or terminal classification |

### Relationships

The collection now knows employers and employees, institutions and members, parents and children, siblings, old
lovers, courting pairs, artistic companies, rivals, teachers and students. What it still rarely knows is **mutual
dependency over time**. People have arcs, but the protagonist does not have to wake tomorrow and continue living
with the person they disappointed yesterday.

The underdeveloped relationships are:

- the protagonist as spouse, lover or rejected suitor;
- the protagonist as parent or daily carer;
- friendship in which both parties ask things and remember refusals;
- creative collaborators who disagree about the work, not the mission;
- a companion who can interpret the protagonist's acts wrongly;
- a rival who is neither obstacle nor eventual ally;
- an enemy with a shared happy history;
- children acting as subjects rather than victims, heirs or moral witnesses.

### Registers and voices

The repertoire now covers dry wit, melancholy, civic seriousness, pastoral warmth, elegy, eerie domesticity, pulp
wonder and broad theatrical affection. It remains short of:

- lyric rapture and sensuous abundance;
- uncontrolled anger in a voice the work endorses;
- shame from inside rather than observed in another;
- erotic attention;
- panic and broken syntax;
- garrulous, digressive first-person storytelling;
- a narrator who is partisan, mistaken or comic;
- silence that is not merely represented by “nobody speaks.”

### Structures of protagonist agency

Kyrie's protagonist can now lie, perform some stage business, keep vigil, redirect a swarm, cause a true wound, join
a circus, and enter a confidence trick. Those are substantial gains. They still seldom:

- originate a project rather than accept a local aim;
- state what they believe in words the game remembers;
- choose the form of a made object;
- want a particular person;
- perform routine care repeatedly;
- make an error before knowing it is an error;
- acquire a habit through practice;
- change their own mind in a way the fiction can recognize;
- remain after the adventure as a member of the community rather than a remembered visitor.

### What Kyrie has not yet learned to express

The central deficit is no longer “persons rather than evidence” in the simple sense; the later works contain many
persons. It is **subjectivity made operational**.

Kyrie represents what the world knows, what happened, what someone told you, where a person stands, what a measure
contains and which settlement obtained. It does not easily represent:

1. **Interpretation.** Two players can see the same face, poem or quarrel and make different meanings without one
   being the hidden correct fact.
2. **Practice.** A repeated human activity changes the practitioner and produces better, worse or simply different
   work.
3. **Attachment.** A person matters because this protagonist chose them, not because the module placed them at the
   hinge of a plot.
4. **Self-implication.** The protagonist's vanity, desire, fear or prior act creates the difficulty.
5. **Revision.** A first attempt can be reworked rather than graded, branched or undone.
6. **Continuance.** Life after a bad day is not an epilogue but the next playable morning.
7. **Ambiguity with consequences.** The game responds to a reading without confirming that the reading was true.

These are not requests for a personality quiz or a universal emotion simulation. They are invitations for authored
works to declare a few interpretive and relational dimensions, and for the general system to let those dimensions be
expressed, revised and shown back.

## 6. A selective portfolio

These five premises address the most consequential gaps. None has an ancient mechanism, buried city, public hearing
or necessary antagonist. Each can remain inside Imbar without turning Imbar's civic identity off.

### 6.1 *The Six-Day Portrait* — domestic drama and creative labour

**Premise.** In a rain-bound manor outside an ordinary generated town, the protagonist has been commissioned to paint
the retirement portrait of **Magdalen Vey**, who ran the district's post for forty-one years. Six sittings are paid
for. On the first morning, Magdalen says the portrait is to show “my family.” Nobody in the house agrees who that is.

Her widowed son **Edmund** expects to stand at her right. His estranged husband **Lio**, who still keeps the orchard
accounts, has been asked to leave before the final sitting. **Nan Vey**, twelve, wants the post-rider **Sukey Marr** in
the picture because Sukey taught her to ride; Edmund regards her as a servant. Magdalen's elder sister **Clare** has
not spoken to her since a letter went undelivered during the fever year. The housemaid **Pella** is visibly pregnant
and has asked not to be painted. Magdalen herself wants to wear the postmaster's red coat, which formally belongs to
her successor and no longer fits.

There is no secret that resolves the family. The undelivered letter can be found, but it proves only that Magdalen
made a choice and still thinks it was necessary.

**Central activity.** Each day the player chooses a sitting, arrangement, light, objects and one study to make: hands,
mouth, coat, empty chair, window, family group. Conversation changes because sitting still beside someone is its own
situation. The player can scrape out, paint over and revise, losing time and preserving a visible trace. Domestic
work continues around the painting: meals, rain leaks, a child's lesson, the orchard payroll, changing a sickbed.

**Structure.** Six days, but no catastrophe at the end. The canvas must leave because its carrier comes on the
seventh morning. The dramatic movement is from preliminary studies through composition to the last decision: who is
present, who is represented by an object or absence, and whether Magdalen is shown as office, mother, old woman or
some unstable combination.

**Resolutions.** Not named moral endings. The game describes the finished picture from the choices and revisions,
then gives three viewings: Magdalen alone; the household together; the portrait encountered years later in one of
several possible places. People may reconcile, separate or merely recognize how the painter saw them. A beautiful
portrait can be cruel. A clumsy one can tell the truth someone needed.

**Why it is different.** The player makes the central object. Attention is not clue extraction. The household does
not need civic ratification. The climax is aesthetic and relational judgment, and no arrangement is the secret
correct family.

**Authorability pressure.** Kyr needs authored **facets of a work** (composition, emphasis, inclusion, finish) and
actions that revise rather than simply assert; the runtime needs a durable work assembled from choices; presentation
needs a compact studio view or textual canvas that shows the current composition. Deem is barely involved beyond
placing the house near a town and supplying weather or local names. The faces, conversations, aesthetic
consequences and six-day rhythm are authored content, not general emotion simulation.

### 6.2 *The Advocate at Candlemas* — argument and professional practice

**Premise.** A junior advocate arrives in a county town for the winter assizes because their principal is ill. Four
small causes share the same week: a girl dismissed from a choir for refusing a prayer; two neighbours who disagree
where a hedge used to stand; a veteran accused of wearing a medal that is his brother's; and an inn charged for
serving a ghost, on the theory that the dead do not pay excise. None conceals a conspiracy. The cases overlap only
because the same people live in the same town.

**Characters.** **Master Avice Rook**, the sick principal, gives brilliant contradictory advice from bed; **Tern**, a
court usher, knows procedure and hates rhetoric; **Joan Bell**, opposing counsel, is the protagonist's former friend
and exact professional equal; the four clients want outcomes that are not always compatible with the arguments that
could win them.

**Central activity and structure.** Interview, choose which facts not to use, formulate a theory of each case, cite or
distinguish precedents, negotiate, and make short arguments interrupted by questions. Winning one broad proposition
can damage another client. The protagonist's style—literal, equitable, theatrical, conciliatory—emerges from acts,
not a creation choice. The week includes bad mornings after bad arguments and chances to amend a position.

**Resolutions.** Cases may be won, lost, settled or abandoned. The deeper resolution is professional: what sort of
advocate the protagonist has become, whether Joan will work with them again, and which clients still believe they
were represented even when they lost.

**Why it is different.** Kyrie has many hearings but almost no argument. This work makes reasons, concessions and
questions the play rather than the wrapper around evidence.

**Authorability pressure.** Kyr needs claims with authored supports and incompatibilities, plus player-selectable
speech positions; the runtime needs interlocutors to respond to the argument actually advanced, not merely to facts
held. Presentation needs a brief/case view that is not the journal and makes trade-offs legible. Deem can supply
minor clients in later reuse, but these four cases should be authored tightly. No universal natural-language debate
engine is required.

### 6.3 *The Long Convalescence* — care, dependency and continuance

**Premise.** After an expedition begins badly, the protagonist is not the rescuer but the injured person. They spend
six weeks above a cooper's shop in a town they meant to pass through. **Mara Vale**, the cooper, has agreed to house
them for money their company has not yet sent. Her father is losing language after a seizure; her son wants the spare
room; her apprentice needs the bench Mara has abandoned to provide care. The protagonist can walk to the window in
week one, the landing in week two, the street later. The adventure is what happens while strength returns.

**Central activity.** Accept or refuse help; keep company; read aloud; prepare medicine badly or well; take on
bookkeeping from bed; listen repeatedly to a story that changes; write to absent companions; mediate only when asked;
learn one small cooper's operation as the body permits. Care flows both ways and is not a replenishment button.

**Structure.** Six playable weeks in recurring rooms. The same breakfast and staircase acquire new meanings.
Recovery is neither linear nor wholly controllable. The household's problems do not wait, but none is an antagonist.

**Resolutions.** The protagonist may leave as soon as able, remain through the first finished cask, arrange different
care, or discover that being needed is not the same as belonging. Some relationships worsen despite good care. The
last scene is a departure or an ordinary morning, not a fate ledger.

**Why it is different.** It makes vulnerability and routine the adventure. The protagonist cannot solve the house
by superior mobility, violence or evidence, and must continue after mistakes.

**Authorability pressure.** The runtime needs injury and recovery states with forecastable limits, scheduled repeated
care, and consequences for accepting as well as giving help. Kyr needs actions whose value depends on repetition and
relationship history. Presentation needs recurrence without verbatim room text and a humane time-skip that previews
what routine will occur. The particular household, illness and ethics must remain authored; do not create a generic
care score.

### 6.4 *A Map of No Use* — exploration and interpretive ambiguity

**Premise.** A small natural-philosophical society pays the protagonist to spend one mild season mapping the upper
reaches of a river that disappears among chalk hills. The river has no lost city, no machine and no monster at its
source. It has tributaries, mills, nesting birds, disputed names, seasonal labour and three incompatible local maps,
each useful for a different purpose.

**Characters.** **Iven Scrip**, an elderly surveyor who cannot climb but distrusts every picturesque view; **Mell
Hart**, a poacher and superlative guide who lies about ownership and never about weather; **Sada Orris**, a young
botanist who wants to publish first; several households who do not want their paths made legible to tax collectors.

**Central activity.** Choose routes, take bearings, sketch, name or preserve local names, collect specimens, camp,
return, compare observations and revise the map. Publishing detail helps some users and exposes others. Blank space
can mean ignorance, discretion or beauty.

**Structure and resolutions.** Three expeditions and two returns to the same base, across a season. There is no final
source revelation. The resulting map can be practical, scholarly, protective, proprietary or openly plural, and
companions may sign, refuse or annotate it. Years later another traveller uses it for a purpose the protagonist did
not control.

**Why it is different.** Exploration is the end, not a corridor to plot truth. The landscape contains competing
interpretations without a hidden canonical map.

**Authorability pressure.** Deem and the assembler matter most here: generated terrain must expose stable features,
local names, work and seasonal change. Kyr needs observations with source and uncertainty, and a work assembled from
selected observations. The runtime must support revisiting field sites after weather and time changes. Presentation
needs an annotatable map or a textual equivalent. The ethical conflicts and companion voices should be authored.

### 6.5 *The Feast of Second Chances* — joy, failure and player-made comedy

**Premise.** A neighbourhood whose annual feast was cancelled after a disastrous quarrel tries again. The protagonist
volunteers to coordinate it despite having no authority. The feast needs a meal, music, games, tables and a procession;
every contributor has a history with somebody else. Nothing terrible happens if it fails. People are embarrassed,
hungry and angry, and next year remains possible.

**Characters.** **Bess Loom**, magnificent cook and impossible delegator; **Odo and Piers**, former musical partners
who each claim the other cannot keep time; **Reeve Ansel**, who will lend tables but not apologize for last year;
**Tib**, thirteen, inventor of games whose rules change whenever she loses; **Father Coil**, cheerful atheist and
owner of the only dry hall.

**Central activity.** Recruit, schedule, rehearse, taste, decorate, improvise around shortages, decide whom to seat
together, and then work the feast while it goes wrong. Preparation creates comic affordances rather than a hidden
success total. A collapsed pudding can become the game everyone remembers; perfect music can leave no room for
dancing.

**Structure and resolutions.** Five days of preparation and one evening rendered in short interactive beats. The
morning after includes cleanup with whoever stayed. There is no best feast. The work remembers what people call it
next year and which failed invention becomes custom.

**Why it is different.** Joy is the project, not an epilogue reward. Failure can improve the story. The protagonist
creates a social event without judging a town or defeating an antagonist.

**Authorability pressure.** Kyr needs combinable prepared elements that create affordances during an Occasion;
the runtime needs schedules, conflicts and recoverable failures; presentation needs a legible run-of-show and quick
beats during the feast. Deem could later generate ingredients or minor contributors, but the comic relationships and
payoffs must be authored. Do not reduce the feast to one quality measure.

## 7. Authorability: what should become general, and what should remain written

The repertoire's friction reports repeatedly tempt the project toward general social simulation. That would be a
mistake. Kyrie needs a few stronger **forms of authorial declaration**, not a universal model of love, comedy or
art.

### General capabilities worth building

| Layer | Capability | Why it generalizes |
|---|---|---|
| **Kyr** | Revisions: change an authored work-state while preserving that it was revised | Painting, argument, rehearsal, maps, repairs and plans all need attempts rather than binary facts |
| **Kyr** | Authored interpretations or positions that may conflict without one being factual truth | Philosophy, criticism, romance, testimony and ambiguous exploration need consequences without epistemic confirmation |
| **Kyr** | Relationship memory keyed to acts between particular people, including refusal, dependence and mistaken reading | The current `knows`/stance grain privileges exhibits; repeated human relations need a small declarative counterpart |
| **Kyr** | Repeated practices whose history can be queried by the author | Care, craft, rehearsal and professional learning require “third time,” “after failure,” and “done together” |
| **Runtime** | Recoverable failure that transforms available action instead of merely withholding success | Comedy, craft and convalescence need the next beat after a bad attempt |
| **Runtime** | Recurring scenes and routines with variation, previewed time cost and interruption | Domestic life, tours, seasons and professional cases should not require one rule per clock tick |
| **Runtime** | Composed artifacts with authored facets and provenance | Portraits, maps, plays, meals and arguments need to be durable things the world can inspect |
| **Runtime** | People reacting to a selected claim, composition or act, not only to held knowledge | Converts dialogue from topic unlocking into answer and counter-answer |
| **Deem** | Stable generated hooks for audiences, households, local work, terrain and history that authored modules can read | Gosse and the circus proved the value of adventures using the world rather than annexing another country |
| **Presentation** | A reusable “workbench” representation: arrange a small set of parts, preview, revise and commit | Casting, painting, mapping, feasts and arguments differ in content but share a need for visible composition |
| **Presentation** | Time-skip with routine and risk preview | Seasons and convalescence must compress repetition without making time opaque |
| **Presentation** | Recap of changing relationships and authored projects, separate from the fact journal | A notebook of facts cannot carry an evolving portrait, friendship or rehearsal |

### Things best kept particular

Do **not** generalize:

- a universal emotion meter;
- procedurally generated romance;
- a single numerical quality score for art, meals or arguments;
- generic “NPC persuasion” that replaces written reasons;
- automatic comedy assembled from failure tables;
- a morality axis that declares which interpretation is correct;
- one ending composer expected to close every literary form.

The portrait's family politics, the advocate's cases, the convalescent household's needs, the map's ethical choices
and the feast's comic payoffs are literature. Kyr should let an author state and recombine them; it should not write
them.

### A practical development order

1. Build the smallest artifact/facet/revision kernel for one commissioned work, not an abstract creative system.
2. Give the browser one compact workbench view, with a textual fallback in the terminal.
3. Add authored reactions to the artifact's facets.
4. Add repeated-practice queries only where the commission demonstrates a second use.
5. Defer generated households, generic cases and large-scale Deem composition until two authored works reveal the
   stable contract.

This order follows the lesson of the existing corpus: the best general capabilities emerged when a specific work
pressed on the engine. Generality should be earned by two different literary uses.

## 8. What should be commissioned next

Commission **The Six-Day Portrait**.

It is not the largest proposal and should not be allowed to become one. Its value is that it crosses the greatest
number of remaining boundaries at the lowest architectural and literary risk:

- It gives the protagonist a **practice**, not merely a role.
- It makes **interpretation** consequential without disguising a correct answer as ambiguity.
- It places **domestic relationships** at the entire scale of the work.
- It produces an object whose form is visibly the player's, teaching Kyrie **making and revision**.
- It can express attachment, exclusion, vanity, age, bodily change and chosen family without requiring romance to
  become a generic system.
- It requires no new province, plane, dungeon, antagonist, council or cosmology.
- It is small enough for cold players to traverse several genuinely different compositions, which is essential if
  the artifact system is to be judged rather than merely demonstrated.

The commission should impose hard constraints: one house and its grounds; seven principal people at most; six days;
no hidden supernatural layer; no hearing; no more than three external excursions; no named ending menu; no complete
fate ledger. The portrait itself is the ending.

After that, commission *The Advocate at Candlemas* if the goal is to deepen dialogue, or *A Map of No Use* if the
goal is to connect authored literature to generated worlds. Do not commission another metropolis yet. Quittance and
Sennet have proved that Kyrie can contain one. The next advance is to prove that it can see six people clearly enough
to paint them—and let the player decide what seeing them means.
