# The Quiet in the Sky — synopsis

*Spoilers throughout.*

## The situation before the protagonist arrives

For nine hundred years the warrior dead of Imbar have fought every day on a field in Ysgard and risen healed every dusk
(the Hour). Whatever is done to their bodies is undone at the Hour. What they make is not. They are sorted at the
Coming-in by the hour they died: before noon to Morrowhall, after noon to Evenhall.

Forty-one days before the story opens, Edith Marl came up the Pass dressed as a picker. She is a living fuller's widow
from Wendmere, whose husband and four-year-old daughter Halvard Eld's free company burned in the vats twenty years ago.
She killed Halvard, by then champion of Evenhall, with a fulling-knife in the press at the ford. At the Hour he did not
get up. Nobody up there remembers that a living hand kills truly in Ysgard. Hild Varley, Marshal of Morrow, was nearest,
and blew the Halt out of fear. Since then the Field has been quiet, in the belief that death has come back for
everyone. In the quiet the dead have built a town out of their shields, Halt, around Halvard's body. Torold Hame, his
sworn brother, stands over it.

Two days before the story opens, Ned Whitlock, the guide's sixteen-year-old son, was killed by robbers at the Hob Bridge
below the valley with his father's blackthorn in his hand.

## The acts

**Prologue: Larum.** No clock runs. The silence. Bet Garside, who cannot sleep and bangs pans. The ballad of Halvard Eld
over the fire, which cheerfully tells how he burned Wendmere. WENDMERE cut in the beam of the loft by a widow who stayed
a week two months ago. Jory, the guide, with two cups on his table and a son three days late. Nance's two-hundred-year-
old sourdough. A chapel bell rung at dusk for no reason anyone knows, with I. S. cut on its frame.

**Act I: The Quiet** (the first day up). Cleeve has no column for breathing. The dead stare at the protagonist's breath.
Then Halt, its lanes of banners and its swapping-blankets. The ring of spears and the frost on Halvard's face. At noon a
boy walks in out of the snow; neither Cleeve nor the reader can sort him at first, until he says his name and the
reader remembers the second cup. At the first Hour, at the ring, Torold names the Testing for the fourth morning.

**Act II: The Breather** (days two to four). The town's own lives and the law learned three ways:

- by fault, sparring with Mab Treloar, whose split lip does not mend at the Hour;
- by reading, Halvard's wound with a priest's eye, and the Sitters' story of Ithel the Smith;
- from Edith, found in the Scrapes by her breath.

The midpoint is a dread, not a solution. The truth will end the peace, because the dead are safe from each other. Hild,
told it, asks the protagonist to kill one man at the Testing so that the fear holds. Meanwhile there are other things
to see to: bread to make rise, a marriage across the line, a song, a strike, a grave, a father to tell.

**Act III: The Testing** (the fourth morning). Evenhall goes down to the ford to fight itself, unless Torold has stood
down (he has buried Halvard, or knows he will never rise) or the Field cannot be fought on (the Runners would not clear
it). At the Hour everyone gets up, or one man does not.

**Act IV: The Morning After.** The horns, or none. The epilogue tells what each of the forty days' makings became, and
Larum hears the sky, loud or quiet.

## The endings (each from causes)

| Ending | Causes |
|---|---|
| **Halt** | No Testing; the truth spoken to all Halt; Hild brave. Peace chosen without fear |
| **The Long Quiet** | No Testing; the truth never told (two endings, depending on whether the protagonist knows it). Peace on not knowing |
| **The Kept Fear** | The Testing held, and a living hand killed a man of Even in it. Peace on a lie |
| **Half a Field** | One hall goes back to the Daily and fights itself: Even, if the Testing was held and Hild was brave; Morrow, if the truth came out and Hild was not |
| **The Daily** | The Testing held, everyone rose, Hild not brave. Two endings, depending on whether the protagonist ever went down to see Halt |

The region goes on after its chapter closes. Halt stands or is trampled. Cleeve's ledger has a new column or does not.
Torold stands at the Coming-in at the Hour listening for Larum's bell, or by the clear ice on the Old Ice, or over the
bier. Larum hears the sky or does not, and so does the country behind it.

## Themes, as carried by action

- **What lasts.** Where nothing done to a body stays done, the makings carry the story: bread, a song, a marriage, a
  bench, a plough, a grave. The epilogue is an inventory of them.
- **The truth that does not save.** The collection's habit is minds changed by exhibits. Here the exhibit (a lip that
  does not mend, a wound, Edith's confession) endangers the peace. What saves it is a grave for Torold and courage for
  Hild, both given by people.
- **Fault.** The protagonist can learn the law by maiming a friend for eternity, can kill to keep a peace, and can give
  a living woman up to be killed.
- **The arbitrary line.** The sorting by hour, the boy at noon, the column that was never a law.
- **Demobilisation.** Who a war needed and never paid (the Runners); what its songs were for (Wystan); what a
  professional killer does at ten in the morning (Mab); whose trade it was (Gorm).
- **The celebrity of killers.** Larum prints the ballads that brought Edith up the Pass.
