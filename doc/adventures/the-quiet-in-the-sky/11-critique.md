# The Quiet in the Sky — the second, harder reading

*Stage 6: the built work read as a finished book and a game, by its author, trying not to be its advocate. Then what
was revised because of the reading, and what was not.*

## What I think works

- **The premise does work in play.** The law of the Field is also its moral question, and the transcripts show it.
  - The moment of the first spar, when Mab's lip does not close at the Hour and the dead look from her mouth to you, is
    the best thing in the work. The protagonist learns what they are by what they have done, and the work did not have
    to tell anyone anything.
  - Hild's request comes straight out of the law. Once she knows, she sees at once that one true death would save the
    peace, and that there is a living person standing in her room.
- **Larum earns its place.** It is not a hub. It is a parish with a mood (insomnia), a trade (ballads and old iron), a
  custom whose reason is lost (the bell), and three people whose stories turn out to be the Field's: the second cup,
  the loft, the Mother. Coming down cold and going into the bakehouse is the loop the game's README promises.
- **The Ned reveal works on the page.** In the transcript `larum.txt` the boy says "Ned Whitlock. From Larum", and the
  table with two cups, seen three hours earlier, turns over. It is cheap to build and lands. Jory's scene does not
  console, and I am glad of that.
- **The Runners' strike puts a caste's dignity at the hinge of the plot**, and the bench costs the strike, because Cuddy
  keeps a bargain. That is a real choice, and it came from the critique, not from the first draft.
- **The cold** is the only systemic pressure, and it shapes play: the halls, the Oven and the warren become places you
  plan around. Two random walkers froze, so it is not ornamental.

## What I am not satisfied with

1. **The protagonist still mostly witnesses.** Fault is now possible (the scar, the strike in the press, Edith given
   up), and the Testing's outcome turns on what the protagonist did. But the protagonist wants nothing for themselves.
   No one up here loves them. They carry other people's makings: Nance's bread, Ansel's letter, Wystan's verses.
   The repertoire's charge stands, at a lower volume.
2. **The decisive acts are mostly conversations.** Torold stands down by a topic, Hild is made brave by a topic, and the
   truth is spoken by a topic. The physical acts (the crock, the climb past the wolves, the bear, the carrying of the
   body, the press) surround the cruxes rather than being them. The burial is the exception, and it is the scene that
   reads best.
3. **The town is a chorus, not a cast.** "The dead of Halt" is one group with a crossbowwoman and a man in a mailed
   hood inside it, who speak only in group topics. The day-by-day signs added in revision help (WEDNESDAY on a shield;
   WHO HAS MADE WHAT). But a town of hundreds deserves three or four more named citizens with errands.
4. **Gorm, Saltash and Ambler are functions.** Gorm's arc (war profiteer to ploughwright) is two topics. It should be a
   story: his first plough breaking on the Field's iron, and the Runners clearing a strip for him.
5. **The Testing day is thin for a protagonist who is not at the ford.** It is a chapter, a heard horn and a dusk. A
   player in Halt that day has nothing new to do. The day should have its own Halt: people deciding, one by one, whether
   to go and watch.
6. **The ending table is a menu again.** It has five outcomes with sub-variants. They come from causes, and the epilogue
   lists the makings rather than choosing a stance. But the collection's other habit is here too: closing on a custom in
   the iterative present (Torold at the Coming-in every evening at the Hour; Jory every seventh day; the bell rung "for
   the ones that do not get up"). I wrote it knowing the repertoire's warning, and I kept the custom where it is earned
   (Torold) and did not cut it where it is only lovely (the chapel door).
7. **The house style's tics are in the prose.**
   - People "look at you a long time" a dozen times.
   - Beats of "He is quiet."
   - Sentences built on absence ("Nobody claps.").
   - Wystan, Aulus and Bet are distinct voices; Torold and Jory are the same laconic man in two coats.
8. **Two revelations rest on rolls a build may not make.**
   - Beating Aulus at Tabula is a lore roll. The gossip after two losses is the backstop, which is right.
   - Reading the wound is a rites roll, and its failure told too little. *Revised*: a failed reading now places the
     killer in the press, where the Runners go.
9. **The elegy is unplayed.** It is the most interesting wrong choice in the work: telling a skald a true and terrible
   thing, and getting the best song he ever made, which armies will sing. It is written and gated, and no journey has
   walked it. *Revised*: Even now goes down to the Testing singing it, heard all over the Field. It still changes no
   outcome, and perhaps it should: a Torold who has heard his friend sung like that may be harder to stand down.
10. **Comedy is texture, not structure.**
    - Cleeve's mounting distress is a structure, a running gag with a payoff.
    - The wedding's comedy is a paragraph.
    - Nothing in the work gets worse in a way that is funnier than before.

## What the second pass changed

Between the first playable build and this one, all because of reading the transcripts or the screen:

- **Halt grows and answers.** `days.kyr` adds a street-sign naming a day nobody remembers, WHO HAS MADE WHAT, a queue at
  the Oven, plaited ribbons, a hummed song, the town talking low once it knows, the Sitters criticising the antiques,
  and offerings on the empty bier.
- **The people grow.**
  - Ned after his father ("He brought my cap ... so it'd be warm. It isn't warm now.").
  - Ned on being neither one thing nor the other.
  - Ansel's death at eighteen in the evening rearguard at his father's ford, knighted that morning in the rain.
  - Mab told of it.
  - Ansel on whether he is happy ("She snores").
  - Torold on why he let Edith go.
- **Hild can be made brave by being heard, not only argued with.** She tells her secret about Wrenning, and one line
  answers it. Before this, a charmless warrior could not reach the best ending, and the scene was a dice roll.
- **Edith's confession is a choice.** Bringing her past the ring had confessed her automatically. It is now a grave
  action, warned.
- **Every description opens on its essential**, because the browser shows only the opening.
- **Portraits fit the people.**
- **Coherence fixes found in play**: fifteen, listed in `10-playthroughs.md`, from Ned's mask to Torold's epilogue.

## What tenfold ambition would mean here

Not ten times the rooms. It would mean:

- **The other hosts.** Ysgard is canonically vast, and this is one field. Somewhere north over the Old Ice there are
  other halls, other sortings (by the weapon you held? by the reason you fought?), and other wars that have not
  stopped. A second author's region of the same plane should meet this one at the Old Ice.
- **The dead of Imbar's own history as witnesses.** The Clear holds every century. A soldier for each layer, asked about
  the piece of iron you brought up, would make the Field a library of the world's wars told by people who contradict
  each other. Cassian's blade is one instance of this, and there should be twenty.
- **The protagonist's own dead.** In a generated world the protagonist has a history (an origin, a brother, a mother who
  wrote). Someone they knew who died under arms could walk in out of the snow while they are there. The engine can name
  kin; it cannot yet place a person from one module's history into another's.
- **A death that is the protagonist's to own.** The `ending_breath` text in friction 21 is the most moving idea the work
  could not express: die on the Field and be asked your hour by Cleeve, and have one.

## Status, honestly

- **Implemented**: everything in the inventories. That is twenty-five files of Kyr, about 1,760 long lines of it, with
  about 1,330 lines of documents.
- **Tested mechanically**: fourteen journeys and six random walks (see 10). The elegy, Gorm's plough, Hild's blessing,
  Torold killing Edith, and any won fight against the bear or the press are built but untested.
- **Encountered in ordinary play**: by me, reading every transcript and six screenshots. I am the author, so this is not
  a cold player's evidence. The one run without privileged preparation is the generated-world wanderer of the placed
  test, and a script chose its steps.
- **Evaluated as satisfying**: the spar and the scar; the Ned reveal; the burial on the Old Ice; Edith at the ring; the
  Testing blocked by a girl on a sledge. The middle days are uneven, and it would take a cold player to say whether
  the Field is a place they would go back to.
