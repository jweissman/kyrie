[To Larum street] It is cold enough to see your breath, and the ruts are hard with frost. Larum’s one street, under the Pass: an inn with a hand cupped to an ear on its sign, a bakehouse breathing warmth out of its door, a squat chapel, and at the top a house with a rope and a long pole hung by the door, which is the guide’s. The houses are stone, turf-roofed, with their backs to the weather. Above everything, the Pass goes up into its cloud. Everyone in Larum, sooner or later, stops in the street and looks up at the Pass and listens. There is nothing to hear. People stand like that for a while with their heads on one side, and then go on. The folk of Larum, shepherds and their wives, standing in the street, are here.

[Into the guide's house] A bare clean room with a table laid for two, and two cups on it, and only one of them used. Ropes coiled on pegs, ice-poles in a rack, a row of sheepskin coats hanging like a row of tired men. Jory Whitlock, the guide, who takes the living up through the White, is here.

[Pay Jory Whitlock six coin to take you up through the White] Jory takes a pole from the rack and a coil of rope, and you go up the street and up the path between the black shoulders, and into the white. He walks a yard ahead with the rope between you, and does not talk, and every so often he stops and puts his hand flat on a cairn with a ram’s skull on top, the way a man touches a doorpost. ‘Keep the last at your back,’ he says, once, ‘and walk till the next comes.’ Then the white thins, and goes blue, and you are standing on a height in a light like the inside of a knife, and the cold has teeth. Jory looks out over it for a moment. Then he turns round without a word and goes back down into the white, and is gone. From the booth by the curtain a thin old man in a faded tabard comes out with a ledger under his arm and a pen behind his ear. ‘Name and hour of death,’ he says briskly, and then sees your breath go up white in the cold, and stops. ‘Ah,’ he says. ‘Breathing.’ He looks at the ledger as if it had let him down. ‘There is no column for breathing. The pickers from Larum come up as far as the booth, and the guide, but they never stay.’ He looks down at the Field, and back at you. ‘If you’re going down there, go carefully. They’ve not seen a breather close in a long time, and they’re frightened enough as it is.’ Wren gains 100 experience: found the cairns through the White. Wren gains 100 experience: stood on the Field alive. You come out of the white onto a height over a white plain five miles across, strewn as far as you can see with nine hundred years of broken arms, between two halls on two dark heights; and the cold goes into your teeth. The sky is the colour of a knife-blade, with a low white sun in it that does not seem to move. Below you the plain is strewn with things: spears standing up, shields, the ribs of a cart, a siege-tower with snow on its top. In the middle of it smoke goes up from a huddle of coloured tents. Behind you, the snow comes down out of a clear sky in a curtain, and does not come any further.

[Through to the White] You walk fast to keep warm, and it does not help much. White, and in the white, if you look for it, a darker shape a dozen yards on: a cairn, rimed thick, with a ram’s skull on top. And beyond it, when you get there, the next.

[To the foot of the Pass] Above the last wall of the last field the path goes up between two shoulders of black rock, steep, through snow that is trodden in a line by sledge-runners. A little way above you it goes into the cloud, and the cloud is not grey, as cloud is, but white, bright, as if lit from inside. It does not move. A post stands at the edge of it with a bell on top and a board: LIVING, GO NO FURTHER WITHOUT THE GUIDE.

[Into the Hark] A low smoky taproom with a peat fire, and over the fire, newest and biggest of a hundred penny ballads pinned to the beams, a woodcut of a man with a sword in each fist and a town burning behind him: THE BALLAD OF HALVARD ELD, CHAMPION OF EVENHALL. The others curl along the beams, armoured men with their names in big letters underneath: BRAND OF THE RED HAND. MAB TRELOAR AND THE SIX FROM EVEN. A shelf of old iron by the door, ticketed for sale. Two pans hang by the stair with their bottoms dented. Here are Bet Garside, who keeps the Hark; and Master Ambler, a collector of heaven-iron, from the lowlands.
