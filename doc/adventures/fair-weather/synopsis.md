# Fair Weather — synopsis, acts, people, themes, endings

## In a sentence

A stranger comes down the coast road to Skeel, a town on a tidal sea that lives by wrecks, three days after a
merchantman went onto the sands in fair weather; the town will have her at the Wreck Court unless something living
came ashore; and something did.

## The situation, before the company arrives

- **The Constancy** (Ferrar and Daughters, master Abel Dunn), carrying grain bought on credit for a famine market,
  steered in the dark for a light high to the south that her master took for the Holm, and struck Scarth Bank. Eleven
  men drowned. Skeel stripped her by morning. Her cargo is in the Wreck-House.
- **The charter**: what the sea brings in is wreck, two parts to Skeel, one to the Lord Admiral, *provided that no
  living thing came ashore of her, neither man nor dog nor cat*.
- **The pressure**: two failed herring seasons, the Admiral's factor at Martinmas, a Lowland salt company. Without the
  grain, Skeel's children go hungry.
- **What happened**: Hender Brask, the Wreck-Reeve, a decent, exact man, son of the best pilot the Reach had, brought
  Loveday Kitto's lamp-oil out to the Holm on the Saturday cut with seawater; walked the Craigs path and hung a horn
  lantern in the Pulpit beside his dead father's ring, where the old pilots' lamp hung, so a stranger would read it as
  the Holm; covered it when she struck; and walked the Strand afterwards at low water, and saw a boy alive on a hatch,
  and walked away.
- **What lived**: the ship's dog, Admiral, running wild on the Strand; and the boy, Nye Gallow, fourteen, washed onto
  Seal Skerry, where the sealer Ewan Marrock, who hates Skeel, has hidden him.

If the company does nothing, the Court sits at four on the third day, on the flood; no living thing is brought; the
ship is wreck; Loveday is turned off the Lantern; Jack Cawse keeps it; Skeel eats.

## The acts

| Chapter | When | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fair Weather** | The first day | The company arrives into a town at work on a dead ship, and learns its rhythm: the tide-board, the withies, the boats that sail only on the flood. The stranger can work the wreck for a share. The questions open: fair weather; the dark Lantern; a light on the Craigs; a dog. |
| **Living Things** | The second day | The world moves without the company: the Wreck Sale on the quay at nine; the Cawse lads hunt the dog at low water; the Reeve, having seen the stranger walk the Strand, offers money for the dog; Constance Ferrar comes in on the evening flood; the Gannet Supper on the Craigs; and at nine at night Piran climbs the Pulpit, alone, unless someone has stopped him or gone with him. |
| **The Wreck Court** | The third day, four o'clock | Three matters, in order: the call (is there a living thing?), the Lantern (is Loveday at fault?), the winter. Nobody votes. The Reeve says the words in front of whoever is in the room. |
| **The Third Ebb** | That night | The Reach gives back the drowned at the wrack line below the Slip. Matthew Ferrar can be found for his sister; the eleven can be buried with names or as A SAILOR. On the fourth evening Ferrar's packet sails, with whoever is going. |

The acts are not gates. A player can reach the Court knowing nothing; can have the truth on the first evening; can
spend three days egg-climbing.

## The principal people

- **Hender Brask**, the Wreck-Reeve. Reads everyone's letters for those who cannot, and so knows everyone's debts.
  Oils the charter board himself. Has not slept since the wreck. He will not be moved by a hook, a cup of oil or a
  stranger's say-so; he cannot lie to Loveday. He could not drown a dog.
- **Loveday Kitto**, keeper of the Holm Lantern for forty-one years, going blind for two winters and hiding it. Took
  Hender in at twelve. Blames herself for eleven men. A pair of spectacles from the wreck's cargo lets her see her own
  lamp, and the sea in her oil.
- **Kerensa Brask**, the Reeve's daughter, nineteen. Rebuilt her grandfather's pilot gig in secret; has sounded the
  bar for two summers for no reason she can say. Means to leave on the packet. Knows what she saw her father do and
  will not say it in the loft.
- **Nye Gallow**, the ship's boy. Wants his mother, his dog, and to look the man with the lantern in the face.
- **Ewan Marrock**, sealer. Skeel says his wife went back to the sea as a seal. The Constancy carried her letter.
- **Constance Ferrar**, for whom the ship was named and carved. Keeps the firm's books. Needs the grain as badly as
  Skeel does. Wants her brother's body and a stone with his name.
- **Old Malachy Sennen**, last of the pilots, on Inish Gall with a goat and the half-burned Rutter.
- **Mother Jago**, **Jinny Rowse**, **Cuddy Pascoe**, **Bartle Tonkin**, **Parson Hosken**, **Old Pender** the huer,
  **Big Jack Cawse**, **Ebrel and Piran Penhallow**: see [inventory.md](inventory.md).

## Themes, carried by circumstance

- **What a town will call providence.** The Wreck Psalm (IF SHIPS MUST FOUNDER, LORD, SEND THEM TO SKEEL), the huer
  who cries wrecks now the herring have gone, the figureheads as trophies with pet names, a church whose god gives.
  The satire is of a recognisable thing (a community that profits from disaster and calls it a blessing) and it is
  sympathetic: they are hungry.
- **Living things.** The charter's clause turns the plot on a wet dog, a boy, and (in a comic aside nobody resolves)
  a canary. The question under it is who counts.
- **Knowing the water.** The same knowledge that wrecked the Constancy (where to hang a light so a stranger reads it
  as the Holm) is the knowledge that could bring ships in. Hender used his father's craft to kill. The Rutter is the
  same craft, written down for the other purpose.
- **Minds changed by people.** Loveday's question; Kerensa telling because she is leaving; Nye looking the Reeve in
  the face; Ewan hearing a letter read, or a kind lie.

## How it can end

The first ending that holds is told when the Court rises. In a world that goes on, it closes the Reach's chapter and
the town remains.

| Ending | Reached by | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **The Pilots** | A living thing shown or the Reeve's confession; the Rutter written; Kerensa's soundings known; Constance and Kerensa in the loft; the Rutter laid on the table in the third matter | Ferrar's pays three years' pilotage in advance, in grain. The station on Inish reopens under Malachy; Kerensa and Jinny are pilots. Skeel has never been paid to keep a ship whole. They sing the Twelve Gigs badly. |
| **The Godson** | Hender confesses: to Loveday, whom the company brought to ask him; or alone, rowing out to her because the company told him to | He refuses to say *wreck* in the loft and says what he did. He waits at home for the Admiral's officers. Loveday rows across and knocks at his door. |
| **The Light in the Pulpit** | Nye brought into the loft, and asked whether the man is in the room | The boy points. Hender's ears go red, the way Loveday said they did when he lied as a boy. NOR BOY is chalked under the charter. |
| **A Living Thing** | The dog or the boy brought forward in the first matter; the Reeve neither confesses nor is named | The grain goes to Ferrar's. A hungry winter. They say it was a dog that did it, and laugh, and stop laughing. |
| **A Stranger's Share** | Wreck called, and the company drowned the dog for the Reeve's shilling, or took his purse to say nothing | Skeel eats; Jack Cawse's Lantern goes out on the first black night; a drunk in the Admiral tells a stranger about a stranger. |
| **Wreck, While You Were Away** | Wreck called, and the company not in the loft | A carter tells it as good news on the coast road. "Funny thing: there was a dog." |
| **Wreck** | Wreck called with the company present | The Psalm sung on Sunday; Hender in the front pew, singing every word. Loveday gone off the Holm. |

The endings are not a menu chosen at a climax: they are who was in the loft, and what they had been told.
