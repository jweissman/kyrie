## The Reach

A shallow bight of the western sea, nine miles across, lying north over the march of a generated fen, forest,
mountain or frost country (data/adventures/countries.json, `skeel`). The commissioning brief asked that a maritime
world not be "land-based adventuring with water painted between locations". Here the water decides everything:

- **The Strand** stands dry for four hours of each low water, morning and night. Low water comes an hour later each
  day, through a four-day cycle of springs. The tide-board on the quay is chalked with the hours every morning.
- **The harbour dries when the Strand does.** Boats run only round high water, by day. So you *walk out on the ebb
  and sail home on the flood* (Malachy's rule), and a careless itinerary strands you: on the Holm, on the wreck's
  stern, on Inish, at the foot of the Craigs.
- **The fifth hour.** A company on the sand when the flood comes is struck by the water every watch until it reaches
  high ground. A crossing begun is finished: the sand ways stay open to a company already out on them.
- **The withies** mark the way. A stranger walking alone off them may be taken by the sinking sand. Cuddy Pascoe
  guides for a coin; he, and Malachy, teach the way, and then you keep to it yourself.
- **The Gut** never drains. It can be waded only at the Sill, and only at slack water, the hour either side of dead
  low; otherwise it takes your legs.

## History, as far as it is told

- Before anyone: a hermit called Hobb on the Holm, who kept a fire (a fish and a flame cut over the bench in his
  cell). Nothing more is said of him.
- Eighty years of pilots: twelve gigs out of Inish Gall, painted all colours; the leading mark, *Pulpit over the
  Holm*; the climbers' song of the Twelve Gigs, which keeps the mark in its chorus without anyone remembering why.
- Forty years ago: the old Lord Admiral buys the Reach's wreck-right and lets the pilot station's lease lapse. The
  Holm well goes brackish the same year ("the island is ashamed"). Jacob Brask drinks, burns the Rutter, is lost in
  his gig. Loveday Kitto takes in his son Hender, twelve.
- Two summers ago: Kerensa Brask digs her grandfather's gig out of the Inish shingle and rebuilds it.
- Two autumns: the herring fail. The Admiral's factor writes about arrears and a Lowland salt company.
- Three nights before the company comes: the Constancy.

## Its place in the world

The Reach is reached the way any authored country is: an innkeeper of the country behind it tells of it when asked
about the road north ("They say there's a dog"), the company crosses the march, and the coast road comes over the
down. Its clock starts the first morning after it is reached. Its ending closes a chapter, not the book; the town
remains, and is changed (see [04-consequences.md](04-consequences.md)). When the Court has risen, the word in the
country behind goes stale, and the innkeeper there has something to say to a company back from it.

**What it does not touch.** No canonical plane, no Kiritarun, no Mathematical Age. The world's Lord Admiral is
named only by office. The southern ports (Callowport) and the Lowland salt company are mentioned, never placed.
