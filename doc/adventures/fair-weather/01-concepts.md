# Fair Weather — Stage 1: competing conceptions

*Written before a line of Kyr, after reading the atlas, the authoring guide, the eleven adventures already in
`data/adventures`, and — most usefully — the collection's own critical survey
([repertoire-2026-10-26.md](../repertoire-2026-10-26.md)). That survey says plainly what the collection keeps doing
(an outsider reads a misread inheritance before a deadline, descends to an ancient mechanism, and the community
chooses how to stand toward it) and what it has never done (a human culprit who chose harm; exploration for its own
sake; the going-out-and-coming-back loop; making something; a person as an end; minds changed by people rather than
exhibits). I used it as a negative brief. Each concept below is scored against it.*

---

## A. *The Lash* — a wandering city of lashed hulls

Three hundred hulls bound gunwale to gunwale, a floating city that has wandered the western sea for two centuries,
puts in to a coast for the first time in a generation. Its streets are decks; its parishes are the old ships it was
built from (the *Gracious*, the *Moll Bray*, the *Third Sister*), each with its own trade and grudges; its houses are
re-planked every winter from timber it can no longer buy. Its law: nothing that touches land may come aboard
without being washed in salt. It has put in because it is rotting faster than it can mend, and the question tearing
it apart is whether to cut the lashings and let each hull go its own way, settle, or find oak.

*Promise:* a hidden city that comes to you; a maritime civilisation that is not land with water painted between;
romance (Lashfolk marry out by an elaborate custom of the plank).
*Against it:* the central dilemma is a vote about an inheritance — the exact template the survey asks us to rest.
And Kyr cannot move places; a city made of ships that do not sail is a pier.

## B. *Fair Weather* — a wrecking port on a tidal reach

Skeel is a salvage-and-herring town on a headland at the head of the Gannet Reach, a shallow sea of sands, holms and
skerries whose eight-fathom tides drain it twice a day. At low water you can walk to two islands across the Strand,
following a guide's withies past sinking sand; at high water the same ground is sea and you need a boat; the harbour
itself dries, so boats cannot run at low water. The town's houses are built of wreck-timber and every gable carries
a ship's figurehead.

Three nights ago, in fair weather, the merchantman *Constancy* went onto Carrow Bank and broke. Ships do not wreck in
fair weather. The Holm Lantern was dark that night, and a light was seen on the Craigs, where no light belongs.
Skeel has stripped the ship. Her cargo — grain, wine, a crate of southern spectacles, a mailbag — is in the Reeve's
Wreck-House, and at noon on the third day the Wreck Court will declare her *wreck*, which by the old charter gives
the town two parts in three of everything — *so long as no living thing came ashore*.

Something did. A ship's dog called Admiral runs loose on the sands, and the Reeve has put a price on it. A boy is
hidden on Seal Skerry by a sealer who hates the town.

*Promise:* a human culprit who chose harm (the Reeve, a good administrator who put out the Lantern and hung a false
light to feed his town through a winter after the herring failed, and is letting a half-blind old keeper take the
blame). The tide as a daily rhythm rather than a deadline. Exploration for its own sake. Comedy that comes from the
situation (a town's whole winter hangs on whether a wet dog counts as a living thing, and the dog shares a name with
the Lord Admiral and the inn). Grief and a person as an end (the shipowner's daughter, for whom the *Constancy* was
named, comes for her brother's body). Making (a pilot's rutter of the Reach). An ending that is an invention, not a
restoration: wreckers becoming pilots.
*Against it:* still has a court at the end, and courts are among the habits the survey names. Risk of being an
investigation followed by a verdict.

## C. *The Under-Choir* — a hidden elven city in a sea-cave system

An elven city under the cliffs of a northern coast, entered only at the lowest spring tides through a sea-arch. Its
people have kept a single unbroken song for nine hundred years, sung in shifts, and believe that if it stops the sea
will come in. The song is failing because there are too few of them to sing it.

*Promise:* beauty, hospitality, a civilisation under siege by its own numbers. *Against it:* a song that holds back
the sea is a misread inheritance with a mechanism under it (Low Water has a hymn that is a machine's program). It
would be the eighth telling.

## D. *The Ledger of Pandemonium* — the outer plane

A voyage through the House of Doors into Pandemonium, plane of the howling wind, where sound is property and the
inhabitants are bankrupted by echoes.

*Against it:* nothing in this repository implements the House of Doors, the Grand Tour or any canonical outer plane.
Writing one here would be exactly the "private duplicate of an established plane" the commission forbids, and its
coordination belongs to the principal builder. I record this as an open question (see [friction.md](friction.md) §1)
rather than resolve it by implementation convenience.

## E. *The Almoner's Fair* — a comedy of charity

A cathedral town's annual fair where the poor are hired out to be given alms, so the rich may earn indulgences.
Strong satire — but A Winter in Carth already satirises bought penance. Too near.

---

## Choice

**B, *Fair Weather*.** It is the only concept that answers the survey on most of its axes at once:

| The collection lacks | Fair Weather offers |
|---|---|
| A human culprit who chose harm | Hender Brask, the Wreck-Reeve, who chose it twice: the false light, and leaving a boy in the water |
| Exploration for its own sake; going out and coming back | A tidal reach whose geography changes twice a day; walking out on the ebb and back before the flood is the basic rhythm of play |
| Making | The rutter: a pilot's book of the Reach, assembled from soundings the player takes |
| A person as an end | Constance Arden and her brother's body; Ewan Marrock and a letter from his wife; Loveday's eyes |
| Comedy as structure | A town's winter hanging on a wet dog named Admiral |
| Minds changed by people | Kerra tells what she saw because she is leaving; Hender confesses to Loveday, not to an exhibit |
| An ending that invents | The Pilots: a livelihood that was not there to be found |
| The small that stays small | No Kiritarun, no plane, no machine under the sands. It ends in the parish it begins in |

It is maritime in the way the commission asks: the sea is not scenery but the thing that decides where you can be,
when, and with whom. Its danger is honest (the flood tide over the Strand outruns a walking man; the Gut has a thing
in it). Its pleasures are real (egg-climbing, the seals hauling out, the salvage sale, a night in the Lantern).

What it needs from me, per the survey's warnings: the Court must not be "a well-written vote"; the minor stories must
not all be clues; and the deadline must not be the whole shape. Those are the critique's job ([03-critique.md](03-critique.md)).
