# Fair Weather — Stage 2: the editor's attack, and the design that was built

*Read as a demanding editor would read the treatment in [02-treatment.md](02-treatment.md), against the commission's
questions and the collection survey's warnings.*

## The charges

**1. Every small story is a clue.** Read the list again: the dog leads to the boy, the boy saw the light, Piran saw
the light, Loveday's oil is the light, Malachy knew the Reeve's father, the mailbag carries evidence, Kerra saw her
father come home. Nine minor stories, eight of them feeding one revelation. This is the "every mystery is secretly
part of the same conspiracy" the commission names, at parish scale. A player who ignored the wreck for three days
would find nothing in Skeel but witnesses.

**2. Hender is a mechanism.** He has a motive (arrears) and a method (watered oil, a lantern). He has no life. Why
*him*? What does he do with his evenings? What did it cost him to walk away from the boy? A culprit who "chose harm"
is only interesting if the choosing is visible.

**3. The Court is a vote with one voter.** If the Court turns on whether the player has collected the dog, the boy,
and Kerra's testimony, it is an exhibit-court: the collection's habit exactly. The survey asks for minds changed by
people.

**4. The protagonist wants nothing.** They arrive and read. The survey's fifth finding again.

**5. Where is the pleasure?** The treatment has grief, guilt, hunger, drowning and a monster. It has no joy, no meal,
no song, nothing the player does in Skeel because it is lovely.

**6. The Pilots ending is a gift from above.** As drafted, the player assembles a rutter, shows it to Constance, and
she founds a pilot station. That is a reformer's ending handed down. Who in Skeel *wants* to be a pilot?

**7. Generic furniture.** "A ruined hermit's cell", "a beacon cairn", "a well": decoration. A tidal island with a
lighthouse is a postcard unless something is done there.

**8. The tide could be wallpaper.** If it only opens and shuts passages, it is a timer. It has to be the thing the
player plans by, fears and comes to know.

## The revisions

**1 → Three stories cut loose from the light.**
- *The Sealer's Wife* now has nothing to do with the wreck except that the *Constancy* carried the mail. The letter
  in the bag is from Morva, alive in a southern port, with a daughter of six. Ewan cannot read. What the player reads
  to him, and whether they read it at all, changes Ewan and nothing else. (He does not, as drafted, become a witness.)
- *The Egg-Climb* is now about Piran Hale and his mother Tamsin: his father fell from the Pulpit eight years ago and
  she has forbidden him the Craigs. The Pulpit has the lantern-hook on it, but Piran's story is whether he climbs, and
  whether he falls, and whether his mother can bear to watch. The hook is there to be found by anyone who goes up; it
  is not why Piran goes.
- *The Gut* holds no clue at all. It is a channel with a great conger in it, called the Gaffer, and a way across at
  slack water, and the reason the pilots needed a rutter.
- *Loveday's Eyes* now has a resolution that is not evidence: the *Constancy*'s crate of spectacles. A pair from the
  wreck, ground for an old merchant's eyes in a city she will never see, lets her see her own lamp. Whether that is
  justice or irony is left to the player.
- *The Wreck Sale* is commerce and comedy: lots sold unopened, the town bidding against itself.

**2 → Hender Brask, given a life.** His father, Jacob Brask, was a Reach pilot, ruined when the station's lease
lapsed; Hender was twelve. He was Loveday's godson: she taught him the lamp. He keeps the charter board by the church
door oiled himself. He reads every letter that comes to Skeel for the people who cannot, and so he knows everyone's
debts. He has not slept since the wreck. He is not hiding the oil because he cannot bear to throw it in the sea. When
asked directly by Loveday — and only by her — whether he watered it, he cannot lie to her. *That* is the confession:
not an exhibit shown, but a woman he loves asking him a question.

**3 → The Court is a man deciding in front of people.** No votes. The Reeve sits; he says the words; the Court is
whether he can say *wreck* to a room with the dog in it, the boy in it, and Loveday in it. What the player brings
changes who is in the room, not a tally. If Loveday has asked her question, Hender does not wait for the Court.

**4 → The protagonist is offered a share.** On the first ebb, a stranger who helps strip the *Constancy* is paid a
day's share (and can come back each ebb for more: a livelihood). On the second day Hender, who has seen the stranger
on the sands, offers them the dog's shilling and a stranger's portion of the wreck-third if the dog is "dealt with".
Drowning Admiral is a grave act, and a company that does it is known for it in Skeel ever after. The protagonist can
be at fault: by drowning the dog, by keeping quiet for a share, by bringing the boy into town before the Court and
getting him hurt.

**5 → Pleasures.** The Gannet Supper: the egg-climbers' feast on the Craigs on the second night, with the gannet
eggs roasted in the ashes and the climbers' song, which the player can join. The seals hauling out on the Skerry at
the evening ebb and singing (they do sing; it is a real sound). The sale. A night in the Lantern with Loveday,
trimming the wick, which is a good thing to do with a night.

**6 → The Pilots come from Skeel.** Old Malachy is not the only one who remembers. Hender's father was a pilot; Kerra
rows better than anyone in the town and has been sounding the channels in secret with her grandfather's lead-line,
because she wants to *go*; Jinny Rowse the ferry-woman has been pilot in all but name for twenty years. The rutter is
their knowledge written down: Malachy's half-burned book, the soundings the player takes, and Kerra's notebook. The
ending is not Constance's idea. It is Kerra's, and Constance is the one who can pay for it.

**7 → Furniture replaced with uses.** The hermit's cell on the Holm is where Loveday keeps the Lantern's oil (and
where the watered barrel is). The beacon cairn on Inish is the pilots' signal (lit, it calls Malachy's boat). The well
on the Holm is brackish since the wreck year (the sea in it, the islanders say, means the Holm is ashamed): a
superstition the player can ignore.

**8 → The tide as the thing you learn.** The Strand is walkable for four hours at each low water; the harbour drives
boats for the four hours round each high. The times move later each day. The town's tide-board at the quay shows
today's times; Malachy can teach the reckoning. A company caught on the sands by the flood is struck by the water each
watch until it gets to high ground (the wreck's stern, the Holm, the Craigs' foot) — and the *Constancy*'s stern at
night with the tide all round it is the adventure's one scene of sustained fear.

## What stays as it was

The situation (the ship, the charter, the dog, the boy, the Court). The geography. Constance. The six endings, now
reached by who is in the room rather than by what is on the table.

## What I could not fix on paper

- The Court is still a deadline, the one the survey warned of. I accept it: a wreck court is how such a town works,
  and the work goes on after it (the third ebb gives back the drowned; the town lives into the winter it chose).
- Hender is still the culprit from the start of the design, and a careful player will suspect him by the first
  evening. The question was never meant to be *who*. It is *what will you do about it, and who will you do it to*.
