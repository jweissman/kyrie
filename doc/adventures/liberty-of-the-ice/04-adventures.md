# The small adventures, and how they touch

Each small adventure as implemented: who wants what, what is uncertain, the ways at it, what complicates it, how it
can end, and what it leaves for later. The test that plays each is named. "Leaves" says what a later scene reads.

## 1. A penny a stair (`liberty.kyr`)

- **Who**: the idle watermen at both stairs; Justice Broom.
- **Uncertain**: what "a penny a stair" means. Twelve stairs: twelve pennies.
- **Ways**: shake (the first Grave act of the game, and the law's first lesson); pay twelve; step over the plank and
  stare a waterman down (brawn); or, at the Court at four on the first day, argue that a stair is the flight and the
  steps are steps (persuasion), and pay one.
- **Complication**: unpaid by the time the Court rises, the hand is broken in front of everyone, and the watermen take it
  out of the purse.
- **Leaves**: stairs open on both banks; the watermen sour (stepped over) or tickled (argued); Broom amused.
- *Played*: `liberty-of-the-ice.test.ts` (every journey begins here), the Unswearing and Long Con argue it.

## 2. Ursel's hand (`ursel.kyr`)

- **Who**: Ursel Cobb, who has never shaken on anything.
- **Uncertain**: why Harker.
- **Ways at the purse**: ask for it; lift it back (stealth); tell her you saw the left hand (insight); or ignore it, and
  find it back at three with a note. Lifting or seeing earns her regard; asking does not.
- **Complication**: she lies about why. Her evasion is visible ("too quickly, looking at the fire"); insight at the Ring
  sees her looking for one face; the ledger names Dickon Cobb.
- **Ends**: a partner who stays; a partner who takes the earnest at the Glove Post and goes (if she was never given a
  reason to trust, or was lied to after she told the truth); a sister found; a handfast, landed or let go.
- **Leaves**: her vouching raises Harker's belief; her trust decides the double-cross; her question at the Ice Chapel.
- *Played*: `small` (Ursel), `score` (trusted: she stays), the flock fixture's first draft (untrusted: she took it).

## 3. The Squire of Hawsfold (`con.kyr`, `supper.kyr`)

- **Who**: Josias Harker, who buys fleece he cannot see; Ursel; Noll; Abel.
- **Uncertain**: whether Harker believes, which he never says. His belief is the shared `believes`/`conviction`,
  moved by what he is told and what he can hold: a story (deception), a printed tally, the tally ruled as a factor's
  own (lore), fell wool, Sir Lionel's coat, Gosling's letter, Ursel's tedium. Four is belief.
- **Ways**: a liar talks; a scholar rules the tally; a plain person brings wool and a coat and a letter, none of which
  needs a roll.
- **Complications, compounding**: a squire believed sups at Fleece Row and is asked to the wedding; a squire at a
  wedding is expected to give (a Frost Company share, or Quarle's frost-cream; nothing, and Harker looks); Harker's
  terms put half the price at the Landing, where the hand will bind; once shaken, Harker's man watches the North Bar;
  and the real shepherd of Hawsfold is in the Cheaping asking loudly for Harker.
- **Ends**: coin (the Score, if over the North Bar before the Landing); the hired (the Long Con); the flock made true
  (the Flock That Was); the pillory (exposed and unable to pay back twice over).
- *Played*: `long`, `score`, `pillory`, `flock`.

## 4. Abel Haws (`con.kyr`, `drowned.kyr`)

- **Who**: the shepherd of the real Hawsfold, who cannot lie and believes everything.
- **Uncertain**: what he will say to whom. Everything, to anyone.
- **Ways**: send him to the Exchange with a lie he believes because nobody has told him anything unwelcome before; make
  him believe you are the old landlord's heir (deception, easy), after which he vouches for the Squire; buy his flock;
  or let him meet Harker, and the lie falls in front of the whole Ring.
- **Complication**: tell him about the dry berth on the hulk, out of kindness, and he goes out with cheese, and tells
  Harker's people who sent him.
- **Leaves**: the exposure; the vouching; the raid on the Patience; forty coin and a buyer.
- *Played*: `pillory` (met Harker), `long`/`score` (diverted), `flock` (sold), `small` (the leak).

## 5. The Perpetual Frost Company (`quarle.kyr`)

- **Who**: Mr. Ambrose Quarle, who is sincere; the Exchange; Harker, two hundred shares deep.
- **Uncertain**: whether it is a fraud. It is not: the salts freeze a tub.
- **Ways**: buy a share (it makes a fine wedding present); explain to the Exchange in chalk why a tub is not a river
  (lore); send the watermen to cut ice for him (work, which keeps their saws off the Reach); tell him what his salts
  would freeze best (cream, with honey).
- **Complication**: exposed, the Company collapses and Harker is short of coin; he then offers the Squire indentures
  of his own accord.
- **Ends**: Quarle ruined; Quarle's Frost-Creams in the Cheaping; frost-cream at the wedding.
- *Played*: `small` (quarle).

## 6. Sir Lionel Gant on the ice (`gant.kyr`)

- **Who**: a ruined gentleman living where no writ runs; Harker, who bought his bond; the bailiffs at both stairs.
- **Uncertain**: the debt. He borrowed three; the writ says thirty.
- **Ways**: pay thirty to Kemble; read his counterpart (lore) and show the Reeve at the Landing a three made into
  thirty; lead him to the Clink at night when the bailiffs go home, or by day through Widow Saffery's starling.
- **Complication**: led past a bailiff by day he is seized.
- **Ends**: free and a witness to Harker's forgery (which, at the Landing, is grounds enough for the Reeve to rule on
  Harker's books without the city's witness); master of the Clink yard; in the compter for thirty.
- **Leaves**: his coat (worn, it brings persuasion; shown, it makes Harker treat you as a gentleman).
- *Played*: `small` (Clink), `builds` (counterpart).

## 7. Absolute (`bear.kyr`, `gosling.kyr`)

- **Who**: Hester Mabb and her bear, who hates the cold.
- **Ways**: honey from the Frozen Lamb; the bear-ward's tune, for a performer; a woodsman's slow approach; holding
  the chain (brawn, hard); or leave him.
- **Complication**: at noon Harker's wharf men come with spears; left alone he runs down the Reach.
- **Ends**: home to his stake; loose, meeting the searchers on the second night (they flee, and he goes through the
  ice: the hired's way down the Reach is clear, at his cost); lent for a walk past the searchers; walked to the wedding,
  which he ends.
- *Played*: `small` (honey, fiddle), `builds` (woodcraft), `alone` (left).

## 8. The Ice Chapel and the alderman (`gosling.kyr`)

- **Who**: Alderman Gosling, who cannot rhyme Thessaly; Bel Ashby of the Frozen Lamb; Parson Doody and old Grigg's
  register.
- **Uncertain**: whom Bel married at the last Frost Fair. Gosling, nineteen, the forty-first of fifty on its last
  night: never landed, because the ice went out.
- **Ways**: give him a rhyme and make him read the poem through; buy, borrow (as one of the cloth) the register;
  show him his name; tell Bel he has seen it. They say it at the Lamb that night.
- **Ends**: Gosling marries Bel at noon; Thessaly marries Gosling (and Harker has his son-in-law at the Reeve's elbow,
  who will not witness what the Reeve is shown); the bear ends it.
- *Played*: `small` (gosling), `builds` (cleric).

## 9. Mr. Icicle (`gosling.kyr`, `ice.kyr`)

- **Who**: Thessaly Harker, who writes the *Frost Gazette*; Noll Brisket, who sets it and loves her.
- **Ways to know**: be at the press at night when she leaves her copy; insight at supper (ink on her finger).
- **Ways after**: keep it (she gives you her father's desk key); sell it to her father (Harker believes the Squire an
  inch more, and locks her in); tell her the printer knows and has never asked; give her the ledger to print.
- **Ends**: married to Noll at eleven on the last morning, by Doody, for a shilling, which frees the alderman; the
  ledger in the Gazette by noon, which the Landing has read.
- *Played*: `small` (thessaly), `unswearing` (the key).

## 10. The Liars' Bench (`ice.kyr`)

- **Who**: Justice Broom, who wants one lie with a stone in it.
- **Ways**: tell the best lie you have (deception, hard; one try a night, a fresh die each night); tell the truth about
  Harker's hired as a tall tale (no roll, anyone who knows it).
- **Complication**: Kemble is drinking at the Lamb. The truth told on the Bench goes to Harker, who will not now hire
  that face at his Ring or believe it a squire.
- **Ends**: the Silver Tongue (worn, it brings deception); the Reeve already knowing, at the Landing, which is grounds
  enough for her to rule on Harker's books without the city's witness.
- *Played*: `small` (bench), `builds` (commoner), `unswearing`.

## 11. The custody of Callow (`callow.kyr`, `landing.kyr`)

- **Who**: Magister Edrin Callow, who teaches that nobody is bound by who they were.
- **Ways**: shake to be his hand at the Court at four on the first day, or let him go to the stocks; answer his
  question three ways: *the hand binds* (the Reeve's answer: he comes, and is convicted), *nobody is bound* (his own:
  he takes you at your word and slips away in the second night, and your hand is broken), or, only for someone who has
  seen a hand shaken on a lie, *a hand binds what its shaker knew* (a third thing: he comes, and argues it with you).
- **Complication**: in the stocks two nights in the frost, unhanded and untended, he dies. A blanket and coal keep him.
- **Ends**: acquitted, and the doctrine cried at the Landing, which is the Unswearing's first half; convicted;
  slipped and found preaching to the river; dead.
- *Played*: `long`, `unswearing`, `small` (slips), `alone` (dies).

## 12. The watermen's channel (`watermen.kyr`)

- **Who**: Gaffer Tredgold and thirty idle watermen.
- **Ways**: hire their sledges for the second night; send them to cut ice for Quarle; tell Tredgold what Harker does
  on the Watermen's ice (he gives sledges for nothing); or nothing.
- **Complication**: with no work they cut a channel across the foot of the Reach on the second night: the sledge road
  south ends at black water, and the ice goes out two hours early on the last night.
- *Played*: `small` (sledges); every journey that does nothing about it cuts the channel.

## 13. The wolves on the sledge road (`wolves.kyr`, `frosts.kyr`)

- **Who**: four wolves off the downs, come after the frozen river, as the Book of Frosts says they came in four of
  seven frosts. Foreshadowed on the second day by Abel (ewes taken from a roofed fold), Nab ("sat in a row like
  magistrates"; fire, meat, a loud voice, or a sledge), tracks at the Reach head (woodcraft), and the Book itself.
- **When**: the second night, from ten until five, across the sledge road at the foot of the Reach: the only way
  south, for the hired, for Dickon's drowned, for anyone. At ten they are heard howling across the whole city.
- **Ways**: fight them (dangerous: four wolves will kill a first-level company that stands); a brand (lit at the ox
  fire for a penny, at Fowke's brazier, or from a greasy fleece at the bondhouse brazier) swung at them; a bucket of
  the ox's offal thrown upwind; round them downwind along the bank (woodcraft); face them down (intimidation, hard; a
  failure brings them on); or Tredgold's sledges, which outrun them. A lit torch from the pack also frightens them
  (`Fears flame`).
- **Complication**: Dickon goes south alone with the drowned at three on that night if nobody brings him the
  bondhouse. Warned of the wolves, he takes fire; not warned, a waterman finds a cap and trodden snow in the morning,
  and nobody goes back to look.
- *Played*: `wolves` (the second night on foot, a brand from a fleece); `offal`; the wanders.

## 14. Paid men (`wolves.kyr`)

- **Who**: Kemble, who drinks at the Lamb; the searchers, paid by the night; old Fowke, who will drink anything.
- **Ways**: stand Kemble gin (three coin) and hear *eight a head, Gallowmoor* (the farm in the hills found out with no
  book and no lock), then lift his bondhouse key off his chain while he sleeps; pay the searchers three coin a man to
  walk the downs road (persuasion, easy; on a failure they take it and tell Kemble); sit with Fowke over a bottle until
  he sleeps, at any hour. A front door into the bondhouse for someone with neither lockwork nor Thessaly.
- *Played*: `paid`; `wolves` (the gin).

## 15. The Book of Frosts and the Statute of Servants (`frosts.kyr`)

- **Who**: Gaffer Tredgold, who keeps the Company's history at the back of its accounts, upside down; Amos Tallis, the
  verger of the Scale Church; Alderman Gosling, who is a justice of the peace on dry ground.
- **What**: seven frosts in two hundred years (the First; the Glove, when the Liberty began; the Black Lambing, when the
  fell farms were sold and the hiring of their children began; the Frost They Hanged the Reeve; the Short Frost, when
  every handfast melted; the Last; this one); the Statute that forbids carrying anyone hired in the city out of it
  but to the place they were hired for, which does not run on the Watermen's ice; Harker's pound of wax "for the
  weight".
- **Ways**: told Gosling that Dickon was hired in the Cheaping last spring, on dry ground, and carried to Gallowmoor,
  the alderman writes it down for the Lady Day sessions, "where a lie is a crime"; in the thaw the indictment is
  nailed to Harker's door. It changes no ending: it is what the land does after the ice.
- *Played*: `statute`.

## 16. What Ursel tells at night (`ursel.kyr`)

- Three confidences, late, to someone she trusts a little: why she never shakes (Swale Head, a note, a factor's glove
  on her father's kitchen table); where she learned to lie (the Bench at fifteen; the prize penny on a string); and,
  if the company has heard Harker's own story, what she makes of a hired child of her own parish. And Tansy Hobb's
  people had the farm below hers. And in the Exchange's old registers, a note of Cobb's landed to "J. H.", which
  may be Harker or may be old Hebden; the company may tell her or not. *Played*: `ursel-night`.

## The principal adventure: the drowned (`drowned.kyr`, `night.kyr`, `harker.kyr`)

The hats on the stake; the tracks in the snow (woodcraft), the slip in the line (insight); Widow Saffery's lantern
and her cellar; the Patience and Dickon Cobb; the searchers; the leak (Abel; a sale to Kemble; being seen going out to
the hulk without stealth); the flight (bar or lock, quiet or a fight, sledges or on foot, the bear, a lie to Kemble
that sends the searchers up onto the downs). It touches everything above: the watermen carry the hidden, Abel betrays
them, Gant and Callow witness, the bear scatters the searchers, the Gazette rouses the city, Broom already knows,
Ursel trusts you or does not.
