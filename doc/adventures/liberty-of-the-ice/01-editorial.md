# Stage 2 — a severe reading of the treatment, and the design as revised

*The treatment was read as an editor who had to approve or refuse it would read it, against the brief's questions,
the repertoire survey's form ledger and the atlas's §30. Each finding is followed by what changed.*

## 1. Findings

### F1. Four genres in one coat

The treatment wants a farce, a romance, a horror and a philosophical two-hander, and gets them by giving each a
district. That is a catalogue, not a work. A frost fair can hold all four, but only if one of them governs and the
others are what it looks like at another hour or from another angle.

**Revision.** The work is a **comedy** (farce and satire) with a romance inside it. Its horror is *the same comedy at
night*: the searchers are looking for the same people the comedy has been joking about ("the drowned"), with the same
law. Its philosophy is *the same law argued*: Callow's doctrine is a legal question the Landing has to answer, and the
custody of Callow is a con the player is running on themselves. Nothing is in the work for its genre alone.

### F2. The informer is a placeholder

"Someone in the network sells the drowned back... a person, chosen, for a reason" names a slot and fills it with
nobody. A culprit invented to satisfy the survey's complaint ("the collection's answer is always a system") would be
worse than none.

**Revision.** Cut the informer as a person. **The leak is the company's own tongue.** The hiding places are things
the company can come to know; what it says and to whom is what Harker's searchers learn. A lie told to protect them
(that Dickon went south, that the hulk is empty) travels as lies do; a truth told carelessly (to Harker's clerk, to the
real shepherd who cannot keep anything in, to anyone who has been talking to Harker) brings the searchers. This is
the survey's **fault** made systemic: the protagonist can be the cause of what goes wrong, by talking. There is still a
human culprit who chose harm, and he is the obvious one, Harker, which is right for a farce: farce's villains are not
mysteries; its mysteries are who is lying to whom.

### F3. Too many small adventures, unequal in weight

Fifteen were listed. Several were a person, a want and a fetch (the ox; the Silver Skate; the pardoner's weights).

**Revision.** Eleven, each re-cut against the brief's seven ingredients (see §3). Cut: the Silver Skate (a wager
plot duplicating the Frost Company's), the pardoner's weights (a second exposure plot, and Tessary has Saint
Evenhand's weighing), the ox roast as a quest (kept as a scene and as a source of a brick, a fire and a smell). Merged:
Mr. Icicle into Thessaly's own arc; the real shepherd into the con.

### F4. Is the con an actual game, or a description of one?

The treatment says "lies told to particular people, held by them, repeated by them, colliding when two of them meet."
That is the whole of the farce, and it is the hardest thing to make play. If lying is a menu of "Tell Harker you own a
flock" with one result, it is a long passage of dialogue.

**Revision.** The con is built of **props, claims and people**, and the farce is in their collisions:

- A **claim** is something a person can be brought to believe (the company's flock; the Squire of Hawsfold; that
  Dickon is dead; that the Squire is engaged to Thessaly). People hold claims; some repeat them to the people they
  meet; some test them against what they know.
- A claim stands on **props**, made things: a tally printed on Noll's press, a tuft of Swaledale-grey fell wool, a
  letter from a shepherd, a borrowed coat, a signet. Harker tests a claim against a prop he asks for. A prop can be
  made better or worse (Noll prints a better tally for a better price, or for a favour; a scholar's forgery is better
  than Ursel's).
- A claim **collides** when its holder meets someone who knows otherwise. Abel Haws, the real shepherd, knows the
  only flock at Hawsfold is his. If Harker and Abel are ever in the same place at the same time, the lie falls, unless
  Abel has been brought into it, or got drunk, or sent to the Flashes, or told that Harker is the man who wants to
  steal his sheep.
- A claim **compounds**: the Squire of Hawsfold, being a squire, is asked to the Gosling wedding; a squire at a
  wedding is expected to give a present; the present is noticed. Each success of the lie makes the next one harder. That
  is farce's engine.

### F5. Does the plot grow from the setting?

Yes, and this is the treatment's real strength, so the revision protects it. The snow shuts the roads, so no claim
about the fells can be checked: the con is possible *because of the weather*. The roads being shut is why Harker
moved the Hiring Ring onto the ice, why the hired cannot be shipped till the thaw, and why the drowned can hide: no
one can leave. The thaw that ends the Fair is when every lie comes due and every hidden person must move. Weather,
law, crime and comedy have one cause.

### F6. Exposition

The treatment explains the Liberty in four numbered clauses. A player must not be told them; they must be bitten by
them. **Revision.** The law is taught in three acts of play inside the first hour:

1. At the Watermen's Stairs, Tredgold's men offer you "a penny a stair". You shake. There are twelve stairs. You owe
   twelve pence, and the Court will say so.
2. Ursel picks your pocket on Freezeland Street and gives it back, laughing: she never shook on anything with you,
   and you never shook on anything with her.
3. At noon the crier cries the Liberty at the Glove Post. By then you know what it means.

### F7. Is the city alive without the mystery?

Partly. The treatment gives it trades and troubles, but its people mostly exist to be marks. **Revision.** Each of
the principal people has a want that has nothing to do with the company or the drowned, and goes after it on their
own timetable: Gosling is writing an epithalamium for his own wedding and cannot rhyme "Thessaly"; Bel is selling the
last of her gin before the thaw takes the tent; Quarle is selling shares; Tredgold wants the river; Broom wants one
good lie before she dies; Noll wants his ink not to freeze; Gant wants to stay on the ice for ever. The hours of the
Fair are fixed (crier at noon, the Flashes at dusk, the Liars' Bench at night, the Court at four), so the city does
things whether or not the company is watching.

### F8. Comic moments must be earned

A joke that is only a line of prose is texture, which the survey already found. **Revision.** Every comic payoff in
the revised design is the consequence of something the player did earlier: the bear at the wedding is there because
you let it go or because you led it; Gosling's bigamy is discoverable because you read a register; the Squire of
Hawsfold's wedding present is due because you invented the Squire.

### F9. Does a character meaningfully change?

The treatment gave Ursel an arc in one sentence. **Revision**, three people who change, each by people rather than
exhibits:

- **Ursel Cobb**, who has never shaken on anything, either keeps that freedom (and leaves you, with or without the
  money) or makes one bargain on the ice: a hand shaken with you, for Dickon. What moves her is not evidence. It is
  whether you kept her secret, whether you went into the bondhouse, whether you lied to her once she had told you the
  truth.
- **Edrin Callow**, who preaches that no oath binds, is either confirmed in it (and slips your custody, breaking your
  hand for you), or comes to the Landing because you kept your hand when you did not have to. His doctrine survives
  either way; what changes is whether he lives by it.
- **Hamnet Gosling**, the man who cannot say what he wants, says it, or does not.

### F10. Real alternatives, and the two principal approaches

The treatment lists endings; it does not say what the player *does* differently to reach them. **Revision.** The
central problem is Harker's bondhouse on Hay Wharf (where the hired are kept until the thaw, and his books with
them) and the Landing that will make the hired his for a year in the south. There are two principal approaches, and
they differ in what the player risks and who they are.

1. **The Front Door: the con.** Be the Squire of Hawsfold; sell Harker the flock; be shown the bondhouse as a buyer
   (Harker likes to show his stock), and make the price of the flock the hired. Risks: collision, exposure, the
   pillory. Rewards a liar, a forger, a scholar, a charmer.
2. **The Ring: the hiring.** Stand in the Hiring Ring with a mop or a crook and be hired; shake Harker's hand; be
   walked into the bondhouse with the others, and locked in for the night; find Dickon's message and the ledger from
   inside; get out by the wharf door onto the ice in the dark. Risks: your own hand is now Harker's for a year if you
   do not get it unmade; the searchers; the cold. Rewards a strong back, a woodsman, a thief, and a plain person with
   no charm, because Harker hires the dull gladly.

Either way in, the hired are freed at the Landing by one of three levers, which can be combined: **the trick** (the
indentures paid over for the phantom flock), **the law** (Callow's argument, carried by the company's evidence, that
a hand shaken on a lie does not bind), or **the flight** (the hidden got off the ice and away down the Reach before the
Landing, which leaves them free and Harker's bargains landed on nobody).

### F11. Rewards for different builds

The treatment says "a liar does well here; so does a thief". It needs to be true in the mechanics, not the prose.
See [06-builds.md](06-builds.md) for the matrix; the revision fixes for each approach at least one step that only a
skill, a calling or a quality opens, and at least one step that anyone can take with time, coin or a companion.

### F12. The romance must not be a meter

**Revision.** The company's own romance, with Ursel, is a set of things done together, each a fact Ursel's words can
read: *skated at the Flashes*; *kept her secret*; *went into the bondhouse for Dickon*; *had both names printed on one
card*. It reaches a question only if enough of them are true and none of the things that end it are (lying to her
after she told you the truth; selling the hiding places). The question is hers to ask, at the Ice Chapel, on the last
day; the answer is the player's. No number is ever shown.

### F13. Scale

The treatment is a large work. The two most recent adventures run near three thousand lines of Kyr. **Decision:** a
city of six districts and about forty rooms; about twenty-five people with their own words; eleven small adventures;
one principal conflict with two approaches and three levers; three days and three nights; between 2,500 and 3,500
lines. Days: arrival at morning on the first day; the hats on the ice on the second and third mornings; the Landing at
two in the afternoon of the third day; the wedding at noon before it; the thaw in the third night.

## 2. The revised design in one page

**The Liberty of the Ice.** The river Lisk has frozen at Pellam for the first time in thirty-one winters, and the city
holds a Frost Fair on the ice, under the ice's own law: *say what you will; keep what you shake on.* The roads are
shut by snow. You come down the North Road on foot.

**What you want.** Money, to begin with. Ursel Cobb has a con: sell Master Harker, wool-factor, a flock that does not
exist, which nobody can go and look at until the thaw. Your half is forty crowns.

**What is going on.** Harker hires the north's poor at the Hiring Ring on the ice for "a year's service in the hills"
and sells their indentures to the lead mines in the south; on the ice no statute of the city reaches him, and at the
Landing on the last day his bargains will be cried ashore and good everywhere. Some of his hired have faked their
deaths by the Black Reach (a drowned man's hand is void) and are hiding in the city; Ursel's brother Dickon, who came
back out of the south, is getting them out. Ursel's con is a way into Harker's books to find Dickon. She has not told
you.

**What happens if nobody does anything.** Harker's searchers find the hidden on the third night; the hired are landed
at the Landing; Gosling marries Thessaly; Callow, with no hand, is pilloried; the ice goes out; Ursel, with or without
you, leaves Pellam with nothing.

**The two approaches** to Harker's bondhouse: the Front Door (be a buyer) and the Ring (be hired).

**The three levers** at the Landing: the trick, the law, the flight.

**The settlements** (the endings, standalone): *The Score*, *The Long Con*, *The Unswearing*, *The Flight down the
Reach*, *The Pillory*, and *Landed*, the default; with romance and the city's people as independent axes
(the Gosling wedding: Thessaly, Bel, or nobody; Ursel: gone, partner, or handfast; Callow: landed, slipped, or
pilloried; Gant: taken, freed, or still on the ice when it goes).

And the principal adventure, **the Drowned**, which they touch: Tredgold's men can ferry the hidden; Abel can betray
them; Gant and Callow can witness; the bear can scatter the searchers; the *Gazette* can rouse the city; Broom can hear
you; Ursel can trust you.

## 4. The protagonist, by the survey's form ledger

|---|---|

| Fault | Yes: what they say can bring the searchers; their own hand can bind them |
| Central activity | Lying, and making the things lies stand on; then keeping a promise |
| Minds change by | Shame, flattery, laughter, being kept faith with; and some evidence |
