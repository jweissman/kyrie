# The synopsis: acts, people, conflicts, settlements

## The situation

The Lisk has frozen under Pellam Bridge for the first time in thirty-one winters. The roads have been shut by snow for
a month. On the ice there is a Frost Fair, and the ice has its own law, kept by the Watermen's Company and its Reeve,
Clemency Broom, and cried every noon from a mast with a glove on it: **say what you will; keep what you shake on.** On
the last day of the ice the Court lands the Fair's bargains, hand to hand, both parties before the Reeve, so that they
hold on dry ground; what is not landed goes out with the river.

**Josias Harker**, wool-factor and churchwarden, has moved his Hiring Ring onto the ice, where none of the city's
statutes on servants reach. He hires the north country's poor for "a year's place on a farm in the hills" and sells
their indentures, landed, to Ossett's agent for the lead mines at Gallowmoor at eight coin a head. He never lies
outright, and never puts out his hand: they shake on his bargains; he accepts them.

Some of his hired have slipped Kemble's line in the crush on Freezeland Street, and had their hats hung by the hole on
the Black Reach that never freezes, because a drowned man's hand is no hand. **Widow Saffery**, whose son went through
the ice at the last Frost Fair and who walks the Reach every night with a lantern (the watermen call her the Lisk
Wife), hangs the hats and hides them in a cellar in the bridge's starling. **Dickon Cobb**, sold to Gallowmoor last
spring, walked back out of it in September, and hides them in the hold of a frozen collier, the *Patience*, until he
can get them down the Reach. Harker's searchers walk the Reach on the second and third nights.

**Ursel Cobb**, Dickon's sister, a liar of forty who has never shaken on anything, has come to Pellam to get into
Harker's books. She has a con, and needs a face: sell Harker, who is buying fleece still on sheep nobody can go and
see, two hundred ewes at Hawsfold under Swale Fell. She tells the company it is about money.

## The acts

1. **The Liberty** (the first morning). Down the North Road in the snow. A penny a stair. Ursel takes your purse and
   holds it up. The Fair. At noon the crier cries the Liberty; at four the Court sits, and charges a preacher,
   Edrin Callow, with *unswearing*, and asks who will be his hand.
2. **The Flock That Is Not** (the first day and the second). The Squire of Hawsfold built of props and claims; the
   real shepherd of Hawsfold in the Cheaping asking loudly for Harker; supper at Fleece Row; the terms, half now and
   half at the Landing, where the hand will bind; the tour of the "stock"; the handshake under the Glove.
3. **The Drowned** (the second morning on). The hats; the tracks; the Lisk Wife's lantern; the hulk; Dickon. The
   turn: Ursel's lie; what the farm in the hills is; who the drowned are and that they are alive. The leak: a careless
   word to Abel, a sale to Kemble, a lantern on the Reach that sees where you went.
4. **The Night on the Ice** (the second night). The flight, if anyone attempts it: the bondhouse's river door, iced in;
   old Fowke asleep on his keg; the searchers and their lurcher; Tredgold's sledges, or the bear, or a lie to Kemble
   that sends them onto the downs; the watermen's saws, if they had no work.
5. **The Landing** (the last day). Ursel's question at the Ice Chapel; the wedding at noon (Thessaly and the alderman;
   or Bel and the alderman; or Thessaly married already, to a printer; or the bear); the Landing at two, case by case:
   Callow, Gant, the flock, the hired, the last hands; the ice goes out that night; the morning after.

## The conflicts

- **The company against Harker**, by deception (the Squire) or from inside (the Ring); and by what is said at the
  Landing.
- **The company against itself**: what it says, and to whom, is what the searchers learn (the leak); its own hands
  bind it (the stairs, Callow, the flock, the Ring, the handfast).
- **Ursel against the company**: a partner who will rob you if you give her no reason not to.
- **The law against itself**: a Liberty that frees speech and binds hands has made the sale of people lawful; the
  knowing doctrine is the law turned on itself, argued from inside it.
- **The comedy's couples against the match**: two weddings that ought to happen and one that is a bribe.
- **The watermen against the ice**: the Fair is their liberty and their ruin.

## The settlements

| Ending | When | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **The Pillory** | exposed with a hand on the flock, and unable to pay it back twice over | the con's farce ending: snow thrown, a joke in every taproom |
| **Five Coin a Head** | the drowned sold to Kemble | the rogue's damnation, told plainly |
| **The Unswearing** | the doctrine argued and Harker's books laid before a Reeve who will rule | the law changed, for as long as there is ice, and the Exchange in a panic |
| **The Long Con** | the hired traded for the phantom flock and given their hands back | the trick; a landed debt left with Abel Haws |
| **Not Landed** | the hired down the Reach before the Landing | the flight; the bondhouse open on the river |
| **The Flock That Was** | the real flock bought from Abel and sold to Harker | the honest swindle; the hired landed all the same |
| **The Score, and Ursel's** | the earnest taken, then taken from you | the double-cross |
| **The Score** | the earnest taken, the bargain unlanded | the rogue's success; the hired landed and nobody tells it |
| **Hired** | shaken at the Ring and nothing done | you go to Gallowmoor |
| **Away** | not in Pellam on the morning after, with no score | news from a drover |
| **Landed** | nothing else | the default: everything landed, including twenty people |

And, as the morning's news, independent of these: who the alderman married; whether Thessaly is a printer's wife;
whether Callow was acquitted, convicted, slipped or died; whether Gant is in the Clink, free or in the compter; whether
the bear lived; Quarle's cream or his ruin; the Lisk Wife's lantern; the Cobbs on the North Road; a handfast landed.
