# The synopsis: acts, people, conflicts, settlements

## The situation

The Lisk has frozen under Pellam Bridge for the first time in thirty-one winters. The roads have been shut by snow for
a month. On the ice there is a Frost Fair, and the ice has its own law, kept by the Watermen's Company and its Reeve,
Clemency Broom, and cried every noon from a mast with a glove on it: **say what you will; keep what you shake on.** On
the last day of the ice the Court lands the Fair's bargains, hand to hand, both parties before the Reeve, so that they
hold on dry ground; what is not landed goes out with the river.

**Josias Harker**, wool-factor and churchwarden, has moved his Hiring Ring onto the ice, where none of the city's
statutes on servants reach. He hires the north country's poor for "a year's place on a farm in the hills" and sells
their indentures, landed, to Ossett's agent for the lead mines at Gallowmoor at eight coin a head. He never lies
outright, and never puts out his hand: they shake on his bargains; he accepts them.

Some of his hired have slipped Kemble's line in the crush on Freezeland Street, and had their hats hung by the hole on
the Black Reach that never freezes, because a drowned man's hand is no hand. **Widow Saffery**, whose son went through
the ice at the last Frost Fair and who walks the Reach every night with a lantern (the watermen call her the Lisk
Wife), hangs the hats and hides them in a cellar in the bridge's starling. **Dickon Cobb**, sold to Gallowmoor last
spring, walked back out of it in September, and hides them in the hold of a frozen collier, the *Patience*, until he
can get them down the Reach. Harker's searchers walk the Reach on the second and third nights.

**Ursel Cobb**, Dickon's sister, a liar of forty who has never shaken on anything, has come to Pellam to get into
Harker's books. She has a con, and needs a face: sell Harker, who is buying fleece still on sheep nobody can go and
see, two hundred ewes at Hawsfold under Swale Fell. She tells the company it is about money.

## The acts

The three days are three skies, and each sky is an act's weather; each morning in Pellam opens on a line that says
which day it is and what the air is doing (`frosts.kyr`), and a bill on the Glove Post says what is on.

1. **The Liberty** (the first morning; *a hard blue frost*: everything bright, every sound carrying, nothing that can
   be checked). Down the North Road in the snow. A penny a stair. Ursel takes your purse and holds it up. The bill:
   HIRING AT TEN, COURT AT FOUR, THE BENCH AT SEVEN. An empty pillory with a blank placard. At noon the crier cries the
   Liberty; at four the Court sits, and charges a preacher, Edrin Callow, with *unswearing*, and asks who will be his
   hand. Callow says he shall not see the ice go out.
2. **The Flock That Is Not** (the first day and the second). The Squire of Hawsfold built of props and claims; the
   real shepherd of Hawsfold in the Cheaping asking loudly for Harker; supper at Fleece Row; the terms, half now and
   half at the Landing, where the hand will bind; the tour of the "stock"; the handshake under the Glove; Harker's
   second-day test (which way the beck runs). At night, in the Lamb, Ursel's three confidences: Swale Head, the
   factor's glove on the kitchen table, the Bench at fifteen.
3. **The Drowned** (the second morning on; *the drowned weather*, a fog off the Reach to the height of a house, in
   which the booths come at you one at a time like ships). The hats; the tracks; the Lisk Wife's lantern; the hulk;
   Dickon, and Gallowmoor, where the overman counts hands and never looks at a face. The turn: Ursel's lie; what the
   farm in the hills is; who the drowned are and that they are alive. The leak: a careless word to Abel, a sale to
   Kemble, a lantern on the Reach that sees where you went. The wolves begin: Abel's ewes taken from a roofed fold;
   four sets of tracks at the head of the Reach; the Book of Frosts, which has them in four frosts of seven.
4. **The Night on the Ice** (the second night: the horror's act). The flight, if anyone attempts it: the bondhouse's
   river door, iced in; old Fowke asleep on his keg, or sat with till he is; Kemble's key off his chain; the
   searchers and their lurcher, paid, lied to, slipped, fought or met by a bear; Tredgold's sledges; the watermen's
   saws, if they had no work. At ten, the wolves, howling from the foot of the Reach, heard across the whole city, and
   sitting across the sledge road in a row: fought (and they are dangerous), driven off with fire, fed the ox's offal,
   gone round downwind, faced down, or outrun on the sledges. Dickon, if nobody brings him the bondhouse, goes south
   with the drowned at three: with fire, if someone warned him; and if not, a waterman finds a cap in the morning.
5. **The Landing** (the last day; *the thaw-wind*: the bridge dripping, the ice grey as old pewter, the glove limp on
   its mast). Ursel's question at the Ice Chapel; the wedding at noon (Thessaly and the alderman; or Bel and the
   alderman; or Thessaly married already, to a printer; or the bear); the Landing at two, case by case, the list
   chalked at the Reeve's elbow: Callow, Gant, the flock, the hired, the last hands. Ursel watching Harker's hands.
   The ice goes out that night; the morning after, the city's news, including, if it was laid, the Statute of
   Servants nailed to Harker's door.

## The hand, as a theme

Every principal person stands somewhere on one question, *what is a hand for?*, and they are made to rhyme:

- **Harker** was stood in the Ring at nine by a father with a note out against the wool, at Swale Head, and shook
  because he was told to. He learned that "a hand is all a poor man has to sell", and so he buys them; he never lies
  about the price, never puts out his own hand, and pays every spring for a pound of wax before the Saint's scales
  "for the weight".
- **Ursel**, of the same parish, watched her father shake on a note at his own kitchen table with the factor's glove
  beside it, and lose the farm by it; she has never shaken since. She has never asked the factor's name. (The dates
  allow it to have been Harker. Nothing in the work says so, and nothing should.) Told that Harker was a hired child
  of Swale Head, she says she would rather he were a devil.
- **Tansy Hobb**, fifteen, from the low farm at Swale Head, shook because it was done, and has heard from her
  grandmother of a Swale Head girl who never shook on anything and did well in the town; she does not know it is Ursel.
- **Dickon**, at Gallowmoor, gave his hand to the overman twice a day to be counted, and wrote letters the overman
  burned.
- **Callow** says no hand binds the one who gave it, because nobody stays; the third answer (a hand binds what its
  giver knew) is the law's way of agreeing with him and with Harker at once.
- **The city's Statute of Servants** forbids carrying anyone hired within its liberties out of them but to the place
  they were hired for; the ice is the Watermen's, not the city's, which is why Harker hires there. The Liberty, which
  frees the tongue, is the gap in the law the hands fall through.
- **The comedy's couples** are hands too: Gosling and Bel married for four hours on the last ice and never landed;
  the Short Frost, when every handfast melted, is why nobody marries on the ice.

## The conflicts

- **The company against Harker**, by deception (the Squire) or from inside (the Ring); and by what is said at the
  Landing.
- **The company against itself**: what it says, and to whom, is what the searchers learn (the leak); its own hands
  bind it (the stairs, Callow, the flock, the Ring, the handfast).
- **Ursel against the company**: a partner who will rob you if you give her no reason not to.
- **The law against itself**: a Liberty that frees speech and binds hands has made the sale of people lawful; the
  knowing doctrine is the law turned on itself, argued from inside it.
- **The comedy's couples against the match**: two weddings that ought to happen and one that is a bribe.
- **The watermen against the ice**: the Fair is their liberty and their ruin.

## The settlements

| Ending | When | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **The Pillory** | exposed with a hand on the flock, and unable to pay it back twice over | the con's farce ending: snow thrown, a joke in every taproom |
| **Five Coin a Head** | the drowned sold to Kemble | the rogue's damnation, told plainly |
| **The Unswearing** | the doctrine argued and Harker's books laid before a Reeve who will rule | the law changed, for as long as there is ice, and the Exchange in a panic |
| **The Long Con** | the hired traded for the phantom flock and given their hands back | the trick; a landed debt left with Abel Haws |
| **Not Landed** | the hired down the Reach before the Landing | the flight; the bondhouse open on the river |
| **The Flock That Was** | the real flock bought from Abel and sold to Harker | the honest swindle; the hired landed all the same |
| **The Score, and Ursel's** | the earnest taken, then taken from you | the double-cross |
| **The Score** | the earnest taken, the bargain unlanded | the rogue's success; the hired landed and nobody tells it |
| **Hired** | shaken at the Ring and nothing done | you go to Gallowmoor |
| **Away** | not in Pellam on the morning after, with no score | news from a drover |
| **Landed** | nothing else | the default: everything landed, including twenty people |

And, as the morning's news, independent of these: who the alderman married; whether Thessaly is a printer's wife;
whether Callow was acquitted, convicted, slipped or died; whether Gant is in the Clink, free or in the compter; whether
the bear lived; Quarle's cream or his ruin; the Lisk Wife's lantern; the Cobbs on the North Road; a handfast landed.
