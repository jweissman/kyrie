# Rewards, and what they cost

Pellam's rewards follow the collection's rule (experience for what is resolved, not for what is killed) and the
register's: most of what the company carries out of Pellam is money, a reputation, a person, or a thing someone gave
it, and almost every one of them was paid for by somebody.

## Experience (`growth.kyr`)

Twenty-eight milestones, 100 to 700 experience each, 7,850 in all, each to every member of the company. No journey
can earn them all: the three levers exclude one another, and the arcs of Callow's answers do. The Long Con journey
earns 2,000 to each member (the bard and Ursel), the Unswearing 2,500 to a scholar alone, the Ring 1,200 to a
labourer, the Score 500 and the Pillory 300. The chart asks 1,000 for the second level and 3,000 for the third, so a first-level character leaves Pellam at the second level, or near the third; that is Pellam's whole "high" reward, and it is honest about it. The high milestones are
the three levers and the flight:

| Milestone | Worth | For |
|---|---|---|
| made the law of the ice say what it meant | 700 | the Unswearing |
| got the hired down the Reach, not landed | 600 | the flight |
| gave twenty hired their hands back | 600 | the Long Con |
| found a third answer to Callow's question | 400 | the knowing doctrine |
| brought Ursel to her brother | 400 | Ursel's arc |
| read Harker's ledger / bond book; found the drowned were not drowned; found Dickon | 300 each | the mystery |
| kept your hand for Callow; went into the bondhouse as one of the hired; saw the alderman say it; married the printer to Mr. Icicle; landed a hand with Ursel | 300 each | keeping faith, and the comedy's marriages |

None is given for the Score, the Pillory or the sale of the drowned. A rogue's ending is its own reward.

## Things

| Reward | Got by | What it does | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Silver Tongue** (`silver_tongue_kind`, a charm) | the Liars' Bench: the best lie (deception, hard), or the truth told as a tall tale | Worn, brings *deception* to whoever wears it: every later lie in Kyrie, in any country, is easier | told by the truth, it costs the company Harker's trust for good (Kemble was listening) |
| **Sir Lionel's coat** (`gant_coat_kind`, a tool) | Gant got to the Clink, or his thirty paid | Worn, brings *persuasion*: "anyone who wears it is spoken to as if they had money"; shown to Harker, +1 belief | thirty coin, or a night walk past bailiffs |
| **A card printed on the ice**; **a card with two names** | a penny at Noll's | nothing, mechanically; the two-name card is part of Ursel's history with you | a penny |
| **The register of the last Frost Fair** | a shilling to Doody, or free to the cloth | read, it is the alderman's first wedding | Doody wants it back, and will say so if you carry it off past him (it is lent) |
| **Harker's ledger**, **Kemble's bond book** | the desk (lockwork, or Thessaly's key), the bondhouse | the law's evidence at the Landing | carried in front of Harker, he objects |
| **Twenty indentures** | the Long Con | the hired, landed to you; given back before the Reeve | a landed debt of two hundred ewes at the shearing |

## Coin

| How | How much | What else |
|---|---|---|
| the Score | 30 (Harker's half in hand) | Ursel takes it if she has no reason to trust you |
| the Flock That Was | 60 from Harker, less 40 to Abel | an honest bargain, landed |
| the Long Con | none | twenty people |
| selling the drowned | 15 | Dickon landed for Gallowmoor |
| the Unswearing | none, and 60 back to Harker if you had shaken on the flock | the law changed |

The money in Pellam is small on purpose: twenty coin to begin, at most sixty in the best swindle. What the company
can win here is out of proportion to its coin, and the work says so.

## People

- **Ursel Cobb** joins (a third-level thief: stealth, lockwork, deception, trapwork) as a partner who does not shake;
  can leave with the earnest; can be landed to you by a handfast at the Landing, "good from here to the sea, as long as
  you both live". The game does not follow a marriage further than the morning after; in a world that goes on she
  travels with you, and the Cobbs' line in the morning's news is about her and Dickon only when she is not.
- **The hired** are not companions; their freedom is the reward of three of the endings.
- **Callow**, acquitted, is not a companion either: he stays in Bankside, preaching his amended doctrine.

## The narrative rewards, and their implications

- **The knowing doctrine** ("a hand is bound to what the one who shook it knew") is cried at the Glove Post and written
  in the Court's book. It is law only on the ice, and the ice is gone by morning; the ending says so, and that the
  Exchange panics because everyone knows which half of their bargains it would void on dry ground. A later author could
  bring it back to dry land. Nothing in the engine or other modules reads it.
- **A landed debt**: the Long Con leaves the company owing two hundred ewes "in any court of the country". Kyrie has
  no courts outside Pellam that read it. It is a debt in the fiction only, and the ending leaves it with Abel Haws.
- **The Liberty's lesson** is mechanical in this module only: Grave on every handshake. A player who has played Pellam
  will read every later `Grave` prompt in Kyrie as a handshake, which is the right lesson.
