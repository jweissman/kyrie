**Stand beside Callow and give the Reeve the argument you made together: a hand binds only what the one who shook it knew**

Callow begins, and then stops, and turns to you, and bows. ‘My hand,’ he says, ‘put it better.’ So you say it, to the Reeve and the whole silent Fair: that a word is free on the ice, and a hand is bound; but a hand is only bound to what was really there when it was given, what the one who shook it knew he was shaking on; and a hand got by a lie about the very thing shaken on was never given to that thing at all. Justice Broom listens with her eyes open. When you have finished she says, slowly, ‘That is not unswearing.
