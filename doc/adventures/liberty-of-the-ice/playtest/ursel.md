Justice Broom sits at the foot of the mast on the Court’s chair, set on a herring barrel, with her trumpet; the waterman-clerk at her side with the Court’s book; and at her other elbow, on a stool, as the city’s witness, Alderman Gosling, in his chain. Master Harker stands at the front with Kemble and his book. Above them all the great frozen glove. The clerk calls Magister Edrin Callow, on the charge of unswearing. Justice Broom turns her trumpet toward the empty stocks, as if they might say something. ‘Discharged,’ she says. ‘By the Flow.’ Nobody laughs.

**Wait › An hour**

The clerk calls Sir Lionel Gant, of nowhere in particular. Master Harker asks the Court that when the ice goes Sir Lionel be cried off it at Hay Stairs, into the hands of the bailiffs who wait there with his writ, for thirty coin. Sir Lionel stands before the Reeve with his hat in his hand and says, very quietly, ‘It was three.’

**Wait › An hour**

Under your feet, all over Pellam, the ice has begun to talk: a long creak like a ship’s timbers, a crack like a gun a long way off, and then a groan that goes on and on, up the whole length of the river. On the ice everybody stops and listens, and laughs, too loudly, and goes on.

**Wait › An hour**

The clerk calls Master Harker’s hired, twenty hands, a year’s service on a farm in the hills. They stand in a line before the Reeve in their blankets, with their tokens in their hats: crooks, pails, whips, mops. Tansy Hobb is at the end, with her milk-pail on its string. Harker stands beside them with his palm open, kindly, and one by one they come to shake again before the Reeve.

*(2 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › A while**

Freezeland Street at lamp-lighting: lanterns on every oar, the ice gone gold in them, and the Fair louder now it cannot be seen so well. Time passes. The clerk cries it, hand by hand: ‘Landed! Landed! Landed!’ twenty times, and writes it in the Court’s book, and Master Harker thanks the Reeve, and Kemble walks his hired back off the ice to the bondhouse, to wait for Ossett’s agent and the thaw. Last of all the clerk calls a hand of this morning, at the Ice Chapel, by Parson Doody, for a shilling: Ursel Cobb, and you. The Fair, which knows everything, goes ‘Ahh.’ Justice Broom looks at the two of you over her trumpet. ‘Land it,’ she says, ‘and it’s good from here to the sea, as long as you both live. Or let it go out with the river, tonight, and there’s an end of it. Your hands.’

**Shake Ursel's hand again before the Reeve, and land it**

Ursel looks at you. Then she puts out her hand, first, in front of all Pellam, and you shake it, and the clerk cries ‘Landed!’ and the Fair cheers as if somebody had won a race on the Flashes. Ursel Cobb, who has never given her hand on anything in forty years, has given it twice in a day. ‘Well,’ she says, very loud, to cover something. ‘Well. That’s me caught.’ Sam and Ursel Cobb each gain 300 experience: landed a hand with Ursel Cobb.
