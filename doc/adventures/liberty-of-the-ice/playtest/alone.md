# Nothing done: Landed

Seed `lisk`, the bard of the title page; fixture `alone`; 72 steps; ended at watch 420, at the ending "Landed"; 0 experience in all. Written by `bun scripts/liberty-of-the-ice-transcripts.ts` from the journey as the page told it.

*(The first page)*

The North Road comes down off the fells between walls of snow higher than your head, trodden to a trench by a month of feet. Below, in a white bowl of hills, Pellam: smoke going straight up from a thousand chimneys, a grey cathedral tower with a gilded pair of scales on its spire, a long bridge on many arches, and under the bridge, where the river ought to be, a street. A street of tents and smoke and pennants and people, on the river, a mile long. Faintly, all the way up here, a sound like a market and a wedding at once. The North Road goes up out of Pellam into the snow.

**By the North Road, to the North Bar**

You set out from the North Road, above Pellam early, with your shadow long in front of you on the road. The North Bar: a gate-tower with its gates open and snowed into the open position, and a toll-house nobody is in. On the gate somebody has nailed a broadside, half torn: THE GREAT FROST — A FAIR UPON THE ICE — ALL WELCOME — NO LAW BUT THE GLOVE. Beyond it the city goes down to the river in steep white streets. The North Road goes up out of Pellam into the snow.

**By Northgate Street, to the Cheaping**

The Cheaping, Pellam’s wool market: a great square with a market-cross and a covered hall on stone legs where the fleeces are sold in summer, empty now, snowed knee-deep, with paths trodden across it in every direction to the Exchange, to Saint Evenhand’s, to Fleece Row, and above all down Swan Lane to the river. Nobody is buying wool here. Everybody is going to the ice.

**Wait › An hour**

A shepherd has come in over the North Bar out of the snow, with a crook and a black-and-white dog, asking the first person he meets in the Cheaping, very loudly, where a man might find a buyer for two hundred fell ewes, from Hawsfold, under Swale Fell. At noon the hiring ends. Kemble walks Harker’s hired off the ice in a line, two by two, across to Hay Stairs and up onto the wharf and in at the door of the long blind warehouse with J. HARKER. WOOL painted on its gable, to be kept there, warm and fed, he tells anyone who asks, until the Landing.

**Wait › An hour**

An hour passes. Bess thumps her tail on the ground without getting up, which is as much welcome as you are going to get.

**Wait › An hour**

Nobody would be Callow’s hand. At five the watermen put him in the stocks at the foot of the Glove Post, in the frost, with a blanket and a brazier, until the Landing; and he preaches from them to whoever passes, between coughs.

**Wait › An hour**

An hour passes. It is a long time since you slept. You are weary, and will fight the worse for it until you sleep.

*(4 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Word comes down Freezeland Street at a run: the bear’s off his chain at the bear-stake, and gone to the ox roast, and is eating the Butchers’ Company’s ox off the spit with his muzzle torn off, and nobody will go near him.

**Wait › An hour**

All over the Fair this morning: another hat on the stake at the Black Reach. Jem Rudd’s, the carter’s lad from Garthwaite, hired yesterday at the Ring; he was in Kemble’s line at noon and not in the bondhouse at dark. The river has taken him, everyone says, and touches the ice with a knuckle.

*(3 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At noon three of Harker’s wharf men come down Freezeland Street with boar-spears, at the Butchers’ Company’s asking. Before they reach the ox, Absolute gets up off it, all at once, and goes away from them down the ice past the last booth, onto the Reach, at a long rolling run, with his broken chain jumping behind him, and Hester Mabb running after him calling, until she cannot run any more.

*(9 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

In the night, from down the Reach, a long sound of saws, a dozen of them, and men singing to keep the time. By morning there is a channel of black water forty feet wide across the foot of the Reach from bank to bank, steaming, and the sledge road south ends at it.

**Wait › An hour**

Late in the night, from down the Reach, a roar, and dogs, and men shouting, and lanterns going every way at once over the ice. In the morning they say Harker’s searchers met Hester Mabb’s bear on the Black Reach in the dark, and ran, all three, and the dog with them, back to Hay Wharf, and would not go out again; and that the bear went on down the grey ice toward the hole that never freezes, and through it.

*(4 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

In the small hours of the last day, in the stocks at the foot of the Glove Post, Magister Edrin Callow stopped coughing. The watermen found him at first light with his blanket round him and his brazier out, and the snow not melting on his face. Someone has written on the board at the foot of the Glove, under KEEP WHAT YOU SHAKE ON, in charcoal: NOTHING STAYS.

*(3 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Another hat on the stake at the Black Reach this morning: Polly Sneath’s, the shepherd’s girl from the second hiring. The watermen have stopped laughing about the Lisk Wife.

*(3 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At noon, at the Ice Chapel, Parson Doody marries Thessaly Harker to Alderman Hamnet Gosling for a shilling, sober, in front of half the Fair. The bride says her part in a clear dry voice as if she were reading a bill. The alderman says his as if someone had died. Master Harker kisses his daughter, and shakes his son-in-law’s hand, and smiles all through the wedding-breakfast at the Frozen Lamb, where Bel Ashby serves it herself and does not look at anyone.

**Wait › An hour**

Under your feet, all over Pellam, the ice has begun to talk: a long creak like a ship’s timbers, a crack like a gun a long way off, and then a groan that goes on and on, up the whole length of the river. On the ice everybody stops and listens, and laughs, too loudly, and goes on.

*(2 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At Hay Stairs, as the watermen hurry the last of the Fair off the ice, the bailiffs are waiting with their writ, and Sir Lionel Gant walks up the stairs into their hands with his hat on, very straight, saying ‘It was three’ to the bailiff on his left. The booths of Freezeland Street are coming down: sailcloth folded, oars carried up Swan Stairs on men’s shoulders, the ox’s bones dragged off on a sledge. The ice is grey under them, and wet.

**Wait › An hour**

The Cheaping, in the thaw: slush to the ankle, the wool-hall dripping, and factors standing about in groups in the wet, talking low about what was landed and what was not. From down the Reach, all at once, a sound like a city falling down: the ice going out. It comes up the river from the sea toward the bridge in the dark, crack after crack, louder, the whole frozen Lisk breaking into plates and rearing up against the starlings and going under, and the black water coming up through it steaming, and people on both banks with torches shouting at it, or crying, or cheering. By midnight there is a river under Pellam Bridge, running, full of ice, where Freezeland Street was.

*(8 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Of the bear: Hester Mabb sat all day at the bear-stake with the broken chain in her lap, and went up the North Road this morning alone. Of the wedding: Alderman Gosling and his bride went home to Fleece Row after the Landing, and at midnight, they say, when the ice went, the alderman was seen alone on the bridge in his chain, looking down at it. Of the debtor: Sir Lionel Gant is in the Fleece Row compter for thirty coin, saying to anyone who visits him that it was three.

**Wait › An hour**

In the morning there is a river under Pellam Bridge, black and running, full of ice. Everything that was landed at the Glove Post is good from here to the sea: Harker’s twenty hired among the rest, who went south at first light on Ossett’s agent’s sledges over the downs, roped, with their tokens still in their hats, to the farm in the hills at Gallowmoor. Pellam goes back to the wool. The watermen put their boats in. Noll Brisket prints a ballad of the Great Frost, at a penny, in which nobody drowns.
