# The Liberty of the Ice — Stage 1: the original treatment

*Written before the engine was read for this purpose, as the brief asks: ambitious first, accommodated later. What
survived, what was cut and why, is in [01-editorial.md](01-editorial.md) and [10-changes.md](10-changes.md).*

## The idea in a sentence

Once in a generation the river Lisk freezes hard under the nineteen arches of Pellam Bridge, and the city holds a
**Frost Fair** on the ice; and because the ice lies in no parish and under no lord, it has its own law for as long as
it lasts, cried at noon from the Glove Post every day of the Fair: **"Say what you will. Keep what you shake on."**

On the ice a lie is no crime. A handshake is a bond the Court of the Ice will enforce to the last penny, the last
year of service, the pillory. You have come to Pellam in the snow to make money out of that, and so has everyone
else.

## Why this, here

The repertoire survey ([repertoire-2026-10-26.md](../repertoire-2026-10-26.md)) found Kyrie's collection telling one
story seven times: a community that misreads what it inherited, an outsider who reads the evidence, a descent to the
mechanism. It found no comedy as structure, no satire, no romance, no lying as a craft, no wanting, nothing small that
stayed small. Its portfolio proposed four works that answer this, each rural and small: a con at a hiring fair, a
wedding week, a snowbound farmhouse, a road with a heretic.

A **frost fair** is the one metropolitan occasion that holds all four honestly, because the historical ones did.
London's frost fairs had printing presses on the ice selling cards with your name "printed on the Thames", ox
roasts, skittles, gin tents, bear-baiting, Fleet-style weddings without banns, debtors who could not be arrested on
the river, watermen out of work charging tolls to step onto it, and people drowned when it broke. They were carnival:
a world turned over for a few weeks, which everyone knew would melt.

So the region is a city, its frozen river, and its fair; the register is comedy with satire's teeth and a romance
in it, darkening at night; the philosophy is literally underfoot (a fair built on water that will be water again);
and the clock is not a festival date but **the thaw**, which nobody can name the day of.

## The satire's law

The Liberty of the Ice is a real social law the player must learn and use, not a background joke.

1. **Speech is free.** Nothing said on the ice is actionable. You can claim to own a flock, be a squire, be married,
   be dying. Pellam folk, who have grown up with frost-fair stories, are fluent in it: they *never* say "I promise";
   they say "as like as not", "I'd not swear to it", "near enough".
2. **The hand is bound.** A bargain struck by a shake of hands on the ice (with a penny across it, "hand and penny")
   is enforced by the Court of the Ice whatever was said to get it. Hiring, buying, marrying, bail.
3. **The ice is nobody's.** No bailiff, warrant, banns, tithe or guild rule of the banks runs on it. Debtors live on
   it. Lovers marry on it without banns. Hirers hire on it without the city's statutes on servants.
4. **The Landing.** On the last day of the ice the Court sits to *land* its bargains: to cry each handshake ashore, so
   that it binds on the banks once there is no ice to stand on. What is landed is good in any court of the country.

The satire is that this is a fair description of a free market and of most courtships, and that a city which has
perfected the distinction between lying and promising has made trafficking in people perfectly legal.

## The region

**Pellam** stands where the Great North Road crosses the Lisk, a broad slow river a day from the sea. It is a
wool city: fleeces come down off the fells, are sorted, sold, spun and shipped. The Old Bridge has nineteen narrow
arches on stone starlings, and the starlings slow the river so that in a hard winter it freezes above them first. The
last Frost Fair was thirty-one winters ago. People who were young at it speak of it the way other cities speak of a
war.

### Districts

- **The Ice** — between the bridge and the Watermen's Stairs. *Freezeland Street*, the double row of booths down the
  middle (sailcloth on oars); the **Glove Post**, where the great leather glove is hung while the Liberty lasts; the
  **Court of the Ice**, a tent of sailcloth and a table on two barrels; the **printing booth**; the **ox roast**; the
  skittle alley and the dice; the **Frozen Lamb** gin tent; the **Ice Chapel**, a booth where an unbeneficed parson
  marries anyone for a shilling; the **Hiring Ring**, moved onto the ice this year by Master Harker; and downstream,
  past the last booth, the **Black Reach**, where the ice is grey and people do not go at night.
- **Pellam Bridge** — houses and shops on the bridge itself, a chapel on the middle pier, the bridge gate with its
  toll, and under it the starlings, where you can climb down to the ice through a trap in the chapel crypt.
- **The City** (north bank) — the Cheaping (the wool market, shut by the snow), Saint Evenhand's with its great
  honest scales, the Guildhall, the Tolbooth gaol, the Wool Exchange where fleeces not yet shorn are bought and sold,
  and on Fleece Row the house and counting-house of **Josias Harker**, wool-factor.
- **Bankside** (south bank) — inns (the Spotted Calf), the shut playhouse, the empty bear-garden, the Watermen's Hall
  full of idle watermen, the Clink, and Hay Wharf, where Harker's **bondhouse** stands with its back on the ice.
- **The Reach and the marshes** (downstream) — where the river widens; the frozen hulk *Patience*; the sledge road
  south over the ice; the reed-cutters' huts; the hole the watermen call the Lisk Wife's.
- **The Flashes** (upstream) — water-meadows flooded and frozen, where the quality skate at dusk and the young of the
  city go to be seen by each other.

### The life of the city apart from the mystery

The city is busy with ordinary winter trouble: the wool can't move, so the factors gamble on fleeces that are still
on the sheep; the watermen can't work, so they charge tolls; the bakers have sold out by noon; a debtor has lived on
the ice since the freeze; the bear-garden's bear is cold and cross; the printer's ink freezes; a wedding is coming.

## The principal people

- **Ursel Cobb** — your partner, or the woman who means to be. A cheerful, efficient liar of forty, with a laugh like a
  door. Sentimental about exactly one thing: her younger brother **Dickon**, hired last spring at Pellam's hiring by
  Master Harker for "a place on a farm in the hills", who has not written since. Ursel tells you the con is about money.
  It is about Dickon. Her arc: a woman who has never shaken on anything in her life, who must decide whether to make one
  oath on the ice.
- **Master Josias Harker** — wool-factor and hiring-master. Churchwarden of Saint Evenhand's. Never lies outright and
  never shakes on anything himself: the hired shake on his bargains, he only *accepts* them. He hires the poor of the
  north country at the fair for a year's "service in the hills", and sells their indentures, landed and good in any
  court, to the lead mines in the south. This year, with the roads shut by snow and the Hiring Ring on the ice,
  everything he hires is ice-bargained and nobody's law can touch it. The mark.
- **Thessaly Harker** — his daughter, twenty-four, the cleverest person in the city. Writes the *Frost Gazette*, the
  scandal-sheet printed on the ice, under the name *Mr. Icicle*, and has been hinting in it at the drowned. To be
  married at the end of the Fair to Alderman **Hamnet Gosling**, a match her father made.
- **Alderman Hamnet Gosling** — fifty, a widower, a bad poet, a good man about everything but his own heart. He will
  sit beside Justice Broom at the Landing as the city's witness. Harker wants a son-in-law on that bench. Gosling's
  heart is with **Bel Ashby**, who keeps the Frozen Lamb, and was his sweetheart at the last Frost Fair, thirty-one
  years ago.
- **Noll Brisket** — the printer on the ice, nineteen, ink to the elbows, who prints names on cards for a penny and
  the *Gazette* for a secret. In love with Thessaly, who does not know it, or knows and is pretending.
- **Justice Clemency Broom** — the Ice-Reeve, elected by the Watermen's Company to keep the Court of the Ice. A
  waterman's widow of seventy with an ear trumpet and a connoisseur's love of a lie well told. She cannot be bought and
  does not need to be: she can be *entertained*. She judges handshakes absolutely and lies as art, and every Fair she
  gives the Silver Tongue to the best liar on the ice.
- **Magister Edrin Callow** — a preacher of the Flow, sixty, consumptive, vain, generous, a mimic. He preaches on the
  ice that nothing stays and no oath binds the one who did not make it ("you are not the man who shook"). The Court
  has charged him with *unswearing*, the one crime of the ice. The ice has no gaol: an accused is given into the
  custody of a hand who shakes on producing them at the Landing. Nobody will shake for Callow.
- **Gaffer Tredgold** — master of the idle watermen, who charges a penny a stair onto the ice and wants the river back.
  He has the tools and the men to cut a channel and end the Fair.
- **The Lisk Wife** — the watermen leave her a penny at the black hole on the Reach. Some say she takes one a Frost Fair.
  Some say she is a madwoman whose child went through the ice at the last one.

## The overarching mystery: the drowned

On the second morning a hat lies beside a hole in the ice off the Black Reach. Everyone says the river has taken its
own. On the third morning, another. The watermen say the Lisk Wife. The *Gazette* says nothing, and then says
something.

What the player can find, in layers:

1. **They are Harker's hired.** Every one of the drowned shook at the Hiring Ring.
2. **They are not drowned.** A woodsman sees that the footprints by the hole go *on*, and that the hole was cut with a
   saw. A drowned man's bargain dies with him: the hired are faking their deaths to slip their indentures, and someone
   in the city is hiding them.
3. **The hiding.** The hidden are in the hulk *Patience* on the Reach, in the starlings under the bridge, in the
   Clink. Dickon Cobb is among them; he came back last month, alone, out of the south, and has been getting the new
   hired out before Harker can land them.
4. **The informer.** Harker's searchers find hiding places they should not know of. Someone in the network sells the
   drowned back. The player's last discovery is who, and it is a person, chosen, for a reason: not the Lisk Wife, not
   the city's law, a neighbour.

The horror is real and human: Harker's searchers with dogs and lanterns on the ice at night, looking for people who
are officially dead; and under it the uncanny the work never confirms, a woman on the Black Reach at night.

## The acts

1. **The Liberty** (day 1). Down the north road in snow. Ursel Cobb picks your pocket in the first ten minutes and gives
   it back, to show you what she can do and what the law is: she never shook on anything. Noon, the Liberty cried at the
   Glove Post. The Fair. Ursel's proposal: Harker is buying fleeces not yet shorn, and the roads are shut, so nobody can
   go and see a flock. Sell him one.
2. **The Flock That Is Not** (days 1–3). Build the lie. A borrowed name (the Squire of Hawsfold), a forged tally (from
   Noll's press), a tuft of fell wool, a shepherd's letter, the real shepherd (who has come down to the Fair to sell
   his real flock, and cannot lie) kept away from Harker. Lies told to particular people, held by them, repeated by them,
   colliding when two of them meet. The comedy compounds: the Squire of Hawsfold is invited to the wedding.
3. **The Drowned** (day 2 onward). The hats on the ice. The turn: Ursel lied to you; Harker's ledger shows where the
   hired go; Dickon is alive and hiding; somebody is selling them.
4. **The Night on the Ice** (night 3). The searchers. The bondhouse: get yourself hired and locked in, or break in
   from the ice, or walk in as a buyer. The hulk. The informer.
5. **The Landing** (the last day). Callow tried. The bargains landed. The wedding at the Ice Chapel. The thaw that
   night: everyone off the ice, the ice going out, and whoever is on the Black Reach.

## The small adventures (first list)

Each with a person, a place or thing, an uncertainty, more than one approach, a complication, a resolution, an echo.

1. **The pocket.** Ursel's opening theft. Catch her at it (insight), let her, or rob her back.
2. **The debtor on the ice.** Sir Lionel Gant has lived on the ice since the freeze, because his creditors' bailiffs
   cannot reach it. At the thaw they will have him. Pay his debt, forge it paid, find that his bond is itself a forgery,
   get him to the Clink (a liberty of its own) by a way the bailiffs do not watch, or marry him to his creditor.
3. **Absolute.** The bear-garden's dancing bear, cold and cross, gets loose on the ice. Honey, a fiddle, a strong pair
   of arms, or leave it: a loose bear at the wedding is a resolution of its own.
4. **The ox.** The Butchers' Company roasts an ox on the ice, and the fire is melting the ice under it. Brick it, move
   it, or watch it sink.
5. **The Perpetual Frost Company.** A natural philosopher, **Mr. Ambrose Quarle**, is selling shares in a scheme to
   keep the river frozen for ever by his patent freezing salts. Half the Exchange has bought. Invest, short it, run it,
   expose it, or find that the salts work a little (they do: salt and ice make a freezing mixture).
6. **The Ice Chapel.** Parson Doody marries anyone for a shilling. Someone was married last night who should not have
   been. His predecessor's register, from the last Frost Fair, holds a wedding thirty-one years old: Hamnet Gosling and
   Bel Ashby.
7. **The Silver Tongue.** Every night at the Frozen Lamb, the Liars' Bench: tell the biggest lie, and Broom judges. A
   lie is a made thing.
8. **The stairs.** Tredgold's penny a stair; the watermen's channel. Give them work (sledges, chair-sledges for ladies),
   pay them, or let them cut.
9. **The pardoner's weights.** A pardoner sells Saint Evenhand's blessed weights, short by an ounce. Expose him on the
   saint's own scales, or learn his trick.
10. **The Silver Skate.** The race on the ice at dusk on the third day, with Harker's money on the favourite.
11. **Mr. Icicle.** Who writes the *Gazette*? Harker wants to know. Protect Thessaly or sell her.
12. **The custody of Callow.** Shake to be his hand, and keep him till the Landing.
13. **The real shepherd.** Abel-who-cannot-lie: keep him from Harker, make him an accomplice, or let the con fall.
14. **The bondhouse.**
15. **The informer.**

## Endings (first list)

- **The Score**: Harker pays for a flock that does not exist; you and Ursel are over the bridge before the thaw.
- **Ursel's Double-Cross**: she takes the money, or Dickon, and goes.
- **The Unswearing**: the Court rules, on Callow's argument and your evidence, that a hand shaken on a lie does not
  bind. Harker's indentures fall; so does every other bargain of the Fair, yours included.
- **The Long Con**: Harker pays for the flock in indentures, thinking he has the better of it; you tear them up.
- **The Pillory**: caught, and put in the pillory on the ice until the thaw.
- **The Weddings**: Thessaly and Noll, Gosling and Bel, perhaps you and someone.
- **Through the Ice**: Harker on the Black Reach when it goes.

## Who the protagonist is

A liar does well here; so does a thief, a strong back, a priest who can marry people, a scholar who can see what
Quarle's salts are, a woodsman who can read the snow, and someone with no charm at all who can be hired.
