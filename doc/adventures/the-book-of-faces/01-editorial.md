# The Book of Faces: a severe reading, and the design as revised

*Stage 2. The treatment ([00-treatment.md](00-treatment.md)) read as a hostile editor would read it, and the design
the build is held to afterwards. Each finding is numbered and answered. The repertoire survey of 26 October
([repertoire](../repertoire-2026-10-26.md)) is the standard: what the earlier works did seven times, and what Kyrie had
not yet learned to say.*

## Findings

**E1. The protagonist is a witness with a day-pass.** The treatment gives the player a Warrant, a ledger and an
offered face, and nothing they *want*. The survey's complaint (§1.5: "wants nothing, is guilty of nothing, loved by
no one") applies in full. A citizenship offered at the end is a reward, not a want, unless the book keeps asking for it.
*Revised:* the offer of a face is made on the first day, in the Gatehouse, by the sergeant, as the Warrant's fee. It is
asked about again by Mother Tibb (who has taken twelve Bare to the gate and sent eleven out), by Peck (who was given a
face once), by Vail, by Pavane (who will cut one), and at the end. The ending is partly the answer. And the player is
given a way to be at fault: a Bare is not bound by Unfacing and so **may Name**, and Naming is the city's cruellest
instrument and the only one that works on the guilty. A player who Names Lacke has done to him what was done to Peck.
Nothing in the book will say that was wrong; Peck will.

**E2. "Nobody killed her" can be an anticlimax.** A whodunit with no culprit teaches the player that they were
misled. *Revised:* there is a culprit in law, and it is Lacke, who Unfaced a dying woman. The guilt the player
can *establish* is Unfacing, with a real penalty, and the question whether to apply the penalty (Naming) is the climax of
the investigation. The four hands are not equally guilty; each is *met* in a scene that is also the pay-off of a small
adventure (Pell in the Burning, the Serene in the Gull, Pip in the cisterns, Lacke at the Hearing), so the player learns
the truth as the by-product of doing things for people.

**E3. The Lie is exposition at the end.** That the Urn is empty, and that the Registrars have chosen by hand since
the Sluice, is the book's secret and its central satire, and if it arrives as a speech it will land as a twist. *Revised:*
it is **deducible** from five places, each of them a small adventure that does not know it is giving the secret away:
(a) the Rain-Wives' tally of thirty years of draws, in which the Offices go to the city's best bakers, ferrymen and
clerks with a regularity no lot would give (Drip); (b) Pavane's Wall, which shows that the nineteen Offices of last year
are on the Wall's *Mender* shelf (Maskers); (c) Peck's drunken testimony about the Sluice year (Hospice); (d) the Urn
itself, which does not rattle (Chapel); (e) the Rounds, which are a visible selection. A **Drawing Table** (a journal
inference) puts two of the five together; three give the secret. A player who has none of them is told by Marrick's last
letter at the end; a player who has three reaches the finale with it in hand and with a choice to make about it.

**E4. Eight callings is a list unless each one matters.** The treatment gives each calling a submodule and a
finale. The risk is eight appended mini-games that do not touch the city. *Revised:* every calling's arc (a) is
anchored in a place that already has other business (the Stair, the Wall, the Wet Stage, the Confessional, the Heronry,
the Cloister, the Drip's trades, the Collegium), (b) contains at least two decision points which fix a flag the
closing account reads, (c) shares a person or a story with the small adventures (below), and (d) offers an
**Intervention** at the Drawing: an optional act in the finale's Occasion that changes how the night goes. A company
without the calling can still finish; a company with it finishes differently. The Interventions are the *Hands*: the
Stair held, the Likeness cast, the Cast-Room opened, the Draw blessed or refused, the Lampoon played, the heron loosed,
the Door unmoved, the Procession walked.

**E5. The Day/Night inversion is the best idea and must be real.** It is easy to say and easy to forget: if every
conversation is simply available at any hour it is decoration. *Revised:* people have a **public** register (masked,
by day, will not answer the bare; will answer a face) and a **private** register (unmasked, by night, at home, will
answer a knock from the bare and take a masked visitor for a burglar). Topics are gated on that register. The
persons who have both (Pell, Vane, Lacke, Tibb, Pavane, Sister Nobody, Rill, Cosimo, Peck) are the cast: seven or eight
who say different things in each. A day-only and a night-only secret each lie behind four of the principal knots.

**E6. A reputation that attaches to faces is the satire as mechanism.** In a city where nobody is anybody, credit goes
to the mask. *Revised:* every good deed in the Rounds leaves **goodwill on the face worn** (`goodwill(face)`) not on the
player. Do the Gull's errand as the Gull and the ferry-people will help the next Gull; do it bare and they thank *you*
and tell nobody. The face is also what the Watch remembers (**heat** is on the face too). A player who swaps faces
to escape consequences leaves a trail of faces with their own reputations.

**E7. Too many things in the Wet Post.** The Dead Letter Office is carrying the Heron and Lamplight romance, the
Burning of the Nobodies, the Pigeon Strike and the Letter-Writer's Dilemma. *Revised:* the Burning is the set-piece
of the Wet Post and the Book's hiding-place; the romance is moved to the *Drip and the Row* (Rill's ferry carries Cosimo
every morning, which is what makes it comic); the Pigeon Strike is the ranger's quarry and not a separate story; the
Letter-Writer stays, because her dilemma is the Wet Post's own.

**E8. The Hospice Count is a joke without a problem.** *Cut* as a separate story: the Hospice has nine beds, eleven
Strangers and the nine-day rule, and Tibb's way of enforcing it (a sheet, a bell and a list of reasons) is the player's
first lesson in the Concord's manners. It is a place, not a quest. The Warrant's fee and the thirty-one-year history
of Absalom Peck take its slot.

**E9. The four portals are not equally good.** The **Stair** (the Contessa's private way) is a topic and a way, not an
adventure. *Revised:* it stays as a cheap, *political* route: the price is a position, and it is bought at the Dinner
of Spoons. The **Cisterns** have real danger and are closed on the sixth day by the flood. The two principal approaches
are the **Warrant** and the **Face**; each must be playable start to finish by a build that has none of the other's
tools: the Warrant by a company with no stealth and no deception, the Face by a company with no persuasion and no
lore, in at most six days.

**E10. Do people change their minds for reasons other than evidence?** The Dead Letter Office's Pell is the book's
weakest link: he has the Book and will give it to whoever shows him enough. That is the collection's habit. *Revised:*
a lever, other than evidence, for each of the nine who matter:

| Person | Holds | Moved by (not by proof) |
|---|---|---|
| Tomas Pell | The Book | Being forgiven by the child who saw him (Pip), or being asked by a cleric *as a penitent* to put it down |
| Ser Corvin Lacke | The Lock-tax; the Warden's mask | Being laughed at by the right audience (the Lampoon); being offered a graceful way to go Spent |
| Orsolya Vane | The Candid; a Book-reading | A Drip woman who says she would rather keep her mask; being *wanted* as a person by Vail |
| Contessa Tarn | The private stair | Being beaten at spoons; being addressed as a person and not a name |
| Madame Pavane | The Wall | Being *seen*: asked to cut a face with no mask, for its own sake |
| Sister Nobody | The soup | A joke, or a free night in a bed |
| Absalom Peck | The Sluice year | Being named kindly by a stranger |
| Sergeant Vail | The Warrant; the Watch | Trust; her order to keep her hands off what she may not know |
| Hobb, the Serene | The Mask | Being allowed to hold it up and see that nobody is behind it |

**E11. The satire may merely be a premise.** The Concord, said once, is a clever idea; to be satire it must be a *game*
the player loses at. *Revised:* three rules the player is under from the first hour. **Unfacing**: a bare face may Name;
a masked face that names is Named; the Watch counts, and the count is **heat**. **The Fit**: an Office's mask sits
properly only on the face cut for it, and every Fit has a tell; the Fit is the one place the world's law and its magic
meet. **The Standing**: a thing said by day to a masked person without a face of one's own is not heard, but it is
not *unheard*: the speech is charged to the speaker's heat. Each one has a comic edge (Tibb's sheet; the Weights Assessor
who must be addressed as "the Pound"; the Heron's Tuesday) and a cruel one (Peck; the cooper; the child).

**E12. The comedy must be earned.** The comic stories (Umbrella War, Spoons, Sweet's Wager, the Hospice) must be comic by
*procedure*, a rule followed to an absurd and humane end, and not by names. Each has a real problem a player can fail
to solve: the umbrellas, if mishandled, lead to a duel with a death in it. The Spoons has a seating chart that
cannot be solved and can be broken three ways.

**E13. The calendar.** Nine days is long for a book that nobody should rush, and short for a player who wants to do
everything. The city's clock is made to bite in five places: **Day 3** the Rains deepen (Cisterns closing); **Day 5** the
Swap (a night on which every citizen exchanges a mask with a stranger); **Day 6** the Cisterns flood; **Day 8** the Lamp
strike if unsettled; **Day 9** the Burning at noon, the Drawing at dusk. Nothing happens only if the player is present
(the Lamp strike, the Pigeon Strike, the soup running out), and each has a **default outcome** if left.

**E14. Exposition.** The Concord is explained in the first hour by seeing it done to the player. The first speech
the player hears is a Watch sergeant refusing to say their own name. The Hospice gives a pamphlet, the **Almanack of
Usages**, a page long. The fuller history is in the Chapel's inscriptions, optional, and in Peck, who is drunk.

**E15. Too big?** The list is the Grand Tour's size, and the Grand Tour took twelve documents and a second pass.
*Cut:* the Hospice Count; the Letter-Writer's second client; a ninth Round (Gaspar and the heir are one story). *Merged:*
the Wall of Faces and the thief's Cast-Room share a place and differ in who goes in. *Kept:* everything else, because
the brief asked for a region with numerous adventures. Where it will be thin, the build says so (document 10).

## The design as revised

### Structure

- **Nine days.** The company comes to the Gate of Strangers on the first morning (watch 0, 08:00). Day *n* begins at
  watch (n-1)·144. The book closes at the Drawing (the ninth night) or by the Warrant's early end.
- **Two registers**, day and night, and **two states of the face**: Bare, or wearing a Face. Faces are Kinds, rented, bought
  or made: *trade faces* (the Gull, the Cockle, the Owl, the Heron), *Office faces* (borrowed, stolen, Fitted), and
  the player's own *White* (earned).
- **Heat** on a 0–6 scale, owned by the Watch: Naming a masked person aloud, an unmasking, a burglary seen, a face
  cried. At 3 the Warrant is *suspended* (Hearing closed); at 6 the Gate shuts and the Bare are put out.
- **Goodwill** per face, and **Marks**: a Mark for each of Marrick's Rounds done to the end. Marks are the franchise of
  the Qualified Draw.
- **The Drawing Table**: a journal inference over five facts to the secret.
- **The Drawing**: an Occasion on the ninth night. Matters: the Urn, the Book, the Offices, the Crowd, and the
  Interventions each calling has earned.

approaches; a complication; a consequence. **R** is a Round. **L** is local (personal, comic, practical), 11 of 30.

### The ends

As in the treatment, seven; the *Qualified Draw* reads the Marks, the *Honest Urn* the Interventions, the *Year of Nobody*
the Lampoon and the Procession. What happens to Lacke (Named, Spent, forgiven, ruined), Pell, Pip, Rill and Cosimo,
Peck and the Warrant's offered face is a separate closing account (the **Fates**).
