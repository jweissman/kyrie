# The Book of Faces: the story

*Synopsis, acts, people, strategic conflicts and the seven positions. Spoilers throughout.*

## Synopsis

Ombra is a delta city of masks. Between the Third Bell and the Ninth, nobody in it is anybody: its bakers are Cockles, its
ferrywomen Gulls, its nineteen great Offices are masks drawn by lot each year from an Urn, and to say aloud who is under a
mask is the one crime the city punishes without mercy (the offender is *Named*, and every grudge he ever escaped attaches to
the name by dusk). A stranger is a **Bare**: no mask, no standing, nobody to speak to. On the eve of the Novena, nine days of
rain-prayer that end in the Drawing, the Registrar who kept the Book of Faces was found dead in the Chapel in her white mask,
the Book gone from its lectern. Nobody may lawfully say who is dead; nobody may read the Drawing; and if it is not held on
the ninth night, all nineteen Offices dissolve at midnight, being only masks.

The seventy-first article of the Concord says it may hire a stranger to look. Sergeant Vail of the Masked Watch takes the
first Bare off the delta road. In the Rounds, a ledger the dead woman left in the Watch's keeping, are nine errands for nine
nights: a ferrywoman whose boat is sinking and who will take no gift, a clerk who writes love-letters to a Lamp he has never
met, a man named thirty-one years ago and dying of it, a soup-kitchen with four days of bread, a short pound, a Warden of
Locks, a lamp-boy in the cisterns, a heronry about to be drained, an old servant dusting for no one. The player does not need
to do them to win. But they are the city, and every one of them leads toward the same room.

The truth is that nobody killed her. **Ysolde Marrick** was drawn Treasurer at nineteen, honestly, in the Year of the Cullet
Sluice, and was bad at it, and six hundred drowned; the Concord's secrecy saved her and the blame fell on the Serene of that
year, Absalom Peck, who was Named and has lived Bare in the Hospice since. She spent the rest of her life becoming the
Registrar, and for thirty-one years the Urn has been a bronze jar with nothing in it: she chose every Office by hand, by
walking the streets in the white mask by night, and let the city believe in chance because a city that believes in chance
has nobody to feud with. She was seventy-four, and her heart was going, and she could not tell anyone the truth, because
every person in Ombra is bound by the Concord, except a Bare. So she arranged the Warrant and left the Rounds.

Four people did something to her last evening: **Tomas Pell**, the clerk, who put the mask straight and posted the Book to
*Nobody* at the Dead Letter Office (which burns its Nobodies at noon on the ninth day); **Hobb**, a cooper, whom she had made
the Serene for his dullness, who came to beg to be let off the Mask, and ran; **Ser Corvin Lacke**, the Warden of Locks, who
threatened her an hour before she died and said her name aloud, which is Unfacing and the one crime the Concord can
prosecute here; and **Pip Treen**, nine, a lamp-boy under the lamp-table, who saw her bare face and has been hiding in the
cisterns since. The player assembles the four and the Book, and, from whose hands what the city is made of, decides what the
city should do with the secret.

## Acts

**I. The Gate of Strangers (days 1 and 2).** The player is hired, takes (or refuses) the Warrant, learns the city by having
it done to them: Mother Tibb's sheet and the Hospice, Moll's Annex where a face is rented for three coin, the first door
closed on a bare face by day and opened on a knock by night. The Registrar's body (three checks and a vigil), the lamp-room.
The Rounds are read; the first errands are taken or not. The city is shown at both its registers.
**II. The Streets (days 3 to 6).** The small adventures come into their own, each with its own clock: the Deepening (day 3),
the Tally (day 4), **the Swap** (day 5, a night on which every citizen exchanges a mask with the first stranger who will), the
Flood (day 6, the cisterns close). The four hands are found; the Lie begins to show its shape; the Warrant is spent and
Heat is counted; the callings find their arcs; the Hearing may be called from day 6.
**III. The Contessa's Dinner and the Strike (days 7 and 8).** The Old Names come down from the Hill to be seen being
quiet; the Lamp-Trimmers walk out; the Heronry's surveyors come. The Book, if it is not found, begins to look like a fire.
**IV. The Drawing (day 9).** The Burning of the Nobodies at noon; the Hearing in the afternoon; the Urn Hall opens at the
Ninth Bell; the Hands are played; one of seven ways is taken; the Concord's offer of a face is answered; at midnight the
book closes, or, if nothing is done, the Offices dissolve.

## People

**The Bare** (the player): no name the city can say, nine days, a Warrant that is a pair of eyes and not a mouth, an offer of
a face of their own at the end. What they want is a face. What they fear is what it costs.
**Ysolde Marrick, the Registrar** (dead): a lamp-trimmer's daughter, a failed Treasurer, thirty-one years of the quietest
good government in the world. Known by her handwriting, her Rounds, the band under the white mask, and the pot she kept for
Sister Nobody. Never seen alive.
**Sergeant Ottoline Vail**, the Masked Watch: a hound who will not say her name, loyal to a dead woman's instructions, who by
the end may take her mask off in a kitchen. Trust is a measure and is bought with candour.
**Tomas Pell**, Clerk of the Book: a frightened man who loved the Registrar like a father and posted her secret to Nobody. Not
moved by proof; moved by being forgiven by the child who saw him, or heard as a penitent.
**Absalom Peck**, the Named: the Serene of the Sluice year, dying in the Hospice, drunk and kind. Asks one thing: to hear his
name said kindly once, not cried.
**Hobb, the Serene**: a cooper in a mask he cannot take off, who is needed to call the Urn. Moved by seeing someone sit bare
in front of him, and be all right.
**Ser Corvin Lacke**, Warden of Locks: a vain, pious, civil crook who collects clocks. The only culprit in law, for Unfacing a
dying woman. Moved by being laughed at by the right audience, or by being offered dignity.
**Orsolya Hart, the Candid**: who has not worn a mask in eleven years, and wants the Book read aloud. Right about nearly
everything except what a name is for. Moved by being refused, as an equal, by someone who stays.
**The Contessa Ismene Tarn**: eighty-three, the last person in Ombra who enjoys being known. Moved by being argued with as a
person, and by a game.
**Madame Pavane**, the Fitter: keeper of the Wall of Faces, which is the real Book. Moved by being *seen*.
**Pip Treen**, nine, a lamp-boy: the book's heart.
**Rill Ketch and Cosimo Dray**: the Gull and the Heron, who love each other's sentences and dislike each other on the ferry.
**And thirty more** in the districts' small adventures: Mother Tibb, Sister Nobody, Goody Pallet, the Umbrella families, the
Censor of the Wet Stage, Mabel Strake of the Dead Letter Office, Old Garrity and his birds, Brother Amery, Brother Alder,
Gaspar and Lisbet Venn, and the rest.

## Strategic conflicts

| Party | Wants | Does if left alone |
|---|---|---|
| **The Watch** (Vail) | The Drawing held lawfully; no feud | Hires a Bare; waits; on the ninth night stands where it can |
| **The Exchange and Lacke** | The Lock-tax unseen; the Offices as they are | Shuts the Wet Stage's licence on day 4; pressures the letter-writer; keeps the ledger |
| **The Candid** (Hart) | The Book read aloud, a name on every lock | Publishes what she has; if she gets the Book, reads it |
| **The Old Names** (Tarn) | The Fourteen restored, quietly, by stair | Waits for the dissolution and the stair |
| **The Rain-Wives and the Drip** | A fair lot; their lamps lit; their bread weighed | March on day 6 on the tally; the Owls strike on day 8 |
| **The Chapel** (Ansel) | A Registrar, a Hearing's honesty, a bell that rings | Holds the Hearing if three Guilds stand behind a caller |
| **The Wall** (Pavane) | Every face worth having | Casts whoever sits; sells what is asked |
| **The dead woman** | A successor who can be told | Left a ledger, a letter, and a pot |

## The seven positions (the ends)

*The Quiet Hand* (the Lie kept, by a hand that knows); *the Open Book* (every name, read aloud: also *the Consenting Book*,
only those who stand); *the Honest Urn* (forty thousand tesserae, drawn by chance, a farce, a danger and a marvel); *the
Qualified Draw* (the Offices by lot from those the Rounds mended); *the Year of Nobody* (no Offices, a carnival, no
government); *the Old Names Come Down* (the Contessa reads the Offices in her own name); *the Stranger's Face* (the
player in the Serene's mask for a year). And *the Interregnum* if nothing is done, and *Put Out at the Gate* if the Watch's
patience is gone. None is the right answer. Each is a position on what a city owes a stranger, a secret and a name, and each
is paid for by what the company did to get there. After the Drawing the company is offered a face, and answers.
