# Wet Drusilla: the story

*Pass 1: fiction before implementation. Everything here is spoilers.*

## The country

The Ebb is a sea that went out and did not come back. Nobody agrees when: six hundred years, nine hundred, "before the roads".
The harbour at Dryquay still stands, Aenean quays eighty feet over a floor of hard sand, with the iron mooring rings
still in the wall, and a ladder down to the sand where a ship's keel would have been. On the sand sail the sand-ships:
runners, wide canvas, a steady wind called the Steady that blows out across the Ebb by day and home by night.

The dead of Dryquay are carried out to the Silver: the lowest place in the old sea, a salt pan two days out where a skin of
brine lies over the salt, so flat that dawn on it has no horizon. Ashes go to the Silver. It is what is done, in the
hope, which nobody says aloud, that the sea will take notice.

## The dead woman

Drusilla Sarsby, called **Wet Drusilla**, water-smuggler and sand-captain, died in her bed at the Beached Anchor at seventy-one,
annoyed. Her will, read in the taproom to forty people, one of them her daughter:

> Ashes to the Silver, in the *Contrary Mary*, with the song. All nine verses, sung by those that know them. If the sea
> won't come for that, it is no sea of mine.

She was born in a salt-cart three days from a well. The carters poured their last cask over the baby and walked on dry.
She spent her life selling water back to the world, at a fair price and a little over. She never said what she thought
she owed.

She also left a daughter of two on the quay, in a basket, with a jib. The sailmaker Cleat found her. That daughter, Sunniva
Cleat, now thirty-eight, has hired the *Mary* because the will pays, carries the urn herself in a sailcloth bag, and will not
sing.

## The people

**Captain Tamar Pomfret**: one hand short two fingers, voice like a block and tackle. Says she sails for the fee. Wants
to put the *Mary*'s keel in water once before she sells her. Her sister walked into the Singing. Will not say so.
She is two hands short, and so the company is hired.

**Sunniva Cleat**: severe, exact, funny when caught. Says she does not care. Sings verse eight to herself every night
with the door shut. If anyone mentions it she will stop speaking to them.

**Bartle Cresswell**: cook. Eleven years Drusilla's cook, and tone-deaf. Cries into soup and denies it.

**Ravel Balfour**: the pilot. Eighty, lies on the deck with his ear to the planks. Deaf in his left ear, so the
Singing never took him. He and Drusilla had one night, on the Bishop's Stair, and he has the verse for it. He won't sing
it in front of Sunniva.

**Tiggs**: eleven, deck boy, keeper of Mrs Bishop the goat. Knows the verse the way children know it, all wrong.

**Mabin Hawke**: keeps the Beached Anchor, raced sand-yachts, wins every argument by buying the next round.

**Alfie Wenlow**: ninety, on the Anchor's step. Drusilla married him for his well and left him a note. He kept the
bucket. He wants it carried to the Silver.

**Old Cleat**: sailmaker. Raised the child. Hates the dead woman precisely and thoroughly.

**Perpetua Pennyfeather**: lives in the belly of the *Magnanimous*, an iron-ribbed ship on its side in the Shallows.
Sells what the Ebb gives up. Sings against the Singing every night, as she has for twenty years.

**Jessamy Teak**: chief of the sledge-folk, who take water from ships. Once, a long time ago, Drusilla gave her
last water to Jessamy's baby.

## The song

*Haul away, Drusilla, the tide's a long time late.*
*A woman with a sail can wait, and wait, and wait.*

1. *Drusilla Sarsby was born in a cart, / three days from the nearest well; / the carters poured their last cask on her head / and walked on dry, as the tellers tell.* (Tiggs)
2. *She married old Alfie for the sake of his well / and drank it down to the mud; / she left him the bucket and left him the rope / and a note that said: Thanks. Be good.* (Mabin, Alfie)
3. *She towed the *Penny* off Garrow Flat / with eleven goats and a mule; / the goats went first, and the crust held, / and the mule was nobody's fool.* (Bartle: send the goat first)
4. *She rigged the *Mary* in Dryquay's sheets / and the town woke up in the dark, / and she brought them back at the end of the week, / every one, burned by the sun, and stark.* (Cleat) hmm
5. *She gave her last water to a raider's brat / on the Chorus, in the sun; / the raider said, "Why?" and she said, "Why not? / It's only the one."* (Jessamy)
6. *On the Bishop's Stair she lost her breeches / and a man found them there; / he kept them close for fifty years, / and he never said where.* (Ravel)
7. *She outsang the Singing in a dune-bowl night / with a note as low as a well; / the dunes lay down, and the sand lay still, / and she left a cask for the next fool, as well.* (Perpetua)
8. *She left a basket on the quay with a jib / and a name wrapped in the cloth; / the name was a good one, the jib was the best / and she never said she was sorry or wrath.* (Sunniva) — needs work.
9. The company's: three ways to end it.

(The verses are the clues: 3 says send the goat first across the sink-pits; 4 says what to do with the sheets
(a point off the wind with the loft sail); 7 says the low note and that the cask is in the hulk.)

## Scenes worth writing

**The reading of the will**, remembered by Mabin: forty people laughing at the will and Sunniva not.

**The Beached Anchor.** Alfie on the step: "She left me a note. I answered it. Forty-one years ago. Hasn't replied."

**The Mary leaving.** The ladder down the quay. The first time the sand takes the sail. The Steady comes up
like a hand under a blanket.

**The Magnanimous at dusk.** A ship on its side, eighty yards of iron rib, and a woman at a stove on the wall of her
home that used to be a deck. The company goes down the gangway into the hold. Perpetua's rules for guests: nothing she
sells is for sale to someone who hasn't heard her sing.

**The sledges.** Raiders at midday on the Chorus. Three runners low on the sand, and a woman standing in the first with a
gourd on her belt. The company's choice: fight, pay, run, or sing verse five at her. If they sing it she stops her sledge so
hard it throws her. "Where did you learn that? She told me nobody knew it." The sledge-folk join the funeral.

**The Singing night.** At dusk the dunes begin. A low chord from nowhere that fills the ears. Sleepers walk. Sunniva
walks, in her sleep, with the urn. The company finds her at the crest of a dune, the urn open in her hands, singing verse
eight to the dark. The company knows her verse now. They did not ask for it.

**The Silver.** Dawn on the pan, and no horizon. The *Mary* rides on the sky. Under the ship, in the brine, hundreds of
small lights: the lost ashes of the dead of Dryquay, in jars, a field of them, sunk for six hundred years. Everyone
sings. Alfie's bucket is poured. The ninth verse is said. And then, for as long as it takes to sing the chorus
twice, the sea comes.

## Endings

- **The Tide Comes In**: eight verses, the waters given. A swell lifts the *Mary*. She floats. Pomfret takes the helm.
- **A Hand's Depth**: enough: the Silver shivers and rises to the ankle; the urn is washed clean.
- **Ashes on the Salt**: the wind takes them. It is a dignified funeral. The sea does not come.
- **The Urn Goes Home**: Sunniva refuses, and the company lets her. Mercy, not failure.
- **Left on the Quay** and **Marooned on the Magnanimous**: the early endings for a company that missed its ship.

## What the player decides

Whether to learn the song (everyone has a reason not to give theirs). Whether to hear Sunniva's verse she did not give.
Whether to fight, pay or sing to the raiders. Whether to carry the bucket, the gourd, the cask. What the ninth verse says:
that she was loved, that she was funny, or that she was wrong.
