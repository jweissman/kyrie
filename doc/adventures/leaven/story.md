# The Mild Intention: the story

*Pass 1. Fiction before code.*

## The ground

Ullamar was a lake, and was drained by an earthquake in the grandfathers' time. What it left is the Pan: forty miles of white salt crust over brine, flat as a table and bright enough to blind, with a town on each shore. Maing on the west, where the sloops are built. Corsey on the east, where the bread is.

Between them nobody walks. A land-sloop, a wagon with a sail, can cross in a day and a night if the pilot can read the crust. A pilot reads it by ear: the Pan rings under the wheels, and where it rings low and hollow the crust is thin, and under it is a brine-eye a sloop can go into like a spoon into a custard.

## What is being carried

Corsey has been without a Mother for nineteen days. A Mother is the sour starter a city's bread rises from, kept alive by feeding and warmth. Corsey's was two hundred years old and died in a frost the cellars should have been able to stand. Without it the bakers of Corsey are selling flat cakes and the city is sulking. Avert Maugh, Master Baker, came across the Pan alone on a hired sled to beg a cutting from Maing's Mother, which is her sister. He has it now, in a stoneware crock the size of a ham, in a basket in his arms. He has not put it down since the bakehouse.

> "She has not been fed since the second bell," says Avert Maugh. "She is not angry. She is *considering*."

## The pilot

Ysmay Dreel is fifty-eight and the best pan-pilot of her generation, which is a small generation. In the spring a fever took the hearing of her left ear. She has told nobody. She sits at the tiller with her head turned a little, like a woman listening to a neighbour's quarrel, and takes every call from the starboard side.

> "Quiet to port," says Ysmay, which is true, and is the worst news the Pan can give.

Her sloop is the *Mild Intention*, forty feet of tarred ash and sail-cloth. Rabin Fende, seventeen, is her ballast and her whole crew, and wishes to be a pilot more than he wishes to live, which is a thing he is working on. She needs a third hand for the Pan. She needs, though she will not say it, a second ear.

## The reaver

Every pan-sloop is hunted by the Weatherwife, Holin Odiot, who was Ysmay's apprentice for nine years and has the best ears on the Pan. Her sleds come out of the glare at the edge of sight, three or four, low and fast with square sails. The town stories say she takes cargoes, sinks pilots, and sells what she takes to Corsey's black market. The truth is that when the road was built round the north end of the lake, the pan trade died, and the Weatherwife's people have been eating gulls and salt-fish for six years.

She does not want the Mother for ransom. She wants what any hungry town wants. When the player meets her, on the second night, the first thing she says is:

> "Is that bread?"

## The singing

At the dead middle of the Pan, on any night when the wind drops, the crust sings. It is a low note, enormous, a note like a bell the size of a country. The old pilots stop and furl and wait until it passes; they say the lake is remembering that it was water. If the Mother is in the crock she rises to it, all night, pressing at the cloth, and the whole sloop smells of apples and beer.

## What can go right, wrong or strange

- Ysmay can be told she is wrong, and refuse. She can be told gently, and be grateful. She can be told by the Weatherwife, and be unforgivably relieved.
- The Mother can be fed too late, too little, or too well. She can be kept warm at the price of the fuel the sloop needs. She can die.
- The Weatherwife can be fought, outrun into the Eyes, or fed.
- If Avert is persuaded to cut a piece of the Mother for the reavers, Holin's people will have bread for the first time in six years, and Ysmay will have an ear.
- If the Mother does not reach Corsey alive, and a piece of her is in a reaver's sled, then Corsey's bread is made after all, and by thieves.

## What I want the player to feel

The comedy of a grown city's survival resting in a ham-sized crock. The fear of listening to a floor. The surprise that the pirate is a person, her price a thing the player is able to give, and the best ending is the one where nobody wins and everybody eats.
