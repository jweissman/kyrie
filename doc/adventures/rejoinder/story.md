# Rejoinder: the story

*Pass 1: the fiction, before the Kyr. Written first; the module is written to it, and where the module departs, friction.md says so.*

## What the place is

Rejoinder stands on the lip of the Reply, a red limestone box-canyon a mile long. What you shout into the Reply comes
back to you after a year. Not a year to the minute: on Return Night, the night summer turns to autumn, from ten at
night till the small hours, the Reply gives back the whole year's voices in the order they were shouted, each to the
cairn it went out from, like a letter to its address. At dawn the village stands at its cairns and shouts the year
ahead (the Sending), and the Reply keeps it.

The village has built a whole manner of living on that delay.

- **Cairns.** Forty-one of them along the Rim, numbered in whitewash, each held by a household; strangers rent one
  from the Warden. A voice lands at its own cairn, so a person stands at theirs to hear their year.
- **Callers.** A Caller shouts for you, from the cairn of whoever you mean, so that the words arrive at the right
  doorstep in a year. They are paid by the word and trusted like midwives.
- **Forestalling.** To say aloud what you shouted is *forestalling*: robbing the Reply. It is merely bad manners, but
  nobody in Rejoinder is more than a day from a sermon on it.
- **Wool.** A twist of wool hung on a cairn is a refusal. Only the holder hangs it. The voice is spent in the wool.
- **The Year's Wait.** Courtship is conducted by echo. A question is shouted in autumn, heard the autumn after,
  answered at the next Sending, heard the autumn after that. A Rejoinder wedding is two years in the making and nobody
  thinks it long. Nobody under forty has ever said "I love you" to a face; it would be like eating with your hands.

## The night (the company arrives on its morning)

**Ines Larkin**, the Warden of the Rim, is eighty-one and blind and has not missed a Return since before the
bridge. She rents the cairns, keeps the gate, and says: "The Reply does not lie, and it does not hurry, and I would
thank you to do likewise." She hears everything said on the Rim, and has never forestalled anything in her life.

**Orla Pendle** wants wool. A year ago, at the dawn Sending, she and her husband Jory quarrelled at cairn three, over
his selling the lower orchard behind her back, and each shouted what they felt. She shouted *then go, and good
riddance*. Jory died in Sowing, of a cough. Tonight her own voice is coming back to her, and she cannot hang the wool
herself, because the village will see her do it, and because the holder is the only one who can. She asks the
company to be the ones to walk beside her. What she does not know is that she walked away before the Reply took his
answer.

**Tobe Merriweather** is the Caller, and a drunk, and the best voice on the Rim. Last Frost a ploughman, **Dacey
Whitlow**, paid him eleven shillings to shout a proposal of marriage from cairn nine, the Calloways', for
**Sabra Calloway**. Tobe was drunk in the way a man is drunk at eleven in the morning on a day of unusual fog.
Cairn nine and cairn fourteen are four cairns apart; he keeps his clients on a cord of knots; the knot for Dacey was
tied one place too far along.

**Sabra** has waited at cairn nine each night this week with a flask. Dacey told her he would shout. He did not tell
her what, because he is a decent man of the Year's Wait and it is forestalling. She is not sure she will say yes. She
is quite sure that if he is a man who can shout a thing across a canyon he is a man who could have said it in a
kitchen.

**Amos Hallet** holds cairn fourteen. Four years ago his son **Wat** left after a quarrel over the dairy, and Amos
has said, in the village's hearing, that the boy is dead to him. He has also shouted the same thing at cairn
fourteen every Sending: the news of the farm, flatly, as a farmer would write a letter. *The roan has foaled. Your
mother's grave is kept. The east field wants a man.* He has never added the rest. Wat, thin and weathered, has come
home on the eve of the Return, to stand in a crowd and hear whether his father ever called, and has told no one he
is here.

So at ten, cairn fourteen will give back a father's list; and at ten past midnight, loud, in Tobe's best voice, a
young man's proposal of marriage to a woman standing four cairns down, who will hear only the wind.

## The company

Strangers rent cairns, and so have a place to stand. The company has a night and one body. They can wool a cairn, walk
a woman down a rim in the dark, make a drunk say a true thing, carry a shy man to the edge of what he said, or sit on
a bench in the cold and let the village go by. Everything they do is small and nothing is hidden, and nobody in
Rejoinder is permitted to tell anyone anything, so the company's gift is that it may.

It may also forestall, and it may be forgiven. The Warden's opinion of it is its own business.

## The dawn

At first light the Rim shouts the year ahead. The company holds a cairn and so may shout: a promise, a thanks, a warning,
a joke, to arrive a year from now at a place it may not be. And Ines, who will not see another Return, has one word,
and no one to say it for her.

## What matters

- A thing said is a thing sent. The adventure is about what lands where, and on whom.
- Nobody is a villain. Tobe was drunk. Amos is proud. Orla was angry. Sabra is stubborn. Dacey is shy. Wat is afraid.
- Wonder is not a spectacle but an arrival: a mile of voices going by in the dark, each to its door.
