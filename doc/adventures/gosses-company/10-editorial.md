# Gosse's Company — the final editorial revision

*Stage 6: what was changed after the adventure was built, and why. Three sources fed it: the journeys (read as a player
reads them, in [playtest/](playtest/)); the full test suite and the wander test; and an independent editorial read of
every authored line, commissioned for a view not anchored on the author's, which returned thirty-eight findings. Most
were acted on; those that were not are listed at the end, with the reason.*

## Structure and rules

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| Endings need the company to have come to Pentrice before the verdict (`cameForTrial`) | the wander test reached an ending from Wending Cross, and a late arrival had the campaign end at the gate |
| A critic's displeasure is its own measure (`slight`); the vote is regard outweighing slight | an action that lowered regard below nothing was silently never offered (four of them, and the tallyman's stick) |
| The tallyman's laid-down stick spares eight notches one at a time as they are cut | the same cause: fines cannot be taken below nothing by an action |
| The player names the author, to Hepzibah, and may name Callow or Fallow and be told why not | the engine used to announce the answer when two clues were in; the critique's chief complaint about the mystery |
| After the play each critic's face in the lords' room shows their vote; the critics stay in the Wheel until eleven | the verdict was an invisible sum; and the critics had been leaving at ten, before Gosse's play ended |
| Gammage's margin raised from 5 to 7 | she was won by almost any night with the bear in it |
| *The Company Broken* when the house's temper is below 7 (it was 5), matching the thin applause | no journey could reach it; a neglected week now does |
| Pentrice is held only west of a forest country (it was four terrains) | it took the west march from too many generated worlds that tests and saves expect |
| No season is named: the Trial of the Wheel, the Trial peal, "all that season", no Michaelmas, Candlemas, Christmas or spring | a generated world's calendar starts in any month; the first generated journey arrived among spring lambs at "the Midsummer Trial" |
| Fallow and the granny are seen live only on the first day, and told of afterwards; the bailiff's visit is told, not shown, on a later return | they were narrated as happening "now" whenever the company first walked in, at midnight on the fourth day if need be |
| A curtain-raiser that Swale cannot give is dropped, and the play begins | gating the lament on Swale would otherwise have stalled the night |
| Played from memory, the Office does not come for a book that is not there | the scene-eight rules assumed a prompt-book |
| Gosse's greeting knows whether the parcel has been opened | he told a company arriving on the first evening that the play had come |
| Hepzibah's account of the casting is of the casting as it came | asked after the recasting, she described the old cast |
| *Dido* is described in the costume rack only while it is there | found-by-searching text is permanent |
| The fates that put the company at the Wheel all season need the Patent; the bear's playbill fate needs the Patent, with a road variant | they contradicted the Road, the Liberty, the Broken and the Refused |
| The Wolf King (was *The Siege of Galt*); the Duke of Morrow (was of Galt); Tom Fyfe (was Ned) | Galt named a town, a play, a duke, a barn and Swale's home; two Neds |
| `spared`, `told`, `counted` renamed | each meant something else in another adventure |

## Continuity in the prose

- The night: the Ghost "comes up out of" the coffin where the trap works and does not also "climb out"; the lantern's
  beam falls "as the Ghost comes up"; Tam cannot be both Bell and the Tallyman (Pudding takes the false beard); the
  Tallyman is signalled by part, not by name; the house is "every face in the Wheel", not always three thousand; the
  applause has Hepzibah come round from the grey bench when she was taken there, and calls Pudding back, not a bear
  who may be in the bear-garden; a company whose costumes were taken plays in its shirts.
- The endings: no ending names who won the Patent (the Children can); no ending sends the bear west with a company
  whose bear is in the bear-garden; the Broken no longer leaves Gosse "alone" beside a wedding; the Road leaves "before
  the week was out", not the morning after the Clink; the Refused is "the only prize" he ever refused (he had refused
  a pension); the Burned is not reached if *The Wolf King* somehow won.
- The fates: married "a month later", not "on the Saturday" after three Sundays of banns; married "before the season
  is out, in the Wheel's tiring-house", which fits whether the company stays or goes; the fines paid "into Gosse's
  hat", not a bear's bonnet; "before ten", not "before nine", for a rule that fires at nine.
- The week: Kit's voice cracks in rehearsal as Bell or as the Widow, each told as it is; Swale is not named climbing
  out of a hamper in a rehearsal he may have left; Hepzibah, not Dorcas, says "Well" when Kit's voice holds; the
  letter cannot be given to a Swale who has walked out; the gulling is not offered when Dorcas has gone; Gosse's
  "I shall turn to the wing" and Swale's verse no longer place people across a loft they may not be in; Gosse's
  "We play it whole!" is to "the company", and the Arle line to "somebody"; Shad is called down by Kit only when Kit
  is there, and his tablet is wiped by the company, not by a Hepzibah who may be in the taproom; the loft's hampers
  and Hepzibah's trestle are described only while they are there; the yard's "voices declaiming" only while the
  company is upstairs; the bailiff's charm needs the yard and the costumes; the trap's "Alleluia" became a pigeon,
  since Tam might be in the loft; the Fountain is not "dry" in texts that can be read after it runs; the pot-boy
  brings word of the bear only to a company not already in the Gullet.
- The history: Gosse was banned from every stage inside the walls, not from the walls (he lodges at the Moon, and the
  company has played the Wheel in the Soke since); the City, not the forty-year-old Office, has sat on the Trial's
  bench for two hundred years; the Office was founded "within the week" in every telling.
- The herald: the reckoning is eight coin and the hat now holds twelve, so Lem's four and the inn's eight add up;
  "bed and board for seven and a bear"; the bear sleeps "on the ground", the whole of `{green}` hears the hat named,
  and nobody is "stuck on a green" in a town without one; its facts name the town.
- Times: Grimsditch comes "on the second morning", not "tomorrow"; Callow heard of the play "in the Chepe", not
  "yesterday"; Gammage's tryst is "four o'clock", not "tomorrow"; Swale's walk-out is told "within the hour", not "by
  suppertime" at noon; Cundy's master "dines at the Guildhall every day"; the peal begins "at six", not "at dusk".

## Repetition and small slips

- "Forty years" was cut from everything that was not the riot or the ban (the Bunces' father, the sluice's oil, the
  applause, the Tallyman's laugh); "eleven years" from everything that was not the Patent; "the voice that carries to
  the back of a field" from two of four; "does not look up" from four of twelve; Hepzibah's rare spectacles come off
  only for Gosse's hearing; "without his hat", "the most cheerful thing", "goes over like a wave" thinned.
- The nested quotation marks around Swale's "I rise" are double inside single.
- "the Court of Aldermen" (not a second Bench); "a clerk's Old Imbric" (not Latin); "as the first players did" (not
  the Greeks); a primrose in the Master's drawer (the pansy belongs to Swale and Gammage); "A coin a night" for a
  tallyman paid a coin; "the lead still bright" (a pencil is not wet); Merriman "put him in with the goat-herd";
  "a woman may play in it, the Wheel being in the Soke" whoever is left to play.

## Not changed, and why

- **Lear and Hamlet** remain as the names of old plays in Gammage's notices ("the Wooden Lear", "the Man Who Played
  Hamlet as a Bell-Tower"). The adventure's theatre is a pastiche of an English one and its repertory is its
  furniture; the names are jokes a reader recognises. A stricter world bible would rename them.
- **The common and the lime-kiln** outside a generated town are the herald's own places, not the town's, and any town
  can have waste ground at its edge.
- **"At dusk"** in labels (Ned over the wall at seven, the swarm at evening) is left: it reads as a time of day, not a
  season, and the actions are gated on the hour.
- **Gosse on the green and in the loft at once**, for a company that crosses without freeing the players, needs a
  channel from a country back to its herald (see [friction.md](friction.md)).
- **The night's interactivity, the third day's sag, Hepzibah as exposition**: named in [11-critique.md](11-critique.md);
  each is a design change, not an edit.

## After this revision

A second pass, through a harder literary lens, is recorded in [12-second-pass.md](12-second-pass.md). It put *The
Alderman's Ghost* on the stage line by line, added the Bench's supper after Molière's *Critique de l'École des
femmes*, gave the riot a death to answer for (the lamplighter, Tunstall's father), turned the third day into the
week's midpoint, and added two dangers with ways round them. Of the design changes named above, it addressed the
third day's sag and part of Hepzibah's load; the night is fuller but still mostly watched.
