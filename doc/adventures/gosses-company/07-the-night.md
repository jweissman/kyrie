# Gosse's Company — the night, the places, and how it ends

*The performance of* The Alderman's Ghost *scene by scene, the environment it is played in, and the resolutions of
the week. Read from `night.kyr`, `verdict.kyr` and the journeys.*

## The Wheel as a place to act in

The Wheel is a wooden O over the river: a beaten-earth **yard** for the groundlings, a **stage** thrust out on
trestles with two doors and a balcony, a painted roof (the **heavens**) over it, three galleries, and the **lords'
room** over the stage where the Bench sits in a row like a jury. In front of the stage, at the level of the players'
feet, is the **grey bench**, where the Office's tallymen sit in Trial week with hazel sticks and knives. Behind the
stage is the **tiring-house** (hampers, a rack of costumes left by companies long gone, a wall of names with A. GOSSE
cut deep halfway up), and under the stage the **cellarage**, with a counterweighted trap, a lead tank and a pump.

Each part of it is a place to prepare during the week and a post to take on the night:

| Post | Taken by | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| the right-hand wing | "Take your place in the right-hand wing, by the prompt-copy of the scenes" | the line to Gosse from his good side (I.1); a word to Swale in the roar (II.4); the Tallyman turned on the bench (II.5); the prompt-book carried to the bench, or a body between Hepzibah and the Office's men (III.8); the nod to the Master (III.9) |
| under the stage | "Go down under the stage, to the trap, the pump and the lever" | a conjured light up through the boards (I.2, a mage); the pump worked (III.7); the honey through the trap (III.8) |
| the grey bench | a tallyman only | the stick laid down (II.4); answering for the book to Tunstall (III.8) |
| the yard | anyone | roaring Garlick down (I.3); being three thousand people |

What is ready in the place before the half decides what the night can do: the **trap** jammed or freed (brawn, or
mending); the **tank and pump** split or patched (mending); a **dark-lantern and mirror** rigged in the heavens, or not
(climbing); the **costumes** in the tiring-house, or in the bailiff's cart; **Patience** in the tiring-house, fed or
not; the **hampers** carried over before the watermen struck.

How full the yard is: three thousand at the most. The house is counted at the half from the boats (or the weir, if the
watermen struck and someone led the crowd over it, wet to the knee and laughing), the playbills or the quarto, and the
midsummer peal. A thin house starts the night cold.

## The night, scene by scene

Each scene takes two watches. It opens with what the book says happens; at its middle, any live choice the week made
possible is offered at the post it needs; at its end, rules read what the week made and tell what happened, moving the
house's **temper**, each critic's **regard** and **slight**, and, on an unlicensed book, the **fines**.

| Scene | Reads | Live choice | Who it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I.1 The Prologue** (Gosse) | whoever is in the right-hand wing; whether Gosse knows what the riot was to the Ordes | give him his line from his good side, or he dries and apologises to the City in his own words (fined) | Clay likes it held; Gammage likes the dry; the apology moves Mallory and the Master |
| **I.2 The funeral** | Act I rehearsed and Swale and Pudding speaking; Meg among the mourners; the trap; the light | throw a conjured light, or open the rigged lantern | drilled pleases Clay and Mallory; ragged pleases Gammage; Meg doing her father brings the house down |
| **I.3 The Widow receives** | who plays the Widow; Garlick hired by Fallow, bought, or planted; Pudding warned | roar Garlick down from the yard, or hiss Pudding an answer from the wing | Dorcas as the Widow is the scene of the night for Gammage; an unanswered heckle ruins it |
| **II.4 The Council** | the lines cut, or Swale knowing, or innocent; who plays the Ghost; a licence | in the roar, tell Swale they love him, or whisper what they are laughing at; on the bench, lay the stick down | clean pleases Mallory and Clay; whole pleases Gammage and Callow; an innocent Swale told "they love you" is the night's turn |
| **II.5 The lovers** | who plays Bell and Tapwell; Kit's voice; the Tallyman (struck by a licence) | signal Tam to turn the Tallyman round and count the grey bench | Kit's voice held, Ned, or Tam in a frock; the company's own player as Tapwell; the turned Tallyman is Callow's delight |
| **II.6 The Ghost's complaint** | Swale and Pudding; whether Swale was shaken in II.4; the gulling | — | played as tragedy with Lob's hand in his, it makes Clay take off his spectacles; Gammage holding a pansy |
| **III.7 The Fountain** | the pump; the real Fountain of Concord | work the pump under the stage | a jet of water on stage, or Tam's watering-can; if the City walked past the real Fountain running tonight, the Mayor's line about it gets the loudest laugh in forty years |
| **III.8 The bear** | Patience in the tiring-house, fed; the Office's men coming for Hepzibah's prompt-book | carry the book round yourself; stand between her and them; push honey up through the trap; answer to Tunstall as his own tallyman | Patience on stage in a bonnet eating honey is Gammage standing on her chair |
| **III.9 Who walks first; the epilogue** | Act III rehearsed; Gosse meaning to ask Hepzibah; Hepzibah in her wing or on the bench; the Master's trust | nod to the Master on the grey bench | the procession; the proposal heard, or spoken to an empty wing; the Author's Bow, after which the tallymen break their sticks and the fines are nothing |

After the play the fines are read by lantern-light at a crown a notch, to be paid before the Bench sits at ten.

## The verdict

At ten on the fifth morning each critic votes for Gosse's Company if their regard outweighs their slight by enough
(Clay by 4, Gammage by 5, Mallory by 4, Callow by 4). Three votes win the Patent. The crier goes round; the fines are
paid by the company, by Callow's subscription at the Owl, by Fallow (if he owes a siege-tower), or not, in which case
Gosse goes to the Clink of the Soke and holds court through the bars; and the city tells what became of everyone, one
facet each: Gosse and Hepzibah, Swale, Dorcas, Kit, Patience, Ned, Merriman and Sukey, the Bunces, the Fountain, the
Master, and the book.

## The endings

Each is reached only by a company that came to Pentrice while there was still a Trial to come to. A company that
never crossed the march, or came after the verdict, hears it as news and goes on with its own life.

| Ending | When | In play |
|---|---|---|
| **The Pension** | Gosse took Fallow's pension | `pension.json`: told to take it on the first morning, he signs, and the Trial is played by two companies |
| **The Fair Copy Burned** | the book seized and burned, and the old repertory played instead | `burned.json`: Shad left on the beam, the quarto to Fallow, the book seized at three and burned at noon; *The Siege of Galt* to a thin house; the company writes it down from memory in the loft |
| **The Author's Bow** | the Master came up on the stage | `bow.json`: the Book route, the author known, her trust earned, a nod on the night; all four voices |
| **The Patent Refused** | three votes, but Gosse knows who knocked the wedge out | `refused.json`: the Book route with the wedge knocked out instead of driven home |
| **The Patent House** | three votes | `book.json` (a bard, licensed: Clay, Gammage, Mallory) and `liberty.json` (a soldier from Wending Cross, whole: Clay, Gammage, Callow) |
| **The Company Broken** | the Ghost played to a house that did not know what it had seen | `alone.json` and `tallyman.json`: nobody helped the company; Dorcas gone, Swale shaken, a thin house |
| **The Liberty** | played whole, the Patent lost | `famous.json`: the Liberty journey without Clay's *Dido* given back; Gammage and Callow, two voices short |
| **The Road Again** | played licensed, well liked, the Patent lost | `road.json`: licensed and half rehearsed |

## Resolutions, by what they cost

- **Paying** solves the reckoning, Lem, the watermen, the heckler, the bailiff, the bear-pen, the forme, Tunstall. A
  company with forty coin can buy most of the week's trouble and none of its affection.
- **Carrying** solves the bailiff and the seizure (the river is a wall the City's law cannot cross) and, at night,
  the prompt-book.
- **Knowing** solves the licence (the Eighth Ordinance), the ringers (the jackdaws), the gulling (the scrapbook), the
  Fountain (where the water goes), the weir, Callow.
- **Saying** solves most of the company: Dorcas, Swale, Kit, Pudding and Swale, Gosse and Hepzibah, the Master. The
  checks are on persuasion; the alternatives are things found (the notice, the letter, the Canon's trick).
- **Doing nothing** is also a resolution, and the timetable resolves it for you: Dorcas goes, the bear is taken,
  the quarto is printed, the watermen strike, the sulphur is burned. The play still goes on.
