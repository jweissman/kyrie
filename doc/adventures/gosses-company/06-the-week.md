# Gosse's Company — the week

*The dramatic synopsis, the acts, the characters and their strategic conflicts, and the inventory of small adventures
as they were built and played. Where the build departs from [03-revised-design.md](03-revised-design.md) it says so.*

## Synopsis

**Prologue: the green.** In a town on the west road a company of players is stuck: their chain-man Lem has run off
with the hat, the hat had the inn's reckoning in it, and the innkeeper has chained their wagon to a post. Absalom
Gosse, sixty-three, actor-manager, does not ask for money; he mentions that it exists. Free them (pay the reckoning;
get the hat back from Lem, who is not a thief but owed four months' pay; or get them to play in the open and take the
hat round yourself) and Gosse offers service for the summer, a share of the house, and the honour of the profession,
"which is worth precisely nothing and which I would not sell for a crown". They are going west, over the march, to the
Trial of the Wheel at Pentrice. A company that never meets them can still go: every innkeeper is talking about the Trial.

**Act I: the book (the first day).** At the Painted Moon by the Westgate a parcel has been waiting for "the one who
played the Fool at the Wheel the night of the riot", and the ostler has been told that by four men this week, one of
them a goat-herd. In it: *The Alderman's Ghost*, a purse, and PLAY IT AS IT IS WRITTEN. It is the best play Hepzibah
Dimmock has read in thirty-four years, and they have cast it like a pig's breakfast. Corin Fallow, who has won the
Patent for eleven years with *The Siege of Arle*, calls in his beautiful plain coat to offer Gosse a pension and Dorcas
Quaile a place in his wardrobe. The bear gets out.

**Act II: the week (the second and third days).** Everything that could keep the play off the stage turns up on a
timetable: the bailiff for the costumes, the pirate printer's boy on the beam, Dorcas's departure, Swale's walk-out,
the Office's seizure, Fallow's heckler and Fallow's watermen. In between, the city's own stories go on (an ostler who
cannot propose, two ringers who will not ring, bees in a chimney, a fountain that has never run), and the company can
watch the other two companies' nights to learn what the four critics of the Bench like. Underneath all of it, the
mystery: who wrote the play, and why send it to the man who made the Office?

**Act III: the night (the fourth evening).** *The Alderman's Ghost* is played at the Wheel in nine scenes, and every
scene reads what the week made: who plays whom, whether Swale knows what he is saying, whether the trap works, whether
the bear is home, whether there is anyone in the right-hand wing when Gosse dries, whether Hepzibah is in the left-hand
wing when he turns to ask her. In some scenes there is one thing the company can still do. On the grey bench in front
of the stage the Office's tallymen count every word not in the licensed book.

**Epilogue: the verdict (the fifth morning).** The Bench votes; the fines fall due; the crier goes round; and the city
tells what became of everyone.

### The week in five acts

The second pass ([12-second-pass.md](12-second-pass.md)) gave the week a shape: **I. Arrival** (the road and the
parcel), **II. Labours** (the Office, the Bench, the Soke), **III. The third night** (the midpoint: while the
Children of the Close play, Gosse rehearses alone on the bare stage, and whoever follows learns he is afraid; the
minster plate and the lamplighter can be found the same day), **IV. The night**, and **V. The reckoning** (the
supper, the verdict, the fates).

## Strategic conflicts

1. **The Book or the Liberty.** Send the play to the Office and play what it licenses (struck, restored under the
   Eighth Ordinance, bought, or warranted by the Master), or play it whole in the Soke and let the tallymen count. The
   Book costs the play's teeth: the Tallyman is struck entire, the Ghost "stands" instead of "rises", Callow writes CUT
   in capitals. The Liberty costs the company its fines (a crown a notch; 21 in the Liberty journey) and wins the
   yard. Neither is the right answer; each makes a different night and a different coalition.
2. **Three of four.** The four critics cannot all be pleased by the same play. Clay and Mallory like it clean, Gammage
   and Callow like it whole. A Patent needs three. In play: the Book journey won Clay, Gammage and Mallory; the
   Liberty journey won Clay (by giving him back his *Dido*), Gammage and Callow; the Author's Bow won all four. A critic
   is moved by the week as well as the night: Clay by his manuscript, Gammage by the gulling and the bear, Mallory by
   Ned Cherry and the angel let off, Callow by not being exposed.
3. **Fallow's campaign.** Fallow is not a villain; he is a professional. On a timetable he offers the pension and
   the wardrobe, takes a pirate quarto to the Office, hires the Gullet's heckler and pays the watermen to strike. Each
   can be met on its own terms (Dorcas kept; Shad stopped or the book carried into the Soke; Garlick bought, frightened,
   or given his daughter a walk-on; the watermen paid, persuaded, or bypassed over the weir), or all of them at once by
   saving his siege-tower on the second night, after which he owes the company and does neither of the last three.
   Knocking the wedge out instead wins the house and loses Gosse: the Patent Refused.
4. **The company against itself.** Dorcas against the Widow, Swale against the joke, Kit against his voice, Swale and
   Pudding against nine years. None of these is solved by a check; each wants a person found, or a thing said.
5. **The Master against the Office.** Clemency wrote a play her own Ordinances forbid. Told that the company knows,
   she can warrant it, return a seized copy, or, if trust is built (knowing, the riot told twice, Gosse's apology),
   come up on the stage at the end. Threatened, she warrants it, and goes cold for ever.

## The small adventures

Twenty-five, counted as a player would count them. Each says who, where, what is uncertain, the approaches as built,
what complicates it, what resolves it, and what it changes later. **Local** marks the ones that matter only to the
people in them.

### On the road

1. **The Players on the Green** (the herald, in a generated town or Wending Cross). *Gosse, the innkeeper, Lem at the
   lime-kiln, the hat.* Uncertain: whether the players go west at all. Approaches: pay the reckoning (8 coin); go to
   the lime-kiln and pay Lem his four coin, talk him into taking his four and giving back the rest (persuasion), or take
   the hat off him (intimidation); get the players to play in the open that evening and take the hat round yourself
   (persuasion), lead Patience round on her chain (woodcraft), or go on between the scenes (a player's craft).
   Complication: Lem is owed four months' pay. Resolution: the wagon unchained; Gosse offers service. Later: whoever
   signed on is one of the company from their first step into Pentrice; Gosse greets them "as you said on the green";
   Lem, paid, stands in the Wheel's yard in a clean shirt on the night.

### The company (the loft)

2. **The Parcel.** *Dick Merriman, the tack-room.* Approaches: Gosse's old playbill from the property chest (Hepzibah
   gives one of the company the key; anyone else picks the lock); a lie (formidable deception); lifting it while he is
   with the horses (stealth); or waiting for noon, when an old woman who threw the first cabbage recognises Gosse.
   Later: the playbill pressed in the book and the parcel's paper are the first clues.
3. **Who Plays the Widow.** *Dorcas.* Approaches: tell her (hard persuasion); bring her Dame Gammage's old notice of her
   Widow, from the ballad stall; let Gosse talk her round (with a flattering lie about what Fallow wants her for, which puts her down for the Widow but does nothing to keep her from Fallow's door); as one player to another (a
   player's craft). Complication: Fallow's offer; unless kept she leaves at eight on the second morning. Later: the
   Widow scene (I.3), the lovers (II.5), and who plays Bell if she goes (Kit, Gosse in a cap, Tam with his beard off).
4. **The Ghost's Lines.** *Swale; the lines that are also blue.* Approaches: tell him (he demands cuts) and cut them
   with Hepzibah; tell him and persuade him (hard) or, as a player, that the laugh and the tears are the same breath;
   say nothing. Complication: told and unsatisfied, he walks out at noon on the third day, and the Ghost is doubled by
   Gosse or taken by the company's own player. Later: the Council (II.4), where an innocent Swale stops dead in the roar
   and someone in the wing can tell him they love him, or whisper what they are laughing at.
5. **Lob and the Ghost.** *Swale and Pudding, nine years.* Approaches: Mrs Swale's letter, kept in Hepzibah's book;
   lock them in the loft together with the Ghost's scenes (lockwork). Later: the procession (I.2) and the complaint
   (II.6), where Lob takes the Ghost's hand and the Wheel cries.
6. **Kit's Voice.** *Kit, a pot of honey.* Approaches: Ma Tolly's honey and Canon Mallory's trick (sing under the
   voice); or, once it has cracked in rehearsal, give him Tapwell, his first man's part. Later: Bell (II.5); his fate.
7. **The Choirboy** (**local** to the Close). *Ned Cherry.* Approaches: help him over the choir school wall at dusk
   (stealth); ask the Canon to lend him (hard persuasion); as one who keeps the faith (a cleric's or acolyte's craft);
   or tell him to stay an angel. Later: Ned can play Bell; Behold on the third night is sung by him or twice by a
   smaller boy; the Canon's regard.
8. **Patience in the Gullet.** *The bear, Ma Tolly's pots, Simeon Bowle.* At two on the first day she gets out, unless
   fed. Approaches: honey held out, walking backward; a hand on her collar (woodcraft); tell Pudding, who hums her home.
   Complication: at three Bowle takes her as a stray. Then: pick the pen's lock; buy her (12 coin); or, having heard how
   Bowle learned bears, ask him for Maud's sake. Later: Act III.8, which is the moment the playbills promised.
9. **The Wing.** *Gosse's left ear, Hepzibah's book.* Seen first in rehearsal; Hepzibah asks for someone in the
   right-hand wing; Gosse, separately, asks that Hepzibah be in hers at the end. Later: the Prologue dry (I.1) and the
   epilogue (III.9), a proposal heard or spoken to an empty wing.

### The Book and the Office

10. **The Licence.** *Tunstall, the forty-one lines.* Approaches: submit and accept the strikes; search the chained
    Ordinances (lore) for the Eighth, of epitaphs, and argue the Ghost's nineteen lines back; bribe him (ten coin and a
    deception); have the Master warrant it; or take it whole to the Soke. Later: what is played, and the fines.
11. **The Pirate.** *Inchbald, Shad on the beam.* Shad climbs to the loft's rafters after the first rehearsal.
    Approaches: call him down and wipe his tablet; go up after him (climbing); buy the forme (6 coin); give Inchbald the
    company's playbills to print instead (persuasion). Complication: a quarto in the Chepe goes to Fallow, and Fallow to
    the Office, which seizes an unlicensed book found within the walls at three on the third day and burns it at noon on
    the fourth. Approaches to the seizure: carry the book over the river first; ask the Master for it back; take it out
    of her desk (hard stealth). Later: the playbills or the quarto fill the yard; a burned book means Galt, unless every
    act was rehearsed and it is played from memory.

### The Liberty and the City

12. **The Watermen.** *Joss Ferriby.* Fallow pays them to strike from four on the last day. Approaches: pay the second
    penny yourself (8 coin); persuade him (hard); be of the water yourself (a boatman's craft); or let them strike and
    lead the crowd over the weir from the bollard. Later: how full the yard is.
13. **The Heckler.** *Oswin Garlick, his daughter Meg.* Fallow hires him at eleven on the third day. Approaches: pay
    him (4 coin); frighten him (intimidation); give Meg a walk-on among the mourners, after which he offers to heckle on
    cue; tell Pudding, who writes him a line. Later: I.3 is a disaster or the biggest laugh of the night.
14. **The Bailiff.** *Grimsditch, the costumes.* He comes at nine on the second morning. Approaches: carry the hampers
    over the river (no City bailiff may distrain in the Soke); pay the tailor (30 coin); hold the loft door (brawn).
    Later: costumes, or the play in its shirts.
15. **The Fountain of Concord** (**local**, then public). *Wat Cundy, the conduit house.* The water goes to the Lord
    Mayor's garden. Approaches: Cundy's master's key (persuasion), or the lock (lockwork); then the wheels (brawn) or the
    screw (mending). Later: the Chepe dances; the Fountain scene (III.7) gets the night's biggest topical laugh; the
    Lord Mayor loses his carp and his action.

### The mystery and the romances

16. **The Hand in the Margin.** Five clues, each a pair: the parcel's watermark and the Office's paper; the margins'
    hand and the hand on a licence; the child's crayon drawing on the riot playbill and its twin over the Orde mantel (or
    the riot story); Sukey Rowe's borrowed hood; the lady with ink on her finger and the Master's. With any two the company knows enough to say it,
    and says it to Hepzibah, who knows the book as well as its author does: name the Master and she takes her spectacles
    off; name Callow ("whoever wrote this can spell") or Fallow ("he would have cut the bear") and she says why not;
    name the Master on one clue and she wants something she can put her finger on. Later: the Master can be told; the
    Bow becomes possible.
17. **The Riot Told Twice.** Gosse tells it as a triumph ("I made an Office"); Clemency, told that the company knows,
    tells it as the night her father was laughed at with her on his knee. Carried back to Gosse, it becomes the apology
    at the end of the Prologue. Carried back to her, it is trust.
18. **The Gulling.** *Pudding's two notes.* Pudding and Dorcas mean Swale and Gammage each to believe the other loves
    them. Carry the notes; copy Gammage's out in a hand like Swale's (deception) or mend its spelling (lore); mix them up;
    find Gammage's scrapbook of her notices of Swale, a pressed flower against each; tell Swale the truth. Outcomes: an
    understanding at four at the Owl or her stall, a pansy taken; a public humiliation; nothing. Later: Gammage holding a
    pansy in the lords' room; her notice; Swale's season of comedies.
19. **Callow's Claim** (**local** to the Owl). Nym Callow says he wrote the play. Ask him, as its author, what the bear
    does in the third act (insight). Expose him before the Owl, or tell him quietly that you know and will not tell.
    Later: his regard; whether he raises the fines by subscription.
20. **Clay's Dido** (**local**, then the Bench). *A manuscript in the tiring-house's costume rack, with Gosse's notes in
    the margin: Beautiful. Too long. Cut nothing.* Give it back to Clay; ask Gosse to play its lament as a curtain-raiser.

### Kept small (local)

21. **The Ostler's Banns.** Merriman cannot get his words in order. Write him a speech (lore), coach him (persuasion),
    or lend him one of Gosse's (it is a villain's). Sukey answers the next evening. Later: their banns.
22. **The Ringers.** Hezekiah and Walter Budge each say the other cut the tenor rope. Search the bell-frame: jackdaws.
    Or splice the rope (mending). Later: the Trial peal on the last evening, and the house it calls.
23. **Ma Tolly's Bees.** Her swarm is in the fishmongers' chimney, and the fishwives mean to burn sulphur. Climb with a
    skep, smoke it down with damp straw (woodcraft), or talk them into waiting for dusk. Later: honey for Kit and the
    bear; Ma Tolly tells of the weir.

### The other nights

24. **The Siege of Arle.** The Servants' siege-tower has a cracked wheel held by a loose wedge. Drive it home (mending):
    Fallow is in the company's debt, "which I dislike very much", and hires no heckler and informs on no book. Knock it
    out: the tower falls in front of the Bench, the yard is in hysterics, and Pudding sees who did it.
25. **The Patient Daughter.** The critics show their tastes again. A tallyman on the bench sees the Fifth Angel, aged
    nine, catch his wing and say "Bum", and decides whether to cut a notch.

### The stranger's way in

A company that never met the players can sign on with Tunstall as a tallyman at the Westgate in the morning, sit on
the grey bench on the Trial nights with a hazel stick and a knife, let the angel off, and, on Gosse's night, lay the
stick down during the Council and refuse to count (eight notches the Office does not have), or tell Tunstall in the
eighth scene that they will answer for the book themselves. A tallyman is paid a coin a night. The stranger can also go
to the Moon and be hired by Gosse, and be both.

## How they bear on each other

- **Honey** is the city's currency of kindness: Ma Tolly's bees (saved or not) supply the pots that keep Kit's voice,
  feed Patience so she does not get out, bring her home if she does, and carry her through the trap in Act III.
- **The river** connects four stories: the bailiff cannot follow the costumes over it; the book is safe from the
  Office over it; the watermen's strike closes it; the weir that Garlick (the heckler) and Ma Tolly (the bees) know of
  opens it again.
- **Fallow's debt** (the wedge) closes two of his own moves (the heckler, the informing) and pays the fines in the
  Book's epilogue; his sabotage (the wedge knocked out) turns the whole week into the Patent Refused.
- **Clues are kindnesses.** The watermark is found by holding the parcel's paper to the window; the drawing by asking
  Gosse about the playbill; the hood by helping Merriman to his answer; Clemency's trust by carrying the riot story from
  one of them to the other.
- **Every critic is a story.** Clay's grudge is Gosse's laugh twenty years ago; Gammage's scrapbook is the gulling;
  Mallory's choirboy is Ned; Callow's claim is his debt and his vanity.
- **Casting is a chain.** Who plays the Widow decides who plays Bell; Kit's voice decides whether Kit can be Bell or
  must be Tapwell; Ned is a Bell if Dorcas and Kit are not; the company itself can take Tapwell or the Ghost.
