## The kernel, revised

Forty years ago, at the Wheel in the Soke, a young player called Absalom Gosse, playing a fool, said something about
Lord Mayor Ezra Orde that the pit took up and carried over the river and through the City for two nights. The Mayor,
who had been in the house with his four-year-old daughter on his knee, founded the **Office of Plain Speech** within
the month. Nothing has been said on a stage in Pentrice since without its licence, and Gosse has never played inside
the walls again.

This summer, the last he can walk, Gosse brings his company back for the **Midsummer Trial**: three companies, three
nights at the Wheel, a bench of four critics, and the **Patent** for the one they choose. On the night they arrive a
play is waiting for them at the Painted Moon, unsigned, with twenty crowns: *The Alderman's Ghost*, a comedy about a
pompous, loving, absurd old alderman who will not stay dead. It is the best thing any of them has read. It is very
obviously about Ezra Orde. It was written by his daughter, who keeps his Office.

She sent it to the one man who would play it as written, because nobody else would dare and because nothing can be
done to him that the road has not done. She did not foresee that her own clerks would strike forty-one lines from it
by the ordinances she drafted, or that she would have to decide, in writing, whether the Master of Plain Speech may
overrule the Office of Plain Speech.

## What the player does

The player spends five days in Pentrice getting a play onto a stage, and one night watching what they made happen.
They cast it, mend its quarrels, fight its law or evade it, find out who wrote it, keep the bear, keep the creditors
off, and get an audience across a river. Then the play is performed, scene by scene, and what they did all week
decides how each scene goes, with a choice or two left to make in the wings.

## The two entries

1. **With the company** (by a generated town). In a town of the first country the company plays on the green on its
   way west. The bear's chain-man has run off with the hat. The player can catch him on the road, take the hat round
   themselves, or lead the bear. Gosse hires them. They come to Pentrice as one of the company: lodged in the Painted
   Moon's loft, cast in the play, on stage or in the wings on the night.
2. **As a stranger** (by the road). A player who never met the company hears in a taproom that Pentrice wants
   strangers for the Trial. At the Westgate the Office's chief clerk is hiring **tallymen** for Trial week ("sixpence
   a night, and you must be able to count"). A stranger can sign on with the Office, go to the Painted Moon and be
   hired by Gosse, or both. A tallyman sees the Office from inside and sits in the Wheel's front row on the night with
   a notched stick; their climactic choice is whether to count.

## Pentrice: districts and sites

Pentrice stands where the Ise falls into the Arle. Seven districts, about thirty-two places.

weir path, which few know.

## The Bench

Four critics, each named by a different authority. Three votes win the Patent. **Their tastes are learned by watching
them**, at the other companies' nights and at rehearsal, as well as by asking.

| Critic | Wants | Is moved by | Is lost by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ormsby Clay** (the Guildhall) | order: verse, decorum, every person's proper end | Swale's complaint played as tragedy; a clean book; the procession | the bear; ad-libs; Gosse in a dress |
| **Dame Philippa Gammage** (the Gullet) | joy | the bear; Pudding; the Widow played for laughs; the house roaring | long speeches; pomp; being made a fool of in the gulling |
| **Canon Mallory** (the Close) | improvement | a moral (Who walks first); the choirboy treated well; nothing indecent | blue lines heard as blue; satire of the dead |
| **Nym Callow** (the Owl) | truth | the play whole; the tallymen made the joke; the Fountain run | cuts; the licence; anything the Office would pass |

## The small adventures (revised inventory)

Each is listed with the person, the place or thing, the uncertainty, the approaches, the complication, the resolution
and what it changes later.

### The company

1. **The Players on the Green** (a generated town). *Gosse; the hat; the bear.* The chain-man, Lem, has run with the
   hat. *Approaches:* go after him on the road (catch, or let him go with half); take the hat round again (a speech, a
   song, a trick); lead Patience round the green so the crowd pays twice. *Complication:* Lem is not a thief, he is
   owed four months' pay. *Resolution:* Gosse hires the player. *Later:* Lem, paid or forgiven, is in the Wheel's yard
   on the night.
2. **The Parcel.** *Dick Merriman; the parcel.* Merriman was told to give it only to "the one who played the Fool at the
   Wheel the night of the riot". Gosse says that was him; Merriman has been told that by four men already this week.
   *Approaches:* Gosse's old playbill from the property chest; Merriman's grandmother, who was there; tell Merriman a
   lie he will believe; take it from the tack-room. *Later:* the playbill is the first clue; the parcel's wrapping is
   the second.
3. **Who Plays the Widow.** *Dorcas; the part.* *Approaches:* persuade her; bring her Gammage's old notice of her
   Widow in *The Miller's Wife* (from Gammage's scrapbook); let Gosse lie to her; give her Bell. *Complication:*
   Fallow's offer on the second day. *Later:* who plays the Widow and Bell shapes Acts I and II; Dorcas may leave.
4. **The Ghost's Lines.** *Swale; the struck lines, which are also the blue ones.* *Approaches:* tell him (he demands
   cuts: make them with Hepzibah, or talk him out of them, or he walks and someone else plays the Ghost); do not tell
   him (he plays them in innocence, and the night decides). *Later:* Act II's centre.
5. **Lob and the Ghost.** *Pudding and Swale; nine years' silence.* The reason is Pudding's joke at the funeral of
   Swale's mother. *Approaches:* lock them in the loft together; find the mother's last letter (in Hepzibah's book of
   cues) asking Pudding to make them laugh at her burying; schedule them by Hepzibah. *Later:* Act I.2 and Act II.6.
6. **Kit's Voice.** *Kit; his voice.* *Approaches:* honey and horehound (Ma Tolly's honey; the herb in the minster
   garden); Mallory's choir-trick; recast him as Tapwell, his first man's part, and find another Bell (Dorcas, or Ned).
   *Later:* Act II.5.
7. **The Choirboy.** *Ned Cherry; the choir school.* *Approaches:* help him run (stealth over the school wall);
   take him back; ask Mallory to lend him (a cleric is heard). *Later:* a Bell; Mallory's regard.
8. **Patience in the Gullet.** *The bear; the honey stall; Simeon Bowle.* She gets loose on the second day.
   *Approaches:* honey; Pudding; a ranger's way with beasts; Bowle's men, if they have her, fought or bought or
   out-sneaked at the bear-garden pen. *Later:* Act III.8; Gammage.

### The Book and the Office

9. **The Licence.** *Tunstall; the book; the forty-one lines.* *Approaches:* argue the lines from the Office's own
   ordinances (one exempts "the words of the dead, spoken as such"); bribe Tunstall; carry the book to the Master;
   forge the seal (found out on the night). *Complication:* the Master reads her own play struck by her own clerk.
   *Resolution:* the licensed book, the Master's warrant, or none. *Later:* the night's fines.
10. **The Pirate.** *Inchbald; Shad's shorthand; the forme.* Shad is taking the play down at rehearsal. *Approaches:*
    catch Shad; buy the forme; pie the type; give Inchbald the playbills to print instead. *Complication:* the quarto
    in the Chepe goes to Fallow, and Fallow to the Office. *Later:* Tunstall comes to seize the book.

### The Liberty

11. **The Watermen.** *Joss Ferriby; the stairs.* Fallow has paid them to strike on the fifth night. Their grievance
    (the fare to the Soke, fixed by the Guildhall at a penny since before the Office) is real. *Approaches:* pay more;
    bring the fare-roll from the Guildhall; the weir path at low water (known to Garlick); row the crowd yourself.
    *Later:* how many are in the yard.
12. **The Heckler.** *Oswin Garlick; his daughter Meg.* Fallow pays him on the fourth day. *Approaches:* pay more;
    give Meg a walk-on; frighten him; prepare Pudding an answer. *Later:* the heckle on the night is a disaster or a
    laugh that was planted.
13. **The Bailiff.** *Grimsditch; the costumes.* On the third day, for Gosse's debt to a tailor. *Approaches:* pay;
    carry the costumes over the river (no bailiff may distrain in the Soke); play a scene for Grimsditch, who is
    stage-struck; fight. *Later:* costumes or motley.

### The mystery

14. **The Hand in the Margin.** Clues found, not told: the Office watermark in the parcel's paper; the margin's hand,
    the hand that signs the licence on the Moon's taproom wall; the playbill of the riot night pressed in the parcel;
    Sukey Rowe, who carried it; Callow, who says he wrote it and cannot quote its third act. *Resolution:* the player
    knows; tells Clemency they know; she denies it, or does not.
15. **The Riot Told Twice.** Gosse tells it in the Moon's taproom as a triumph. Clemency tells it, if she trusts the
    player, as the night her father was laughed at with her on his knee. *Later:* Gosse's epilogue can say sorry.

### The romances

16. **The Gulling.** Pudding and Dorcas mean to make Swale and Gammage believe each loves the other. The player
    carries the notes, arranges the overhearing at the Owl and in the Moon yard. Gammage's scrapbook of her own bad
    notices of Swale, each with a pressed flower, shows the joke is true. *Outcomes:* a real understanding; a public
    humiliation and a lost vote; nothing.
17. **The Letters in the Chest.** Gosse is going deaf (seen first, in rehearsal). Hepzibah asks for someone in the
    opposite wing on the night. Gosse asks the player to see that she is in the wings for the epilogue. Under the
    paste crowns, his letters to her, never sent. *Outcomes:* a proposal heard, or spoken to an empty wing.

### Kept small

18. **The Ostler's Banns.** Dick Merriman cannot ask Sukey Rowe; he has the words and not the order of them. Write him
    a speech, coach him, or lend him one of Gosse's (it is a villain's). She says yes, or laughs, or both.
19. **The Ringers.** Hezekiah and Walter Budge will not ring the midsummer peal together; the tenor's rope is frayed
    and each says the other did it. Mend the rope, climb to the frame, or find who did (a jackdaw's nest). The bells
    ring, or not.
20. **Ma Tolly's Bees.** Her bees have swarmed into the fishmongers' guild chimney and the fishwives mean to smoke
    them. Climb for the swarm, smoke it out kindly, or talk the fishwives round. Honey for Kit and for Patience is
    the natural consequence, not the point.
21. **The Fountain of Concord.** Wat Cundy knows the water runs to the Lord Mayor's garden. Follow the conduit to the
    conduit house on the Hill; open it (lockwork, or Cundy's master's key); turn the valve back (brawn, mending). The
    Fountain runs, the Chepe dances, the Lord Mayor's garden goes dry. It is done for the Chepe; if it coincides with
    Act III, so much the better.

### The other nights

22. **The Siege of Arle** (Fallow's company, the third night of the Trial) and **The Patient Daughter** (the Children,
    the fourth). Watching them shows each critic's taste. Fallow's siege-tower is rickety; the player can let it fall,
    or help.

## The night: *The Alderman's Ghost*

Nine scenes. **Each scene reads what the week made**, and offers one or two live choices that preparation made
possible. Throughout, the house's **temper** and each critic's **regard** move; on an unlicensed book, the tallymen
count, and the **fines** grow.

| Scene | Reads | Live choice |
|---|---|---|
| I.1 Prologue (Gosse) | Gosse's deafness; who is in the opposite wing | give him the line from his good side |
| I.2 The procession | the size of the cast (Meg, Ned, the player); Pudding and Swale reconciled | — |
| I.3 The Widow receives | who plays the Widow; Garlick paid by Fallow, or a plant | answer the heckle |
| II.4 The Council sits | Swale told or not; cuts; licence | in the wings, what to say to Swale when the house roars |
| II.5 The lovers | who plays Bell and Tapwell; Kit's voice | turn the Tallyman on the tallymen |
| II.6 The Ghost's complaint | Swale and Pudding; Swale's state after II.4 | — |
| III.7 The Fountain | the fountain-prop (Tam, the trap); the real Fountain run | work the pump under the stage |
| III.8 The Bear | Patience home and fed | the honey through the trap |
| III.9 Who walks first; the epilogue | Hepzibah in the wings; the riot told twice; Clemency's state | Clemency stands, or does not |

## The powers

- **Corin Fallow**: day 2, offers Dorcas a place and Gosse a pension; day 3, the quarto to the Office (if there is one);
  day 4, pays Garlick; day 5, pays the watermen.
- **The Office**: Tunstall strikes, seizes and counts by the ordinances; the Master may countermand.
- **The creditors**: Grimsditch on day 3.
- **The Trial**: day 3 the Siege; day 4 the Children; day 5 Gosse; the verdict the morning of day 6.

## How it ends

Named endings, each stitched with the outcomes of the week:

| Ending | When |
|---|---|
| **The Patent House** | Gosse's company wins three of the Bench |
| **The Liberty** | the play played whole, the Patent lost: famous, fined, banished from the City |
| **The Author's Bow** | Clemency claims her play from the front row |
| **The Pension** | Gosse takes Fallow's pension before the night; the company goes on under another master, or not |
| **The Fair Copy Burned** | the book seized and burned; the old repertoire played to a thin house |
| **The Company Broken** | the night falls apart |

Facets told in the epilogue: Dorcas, Swale and Gammage, Gosse and Hepzibah, Kit, Patience, the fines, the Fountain,
the Budges, Merriman and Sukey.

## Rewards

- Experience by milestone (cast, mended, licensed, found out, performed).
- **A share in the company**: the Painted Moon's loft (or the Wheel, if the Patent is won) as lodging for life.
- **Gosse's paste crown**, to whoever played the Ghost if it was not Swale.
- **The Master's licence**, if Clemency gives one, for one play of the player's own.
- A share of the house in coin.
