# The Lull: the second pass, and what I am not satisfied with

*Stage 6. Written after the module played end to end, reading the transcripts as a reader, not as its author; then
revised; then read again. What changed is recorded first, then what is still wrong.*

## Read as a finished work: the charges

**1. The Working Day was watched, not lived.** The climax of the story was a series of waits on a bank. The company
could work (one action) and deal with the wardens, and otherwise the hours went by and the page reported them.
*Revised*: on the bridge there are now things to do and people to talk to while the day goes: Corin, filthy and
delighted ("I've never been tired before. Tired from something."), Tobias with his hands remembering what he did for a
shilling ("That's the worst of it. And the best."), Davy asking whether this is a war, Hester in her quilt telling them
they are doing it wrong, and Linnet putting down a stone to play a working tune the Lull has never heard, which is the
tune with a next bit she has wanted all along. At dusk, if Hester is on the bank, she has one hand out of the quilt,
open. It is still too much a matter of waiting for noon and dusk.

**2. The Watching was the best idea and the worst-told scene.** It arrived as a wall: every making that ravelled or
held, each beginning "At sunset", told after "Sit down at the Long Table and eat". *Revised*: the Watching opens with
the light going up the Tump and the town looking at the row of makings; then each making in turn with its own first
words; the company can choose to sit and watch until the sun goes, so the moment is chosen rather than tripped over;
the clock that holds no longer re-describes the sunset.

**3. Tobias confessed too easily.** He told a stranger which side of the window he was on in the first hour. *Revised*:
not on the first Lullday, and only after he has talked with the company twice (the strike; Hester's request), or after
the company has done something for his house (rites, poppy). A watchful priest can see it in his hands without
Hester's hint.

**4. The Sixnight explained too much in its first draft and too little in its doors.** I had written the doors of the
Mill as pushed *from the yard*, as if something outside wanted in, which made no sense of the *Monday* ending, where
the doors open outward and the Hands walk out into the world. *Revised*: the doors lean outward against the beam from
inside, a finger's breadth, breathing. Nothing else about the Hands is explained.

**5. The company had no way to learn anything but by being told.** *Revised*: the Old Shift will teach the strikers'
hymn to anyone who has heard them argue about what was sung, an hour of singing with eleven old people, after which the
company has a performance it did not have, and the Overseer flinches from it. It is the module's training, and it is
the kind of training the Lull would give.

**6. The rewards were coin and experience.** *Revised*: Mabyn gives her mother's Lull-cloth to whoever made Lettice
give a thing she meant; Haskett buys the Long Shift's grey cloth (and does not want to look at it) and the Wroughts'
shillings; the notched shilling, given back to Tobias, forgives him and reopens the love path after Hester's death by
his hand. Things now carry their stories out of the valley.

**7. The valley stopped at the ending.** *Revised* (`after.kyr`): come back after the Working Day and the bridge stands;
the stone has AND THEN MONDAY cut under the rows of seven; the board by the pump says LULL EVERY DAY or CARTING WANTED,
APPLY HASKETT; Corin has a workshop at the bridge and makes a clock that keeps both times; Tobias tells the strike from
his chair on the bridge, from the inside of the window; in *Monday* Haskett's men restring the Mill and the Lull-born
watch them "the way you help with something you might be blamed for". Tested in a world.

**8. The young were a plural noun.** *Revised*: the Club speaks: a boy who wants to be bored, properly, for a whole
day; a girl who wants to start something and not finish it; Tam Hollis who wants burnt hands from a smith's; a girl with
a squint who wants a birthday on a different day from everybody else's; and, asked whether they mean to end it, "We
don't mean to end it. We mean to start something." "Same thing." "It isn't, though."

**9. Smaller corrections that mattered more than their size.** The glance in the browser showed the first sentence of
Linger green, which was about houses; it is now about the Long Table. The sleep's result is in the past tense, so that
the next day's chapter heading reads first and the night after it. A betrayed girl no longer "walks off" and then is
found on the same pier. Fates were written for every ending (Dorcas and Piran when the week comes back; Nan, who
misses the race against the sunset; Linnet's tunes remembered because there is a tomorrow to remember them in; Lettice
widowed). Simeon, who sat with Hester at the end, stays in her room and has his own afterward.

## Tenfold, if I had it

Not ten times the rooms. The things I would do with the ambition:

- **Linnet's tune as the work's spine.** Four notes for the company on the first day, four that answer them on the
  second, a working tune on the Working Day, and what the town sings at the Watching afterwards; each variation told,
  so the romance would be heard growing rather than declared.
- **The Working Day by the hour,** each Hand's coming a scene in its own right (Wenna in a borrowed coat; Piran
  setting a stone and looking up the Tump road for Dorcas; Aldith in her apron reciting Gloss 2,214 at Hobday), and
  each Hand's work standing or falling separately at the Watching, by name.
- **Weeks that accumulate.** Linnet counting the company's weeks in charcoal on the window frame, a mark for each; the
  Lull-born noticing a scar, a new coat, a companion gone; Dorcas's heads continuing on Piran's shelf whether or not
  the company comes back.
- **A Sixnight that lasts.** Six days awake in a sleeping valley, with nobody to talk to and the Mill going: the fear
  is in the length, and the length is barely played (it is one loom action, or one crawl).
- **The law against the company's own violence** (F1): a hired guard whose blows do not hold would be the funniest and
  most disturbing fight in Kyrie, and the engine does not let a module say it.

## What is still weak

- **Minds still change by being told.** Abel, Wenna, Jenet, Piran, Dorcas, Lettice and Corin each change when the
  company tells them a true thing. The things told are about people (what someone loves, that someone is ill, that a
  ribbon was sold) rather than exhibits, and the strongest changes are made by gifts (a clock, a receipt, a shilling,
  a carving), but the grammar is the collection's: knowledge, then a topic.
- **Day two is thinner than day one.** Most of the people and most of the discoveries are on the first Lullday; the
  second is the day of consequences, but a player who sleeps through it loses little but Hester's Watching.
- **The comedy of the Open Houses can resolve too early.** A player who knows the law in any form can warn Lettice on
  the first morning, and the escalation (the house given in spite, the Warden under his enemy's roof) never happens.
  It is a good outcome and a less good story.
- **The Overseer and the Hands are thin as encounters.** One warning, one seizure, one fight; the Mill has five rooms.
  It is frightening on the page and simple to play.
- **The paid-hands mechanism reads as arithmetic once seen.** Five who mean it and one who asked for it is a quorum,
  however well disguised by people. I said in the first critique it must not be a vote; it is less of one than it was.
- **Random play ends in the darkest ordinary ending.** Five of six random walks end with Corin's hand broken. That is
  right for a company that does nothing, but it means the ending a disengaged player sees is the bleakest, and it
  may read as punishment rather than consequence.
- **Hired harm, the law the setting is most proud of, is enforced once** (the Brock) and otherwise told (F1).
- **Untested by dice.** Shaming or threatening Nan, delaying her past four, talking Hobday down: they work when they
  succeed, and I have read their failures, but no test drives them, because their checks are dice.
- **I have not watched anyone else play it.** Everything I say about whether it is enjoyable is a reader's opinion of
  his own book.

## What I think is good

The valley's law is a moral question the player can answer with their hands, and the Watching makes the answer public
every evening. Hester's request is the most serious thing in the module and it is not an errand: it has a clock nobody
states, a man who will do it himself, and four different answers, none right. The shelf of two hundred heads. The
cairn that falls because each stone remembered where in the river it was lying that morning. Tobias at the window,
which a player can discover from his wife, from his hands, or from a beam in the dark, and which he tells himself, and
which a granddaughter can hear from him or from you. A night inside that costs a week of the world, which no other
place in Kyrie can say, and which the engine said in one line.
