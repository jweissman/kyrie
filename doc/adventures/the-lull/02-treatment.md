# The Lull: the treatment, as first conceived

*Stage 1, written ambitiously and before accommodating the engine. Stage 2 ([03-critique.md](03-critique.md)) cuts
and bends it.*

## In a paragraph

Over the march a road climbs to a standing stone and divides. One way goes round a bare hill and comes back to the
stone. One day in seven the other way is there, and goes down into the Lull: a bowl of beech hangers round a tarn and a
river called the Idle, with a weaving town, Linger, at its heart and a dead seven-storey mill above the town. For
fifty-seven of its own years, four hundred of the world's, the Lull has kept only its holy day. Whatever is done there
without meaning it comes undone at sunset. Its people have built a whole civilisation out of meaning it: gift and play
and casuistry and a strenuous, policed love of their crafts. Now the young, who never chose rest, mean to end it. They
will work for wages, in public, from dawn to dusk on the third Lullday from now, and see whether the valley holds.

## The kind of thing it is

A **civic comedy with a tragedy inside it**, after Gaskell's *Cranford* and Le Guin's *The Dispossessed* by way of
*Brigadoon*, with the Sixnight as a horror annex and a romance across the fold. The register is warm, exact and funny
on the Lullday, and black and slow in the Sixnight. Its counter-voice is the **Glosses**: the Lull's casuists'
rulings on what counts as work, quoted on boards, sometimes absurd, sometimes very wise ("Gloss 1,104: whistling
while sweeping makes the sweeping play, if the tune is one's own").

Its subject is **what we mean by what we do**: wages and gifts, love and duty, rest and stagnation, mercy and harm.

## Geography

**Sixways** (outside, in ordinary time). A hamlet where the march road meets the Lull road at the **Fold-stone**, a
waist-high stone notched with the Lull-count, the only seven-day reckoning in that part of Imbar. Its people live off
the Lull: carters, a smith, a herbwife, a cloth-factor, an inn, and the exiles and spouses of Lull folk. It is a
weekday place: busy, practical, slightly sour, and full of people waiting.

- *The Waiting Woman*, the inn: Dorcas Trewin's. Sign: a woman at a window.
- *Nettle's forge*: Gil Nettle, smith, whose apprentice walked into the Lull three months ago and has been there
  thirteen days.
- *The carters' yard*: Abel Rooke and his mare Bess.
- *Ruth Coll's*: herbwife; poppy, nightshade, bandages, opinions.
- *The factor's house*: Mercer Quarles, who buys the Lull's cloth (cloth woven with love lasts forever) and would
  like to buy its labour.
- *Exiles' Row*: three cottages of Lull-born who left, the **Outlived**. Simeon Marrable lives in one.

**The Fold** (the road between): over the Tump's shoulder, under the **Strike Post**.

**The Lull** (inside, only on its day). North to south:

- **Lammas Tarn**, at the head, where the Idle rises; a hermit beekeeper, a boathouse, the reed-beds.
- **The Hangers**, beech woods on both slopes; the Old Brock's sett; charcoal hearths cold for fifty-seven years.
- **The Tump**, a round hill east of the town, with the **Strike Post** on its top hung with fifty-seven years of
  ribbons, one tied by each child on the Lullday they were born.
- **Linger**, the town, on both banks of the Idle:
  - *the Green and the Long Table*, where the whole town eats together at dusk and watches what holds (**the
    Watching**);
  - *the Gift Hall*, once the Cloth Hall, where "courtesies" are exchanged (commerce as two simultaneous gifts,
    certified by a Glosser);
  - *the Glossary*, the casuists' house and library of rulings;
  - *the Open Hand*, an inn kept by Matthias Gale, who adores innkeeping so violently that guests are overwhelmed;
  - *the Chapel of the Birds*, Rhiannon's, roofless, with rafters for starlings;
  - *the Almshouse of the Old Shift*, where the surviving strikers live;
  - *the Pleasure Gardens*, the allotments, where the town's food is grown as a game;
  - households: the Ashbys, the Tallows (chandlers), the Pryces and the Gales (rival Open Houses);
- **The Wrought Mill**, above the town on the mill-leat: seven storeys of dead windows; its great doors barred from
  inside fifty-seven years ago with an oak beam carved with the strikers' names.
- **The Weir and the Swallow**, at the foot of the valley, where the Idle goes underground. In the Sixnight the old
  tail-race under the Swallow is the only way out: **the Weaver's Gap**.

**The Sixnight** (inside, on the six days the Lull does not keep): the same valley, black, starless, everyone asleep
in their beds and impossible to wake; the **Ravel**, the lint of unmade things, drifting; and in the Mill, the
**Hands**: shapes of grey fibre working the looms of the Long Shift, which never stopped there; and the **Overseer**,
a tall shape with a bell, the one that means it.

## History (much of it disputed in play)

- *The Wrought* held the dale for a wool staple four hundred years ago. In the war that ended the old kingdom of the
  march (nobody in Sixways remembers which), the mill ran seven days for soldiers' cloth: **the Long Shift**.
- *The Strike.* The weavers stopped. Seven days later, on the Tump, they asked Rhiannon for their holy day and never
  another. Every family tells it differently: who spoke, what exactly was said, whether a child said it. The Old Shift
  survivors disagree about the words, and the disagreement matters.
- *The Barring.* The strikers barred the Mill from inside and climbed out of the windows. The beam says WE HAVE
  STOPPED and fifty-one names.
- *The early years.* Things learned the hard way: the first market came undone at dusk; the Wrought's bailiff, sent in
  with hired men on a Lullday, beat three weavers who were well by nightfall; the bailiff himself, beaten in anger by
  a weaver's wife, was not. The Lull learned that it could be hurt only by those who meant it.
- *The Glosses* began as arguments at the Long Table and became an order.
- *Sixways* grew at the stone in the world's time, generation after generation, while inside the strikers grew old.

## Institutions

- **The Glossers.** Casuists in white aprons with no pockets. They rule what counts as work; their rulings (the
  Glosses) are the Lull's law. Their doctrine: what is done for wages or under compulsion comes undone. They are
  partly wrong (see the minor story *On What Holds*).
- **The Rest-wardens** (the Idle Guild). Once the strike committee; now a constabulary of leisure, who fine you, in
  gifts, for hurrying, for haggling and for being seen to try. Led by **Hobday Pryce**.
- **The Open Houses.** Families who keep table for anyone. Status is what you have given away. The Pryces and the
  Gales are at war, in generosity.
- **The Watching.** Dusk at the Long Table: the whole town eats, and watches what holds. It is the Lull's theatre,
  court and gossip column. When your work comes undone in front of everybody, everybody knows you have stopped loving
  it.
- **The Weekday Club.** The young, who want the week back. Led by Corin Ashby.
- **The Old Shift.** The surviving strikers in the Almshouse, now in their seventies and eighties.

## The people

- **Corin Ashby**, nineteen, clockmaker whose clocks come undone every dusk. Fierce, funny, contemptuous of casuistry.
  She does not want to leave the Lull; she wants it to *start*. "I don't want to go. I want all of us to go *on*."
- **Tobias Ashby**, eighty-two, her grandfather, a weaver of the Old Shift. Gentle, a great teller of the strike, the
  most respected voice against the Working Day. His wife **Hester** has been dying of a wasting sickness for years of
  the world's time. In the Lull nobody dies quickly. Tobias has stopped believing in the Lull and has told nobody.
- **Hester Ashby**, eighty, dying, lucid and dry. She wants her Monday.
- **Simeon Marrable**, the companion. A Glosser, expelled twenty years ago (outer) for ruling that *a wage paid in
  love is not a wage*. Lives in Exiles' Row, now an old man by the world's reckoning. Erudite, funny, wrong about the
  law in an interesting way, frightened of going back, and in love, sixty Lull-years ago, with Hester, who chose
  Tobias. Banned by the wardens; wants in.
- **Linnet Gale**, twenty-three, fiddler of the Watching, the romance. Her tunes have never come undone. Warm, quick,
  frightened of the world (she went to Sixways for one day and it was "so *loud*"). For her you are there every day.
- **Abel Rooke**, Sixways carter, engaged to **Wenna Tallow** of Linger. Sixty days of courtship for her; sixty weeks
  for him. His wedding gift comes undone every dusk.
- **Dorcas Trewin**, the Waiting Woman's keeper, fifty-eight, married thirty years ago (outer) to **Piran**, who is
  thirty-four. She walks in every Lullday. He asks her why she looks tired.
- **Hobday Pryce**, Chief Rest-warden; **Lettice Pryce**, his wife, most aggressive giver in the Lull; **Kezia
  Pryce**, their daughter, secretly in the Weekday Club.
- **Matthias and Mabyn Gale**, of the Open Hand, Linnet's parents; Mabyn's war with Lettice.
- **Aldith Mote**, a young Glosser compiling the Gloss *On What Holds*; **Davy Mote**, her brother, seventeen, in the
  Club, wants to be a soldier.
- **Quick Nan Fossick**, pedlar, and **Gurney**, her bully.
- **Mercer Quarles**, cloth-factor, frank and charming, who would hire the whole valley.
- **Gil Nettle** and his lost apprentice **Tam**.
- **Ruth Coll**, herbwife.
- **The Old Brock**, a badger the size of a pony, killed for a bounty last Lullday, up again at dusk, and since then
  meaning it.

## The principal story, in three Lulldays

**Prologue: Sixways.** The company comes to the stone on an ordinary day and must wait for the Lull. Sixways is where
you learn what the Lull costs the people who love it: Dorcas's face, Abel's wedding, Gil's apprentice, Simeon's ban,
Quarles's ambitions, Nan's trade. You can work (cart for Abel, strike for Gil, run cloth for Quarles), and you can
wait at the stone.

**Act I: The First Lullday.** Wonder, comedy, and the law discovered. The town at rest; the Gift Hall; the Glosses; the
Long Table. At dusk, the Watching: Abel's gift comes undone in front of everybody, a pie bought at dawn goes back into
its dish, Corin's clock falls apart and she kicks it across the Green. Corin finds the company afterward: an outsider
can carry coin across the fold. Hester asks the company for her Monday. At midnight, sleep (and lose a week) or leave
by the road, or be awake in the Sixnight.

**Act II: The Second Lullday.** The Club organises. The Rest-wardens hear of it. Hester is dead (by Tobias, if nobody
else intervened), and her Watching is held at dusk; the town believes she died of her sickness. Hobday moves to close
the road to Sixways. Abel's wedding is on or off. The company can recruit Hands for Corin or talk them out; and can
learn, from Tobias, from the Sixnight and from the Glosser's experiments, what the Lull is.

**Act III: The Working Day.** Dawn: the Club begins to work in public on the Green, for Sixways coin. The town comes to
watch. The wardens come with staves. At noon the day turns on what the company did: who has joined, who has been
talked out, whether Tobias comes down from the Almshouse. At dusk, the Watching: does the work hold, and what does that
mean?

## Endings

1. **The Long Sunday.** The work comes undone at dusk; the Lull holds; the Club is broken. Corin goes out to Sixways
   alone, to outgrow everyone she loves, or stays and stops making clocks.
2. **Monday.** Enough of the valley worked for wages, with one of the Old Shift among them: the Lull ends. The week
   comes back. The Mill's doors open at dusk and the Hands come out and walk away over the Tump into the world. The Old
   Shift begin to age at the world's pace; Tobias dies in the autumn.
3. **The Seventh Day.** Tobias came down and worked beside his granddaughter, for love and not for wages: the week
   comes back to the valley, and the Lullday keeps its law. Linger becomes the one place in Imbar where, one day in
   seven, nothing done without meaning it lasts.
4. **What Was Meant.** The Working Day became a fight, and someone was killed by someone who meant it.
5. **Lingering.** The protagonist stays in the Lull (with Linnet, or alone), and the world outside goes on without them.

Personal codas for Abel and Wenna, Dorcas and Piran, Simeon, Hester, Nan, the Pryces and Gales, Gil and Tam.

## Smaller stories

1. **The Bride-Gift.** Abel's chair comes undone because he is making it to win Wenna. What does Abel actually love?
2. **The Waiting Woman.** Dorcas and Piran: who moves across the fold, and at what cost?
3. **Hester's Monday.** A grave request; a mercy that holds; Tobias's secret.
4. **The Open Houses.** A war of generosity between Lettice Pryce and Mabyn Gale, with the protagonist as its
   battlefield.
5. **On What Holds.** Aldith Mote's experiments: make, sell, give, carry for pay, and watch the dusk. The doctrine is
   wrong: grief holds, spite holds. The law is about meaning, not love.
6. **The Old Brock.** A badger that was killed for a bounty and got up, and now means it.
7. **The Dusk Market.** Quick Nan sells at dawn and leaves before dusk with the coin.
8. **The Sixnight.** The Mill, the Hands, the Overseer, the barred door, the Weaver's Gap.
9. **Simeon's Return.** A banned man brought home.
10. **Linnet.** Three days for her; three weeks for you.
11. **Gil's Apprentice.** Tam, thirteen days in, three months out.
12. **The Rest Cure.** A weary merchant's widow comes to Sixways to buy a day of rest and cannot stop bustling.

## Why someone would want to be here

Because the valley is beautiful and its days are long and nobody hurries; because the food is free and the people are
funny; because there is a law of nature you can test with a loaf of bread; because a fiddler is there every day you
come back, and remembers that you were there yesterday; because the dark between its days is the most frightening
place in Imbar; and because what you do there either lasts or does not, and you find out which every evening, in
public.
