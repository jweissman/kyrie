**[To Sixways street]** Six days of the week Sixways is a hamlet on the march with too many carts for its size, and the seventh it is a door. Everyone here lives by the Lull: they cart for it, buy its cloth, sell it what it will not make, marry into it, wait for it. They count its days on a board by the pump and a stone on the rise. They say of someone who has gone in and not come out for a long while that they are lingering, and they do not say it unkindly. Sixways street: the pump, the inn, a carter’s yard, a herbwife’s door with bunches hung in the porch, and the lane to a row of three cottages that stand a little apart. Everybody here seems to know what day it is in a way that people elsewhere do not. The board by the pump says, in chalk: LULL IN 2. Here are Quick Nan Fossick, a pedlar with a tray; and Gurney, a big silent man who carries Quick Nan’s baskets.

**[To the Fold-stone]** The Fold-stone is a grey stone, waist-high, on the bare top of the rise, notched along both edges with seven notches to a row, row on row, so many that the lower ones are worn to dimples. Nothing hangs on it but the weather. From it the road goes on round a bare green hill, and comes back to the stone. The newest notches on the stone say that the Lull is two days off. Today the road goes round the bare hill and comes back to where it began.

**[Sit down by the Fold-stone and wait for the Lull]** You sit with your back to the stone and watch the road go round the bare hill and come back, all day, and sleep in your coat against the stone, and wake, and watch it again; people come and go past you from Sixways and do not remark it, because people do. Then one morning at six the light changes, the way it does, and there is another road, going down. There is another road from the stone this morning: down, through a gap in the turf that was not there, into a valley full of beech trees and smoke and birds, with a town in it, and a mill.

**[To the Tump's shoulder]** You leave the Fold-stone with the sun low behind you and the dew still on the grass. It is the holy day. It has been the holy day in this valley for fifty-seven years, which is four hundred of the world’s, and nobody you meet here has done anything today that they did not mean to. The road you came in by will be there until midnight. Wren gains 50 experience: walked into the Lull. The road comes over the shoulder of a round green hill and the whole valley is below you at once: beech woods on both slopes going copper, a tarn at the head of it like a dropped coin, a river, a grey town of long-windowed houses, and above the town a mill of seven storeys with every window dark. Smoke goes straight up from the chimneys. Nothing moves fast. The noise that comes up to you is birds, and a fiddle, and somebody laughing a long way off, and under it all, so faint you have to stop to hear it, nothing at all: no hammer, no wheel, no cart. The Rest-wardens, in grey-gold sashes, with staves, are here.

**[To Linger green]** Down the middle of Linger green runs the Long Table, two hundred yards of trestles end to end, and people are wandering out with cloths and bowls to lay it, in no order, as the fancy takes them. Round the green stand grey weavers’ houses whose top floors are all window, the long loom-lights of the old trade, now hung with plants and curtains and, in one, a cello. Nobody is hurrying. A man is asleep on a bench in the sun with his hat over his face and a child asleep on him. Starlings everywhere: on the ridges, on the Table, on the ropes of the lanterns, talking. Here are the people of Linger, townsfolk of the Lull, in grey-gold coats fifty years old and good as new; and Hobday Pryce, the Chief Rest-warden of the Lull.

**[Into the Glossary]** The Glossary is a tall narrow house full of boards: wooden boards, painted white, each lettered with a Gloss and its number and hung on pegs, floor to ceiling, room after room, like a library written on doors. Gloss 1: THAT REST IS NOT IDLENESS. Gloss 7: THAT A KINDNESS EXPECTING THANKS IS A LOAN. Gloss 1,104: THAT WHISTLING WHILE ONE SWEEPS MAKES THE SWEEPING PLAY, IF THE TUNE BE ONE’S OWN. Gloss 2,213, newly painted: THAT A CAT WHICH BRINGS A MOUSE IS NOT IN SERVICE. A young woman in a white apron stands on a ladder, painting. In a corner one board hangs face to the wall. Aldith Mote, a young Glosser in a white apron with no pockets, is here.

**[Talk to Aldith Mote]** The young woman on the ladder looks down, with paint on her nose. ‘Oh! A Sixer. Hello. I’m Aldith Mote, I’m a Glosser, the most junior one, they give me the painting.’ She climbs down. ‘Could I ask you something? You don’t have to answer. Do things come undone outside? At sunset? Ever?’

**[Ask Aldith Mote what the Glossers hold the law to be]** ‘Gloss twelve,’ says Aldith promptly. ‘That what is done for wages, or under compulsion, is undone at the going down of the sun. And everything else is a footnote to it.’ She looks at the boards, and lowers her voice. ‘Two thousand two hundred footnotes. I paint them. And sometimes I think if you need two thousand two hundred footnotes, perhaps the text is wrong.’ She looks horrified at herself. ‘Don’t tell the Reeve.’

**[Offer to help Aldith Mote test the twelfth Gloss]** Aldith goes pink to the ears. ‘Would you? Nobody in the Lull can, you see; we can’t do anything for wages without meaning to see if it holds, and then we mean it, and it’s spoiled.’ She is already writing. ‘Two things. First: I give you a coin, and you stack me a cairn on the Glossary step, for the coin. Second: there’s Hob Linsey at the tarn, who loves his bees more than his life. If he’d take a coin to set a skep in the Gardens, he’d be working for wages at a thing he loves. Gloss twelve says it ravels.’ She bites the pencil. ‘I don’t think it will.’ She gives you two coins. ‘And anything else you see at sunset, tell me. Anything that holds that shouldn’t, or goes that shouldn’t.’

**[Take your leave]** ‘Look after yourself,’ says Aldith Mote. ‘I mean it.’

**[Into the Pryces' house]** The best parlour in Linger, and it knows it: a carpet, a clock that does not go (on the mantel, as an ornament), a glass-fronted cabinet of gifts received with each card turned outward, and a second cabinet, larger, of gifts given, represented by the receivers’ thank-you letters, framed. Everything smells of beeswax and of being looked at. Lettice Pryce, who keeps the Pryces’ open house, and is the most generous woman in the Lull, is here.

**[Talk to Lettice Pryce]** A tall woman with a great deal of grey hair piled up like a loaf sweeps across the parlour to you with both hands out. ‘A guest. And nobody has given you anything yet, I can see it in your face. Sit. Sit! Lettice Pryce. You’ll have been to the Gales’.’ The smile does not move. ‘Lovely people. Simple.’

**[Accept what Lettice Pryce wants to give you]** Lettice opens the larger cabinet and takes out a cloak: grey-gold Lull-cloth, lined with red, with a silver clasp in the shape of a starling, and puts it round your shoulders herself. ‘There,’ she says. ‘Now you can tell the Gales you’ve been given something.’ She stands back. ‘Wear it to the Watching. **[To the shore of Lammas Tarn]** Lammas Tarn lies at the head of the valley in a bowl of beeches, black and still, so clear at the edge you can count the stones. **[Talk to Hob Linsey]** The man in the boat sits up with bees in his beard and looks at you without hurry. ‘Don’t flap,’ he says. ‘They don’t mind you. They mind flapping.’ He climbs out. ‘Hob Linsey. These are mine, or I’m theirs; it’s been argued both ways.’

**[Ask Hob Linsey to set a skep in the Gardens for one of Aldith Mote's coins]** Hob looks at the coin as if it were a wasp. ‘Money,’ he says. ‘For bees.’ He looks at it longer. ‘For the Mote girl.’ He sighs through his beard. ‘She wants to see if it ravels.’ He takes the coin and puts it in his cheek, which is apparently where he keeps things, and picks up a skep from the bank, and a smoker, and walks off down the leat path without another word. When you see him next, in the Gardens, there is a new skep on a plank by the bean-rows, and bees going in and out of it, and Hob Linsey is sitting beside it with the face of a man watching his child’s first day at school.
