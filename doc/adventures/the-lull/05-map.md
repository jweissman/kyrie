# The Lull: the country, and where it lies

## In the world

The Lull lies **north over the march** of a country drawn from the seed, of forest, mountain or fen. It is never on
the first ring (Merrowdale holds every first country's north march), so a traveller hears of it a country off: in the
first country the innkeeper says, of the country beyond, *"North of it there is the Lull, forest country, they say,
where there is a valley you can only walk into one day in seven, where nothing done without meaning it lasts the
day. Folk in Tinfells would know more of it than I do."* In that country the keeper of travellers tells it in full,
and its north march road comes down into Sixways (`data/adventures/countries.json`, id `lull`; placed by
`Countries.plan`, and moving no other country: `test/the-lull-placed.test.ts`).

Played alone (*The Lull* on the title page, `data/campaigns/the-lull.campaign.json`), the company comes down the same
march road at eight in the morning, two days before the Lull.

**The Sixnight**: the same valley on the six days it does not keep: unlit, its people asleep where they lay down and
not to be woken, the Ravel drifting, the Mill working. Entered only by being awake in the Lull at its midnight (or by
the Gap from Sixways); left by lying down open-handed, by working the loom, or by the Gap.

## Time as geography

The road down from the stone is there from six in the morning of the Lullday until midnight. The Lull's sennight is
counted from the hour the country is reached; Sixways chalks it. Each Lullday is a week further on in the world's year,
so the Gardens describe the season the calendar is in, and the Lull-born speak of seven weeks as "Tuesday". A night
slept Lull-fashion takes the company to the next Lullday's dawn: everything outside, in every other adventure of the
world, goes on a week. Sixways is a day's carting from the mill towns along the march road (named, not built).

## History, as the player can assemble it

Fifty-seven years ago in the Lull's count, four hundred of the world's, the Wroughts' mill ran its looms seven days a
week for a war's cloth that nobody remembers (*the Long Shift*). The weavers struck. On the seventh day they barred the
Mill from inside and climbed out of its windows, and on the Tump asked Rhiannon, whose birds lull the living, for their
holy day and never another; what exactly was said is disputed by the Old Shift in four versions. One weaver, Tobias
Lindsell, was inside the Mill working for a shilling and climbed out last. The Glossers' order grew out of arguments
at the Long Table; Sixways grew at the stone in the world's time while the strikers grew old inside; Simeon Eddowes
was put out twenty years ago (outer) for Gloss 912. The war the cloth was for, the Wroughts' fate, and what the Hands
are, are not told.
