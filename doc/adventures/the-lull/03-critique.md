# The Lull: the first critique, and the design revised

*Stage 2. The treatment ([02](02-treatment.md)) read as a demanding editor and as a designer who has run too many
modules. Each charge is followed by what it changed. The design that was built is at the end.*

## The charges

**1. The asymmetry is told three times.** Dorcas waits for Piran; Abel waits for Wenna; Gil waits for Tam. All three
are *someone outside ages while someone inside does not*. The first is heartbreaking, the second becomes comic if
handled right, the third is the first again with less in it. *Cut Gil and Tam.* The Rest Cure (a widow buying a day
of rest) is satire of a softer target than the passion economy and repeats the Open Houses' joke. *Cut it.* Abel's
story is redirected away from asymmetry and toward its real subject, which is what Abel loves.

**2. The law is three laws.** The treatment says "without meaning it", the Glossers say "for wages or under
compulsion", Simeon says "without love". A player cannot investigate a law the author has not decided. *Decided:*
the law is **meaning it**: whole intent. Wages are beside the point: a thing made for wages by someone who loves the
work holds; a thing given grudgingly comes apart. Grief holds. Spite holds. The Glossers' doctrine (wages and
compulsion) is a useful approximation that is *wrong at the edges*, and Simeon's (love) is wrong in a different and
more interesting way. Finding the edges is a minor story (*On What Holds*) and it pays off in the climax.

**3. The climax was a vote in a hat.** "Enough Hands" and "one of the Old Shift" was a quorum. Worse, it assumed the
Club's theory (a day's wage-work breaks the Hallowing) was the mechanism, which makes the Working Day a ritual whose
result an outsider could compute. *Revised:* the Hallowing was asked by the valley and holds while the valley wants it.
The Working Day ends it only if the people working **mean** to have another day, and it reveals who does. At the dusk
Watching, every Hand's work stands or falls to lint *in front of the town*. Wages paid to swell the numbers make
nothing; a paid Hand's work comes apart and everybody sees whose. It is a vote only in the sense that a wedding is. The
crucial Hand is not a quorum but a person: whether any of those who asked for the Lull will ask for its end, and why.

**4. Tobias was a saint with a secret.** "Gentle, respected, privately disillusioned" is a type. *Revised:* Tobias was
not a striker. On the seventh day of the strike he went into the Mill to work, for his mother's physic, and was inside
when the Hallowing came; he climbed out of a window after the others had barred the doors. His name is not on the
beam. For fifty-seven years he has told the strike better than anyone, always from outside the window. This is not an
exhibit to be shown to a council. It is his to tell, and only to someone he trusts, and it changes what his opposition
to the Working Day means: the one man in the valley who knows what work-for-need costs is the one who most fears it
coming back. The beam, seen in the Sixnight, is a question the player can put to him; he answers it himself, or does
not.

**5. Hester's Monday is the heaviest thing in the work and was written as a request.** A request is an errand.
*Revised:* it is a situation with a clock nobody states. If nobody intervenes, Tobias does it himself on the first
Lullday night and carries it alone into the Working Day. The company can bring poppy, which eases her and keeps her
alive and asks something else of them; bring the nightshade and give it, which holds because they meant mercy, and is
a grave act; bring Simeon, who loved her first; or tell Corin. None is right. Each changes Tobias.

**6. The Sixnight is the collection's descent.** A dark annex below the utopia where "the truth" is would be the
reflex the survey names. *Revised:* the Sixnight explains nothing. It is the six days the Lull does not keep, dark and
unwakeable, with the Mill working in it. Nobody knows what the Hands are. It has three uses and no revelation: it is
the most frightening place in the region; it has the beam; and it has a way out (the Weaver's Gap) for whoever is
trapped. It is entirely optional and you enter it only by staying awake.

**7. The Open Houses are a joke, not a story.** Competitive generosity is a sketch. *Revised into a comic structure
with consequences:* (I) both houses compete to give to the guest; gifts given to win come apart at the Watching, in
public; (II) humiliated, Lettice Pryce gives Mabyn Gale *her house*, out of pure spite; and spite holds, so the Pryces
are homeless by their own gift, and the Chief Rest-warden is sleeping in his enemy's spare room; (III) Hobday, under
Mabyn's roof, is either softened by being looked after or made dangerous by being laughed at, and that decides
whether the wardens come to the Green at noon on the Working Day. The comedy is how the climax is staffed.

**8. Linnet is a prize.** "Warm, quick, frightened of the world" is an adjective list. *Revised:* Linnet wants to
write a tune for the Watching that the whole Lull will sing, and every tune she writes is sung once and forgotten,
because a tune cannot come apart and nobody needs to keep it. She is the one person in the Lull for whom the law means
nothing, and she is the most afraid of it ending. For her you are there every day; she notices what a week has done
to you. On the second Lullday she counts your weeks aloud. What she wants of you on the third is not a declaration but
an answer: will you be here tomorrow, and what does tomorrow mean?

**9. The protagonist wants nothing.** *Revised:* the protagonist is the only wage-earner in a valley without wages,
and both sides want to pay them. Corin's coin comes from Mercer Quarles. Hobday offers a "gift" for the names of the
Club. Abel will pay for help with a chair. Each pays in a different coin and the Lull judges each at dusk. And there
is Linnet, which is wanting of another kind.

**10. The danger is set dressing.** The Brock is a wandering monster; the Overseer a boss. *Revised:* the Brock was
killed last Lullday for Hobday's bounty by a Sixways trapper and got up at dusk, as hired harm does, and has meant it
since. It can be fought (it is far too strong for one novice), lured with the hermit's comb, smoked out of the
gardens with flame, or killed by someone who means it: not for the bounty. *Killed for the bounty, it gets up at
dusk.* The Overseer is avoidable by lying down with open hands, which Tobias's strike-song tells you to do, and which
is the strikers' own sign; by working (or seeming to), which it permits; or by running for the Gap. The wardens on the
Working Day are people, and anything the company does to them it means.

**11. Too much is said.** The treatment explains the Long Shift, the Wrought, the bailiff and the strike. *Revised:*
the war the cloth was for is not named; the Wrought are a name on a beam-end and a crest on the Mill gate; which words
were said on the Tump is disputed in three versions by three of the Old Shift and never settled. The valley has
a history the player assembles from people who disagree about it.

**12. Is it inhabited without its plot?** Mostly yes: the Long Table, the Gift Hall, the Gardens, the Tarn, the
Chapel, the Glossary. But Sixways risked being a waiting room. *Revised:* Sixways is where the work is. It has a
carter's run you can ride (paid, and it keeps), the herbwife's trade, a factor who buys Lull-cloth (which never wears
out, because it was woven meaning it), and Nan's dusk racket. The stone is notched with the count. Days in Sixways are
spent doing things, and the stone can be waited at when you have done them.

**13. What is beautiful here, and does anything do the beauty's work?** Three images earned their place because they
do work: the **ribbons on the Strike Post** (one per child, so the post is a census and a calendar); the **Watching**
(the town's theatre and court, where the law is seen); and the **year that passes in fifty-two days** (each Lullday is
a week further into the world's season, so from inside, blossom is fruit in a fortnight and the gardens are a harvest
every morning; the Lull's plenty has a reason, and its children have never seen a slow spring). One image was cut:
the old looms played as instruments was lovely and did nothing.

## The design that was built

**Cast (twenty named, and three groups).** Sixways: Dorcas Trewin, Abel Rooke, Ruth Coll, Mercer Quarles, Simeon
Marrable, Quick Nan Fossick and Gurney. The Lull: Corin, Tobias and Hester Ashby; Linnet, Matthias and Mabyn Gale;
Hobday, Lettice and Kezia Pryce; Aldith and Davy Mote; Wenna Tallow; Piran Trewin; Hob Linsey the tarn hermit.
Groups: the Weekday Club, the people of Linger, the Rest-wardens. Creatures: the Old Brock, the Hands, the Overseer.

**The principal story.** Prologue in Sixways; three Lulldays (I: the first Lullday the company spends inside; II: the
next; III: the Working Day, the one after). The Working Day happens whether or not the company is there.

**Ten smaller stories.** The Bride-Gift; the Waiting Woman; Hester's Monday; the Open Houses; On What Holds; the Old
Brock; the Dusk Market; the Sixnight; Simeon's Return; Linnet.

**Endings.** *The Long Sunday* (the Lull holds); *Monday* (the Lull ends, and its law with it); *The Seventh Day* (the
week returns and the Lullday keeps its law); *What Was Meant* (someone is killed on the Green by someone who meant it).
Then a few lines for each person, by what happened. Staying in the Lull is not an ending: it is a choice of the
company's afterward, and the world goes on outside.

**What the Working Day turns on.** Each Hand on the Green either means it or does not. Who means it is settled by
what the company did with them: Corin (her clock); Davy (he means it already); Kezia (spite, which holds); Wenna (if
she is leaving with Abel); Piran (if he will age with Dorcas); Aldith (if her experiments made her want to see); any
townsfolk paid with Quarles's coin (they do not). Of the Old Shift, only Tobias can be brought: for love of Corin
(*The Seventh Day*) or in grief and guilt after Hester (*Monday*). Without him the Lull holds whatever else stands at
dusk; but what was made meaning it still stands, which is not nothing to someone who has watched her clocks fall
apart every night of her life.
