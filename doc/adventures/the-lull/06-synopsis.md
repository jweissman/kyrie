# The Lull: the story, its acts, its people, its ends

## The situation before the company comes

The young of Linger, born into a valley that has only one day, have formed the **Weekday Club** under Corin Lindsell
and mean to work for wages, in public, from dawn to dusk, on a Lullday, believing (from the Glossers' doctrine) that a
day of wage-work will break the Hallowing. Corin's grandfather Tobias, the most loved teller of the strike, sits by his
dying wife Hester and has stopped believing in the Lull, and has told nobody; Hester has asked him every day for a
month for her "Monday", and he will not buy physic across the Tump. The Chief Rest-warden's wife is at war in
generosity with the keeper of the Open Hand. A pedlar from Sixways sells ribbons that vanish off the Strike Post.
A carter's wedding gift keeps coming apart. A badger killed for a bounty got up. The Mill works in the dark.

Corin names the Working Day for the Lullday after the one she tells the company of it. Nothing waits for the company
after that.

## The acts

| Act | When | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Prologue: Sixways** | The world's days before the company's first Lullday | The cost of the Lull to those who love it: Dorcas, Abel, Simeon, Ruth, Haskett, Nan. Work that holds (carting). The wait at the stone. |
| **I. The First Lullday** | The first Lullday the company spends inside | Wonder, comedy, the law seen at the Watching. Corin's forty-second clock is kicked across the Green; she tells of the Club. Hester asks for her Monday. At midnight: sleep a week, leave, or be awake in the Sixnight. If nobody intervenes, Tobias gives Hester her Monday that night. |
| **II. The Second Lullday** | The next | A week on for the company, a morning for the town. Hester's Watching, or Hester eased. The Pryces homeless by spite, or at peace by a receipt. A clock made for someone. The wedding, if it is going to happen. The company can learn what holds, which side of the window, and who will come down. |
| **III. The Working Day** | The one after | The Club at the bridge from dawn; Hands coming down through the morning; Tobias at ten, if he comes; Hobday at noon; the Watching at dusk, at which every Hand's work stands or falls in front of the town. |
| **Afterwards** | Every Lullday (or day) after | The book closes at midnight on each person's afterward and on the valley; in a world, only its chapter closes, and the valley is there to come back to, changed. |

## The people, what they want, and how they can change

| | Wants | Hidden | Can become |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Corin Lindsell**, 19, clockmaker | For all of them to go *on*: "We're not resting. We're stopped." | Makes clocks to get out; they ravel | Someone who has made a thing that holds (a clock for her grandfather), reconciled with him, or bitter with a broken hand, or gone out to outgrow everyone |
| **Tobias Lindsell**, 82, of the Old Shift | That the week never come back | He was inside the Mill working for a shilling on the seventh day; his name is not on the beam; he will give Hester her Monday himself | A man who tells the strike from the right side of the window, keeps the bridge, and comes down for love; or comes down in grief and dies in the autumn |
| **Hester Lindsell**, 80, dying | Her Monday | She knew which side of the window | Eased by poppy or by a priest's words and carried down to watch; or dead by Tobias's hand, or by the company's, with Simeon beside her |
| **Linnet Gale**, 23, fiddler | A tune that is about something | She is afraid of the world and loved its noise | Someone who counts your weeks; who asks whether you will be here tomorrow; who comes out over the Tump, or keeps you |
| **Simeon Eddowes**, old, put out | To see Hester; to be right about the law | He loved Hester sixty years ago | Brought home past the wardens (after eleven, by the sheep-walk, by the Gap); delighted to be wrong; at the end of Hester's life |
| **Hobday Pryce**, Chief Rest-warden | Rest, kept | He finds he does not know what to do with people who try hard and whose work holds | Softened by cocoa under his enemy's roof; or the man on the bank at noon |
| **Lettice Pryce** | To beat Mabyn | Whether any of her gifts held | Humbled into a real gift (a receipt for the croup), or homeless by spite |
| **Abel Rooke** / **Wenna Spurling** | Each other; the road (both, secretly) | Each thinks the other wants the Lull | Married on a cart-seat that holds |
| **Dorcas** / **Piran Penhallow** | To grow old together | She is ill; he carves her ageing every week | He goes out for her last years; or she moves in to be ill a day a week |
| **Aldith Mote**, junior Glosser | To find out | She suspects the twelfth Gloss | The finder of Gloss 2,214, carrying stones in her apron |

## Themes, and how they are carried

- **What we mean by what we do.** Carried by the law itself, tested by the player's hands (a cairn for a coin, a
  carving for someone, a ribbon bought), and by every person's story: a chair made to give oneself up, gifts made to
  win, a pillow held in mercy, a hymn sung badly at a thing of iron.
- **Rest and stagnation; work and need.** The strike that became a covenant; the young who are not resting but
  stopped; the old man who knows what he would do for wages because he did it.
- **Time owed between people.** Dorcas and Piran's shelf of heads; Linnet counting your weeks; Corin's fear of coming
  home a stranger; every night inside costing a week of the shared world.
- **Motifs.** Open hands (the strikers' sign, the sleepers, Tobias at the Table, Tobias found dead); clocks that do not
  go (the Pryces' ornament, Tobias's watch stopped at the Hallowing, Corin's forty-two, the one that ticks); starlings
  going over at dusk (and on the Monday not coming back); ribbons (a census, a cruelty, an ugly blue that holds);
  Monday (the day that never comes; death; the ending's name).

## The ends

| Ending | When |
|---|---|
| **The Seventh Day** | Tobias came down for love (his clock, or Corin told and reconciled, or Hester carried down to watch, or his own shilling given back), and at least five Hands meant it, and nobody broke the bridge: the week returns and the Lullday keeps its law |
| **Monday** | Tobias came down in grief, after giving Hester her Monday himself; the week returns and the law goes, and the Hands walk out of the Mill into the world |
| **The Long Sunday** | The Club worked and the wardens sat down, but none of those who asked for the Lull came to end it; or the Club was talked out of it after Corin's clock held |
| **The Bridge in the Leat** | Nobody kept the wardens off; a warden meant it, and Corin's hand is broken |
| **What Was Meant** | The company stood against the wardens and killed |

The company's own afterwards (staying in the Lull with Linnet, a promise kept or broken; Linnet on the road; Simeon at
the Glossary) are told among the fates, not as endings.
