# The Lull: five conceptions, and why this one

*Stage 1. Written after reading the collection's critical survey ([repertoire](../repertoire-2026-10-26.md)), the
atlas, the shipped adventures, and the four unmerged adventure branches (`adventure/big-tent`, `fair-weather`,
`grand-tour`, `winter-in-carth`), and before deciding anything about the engine except that it exists.*

The survey is blunt about what the collection keeps writing: an outsider reads a misread inheritance before a
deadline, descends to the mechanism, shows the exhibit, and the community chooses a stance. It is short of
**wanting, fault, a person as an end, making, comedy as structure, fear that lasts, the small that stays small,
and minds changed by people rather than by exhibits.** It asks for no third creditor beneath a city, no ledger as the
decisive exhibit, no clerk in a grey coat, a rest from votes and hearings, and a work that ends in the parish it began
in. I held each concept below against that list, and against the brief's own request for somewhere *profoundly unlike*
Imbar in its time, laws or moral assumptions.

---

## A. The Ambling

*A city on the backs of eleven walking beasts.*

Kessendry crosses the Tessel steppe on eleven Ammonts, grazing giants with shells like hills, on a circuit of forty
years. Each district is a beast; each beast is a family's. Bridges are thrown between backs every morning and taken in
every night. The city passes the march of a settled country once in a generation, and that is now. The eldest beast,
Grandmother Ure, who carries the temples and the song-archive, is slowing. She will lie down on this crossing. The city
must choose whether to leave its oldest quarter, and the old who will not leave their houses, on a dying animal, or
halt the Round and starve the herd.

*Promise:* geography that moves; the pleasure of a market on a back; a city you travel *with*; grief lived through;
a calf born unclaimed and the politics of who rears it; a romance across two beasts.
*Doubts:* its drama is a deathwatch, and a dying old keeper of the archive is the collection's habit in a shell. Kyr
places are fixed; the city's motion would be faked by moving one gangway. The best of it (an animal's death) is an
elegy, which the collection already does well.

## B. The Week of Guests

*A fen town where the dead come home for seven days each autumn.*

At Hallowtide the dead of Fennick walk up out of the mere and live in their old houses for a week, and must be back in
the water by the seventh dusk. The living keep their rooms aired all year. This year a grandfather contests his own
will, a widower's two wives meet at one table, a girl is in love with a boy who drowned at sixteen and has come back
sixteen again, and there is one guest whom nobody buried.

*Promise:* grief as something lived; comedy of manners among the dead; tenderness; fear in a bounded place (the
unclaimed guest); a story that stays small.
*Doubts:* a ghost story's last move is always "who is the extra guest", and its answer goes down into the mere. The
Banns at Hessel already has a knock at two in the morning, and The Third Night a homecoming to a dead father. It would
be well made and familiar.

## C. The Vizard Country

*A valley where a mask is a person.*

In Mumming everyone goes masked, and the law follows the mask: whoever wears the Reeve's face is the Reeve, with his
debts, his wife and his enemies. The old lord's mask has been stolen on the eve of his funeral.

*Promise:* comedy as structure (mistaken identity is the oldest comic engine there is); satire of office; a
protagonist who can literally become somebody and be wrong about themselves.
*Doubts:* the collection has two theatre-adjacent works (Gosse's Company, Pentrice's licensed stage) and Hessel's
comedy of manners. Kyr's "Known as … when" would carry it, but the premise would be a single trick played many ways.

## D. The Lumber of Heaven

*A plane where the gods keep what they threw away.*

Beyond the canonical planes, an attic: discarded miracles, superseded commandments, a sun replaced in the second age,
pickers who live by salvage. A satire of obsolescence.

*Promise:* wonder; comedy of the divine; a plane unlike the elemental doors of the Grand Tour.
*Doubts:* it ends outside the world, which is the reflex the survey asks us to break; and it is a cabinet of curiosities
before it is a society. I could not find the people in it.

## E. The Lull

*A valley that keeps one day in seven, and no other.*

Over the march there is a valley you can walk into one day in seven. On the other six the road goes round a bare hill
and comes back to the stone. Inside, at the town of Linger, it has been the holy day for fifty-seven years: four
hundred of the world's. It began as a strike. In the Long Shift the Wrought Mill ran its looms seven days a week, and
on the seventh day of the strike the weavers climbed the Tump and asked Rhiannon, whose birds lull the living, that the
valley might keep its holy day *and never another*. It was granted, and nobody knows exactly what was granted.

The valley's law, as far as anyone understands it: **what is made there without meaning it comes undone at sunset.**
Bread baked to sell is dough again at dusk; a wall built for wages is a heap; a coin paid for a loaf is back in the
buyer's purse and the loaf back on the shelf. Things given, played, or made in love stand. Harm done for hire heals by
nightfall; *in the Lull you can only be hurt by somebody who means it*. And a night slept in the Lull is a week gone
outside, because the Lull's night is the six days it does not keep.

*Promise:*

- **A material law that is a moral law**, open to experiment, so the player can *investigate a physics of intent*
  with their own hands (make, sell, give, carry for pay, and watch the dusk).
- **Satire with a recognizable target.** A society that abolished work has rebuilt every institution of work out of
  play and gift: commerce as simultaneous gifting certified by casuists in pocketless aprons; status by competitive
  generosity; Rest-wardens who fine you for hurrying; and, under it all, a demand that everyone *love* what they do,
  because what you do not love will not last a day. It is "do what you love" made into weather.
- **Making as play.** The one place in Imbar where the quality of a thing depends on whether you meant it.
- **Romance with a shape.** Time runs seven to one across the fold. A lover inside lives a day for every week of
  yours. Courtship there is fast for them and slow for you; a marriage across the fold ages one partner seven times as
  fast as the other.
- **Fault.** The protagonist can be asked, by a dying woman who has lingered for years of the world's time, to bring
  her "a Monday" from outside.
- **Fear that lasts.** Whoever is awake in the Lull at midnight on its day is in the six days it does not keep: dark,
  everyone asleep and unwakeable, the unmade drifting like lint, and the Mill working.
- **Minds changed by people.** The central conflict is between the young who inherited rest and want time to start,
  and the old who won rest and will not give it up. Neither has misread anything. There is no machine to repair.
- **A shared-world consequence no other module can offer:** every night inside costs a week of the world outside,
  where every other adventure's clock keeps running.
- **The small stays small.** It ends in the valley it began in.

*Doubts, honestly:* the Patient Star and the Ghessil Uplands already have slowness as danger; this must stay about
*work and meaning*, not about time as such. The valley's law is a world-law, and the survey warns that a world-law
"makes the law the protagonist". The answer has to be people whose wants cut across the law. And a youth-against-elders
politics risks becoming a vote; it must stay personal.

---

## The choice

**E, the Lull.** It is the only one of the five whose strangeness is also a *question the player can answer with
their hands*: what do I mean by what I do? Every system in it (trade, harm, making, sleep, courtship) is bent by one
law, so the setting is unlike Imbar everywhere at once, while the valley itself is ordinary: beech hangers, a tarn, a
weaving town, a mill. Its central conflict has no culprit and no inheritance to read correctly; it has a grandfather
and a granddaughter who want incompatible good things. It allows comedy (the casuistry of rest), satire (the
passion economy), romance (the asymmetry of the fold), tragedy (a mercy that holds), horror (the Sixnight), and
domestic smallness (a wedding gift that keeps coming apart) without forcing any of them into the others. And it is
reached the way the brief asks: by an ordinary traveller who hears in an inn that over the march there is a valley you
can only walk into one day in seven, and decides to see.

The wanderer origin, which comes "to see what there is for someone who will work", arrives at the one place in Imbar
where work does not keep.
