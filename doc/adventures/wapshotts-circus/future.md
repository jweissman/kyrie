# Wapshott's Peregrine Circus — possible futures

*Not built. Nothing here is an obligation of Phase I, and nothing in Phase I decides between these. They are threads
the first adventure leaves loose on purpose.*

- **The Household of the Progress.** A letter with a crowned-wheel seal asks the Colonel to bring his company, and his
  tortoise, before the Household. Hench must teach him to bow to a dowager who is also an archduke. Whose tortoise
  is Methuselah now, and does the Colonel give him back, in person, with a band?
- **A royal estate.** If the Household likes what it sees: winter quarters on a royal estate, a Royal Warrant framed
  in the ticket office, and every ambition the Colonel has ever had arriving at once.
- **The bone in the marl-pit.** A thigh-bone as long as a man, in the third town's shrine. The Colonel's eyes. What is
  bigger than an elephant. An enormous menagerie, and whatever catastrophe a showman can make of something that
  should have stayed in the ground.
- **The Gentle Art, second degree.** The Riposte; and the Mirror, which Mumchance saw once at Dunmere and is not sure
  he did not dream. A clown who can send a conjuror's fire back the way it came.
- **Zéphyrine's Ascension.** A wire from a steeple across a river, with all the town underneath and Mrs Pruett with
  her apron out. Somebody has to find her a steeple, and a priest who will lend it.
- **Honoria and Mumchance.** Wakeham, and a bridge that washed out thirty years ago. A tea-shop with a bow window, and
  an old clown in a chair by the stove, or one more season.
- **Pip on the road.** A loud clown with a trick bicycle and a goose; what she brings back from the world.
- **Cordelia Sallow.** Next year she means to beat him fairly; or, united, she has the strongbox and he has every
  idea. Either way, the oldest unfinished business in the circus.
- **Ada's quarter-share**, and what happens when the Colonel reads part twenty-two of *The Brigand's Bride* and
  recognises himself.
- **Ninepence at the inn**, if he went there: what a hob does with a brewhouse, and a town that never thanks him.
- **The Grand Circus Overture** for a band of twenty, with the low G.
- **Other circuits.** The same company could go round another country's towns by the same placement needs, or cross a
  march to Pentrice and meet Gosse's Company, who have their own opinion of circuses.
