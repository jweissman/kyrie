# Wapshott's Peregrine Circus — possible futures

*Not built. Nothing here is an obligation of a later phase, and nothing in Phases I and II decides between these.
Phase I is [README.md](README.md); Phase II is [phase2.md](phase2.md). What each phase left open on purpose:*

## Left open by Phase II

- **The rest of him, under the field.** The Leg in the shrine, the claw (in the shrine, in the marl, in the Colonel's
  wagon, or on Sir Gervase's desk), the stone that says the King's diggers stopped because he was not dead enough,
  and a ground that is, once, warm underfoot. Sir Gervase knows the Royal Society would pay to see it dug. Ada will
  have the Colonel's pen again if he so much as looks at a spade. Nobody has said what he is.
- **A royal seat, and an estate beyond anyone's means.** If the Warrant was won, the King's Rest is the circus's
  winter seat and the Inventory its charge: a roofless palace, a raven, a tortoise, a dozen salamanders, an old man
  with a broom, and a warrant to hold a Progress every year at the Master's own expense. The Treasury's iron wagon
  is "not to be found"; the Steward has told him not to look for it. He will look for it.
- **The Keepership in other hands.** If Sir Gervase holds the first right to the beasts, every beast of the Inventory
  has a price; if Pell holds the Revels, the Rest will become a theatre, very good and very indoors, and Pell has
  asked to borrow the Bishop; if Sallow holds it, she has asked the Colonel what the Inventory is for.
- **The Gentle Art, toward the Mirror.** The four foundations, then the Riposte, the Echo, and the Reversal (the
  mirror-door of Pell and Fell, the door to the Mirror, not the Mirror). The Mirror itself, which Mumchance saw once
  at Dunmere when an old clown gave a conjuror's fire back the way it came, is a grandmaster's art and has not been
  touched.
- **Pell and Fell.** Two old clowns who have found each other again after forty years, one of them newly married
  to the Ox of Abernant. Whether Polly Pell comes outdoors, or Gus Fell goes in.
- **Pip and Kit.** A loud clown and a boy who falls like a cupboard; or Pip gone indoors for a season with Pell's,
  and what she brings back.
- **Ada Thripp, author.** Count Wapsotti is to be hanged in part twenty-three. The Dowager would give a province to
  meet A Lady of the Road.
- **Hench.** In the circus with the Margravine's green spangle given to a raven, or at court with the Dowager's
  forty forks.
- **The Sylph after the Ascension.** She has crossed the Long Court from a tower. A river is wider.
- **Nell and the seventh broom.** The next Keeper of the King's Rest may be eight years old and the daughter of a
  canvasman who passes the gate once a round.
- **Billie's standards.** A Keeper who will not keep beasts for a man with a microscope, in a world where a man with
  a microscope may own the beasts.

## Larger horizons

- **The Household of the Progress, revived.** A Little Progress becomes a real one: a court on the roads again, with
  the circus as its Menagerie Royal, and every town of the country to be passed through in order of precedence.
- **The old inventories elsewhere.** Hench says the Margravine of Oster's great-uncle had the last inventory but
  one. What else of the Menagerie Royal strayed, was sold, or went over a wall, and where its grandchildren are
  eating bees now.
- **Other circuits, other companies.** The same placement needs (`largest`, `along:`, `between:`) would take a company
  round any country's towns. Gosse's Company, at Pentrice over the march, have their own opinion of circuses, and Mother
  Pell remembers Absalom Gosse as a young man.
- **The magnificent catastrophe.** A royal estate, an ambitious proprietor, an Inventory that wants filling, a
  naturalist with funds, and something enormous under a field in the third town. Whether, how, and with whom it goes
  wrong is not decided.
