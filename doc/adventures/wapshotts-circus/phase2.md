# Wapshott's Peregrine Circus — Phase II: The Crowned Wheel

*The design of the second phase. Spoilers throughout. Phase I is [README.md](README.md); what Phase II leaves open is
[future.md](future.md).*

## What Phase II is about

Phase I was poverty: an old debt, a rival, a Fair. Phase II is renown, which is worse. Word of the Goose Fair, a
tortoise with a crown cut in his shell, and a company that has been going round the old kings' road for nineteen
years without knowing it, reaches the last two officers of a defunct royal institution: the **Household of the
Progress**, which once went round the country with two hundred wagons and the **Menagerie Royal**, and has not
moved in three hundred years. Its Steward has leave from the Crown for one *Little Progress*: a night of **Revels** at
the **King's Rest**, the Household's ruined winter seat on the hill above the second town, where the office of
**Master of the Revels and Keeper of the Menagerie Royal**, vacant since the last Progress, may be given to whoever
best deserves it.

The Colonel wants it more than he has ever wanted anything. Getting it means restoring a ruin he cannot afford,
reconstituting a three-hundred-year-old inventory of beasts from a circus that owns a cockerel, taking money from a
charming naturalist with a contract, putting on a court masque under rules no living person understands, and
beating a company of real actors who think circus is a disease. Winning it would give him a royal estate. That is the
problem.

The spine is the **Revels**: twelve days from the Letter to the night itself, one full round of the circuit, with
the circus's last stand moved up the hill to the King's Rest.

## Chapters

- **IV. The Letter** — the first day of the stand after the Fair: a shabby young man in a good coat on the steps of
  the Colonel's wagon, with a seal of a crowned wheel.
- **V. The King's Rest** — the first time the company goes up the hill: gates with the wheel on them, a court big
  enough for two hundred wagons, and one old man with a broom.
- **VI. The Revels** — the night itself.
- **VII. After the Revels** — the strike, and what goes round with the wagons now.

## New people

- **Tobias Quarrendon**, Clerk of the Household of the Progress: twenty-six, all elbows and Latin, the Household's
  entire staff below the Steward; brings the Letter; falls slightly in love with the whole circus and specifically
  with the wire.
- **Dame Honorine Quarrendon**, Steward of the Household, eighty, never seen until the Revels; the letters are hers.
- **The Dowager Archduchess Ottilie**, who is also, by a muddle of treaties, a Margravine and a Princess of the
  Progress (the dowager who is all three that Hench once mentioned); comes to the Revels; a secret reader of
  *The Brigand's Bride*.
- **Sir Gervase Fenwick**, baronet, naturalist and patron: kind, curious, rich, and blind to anything that cannot be
  catalogued. Offers the money the restoration needs, on a contract.
- **Jabez Orme**, ninety-one, hereditary Keeper of the King's Rest: the last of a family of keepers who have swept
  empty cages for three hundred years without being paid. He has the **Keepers' Book**.
- **Old Tom**, the Rest's raven, who speaks in fragments of court ceremony learned from six generations of Ormes, and
  steals.
- **Augustus Pell**, tragedian, manager of **Pell's Comedians** of the Dunmere Theatre Royal, who bid for the Revels
  on the ground that a masque is a play; his leading lady **Clemency Vane**; his apprentice **Kit Darrow**; and his
  grandmother **Mother Pell**, eighty-eight, granddaughter of Joey Pell, the first joey, and the last great clown of
  the theatre tradition: Mumchance's old rival.

## The touring world

The circuit continues: the same three towns, with **Pell's Comedians always one town ahead**, skimming the houses
(the town that saw *The Tragedy of the Unhappy Margrave* last week has spent its coin), and the **King's Rest** taking
the place of the second town's field for the Revels stand. The third town keeps the **Marl Wake** on the Revels round:
the diggers' festival, with the crowning of the Marl Queen and the marl-pit opened for the year. Every road of the
circuit has old milestones with a crowned wheel worn on them: the circus's road was the Progress road all along.

## Threads (any order; each with several ways)

1. **The Warrant and the Revels** (the spine): restore enough of the Rest; present what can be found of the
   Inventory; give a court masque; deal with Fenwick's contract; beat Pell (and Sallow, if a rival).
2. **The Keepers' Book**: read with Jabez Orme; trace the Bishop (Methuselah), the line of lions (Nebuchadnezzar),
   the Glimmer of the Fens, the Kitchen Hob (Ninepence was the Rest's before the mill), the Salamanders of the
   Warm-House, and the Leg of the Great Worm (*the rest of him under the field at the marl-pit; do not dig; he is not
   dead enough*).
3. **The Bishop goes home** — whose tortoise is he? Give him back to the Crown (the Steward), sell him to Fenwick,
   or keep him.
4. **The Salamanders of the Warm-House** — fire-lizards in a ruined glass-house over a warm spring. Glaze it and keep
   them there, or carry one in an iron brazier-cage (and keep the brazier lit on the road, or the straw goes up).
5. **Old Tom and the Seal** — the raven has the Steward's seal-ring in his hoard in the gatehouse tower. Climb for it,
   trade spangles for it, or talk to him in his own language (the Echo).
6. **The Marl Wake and the Pit** — the diggers' festival at the third town; the flooded marl-pit; a great pike that
   took a dog; drain it by the old sluice, go down by night, or dig in the wrong place. What is found is a claw the
   size of a sickle, and a decision about who gets it.
7. **Nebuchadnezzar's last journey** — the old blind lion, wanted for the Revels; how he is moved, kept and shown.
8. **Pell's Comedians** — rivalry and friendship across companies; Mother Pell and Mumchance; Pip and Kit Darrow.
9. **The Gentle Art, second degree** — the Riposte (Mumchance), the Echo (Mother Pell), the Reversal (both); and what
   it costs Mumchance to teach the last of it.
10. **The company's own lives** — Honoria and Mumchance at last (a wedding in the Rest's chapel, if the bridge holds);
    Ada and the Dowager's favourite novel; the Sylph's Ascension from the gatehouse tower; Fipps's Overture for twenty
    with Pell's orchestra; Hench at court; Lusk's Nell; Billie made Keeper, with standards.

## The Revels (culmination)

At the King's Rest on the twelfth night: the Dowager, the Steward, Fenwick, Pell's Comedians and (unless united)
Madame Sallow. Court rules, as Hench teaches them: nobody laughs before the Dowager laughs; nobody turns their back
on the dais (a ring is round); a masque must have Virtues in it. The Colonel's masque is *The Triumph of Wonder*.
The company chooses its staging, may take a part, must improvise when something goes wrong (and something will:
Old Tom, the cloud machine, the Colonel), and the Dowager and the Steward weigh it with what the Rest has become,
what the Inventory holds, how the Bishop was treated, and on what terms the money was taken.

Outcomes:

- **The Warrant** to Wapshott's (or the United Circus): Masters of the Revels and Keepers of the Menagerie Royal,
  with the King's Rest as winter seat; from then on the Long Court is the circus's stand on that round instead of
  the second town's field, and Ninepence (if invited) stays in the Rest's kitchen.
- **The Warrant, with the Keepership to Fenwick**, if the Colonel signed the first clause of his contract unchanged:
  the circus wins, and the Inventory's beasts are a man with a microscope's to buy.
- **The Warrant to Pell** (if the theatre scores higher): the Dowager gives the circus a framed appointment instead,
  *By Appointment to Her Serene Highness*, which is all the Colonel ever wanted, and not what he wants now.
- **The Warrant to Sallow** (if she is a rival and beats both): she asks the Colonel to tell her what the Inventory is
  for; the Dowager's appointment goes to the circus "for the cockerel".

In every case the wagons go out on the round again, changed.

## How it is built

Seven new files of the same module (`data/goings-on/circus/`): `rest.kyr` (the King's Rest, Jabez Orme and the
Keepers' Book, Old Tom and the seal, the salamanders, the Bishop's homecoming), `progress.kyr` (the letter, Tobias,
the Revels schedule and the Rest stand, Sir Gervase's money and contract, the state coach), `pells.kyr` (Pell's
Comedians one town ahead, Mother Pell and Mumchance, Kit and Pip), `art2.kyr` (the second degree of the Gentle Art,
court forms, the turn of voices, Pip's entrée, the ladder for two), `lives.kyr` (the wedding, Ada and the Dowager,
the Ascension, Clemency as Folly, the Overture for twenty, Nell and the seventh broom, the hob's kitchen, Cordelia
inside or across), `marl.kyr` (the Marl Wake, the pit, the claw) and `revels.kyr` (the Revels, the verdict, after).
The King's Rest is a cluster of rooms of the second town, up a hill road from its green: it exists from the first day
of every world, for whoever asks the innkeeper about the walls on the hill, and in Phase I is only a ruin with an
old man sweeping it.

Shared additions: the techniques **Echo** (distracted on every foe for a turn, unless a wisdom save) and **Reversal**
(a foe's own weight: 1d8 unless a dexterity save) in `data/catalog/abilities.json`; Orsino's **Riposte** is taught as
it stands. No engine change was needed for Phase II.

## Scale of the scoring

The Revels score adds what the company made true: the Rest fit (roof +2, dens +1, glass +1, cloud +1; no roof −2),
the seal (+1), the bow (+1/−1), Hench staying (+1), the Overture for twenty (+2), each Inventory beast shown (the
Bishop at home +3 or shown +1, the lion +2, the Glimmer +1 or +2 for one let go, the hob +2, the owlbear +1, the
salamanders +1, Sir Roderick honest +1, the Leg +1, the claw +1), Clemency as Folly (+2), Pip's entrée (+1, or +2 with
Kit), the ladder for two (+4) or alone (+1), the Ascension (+3, +1 with Mrs Pruett), the mishap answered (+1 to +3)
or not (−2), the Bishop sold (−3). Pell's masque is 9 (less without Kit or Clemency); Sallow's offering 8 (+1 with
her lion).
