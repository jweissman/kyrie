# The Weather Drove: the story first

*Written before any Kyr. The adventure follows it; where play changed it, friction.md says how.*

## The place

Wickering is a one-street town of tin roofs on the lip of the Sere, a plain the colour of old biscuit, where it rains
once a year or does not. It rains because of the Widow.

The Widow is a cloud. She is also a beast, and has been for as long as the drovers have kept books on her: a low grey
cow of weather, nine furlongs across, that walks in off the sea-mountains every autumn and must be *driven*. Left alone
she wanders, thins, sulks on the salt pans, and goes home dry. Driven with smoke and whistle down the old drove road, she
comes over the Seven Gardens at the end of it and rains herself out on the date-palms, and the town drinks for a year.
The Shales have driven her for nine generations.

This year she came down off the mountains, as she always does, and went into a glasshouse.

## The people

**Dorcas Shale**, seventy-one, drove-mistress, blind in one eye from lightning. She speaks of the Widow as one speaks of
a difficult in-law, with exasperation that is love. She knows the Widow is old. She knows what an old cloud's last rain
is. She has been late to the drove for three years running and has told nobody why. 'A drove is a thing you do *to* the
end,' she says. 'I never said I was in a hurry to get there.'

**Nettle Shale**, eleven, her granddaughter. She whistles the Calling, the first third of the drove-call, which her
grandfather taught her before he went into a lightning-storm after a straggler. She does not know the other two thirds
and does not know she does not.

**Fidelis Gillyflower**, sixty-six, lemon-grower. Eleven years ago he built the Gillyflower House on the ridge, forty
panes by sixty, with a boiler under it, to grow lemons for his wife Thea in a country where nothing grows. She is buried
on the terrace under a rain-gauge. The Widow came down the ridge in the first week of the drove, found the warm wet
breath of four hundred lemon trees, and walked in through the vents. She has not left. She rains on the lemons, a
quarter-inch a night, and in the evenings Fidelis winds his wife's music-box in the gallery and *the cloud sings it back*.
He has told the town the cloud is "taking the air". He knows exactly what it is.

**Ysolde Skewes** keeps the salt pans, forty tons of salt stacked open to the sky. Rain on the heaps is a year's ruin to
her and to nineteen pan-hands. She is not a villain; she has done the sum on both sides. She has also lit brine-fires
on the east ridge, whose bitter smoke a cloud will not walk through.

**Professor Augustin Maundy** is a rainmaker. He arrived with a brass howitzer, eleven barrels of something, and a
testimonial from a duke. The Reeve has paid him eighty crowns. At dusk on the third day he will fire the howitzer at the
sky, because that is what the testimonial says he does. He is sincere. He does not know that you cannot shout at a cow
until it is unfrightened, or that the glasshouse roof is directly under his line of fire.

**Reeve Hannibal Muckle** has a cistern, a clerk's notice and four days of water. He is frightened and therefore polite.

## Scenes

**The dry cistern.** The first thing a player sees is Wickering's cistern with a tin cup on a chain and a painted line a
child has drawn at the waterline, with a date beside it, daily. The line has gone down eleven times.

**The camp.** Dorcas and Nettle sit by seven wagons. The smoke-pots are cold. 'We'll go when she's out,' says Dorcas.
'She is not out.' Nettle whistles three notes, rising, and stops, as at a door she cannot open.

**The glass.** From the terrace the Gillyflower House is a ship of light, and in it, filling its roof like breath on a
mirror, a grey weight hangs among the lemon trees. Inside it is warm, scented and raining softly. The Widow is not
frightening. She is plainly happy.

**The music-box.** In the gallery Fidelis winds a brass box. Eleven notes fall. Far off, from inside the cloud, a lower
voice gives back the last four, a fraction late, like a person finishing another's sentence. 'The first year I thought
it was the glass,' says Fidelis. 'It is not the glass.'

**Thea.** Thea Gillyflower, born Shale, left the drove at nineteen to marry a man with a lemon obsession. She taught the
Widow nothing; she *added* to the Widow. Every drove-call has three parts: the Calling, which makes a cloud look up; the
Turning, which makes it come after you; and the Letting, which tells it that it may rain. Thea's grandmother taught the
Letting to the sisters and Dorcas sang it badly and Thea sang it so well that after Thea left, Dorcas stopped. The
music-box plays it. Fidelis has been playing the Letting to a cloud every night for eleven years, and it has been
declining to take the hint.

**The sisters.** They have not spoken since the wedding, which was nine winters before the Gillyflower House. Dorcas
will not set foot in 'that man's greenhouse.' Fidelis does not know she is Thea's sister: Thea never said, because Dorcas
never answered her letters, and she did not wish to be pitied. Both have been loyal to a silence.

**The three voices.** The call needs all three parts at once, by three people who each know one: Nettle the Calling,
Dorcas the Turning, and the Letting, which only the box plays. If Fidelis opens the roof-vents and sets the box going,
and Dorcas whistles from the terrace, and Nettle goes in among the lemon trees to where the cloud can see a child — the
Widow will come out. 'It will not be a thing you do,' says Dorcas. 'It will be a thing we do. And you will stand at the
back and keep still.'

**The question.** Which way to turn her? Left is the Gardens and the town: the Widow comes down in one night, out of
the sky entire, and is a thin bright rag by morning. Right is the sea. 'There she'll go on a long while,' says Dorcas.
'Over water. Nobody will have her rain. Nobody will own her. I think she would like that.' The town has four days of
water, and Nettle is eleven.

**The cannon.** Maundy and his howitzer. A scheme that seems stupid proves to be only a very large noise: if the gun is
turned from the sky, it can be fired where there is a use for noise. If it is not, the glasshouse roof catches the
shock. Forty panes by sixty come down in one note, and the Widow goes up out of it screaming white, runs out over the
town and drops everything at once: the lower street is a river by morning, the pans are a lake, the Gardens are
untouched. It is not a catastrophe. It is a very expensive way to find out what a cloud thinks of being shot at.

## What can go otherwise

- Nobody opens the roof. The third day ends; the Widow, cold at the vents, thins in the dry air and is gone by the
  fourth morning, as a cloud goes when it has stopped being asked. Wickering has a cup of water.
- Ysolde's fires are not put out, and the Widow will not walk through bitter smoke to the Gardens at all. She will go to
  the sea or to nothing. Salt is saved; the town is not. (The player may cover the salt, or put out the fires, or
  persuade Ysolde, who has her reasons.)
- The Professor is let fire. Or he is talked out of it. Or he is made *useful*, once, in the right place.

## Endings

1. **The Whole Rain.** Called, roof open, turned left. One night of rain on Wickering that nobody sleeps through. In the
   morning the Widow is a rag; in the afternoon a small bright cloud the size of a hat is following Nettle, who has
   named it already. Dorcas and Fidelis sit on the terrace and begin, formally, to disagree about lemons.
2. **Over the Water.** Called, roof open, turned right. The cloud goes out to sea whole and the town goes on a long
   dry month. The Reeve organizes carts. Dorcas drives the last drove alone to the shore and sings what she never
   sang, and Nettle, for the first time, hears the Letting from someone's mouth.
3. **The Cloudburst.** The gun. The roof comes in. A flood, a funny one, then a sad one: Fidelis sits in the glass with
   the music box in his lap, which is still, somehow, going.
4. **The Cup.** Nobody does anything in time. Wickering has a very dry winter. Everyone is gracious about it.

Each carries small fates (Ysolde, Maundy, Nettle, the sisters) set by what was done.

## What the player does

Walks a small town in three days, listens, carries one family's silence from one end to the other, covers salt with a
pan-hand, takes a lemon off a tree that is raining, and decides at the last, with a cloud over them, which way a
living thing goes. The strongest verbs are *carry*, *ask*, *keep still* and *decide*.
