# Low Water — editorial assessment

*Written after the Kyr was finished and four long journeys were written out as the page told them
([doc/playtests/low-water-2026-10-25](../../playtests/low-water-2026-10-25)). All play described here was played by
the developer through the journey driver and the browser driver. It is not independent human testing.*

## The plain answer first

**Low Water as it stands is a good bounded adventure. It is not a supermodule.** It is one city over six days, about
45 rooms, about 29,000 words. A thorough player who reads everything and tries several approaches gets perhaps three
to five hours from it. That is the scale of *Tomb of Horrors* or of a long chapter of a campaign. It is nowhere near the
giants–drow–abyss sequence, which starts in a slavers' ring and ends sixty-six layers down facing a demon queen.

The comparison exposes four weaknesses. I am stating them before the strengths because they decide what the next pass
has to be.

1. **There is no strategic adversary.** "No villain" was a deliberate choice in the conception. It gives the city its
   moral texture: every party is partly right, and the cause is the gaps between people doing their jobs. The cost is
   intensity. The threat is a lake falling a hand a day. Nothing is *coming*. The cosmology (the census, WHAT IS COUNTED
   IS GIVEN, the copied city in the Interval) is set up and then never acts.
2. **The deep places are small.** The Undercroft has seven rooms and one rule. The Interval is two rooms and an image.
   The best wonder in the work, the Bowl copied out of water, is a coda you can walk through in twenty minutes.
3. **Danger is thin.** One fight that can be avoided (the bailiffs), one that should be avoided (the Equals), two
   drownings. There are no tactical encounters with real choices in them.
4. **The historical layers are named more than they are found.** The Mathematical Age, the Aeneans and Ammerlin each
   leave a few inscriptions. There is no Aenean voice anywhere in the adventure, and no account of what the Aeneans
   thought they were doing or what happened the last time a Remainder was cut.

## The ten questions

1. **Strongest artistic achievement.** The hymn as an order. The idea fits one sentence: a city's liturgy is the
   operating program of a machine nobody remembers. The adventure delivers it as play, not as a reveal. The player
   watches the Deep Gate shiver at the Sixth, has the Seventh sung once and watches the gate lie down, and reads the
   machine's grammar out of a dye-basin arch, a keystone and a hymnal. Then they speak to the machine in phrases the
   choir never sang. The column puzzle is the work's best interaction.
2. **Weakest major act.** The Reckoner (Movement III). It is the place the whole investigation points at, and it is
   seven rooms, one weighing puzzle and a set of bars to strike. It should have been the adventure's *Tomb of Horrors*
   and it is its antechamber.
3. **Most memorable character.** Isaura Vant. Her development is earned through what she sees, not through what she
   is told: her tally, Piet's letters ("we'd have answered with a form"), the machine that is a ledger, the Equals that
   count heads. The arc runs from *measuring settles things* to *measuring tells you exactly how big the thing is that
   you will have to decide anyway*. It holds over 39 conversations, but it gets no test hard enough to break her.
   Cantor Orr is the close second, and she has the better scene ("It was somebody counting, wasn't it").
4. **Puzzle most likely to frustrate unfairly.** The two-tone grammar, *if* the player never finds the hymnal or the
   keystone letters. The arch's four mouths alone do not give it. Halm, the glossary and the hymnal are three
   independent routes, but all three sit on the terraces, and a player who goes straight to the Undercroft gets
   nothing there to teach them.
5. **Least convincing causal transition.** The bailiffs reaching the dig on the third afternoon as a fixed appointment.
   It is fair (the purchase is announced, and the freeze or the warrant prevents it), but Quill never decides
   anything on stage. He is a clock wearing a coat.
6. **Most important optional discovery.** The Fourth Remainder: the old liturgy held the last door shut. It turns
   WHAT IS COUNTED IS GIVEN from a promise into a question: someone built the count, and someone else built a lock on
   its door. It is also the thread the next pass pulls (below).
7. **Largest technical compromise.** The Hearing as a sequence of exhibit actions scored by credence. It reads as a
   case being built, but it is not an argument. Nobody answers a specific exhibit except Quill.
8. **Most valuable new general capability.** Small ones only: underground journeys told without the sky, and a
   thing's state said as words. The authoring findings (05 §3) are worth more to Kyr than the code: a teaching
   observation silently replaced by its journal line; `Buys` with no purse; `carries … or carries …` never offered;
   descriptions repeated in full on every arrival.
9. **Most likely difficulty for an unfamiliar player.** Knowing that time matters, and how much. The six days are
   stated once, by Isaura, and the Mere's fall is shown only on the Ward gauge. A player can spend two days on the Rim
   and miss the breach. The gauge-and-thresholds design is fair, but it needs a felt pressure it does not yet have.
10. **What remains less ambitious than the conception.** The Interval (the conception's "someone has been using it"
    is one image), the lake's danger, and above all the cosmological question, which the work raises and then declines
    to pursue.

## What is good, with evidence

* **The causal city.** Left alone, the Bowl goes on (`alone.md`): the *Tench* on the mud, Marra Fen signing her plot,
  the fight at the dig, the well failing, the Registrar's wrong but honest finding, the bells ringing with nobody at
  the ropes, and an ending that is a consequence, not a failure screen. Fixing the hymn early saves the diggers because
  the Mere stops falling below the throat, not because a flag says so.
* **Responsive conversation.** Ruan's taproom news changes with events. Fisk, right for the wrong reasons, gets to say
  so ("That's not faith, Assessor. That's luck wearing a cassock."). Isaura dissents at the Hearing if the Finding
  names the Guild over her figures.
* **Puzzles that teach a physics.** The Dyers' Basin teaches *water is moved, never made* in ten minutes of play, and
  the Deep Gate is the one place that rule breaks.

## Verdict

Finished as a bounded adventure: every route plays, eleven endings are reached in tests, and the Bowl survives being
left and returned to. **Unfinished as the commission's supermodule.** The second pass that would make it one is
[07-second-pass.md](07-second-pass.md).
