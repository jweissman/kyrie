# Low Water — the second pass: ten times the ambition

*2026-10-25, written after the editorial assessment ([06](06-editorial.md)) and in answer to the question of whether
this is a supermodule. It is not yet. This is how it becomes one without throwing away what already works: the hymn as
an order, the city as a causal system, and Isaura.*

*Built: see [08](08-second-pass-built.md), which records where the build departed from this plan.*

## Where to start: answer the question the adventure already asks

The built adventure asks a question three times and never answers it: WHAT IS COUNTED IS GIVEN. *Given to whom?*
Halm asks it at the last door. The Fourth Remainder held the door shut for three thousand years, against someone. The
Interval contains a city built out of the counted water by someone who has only seen Ammerlin reflected.

The second pass answers it, and the answer is the strategic adversary.

## The adversary: the Auditor

Beyond the planes, in the outer darkness where the Varakiiri raised their citadels, one of them has spent three
thousand years doing the only thing it can do across the settlement: **reading Ammerlin's water**. Every measure the
Reckoner counts and spends goes through the last door into the Interval, and the Interval is the shallow edge of its
domain. The copied city is its work. It has been learning a mortal city the way a prisoner learns a guard's footsteps.

What it wants is **the number**. The settlement the Virina imposed binds the planes with the prima materium: the
binding of souls to bodies holds the walls of the world up, the way the weight of the Mere held the Deep shut. The
Mathematical Age built the Reckoner as an **audit**: a census of everything bound, to tell the Arbiters whether the
settlement still held. Something else, later — the Aeneans, and the first verse they ever wrote themselves — added the
Fourth, the lock on the door, when they learned who else was listening. The Varakiir that receives the count can find,
in the exact number, the place where the binding is thinnest: a seam. A Varakiir that knows where the seam is can come
through it.

The adversary is not hidden in a dungeon waiting for the player. It is already acting, and the player can trace each
of its instruments:

* **The Reflected.** As the Mere falls, figures of water climb out of it at night and stand in the Ward, copies of
  people, left for right. They have no voices, only the hymn. They take the places of those the water takes. The
  drowned diggers come back as Reflected, and Dov sees Annet Kettle standing on the landing. Fisk's "every soul a drop"
  was a superstition, and it is also *exactly the shape* of what is happening. That is the first chilling revelation.
* **The Second Reader.** Halm's correspondent at the Collegium, who sent him to Ammerlin and pays for his stones. Halm
  is innocent. The Collegium's twice-yearly letter carries a cipher in its footnotes. Collegium Fellows across Imbar are
  restarting every counting instrument they can find, the Long Man's Mower among them. *That* is what the bell-teeth
  are for, and it gives the cross-adventure link real stakes.
* **The Short Hymn itself.** Sabeth Orr cut the Fourth and the Seventh herself, but the old hymnal she worked from had
  been annotated, in a hand nobody at the Sounding can account for, with the cuts already marked. The reform was
  suggested to her. The adventure's "no villain" was a mask, and the reveal is earned because everyone in the city
  still did their job.

The strategic clock is no longer a lake falling a hand a day. It is a count approaching nine ninths, an audience on
the far side waiting for it, and Reflected multiplying in the Ward.

## The climax and Isaura's arc

The Auditor cannot be fought the way the drow queen is fought. It can be **given the wrong number**. The final choice
asks the work's one honest person to do the thing she has refused all along: write a lie with a number in it, because
people believe the number. Isaura can falsify the count and close the seam, and she will never measure again. The
player can find another way: the Fourth, held for ever; the count broken before the end; an appeal to the Arbiters
through the census's own original address, which is in the Ear's Library. Or the number can be given, and something
comes up the Stair at the Sixth.

## Wonders, danger, depth

* **Wonders**: the Listening Hall singing when the whole Ward sings; the Bowl purring at the Fifth; a gallery of the
  Reckoner where the unborn are counted in bright notes; the mirror city; the citadel of water-glass.
* **Danger**: the Reflected (they cannot be cut, only out-sung, or led into dry stone); the drained galleries filling
  again as the hymn changes them; the Equals' foundry; the reversed Stair; the citadel's audit-guards, who weigh what
  you are.
* **Depth**: the Ear's Library makes the Aeneans speak; the marked hymnal gives the Short Hymn a second history; the
  Collegium letters carry the adversary out into Imbar and back to Merrowdale.

## Scale and order of work

Roughly 150–200 rooms over four planes, a dozen new principal characters (the Aenean chronicler in the library's
recordings, the Second Reader, the Reflected Annet, the Auditor), and a second strategic clock. The order of building:

1. The Reflected and the marked hymnal, in the existing city. They change the tone of what is already playable.
2. The Ear's Library (Act II).
3. The megadungeon Reckoner (Act III).
4. The Interval as Act V.
5. The Citadel (Act VI) and the endings rewritten around the number.

This changes the adventure's founding premise: there *is* a villain now, behind the city's honest mistakes. The
change is deliberate. The honest mistakes stay honest. What they were exploited by is new.
