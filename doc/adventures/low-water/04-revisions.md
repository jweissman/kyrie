# Low Water — what changed, and why

*Every substantive change from the first conception (00) and the comparison's revisions (01). For each: what it was,
what it became, and whether the reason was artistic or an implementation constraint. Constraint-driven changes are
marked ⚙.*

| # | Was | Became | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mother Cantor Orr; Gammer Lusk; lake-mothers; Tallymen; a Council that votes; a drowned *town*; the Undercroft "in the Tomb tradition" | Cantor Orr; Abel Lusk; no eels; the Equals; the Hearing; the Listening Hall (an instrument, not homes); a machine governed by one rule and one code | The corpus comparison (01): these were already The Long Man's, The Patient Star's and Thrume's furniture |
| 2 | A keeper who closes the Deep Sluice by hand at the Seventh Remainder | The machine moves the Deep Gate itself, by what it hears down the Sounding's shaft; the keeper's charge is to *watch* and *send to the Sounding* | A keeper who saw the sluice stay open for three years would simply have closed it. The revision makes the hymn literally an order to the machine, and Piet's silence plausible (01) |
| 3 | The Mere falls by both causes about equally | The Deep Gate drains −3 spans a day; the Channel −½ (its mill water returns to the Stair below the Rim, less what fulling and the frames give to the air) | The first build had the Channel at −1 a day. With the Seventh restored on the first night, the Mere still fell to the throat by the sixth day and the diggers drowned in the "best" route. That contradicted the story both Quill's engineers and Isaura's tally tell (the Channel is a sliver). The rate was the bug, not the drowning |
| 4 | "Down we go" taboo: *never the high voice* | The keystone's oldest marks read THE HIGH VOICE IS NOT SUNG BRIGHT; the keepers' rule is that warning *worn smooth by handling*. The Fourth Remainder *is* high-then-deep: the last door, least — hold it shut | Adding the Fourth and Fifth (as optional discovery) required the high voice to be sung in the old liturgy. The layered misreading is better than the original blanket taboo: the keepers' rule overgeneralises a precise warning, which is what institutions do |
| 5 | The census delivered on completion, always | The last door opens on completion unless the Fourth is sung (or sounded at the column) — *the kept count* | An optional discovery that changes the meaning of WHAT IS COUNTED IS GIVEN. WHAT IS GIVEN IS KEPT: someone built the count, someone else built a lock on its door |
| 6 | The Tallymen remove whatever weight tips a reckoning (including a standing person) | The Equals keep the Hall's floor level: one weight-stone in the far pan for each walker, *by how many, not how heavy* | ⚙ Kyr has item weights but nothing that sums a pack against a scale. Counting walkers and stones with two small counters is exact and legible. The change improved the idea: Isaura's line that it is *a census too, counting heads* only exists because of it |
| 7 | Every Assessor action at the Hearing a free argument | Exhibits laid one by one, each gated on a known fact or a carried paper or a consent; witnesses present by agreement; the Council's verdict by a credence count with Quill's rebuttal | ⚙ Kyr has no dialogue trees with free composition; a sequence of actions in one room is how a structured argument plays. It reads well: the player builds the Finding like a case |
| 8 | Quill's engineers' report taken from his strongbox | Sella's *copy*, brought for a promise; the original, if the strongbox is picked, is his and he objects in the Moot Hall | ⚙ An owned item carried in front of its owner provokes the shared objection and hostility. A copy fits Sella's word ("I'll have the copy on the bench") and keeps lifting the original a real, costly choice |
| 9 | The Tench, the well, the Ward leaving as narration | Thresholds on the Mere that change the place, the people and the taproom's news | Artistic: consequences shown where the player stands (Ruan's rumours, Tench Row with handcarts, the dry well) |
| 10 | Halm a voice for knowledge at any cost | Halm also *acts*: if he can reach the column (breach, or the gate down) and nobody has persuaded him, he goes down on the fifth night and ends the song | The brief: characters who are already doing something and would do more if left alone |
| 11 | The breach a flat risk on the fourth morning | The diggers can only reach the wall once the Mere has fallen below the throat (34); a breach drowns three unless a dam was built (Dov warned with Isaura's gauged figures) or the Mere is below 26; the bailiffs taking the dig first prevents the breach and costs Dov his freedom | Causal coherence: fixing the hymn early saves the diggers *because the Mere stops falling*, not by fiat |
| 12 | Equals arrive at once to defend the broken governor | They come a watch later | The first build killed the whole company in the action that broke the spindle: a death the player could not see coming or answer. Now the sound of them coming is told and there is time to go |
| 13 | Full descriptions on every arrival | A place once left is told in a line; its changing details (gauges, gates, who is there) are still told in full | ⚙ The page repeats a place's observations on every arrival. The Bowl is vertical: every errand passes the same landings. The first full-play transcript repeated the fountain sentence fourteen times |
| 14 | `Observe … when not knows(f) … teaches f` | `Rule … at room when at(party, room) and not knows(f) says … teaches f { }` | ⚙ An observation conditioned on not knowing what it teaches stops holding the moment it teaches, and the page then shows the discovery's journal text in place of the scene. A rule's words are told as the event they are |
| 15 | Halm sells the glossary (`Sells`) and buys finds (`Buys`) | An action to buy the glossary; Halm given coin | ⚙ `Buys` needs the buyer to hold the coin (`quantity(buyer, gold) >= pays`); people start with none. `Sells` of a catalog item from a non-trader did not appear on the page; the action is simpler and says what it costs |
| 16 | "The engine" in prose | "The Reckoner" (named by Halm), "the machine" | ⚙ Lint reads *the engine* as prose explaining the implementation. The name is better: *a reckoner, the Collegium would call it* |
| 17 | Terraces as levels of the city | Rooms of the settlement joined by *steps* | ⚙ A level must be inside a site or building, and a site's name then labels every way into it ("Into the terraces of Ammerlin"). The Stair between terraces is called steps, which lint accepts between rooms of one area |
| 18 | One lake-level sentence in the gauge observation | Gauge readings also in each gate's own description | ⚙ Presentation: working a gate opens the thing's own view, which shows its state, not the place; the levels must be where the player is looking |
| 19 | A single hymn line every night | Eight, chosen by what has happened (the Ward leaving, after the Hearing, after the count, after the governor) | Artistic: the hymn is the adventure's clock and its most repeated text; it should register the city's changes |
