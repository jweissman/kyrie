# The small adventures, and how they hold together

*An inventory of the eleven strands as built, each against the commission's seven parts: a person with a motive, a
place or thing that matters, something uncertain, more than one approach, a complication, a resolution that changes
something, and a later effect. Then the threads that tie them to one another, and to the one question that the
morning the Season ends turns on.*

The overarching mystery is small and domestic: **why is Mrs Cherrett sleeping so much?** Nobody in Hessel asks it,
because everybody knows she is dying, and a dying woman sleeps. The answer is the Hessel drops (S9). Each other strand
either leads to that answer or competes with it for the company's four days. The overarching satire is the Season
itself: its Rules, its rumours, its marriage market, its doctors, and its chairmen, who carry all of it uphill for
ninepence. The romance is plural: Ismay and Kit; Rob and Celia; Mopsa and the protagonist, if the player wants it; and,
at the far end of a life, Mrs Cherrett and a dead navvy.

Five threads carry the weight:

1. **Standing beside.** Ismay will only ask her grandmother anything, or stand at the reading, if somebody has told
   her they will be in the room (`ismayStoodBy`). One small promise in S4 opens two resolutions in S9 and S5.
2. **The chairmen's goodwill** (`goodwill(chairmen)`, `stoodWith`). Small acts: defend Kit, stand with them, give back a
   chair. These add up to the Old Town's key: Jem's free answer, Hetty brave enough for church, Hartopp made to walk
   to the Ball in boots, Nan named, Mrs Cherrett carried uphill on a strike night.
3. **Mr Twiss's favour** (`twissFavour`). The apology in form, Brocca in time, the chairmen's warning. These add up
   to the one Rule he will break, and he breaks it only if she is awake enough to dance (`failing < 3`).
4. **Mrs Scrope** makes every secret public. That is always quicker than the private way, and it always costs someone
   else something.
5. **Mrs Cherrett's failing** (`failing(cherrett)`, 1 to 5). Each doubled night is a measure of the company's
   delays; the closing page is chosen by it.

## The purely local stories

Several strands never touch the Cherretts' main question and are complete on their own:

- **Brocca**: a drunk violinist, a pump, and a hill. It ends in Twiss's debt or his disgrace.
- **The shepherd's child** on the Orm road, and what the company tells her father.
- **The footpads at the gibbet**: pay, fight, or be robbed and left by the road.
- **Number Four**: Mopsa's lease, with three different ways to pay for it.
- **Pocklington's bribe**: a lesson about the Rules, learned at the door in front of everyone.
- **Hetty's ultimatum**, which runs whether or not the company ever goes to church.

## Distinct resolutions, by strand

Every strand has at least three outcomes, and the closing account (`endings.kyr`) reports each in its own line. The
line is chosen by the fact the strand left behind, not by the route that produced it. Ismay has five lines: married
by licence; engaged; claimed by Hartopp; jilted at the altar; free. Rupert has five, Mopsa five, Carth House five and
Ottery five; Hartopp has four, Rob four, and the chairmen three. There is also a line for the prayers said over Mrs
Cherrett, if she died and a cleric was there.
