# The Banns at Hessel: environment and resolutions

## The environment as design

Hessel tells its story mostly through what is where, and when. The rules that keep it are in `clock.kyr`,
`city.kyr` and each strand's own file.

**The hill.** Class is height. The Rooms are at the top, the waters in the middle, the Old Town at the bottom, and
the Parade between them is too steep for satin slippers. That one slope is why the chairmen matter, why a strike is
a weapon, why Mrs Cherrett needs legs, and why Hartopp in riding-boots is a disgrace. A chair up costs two coin; the
company can pay or walk.

**The Season's round.** Pump Room at eight, baths till noon, walks at noon, dinner, the Rooms from seven, home by
eleven. Everybody keeps it, and so everybody can be found by knowing it. Mrs Scrope is at the Pump Room in the
mornings. Ismay is at the parlour window while Kit's chair waits below, then behind the Sham Castle at noon. Rupert
goes down to Quay Lane at noon. Each routine yields while its person is going along with the company, so that a
person who has agreed to come does not walk off to keep an appointment.

**Doors with hours.** Twiss receives between ten and six. Lady Wenlock is never at home before ten or after six.
Saint Ebba's is locked at night. The area gate at Carth House is open for the coal from eight to nine. The ballroom
doors open to those admitted. An hour is a key in Hessel as often as a skill is.

**The things that speak.**

| Thing | Where | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| The bath-chair | the Parade | the inciting accident; the first test of a build (brawn, or anybody's arm) |
| Kit's book, with a pressed violet | Kit's pocket | Ismay's love, before either says it |
| The beaver hat on Hetty's peg, R. W. and a beetle | Hetty's shop | Rupert's secret, to anyone who looks closely |
| The brown bottle of drops, its label overwritten | Mrs Cherrett's dressing-table | the mystery, its proof, and the swap |
| The Rules of the Rooms, framed in gold | the vestibule | every rule the Ball turns on: boots, two dances, eleven o'clock, companions |
| The clocks of Carth House, all at two | the hall | Sir Gervase's crime, before he tells it |
| The crack in the shutter | the drawing room | worn by a fingertip; Rob's poem goes through it |
| The coal-hole hasp | the area vault | a way in for someone who searches |
| The card in Number Four's window | Quay Lane | Mopsa's want, on the street |
| The gibbet with a garland | the crossways | the footpads, and Ottery's second question |

**Light and dark.** Carth House changes most by hour. By day it is shuttered and smoking; at dusk a candle appears at
the crack; late at night a hand lies flat on the glass; at two a woman knocks. The window can be climbed only at
night, and Celia goes out by it only at night. The Crescent at night says "and then, at the end, nothing" until the
front door stands open.

**Gossip as weather.** Whatever the company tells Mrs Scrope is public at three in the afternoon. From then on it
comes back in her morning news, which reads differently in a quiet town and a loud one.

## The five endings

The closing page is chosen by the one thing the morning turns on, in this order:

| Ending | When |
|---|---|
| **Turned Away** | the company told the town a lie about her and was dismissed |
| **The Season's End** | Mrs Cherrett did not wake |
| **The Last Dance** | she danced the last minuet with Mr Twiss after eleven |
| **The Morning After** | she lives, and the company was her legs |
| **Passing Through** | the company was in Hessel for the Season and never took her up |

Under each closing page the account is told line by line, one for each strand the company touched. That account is
what makes two playthroughs with the same ending read as different Seasons.
