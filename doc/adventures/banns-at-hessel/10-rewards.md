# The Banns at Hessel: rewards and what they imply

Hessel is a level-1 country: the company that walks in over the north march will usually be new. Its rewards are
deliberately small in kind and large in consequence. They are built from things the world already has (coin,
experience, a granting item, a weapon, companions), so they mean the same thing outside the valley as in it.

## Experience

Twenty-eight milestones (`growth.kyr`), worth 4,600 in all. No single Season reaches them all: several are exclusive.
The banns are stopped *or* withdrawn; Celia goes to her mother *or* the door is opened for her mother. The worth
follows the comedy's values, not danger:

| Worth | For |
|---|---|
| 400 | seeing the door of Carth House opened by the hand that shut it |
| 300 | Mrs Cherrett's last dance |
| 250 | keeping her alive; Jack in Lady Wenlock's arms |
| 200 | Ismay and Kit at the Sham Castle; the blessing; Celia out; Celia to her mother; Mopsa's house; Mopsa's yes |
| 150 | the Rooms; Brocca in time; the banns stopped; the poem; the knocker; sitting up with Sir Gervase; walking Ottery to Orm; standing witness; Hartopp out of the Rooms; a licence used |
| 100 | the chair; the fares; standing with the chairmen; the drops found out; Rupert's secret; the shepherd's child |
| 50 | Kit kept on the poles; becoming her legs |

The journeys in `playtest/` earn these from milestones: the Rooms 2,150 (for the bard and the soldier alike), the
Road to Orm 1,800, the Old Town 1,500. Each reaches level 2. A Season that leaves Hessel alone earns nothing, and
one that only gossips earns 150. The fights (the footpads; Sir Gervase, who takes
intruders prisoner) give only the engine's ordinary experience for an encounter. Nobody in Hessel is worth more dead,
and nobody needs to die. The design has no enemy to kill: the only villain is a physician defeated by a dress code.

## Things

| Reward | When | What it does | What it implies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mrs Cherrett's five coin** | on taking her up as legs | coin | Hessel pays for errands. The chair up the Parade costs two coin, a bed one, the licence ten, Jem's answer two, Pocklington five, Number Four forty. The Season's economy is a constraint on the Old Town's paid ways, not on the Rooms' talked ones. |
| **Her thanks: twenty coin and Josiah Cherrett's pocket compass** | the fifth morning, if the company was her legs: from her own hand if she lives, by a codicil read by Mopsa if she does not | the compass grants **wayfinding** (`like old_glossary granting wayfinding`) | The Season's lasting gift is a capability. A navvy's compass that found the line of three canals helps the company find its way in the wider world. Inside Hessel it opens the sheep-path to Orm, which only a ranger had before. |
| **Kit Danby's chair-pole** | the fifth morning, on Stall Street, if Ismay and Kit are engaged or blessed | an ash pole that fights as a **quarterstaff** | Kit gives up carrying. The pole is the Old Town's thanks, and the only weapon Hessel gives. |
| **The surrogate's licence** | bought at Orm's gate for ten coin | lets Mr Pyne marry a couple before the Season ends | Treasure that is spent on someone else. Kit and Ismay, Rob and Celia, or the protagonist and Mopsa. |
| **Rob's poem, Ismay's note** | carried | given to Mrs Brannagh or Celia; to Kit | Things whose value is in where they are delivered. |

## People

| Companion | How | What they bring | What it implies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dr Jonas Ottery** | back from Orm, licensed or not, he is willing to join (hire 0) | a level-4 physician with compassion; a monk's training | the company leaves Hessel with a healer. If he went north, the Hospital gets Hartopp's man. |
| **Mopsa Gedge** | after her yes, if she has no house to keep (hire 0) | a sharp tongue, a book, and her own terms | the romance goes on the road; with Number Four, she stays and keeps lodgers, and the company has a door in Hessel that is always open |

## What the rewards change outside Hessel

- **Wayfinding** from the compass: the world's routes and wildernesses read differently for a company that carries it.
- **A companion** who is a physician, or a wife with a library.
- **Gold**: twenty coin, a modest purse for a level-1 company. Much of the Season's coin will have gone on chairs,
  beds and bribes.
- The region itself stays open after the book closes, so the company can come back to Hessel. The fates are told
  once and then the town is a town.
