# The Banns at Hessel — the revised design

*Stage 2's answer to [02-critique.md](02-critique.md). This is the design the implementation is held to; Stage 3
([04-adaptation.md](04-adaptation.md)) says how each part is said in Kyr, and what changed in saying it.*

## 1. Form

| | |
|---|---|
| Form | A comedy of manners about matchmaking, in four days and a morning, in a spa city at the end of its Season; with one gothic house in it, and one walk out of it |
| Register | Warm, quick, amorous, arch; tenderness; social fear; one night's dread |
| Narrator | Has opinions. Says what the Season thinks and then what it does. Allowed a joke's timing (set-up, then the line). Never explains the game. |
| Protagonist | A stranger, taken up as a dying woman's legs; a meddler; at fault for what they arrange |
| Central activity | Arranging: carrying, introducing, overhearing, telling, persuading, standing beside, opening doors |
| Minds change by | Being asked; being stood beside; being laughed with; being reminded who you were; a baby in your arms; a voice at the door |
| Clock | The Season's last four days and the morning of the fifth |
| Ends | In Hessel. An account of each strand, and a closing page for the morning the Season ends |

## 2. The calendar

Day 1 is the day the company arrives, whatever the world's calendar says; the Season is Hessel's own, and the text
never names a month.

| Day | Fixed | The round, every day |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The runaway chair on the Parade, late morning | 8 Pump Room · 10–12 the baths · 12–15 the walks (the Beacon, the Crescent) · 15 dinner · 19–23 the Rooms · 2 at night, the knock at Carth House |
| **2** | Dr Ottery due before the College at Orm at noon; the carrier back at dusk. Hetty's ultimatum to Rupert by evening | |
| **3** | Morning service at Saint Ebba's: the third reading of the banns. The chairmen decide on their poles | |
| **4** | The wedding at ten, if the banns stood. The **Closing Ball**, seven to eleven, and after | |
| **5** | The Season ends at noon: the coaches go; the book closes | |

## 3. The region

**Hessel** stands on one hillside, so that class is height. The places the company can stand:

- **The Downs** (top): the Orm road comes in over the down from the south; the Beacon walk; the Sham Castle.
- **Upper Hessel**: the top of the Parade; the Crescent; Carth House (its steps, area and coal-hole, hall, drawing
  room, the master's study, the stair); the Circus and the Cherretts' lodgings (parlour, Mrs Cherrett's room); the
  Assembly Rooms (porch, ballroom, card room, tea room); Mr Twiss's house.
- **The Baths**: the foot of the Parade; the Pump Room; the King's Bath gallery; the Cross Bath; Saint Ebba's (nave,
  vestry); Doctors' Walk (Dr Hartopp's consulting room); the apothecary's.
- **The Old Town**: the chairmen's stand; the Two Poles; the laundry yard; Hetty's shop; the Hospital of the Waters;
  Quay Lane (Mopsa's house to let); the bridge over the Lim.
- **The Road to Orm**: the stile on the down; the shepherd's hut; the gibbet at the crossways; the Orm gate.

## 4. The strands

Each is a small adventure with a person, a place or thing, something misunderstood, more than one way, a
complication, a consequence and a later effect. **R** marks the Rooms' way of doing it (above), **O** the Old Town's
(below), **P** a personal way that is neither.

### S1. The Runaway Chair (day 1)

Coming down the Parade, the company meets Mrs Cherrett's bath-chair loose on the hill, Kit Danby running behind it.

- **Catch it** (a strong or quick member): she is unhurt and delighted. **Steer it** into the railings (anyone, at a
  cost: a member hurt, the chair broken). **Fail**: she is thrown, bruised, bedbound for the first day; Mrs Cherrett
  blames Kit, and dismisses his chair.
- *Discovery*: Kit let go because Ismay came out on the Circus steps and he looked up.
- *Effect*: Mrs Cherrett takes the protagonist up as her legs. If Kit was dismissed, the Ismay strand starts
  harder, and the chairmen are cool to the company until it is put right.

### S2. Into the Rooms (days 1–4)

Mr Twiss has barred Mrs Cherrett for laughing aloud at the minuet. Without his leave none of her household is
admitted to the Closing Ball.

- **R**: make her apology to him in proper form, by a person of standing (Lady Wenlock, who needs the match; or the
  protagonist, if they have learned the form from Mopsa).
- **O**: the Rooms' first violin, Signor Brocca, is drunk at the Two Poles on the second evening; sober him and get
  him up the hill in a chair, and Twiss, saved a disgrace, is grateful.
- **P**: Mrs Cherrett will not apologise. Unless the protagonist tells her, plainly, that Ismay will never be at a
  ball otherwise.
- *Complication*: bribing Twiss's footman gets the household in, and Twiss, finding out at the door, turns them back
  in public. (A lesson about the Rules.)
- *Effect*: the Ball. Later, Twiss's one exception: the last dance after eleven.

### S3. Mrs Scrope (a tool, every morning)

At the Pump Room at eight, Mrs Scrope takes the waters and anything else. What the company tells her is all over
Hessel by dinner and comes back at the next morning's Pump Room as fact.

- Tell her Rupert has a wife and child: a public scandal on day 2 or 3; Lady Wenlock withdraws the match in fury
  before Sunday; Rupert is disgraced, Hetty shamed; Ismay freed the hard way.
- Tell her Ismay loves a chairman: Mrs Cherrett hears it from the city before she hears it from Ismay, and is
  humiliated; harder to move.
- Tell her Hartopp has doubled the drops: Hartopp's patients cool; he comes to the Circus in a fury.
- Tell her nothing, and she supplies her own: by day 3 she has the protagonist marrying Mrs Cherrett.

### S4. The Chairman and the Heiress (days 1–4)

Kit and Ismay each love the other; neither will speak.

- *Learning it*: Ismay watches for his chair from the window (seen, at the Circus, in the mornings); Kit's book in
  the chair has pressed in it the flower she dropped (looked at); the chairmen tease him (the Two Poles); Mopsa
  knows.
- **R**: tell Mrs Cherrett. She refuses, until reminded — by Mopsa's story or by the protagonist — that she married a
  navvy against her own father, and he never had a pole to his name. Then she laughs, and that is the end of
  Rupert.
- **O**: get them together without anyone's leave: carry a note (Ismay writes it only if asked in private); bring
  Kit to the Sham Castle at noon; Kit carries her chair to it.
- **P**: Ismay herself says it, if someone has stood beside her (S5, the third reading).
- *Complication*: the chairmen's grievance (S8) means Kit is on strike on day 3 and not at the stand.
- *Effect*: an engagement; a licence from Orm (S7) can make it a wedding before the Season ends.

### S5. Rupert's Secret and the Third Reading (days 1–4)

Rupert has let the banns be read twice; he has been married two years to Hetty Quiney, milliner, and they have a
son.

- *Learning it*: Rupert goes to Quay Lane at noon (followed); Hetty's shop has a man's hat on the peg with his
  initials (looked at); Rupert, drunk at the Two Poles on the second night, tells whoever sits with him; Hetty tells
  a customer she likes.
- **R** (private): go with Rupert to his mother. Lady Wenlock is given the baby; she becomes a grandmother in the space
  of a sentence, and the banns are withdrawn before Sunday. Mrs Cherrett hears it from Lady Wenlock, as one mother
  to another.
- **R** (public): tell Mrs Scrope (S3): scandal.
- **The third reading** (Sunday, eleven): Mr Pyne reads "If any of you know cause, or just impediment, why these two
  persons should not be joined together…" and pauses, as he always does, and dreads. Whoever speaks: Hetty (if she
  was brought); Rupert himself (if he has been given the courage); Ismay (if she has been stood beside); the
  protagonist; nobody.
- **O**: bring Hetty and the baby to the church.
- *Doing nothing*: Monday at ten, Rupert marries Ismay, and Hetty is at the door.
- *Effect*: whether Ismay is free, and how publicly; Lady Wenlock's temper at the Ball.

### S6. A Winter in Carth (days 1–4, nights)

The house at the end of the Crescent is shuttered and smoking.

- *Getting there*: Rob's poems. Rob has written eleven, and they are bad. Help him make a better one, from what he has
  seen of her (the window, the candle, the hand on the glass); deliver it (climb the area railings to the
  window; give it to Mrs Brannagh at the area door with a coin; or send it up with the coal).
- *Getting in*: by the front door, which nobody answers, unless the protagonist comes as a physician's messenger (with
  Ottery's or Hartopp's name); by the area door, with Mrs Brannagh's goodwill; by the coal-hole, which is locked and
  low; by the window, if Celia opens it.
- *The house*: the heat; the clocks at two; the furs; the candles; Celia; Mrs Brannagh; Sir Gervase in the study.
- *The knock*: at two in the morning, once. Watch from the Crescent and see a woman in a laundress's cloak; follow
  her to the laundry yard. Or sit up in the hall with Sir Gervase (if he will have you) and hear it from inside.
- *The laundress*: Nan Bellamy is Lady Carth. Learned by following her, by Mrs Brannagh's word if she trusts the
  company, or from the chairmen, who have seen her go up every night for years and never asked.
- *The decision*:
  - **Open the door** at two (Sir Gervase must be persuaded, or the protagonist does it past him): Lady Carth comes
    in, or does not. If Sir Gervase has been sat up with, he goes down on his knees; if not, he may draw.
  - **Take Celia out**: by the window or the area, to Rob, to the Ball. The winter goes on without her.
  - **Bring Nan by day**: to the front door, with the shutters open.
  - **Leave it**.
- *Danger*: Sir Gervase finds an intruder in his house at night: he is armed and not sane at that hour. Talk him down,
  fight him, or run.
- *Effect*: Celia at the Ball, or not; Rob's marriage; the house's shutters.

### S7. Dr Ottery and the Road to Orm (days 1–2)

Ottery has been turned off the Hospital and is summoned before the College at Orm at noon on day 2. He needs a
witness of good character to stand with him; nobody in Hessel will be seen to.

- *Learning it*: the Hospital, where twenty patients have no doctor and Ottery is packing; or Mrs Cherrett, who asks
  for him and is told by Hartopp that he has left.
- *The walk* (day 2, first light, three hours; an hour less by the sheep-path, for someone who can find it):
  - **The stile**: he asks whether the dying should be told. The protagonist answers (truth; comfort; it depends on
    whom). Remembered.
  - **The shepherd's hut**: a shepherd's child with a fever. Ottery treats her. The father asks if she will live.
    Ottery looks at the protagonist. What is said is said.
  - **The gibbet at the crossways**: he asks whether the protagonist has ever lied to someone for their good. Answer
    remembered.
  - **Orm gate**: he turns the protagonist's answers on them. Then: **go in with him** and stand witness (he keeps his
    licence, reprimanded); **let him go** (he walks north and is lost to Hessel); **bring him back** to Hessel
    unlicensed (he can see Mrs Cherrett, as a friend, not a physician).
- *Also at Orm*: the bishop's surrogate grants marriage licences, without banns, for a fee. Kit and Ismay can be
  married by licence on day 4 if someone fetched it.
- *Effect*: whether Ottery is in Hessel for the drops (S9); a companion who may go on with the company.

### S8. The Chairmen's Fare (days 1–3)

The Corporation has cut the chair fares for the Season's end. The chairmen, led by Big Lanyon, mean to down poles on
the day of the Ball, so that nobody of quality can get up the hill to the Rooms in their slippers.

- **R**: put it to Mr Twiss, whose Ball it ruins; he makes the Corporation restore the fare.
- **O**: stand with the chairmen; take a pole yourself on day 3 (a strong member) and carry for them, so Kit can stay
  at the stand.
- **P**: break the strike by carrying for the gentry yourself. The chairmen remember.
- *Effect*: the chairmen's goodwill, which is the Old Town way into everything: they watch Carth, refuse Hartopp,
  carry Hetty to church, carry Mrs Cherrett to the Ball.

### S9. The Hessel Drops (days 1–4)

Mrs Cherrett sleeps too much. She knows the drops are laudanum; she takes them for her nights. She does not know
Hartopp has doubled them since she changed her will to leave him a hundred pounds, or that he means to marry Ismay.

- *Learning it*: she is sharp on a morning she missed a dose (seen); Jem Mallender mixes them and can say the dose
  doubled (a friend of Mopsa's; or paid; or the chairmen's cousin); Ottery names them at a sniff; Mopsa suspects.
- **P**: someone she loves asks her to stop: Ismay, Mopsa, or Ottery. Showing her the bottle does nothing.
- **O**: change the drops for Pump water (the apothecary's after dark, or her own dressing-table); she sleeps worse,
  wakes sharper, and in two days is herself.
- **R**: disgrace Hartopp at the Ball (he dances three running with Ismay against the Rules, if provoked; Twiss bars
  him in front of the Season).
- *Doing nothing*: she does not wake on the morning after the Ball.
- *Effect*: Mrs Cherrett's last day; whether she dances; who is in her will.

### S10. Mopsa's Lease (days 1–4)

Mopsa wants the lease of No. 4 Quay Lane, a lodging-house: forty pounds down and a character from someone the agent
respects.

- **R**: get her a character from Twiss or Lady Wenlock.
- **O**: the agent is a chairman's brother; Lanyon can vouch.
- **P**: offer her the money. She will not take a gift; she will take a partnership, or a loan at interest.
- *The courtship*, if the player wishes: a set of shared acts (walking the Beacon with her; laughing at the
  warming-pan; going with her to see the house; dancing with her at the Ball — she is a companion and is not
  supposed to dance). It fails if the protagonist tries to rescue her, lies to her, or arranges her.
- *Effect*: Mopsa's house, Mopsa's marriage, both, or neither.

### S11. The Closing Ball (day 4 night)

Five dances: the minuet (Twiss opens it), three country dances, the last. Who the protagonist dances with is seen. In
it, if they have been set up: Celia comes in (the room goes quiet); Hartopp's third dance and Twiss's bar; Kit at the
porch with the chairs (chairmen may not come in; Ismay goes out); Lady Wenlock with a grandchild's ribbon in her
cap; Mrs Cherrett's last minuet after eleven, if Twiss bends the Rule.

## 5. The two principal approaches

| Problem | The Rooms (above) | The Old Town (below) |
|---|---|---|
| Ismay's banns | Rupert to his mother; or Mrs Scrope | Hetty to the church |
| Ismay and Kit | Mrs Cherrett's consent | a note, a meeting, a licence from Orm |
| Hartopp | barred by Twiss at the Ball | the drops changed; the chairmen won't carry him |
| Carth House | the front door, by day, with Nan | the area, the coal-hole, the window, at night |
| The Ball | Twiss's leave by apology | Twiss's leave by the drunk violinist |

The two must produce different worlds: the Rooms' way is public, quick and leaves somebody humiliated; the Old Town's
is private, slower, costs effort and coin, and leaves the Rooms not knowing.

## 6. Endings

The book closes at noon on day 5, when the coaches go. One closing page, chosen by the one thing the morning turns on:
whether Mrs Cherrett is alive; and within it, an account of each strand, told as the morning after:

- **The Morning After** (she lives).
- **The Season's End** (she died in the night, in her sleep).
- **Turned Away** (the company was barred from the Rooms and dismissed by her: it never got near).

Plus a fall-back for a company that walks away early.

## 7. Rewards

- Experience for what is resolved, by strand.
- Mrs Cherrett's thanks: coin, and, if she lives, a place in her heart and her will.
- **Dr Ottery** as a companion (a physician who goes on with the company), if he was brought back from Orm or kept
  his licence and owes the protagonist.
- **Mopsa** as a companion, if she chooses the road.
- Kit's pole (a chairman's ash pole, a fine quarterstaff), if he gives up the chairs.
- Small things: Twiss's ticket of the Rooms; Rob's poem; Ottery's case of instruments.
