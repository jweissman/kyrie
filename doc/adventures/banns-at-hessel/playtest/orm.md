*(The first page)*

The south road comes down out of the green country to a river, and on the far side of the river is Hessel, all of it at once, going up its hill: first the Old Town's roofs, wet and close, with steam coming out of them as if the whole place were a kettle; then a great dark church and a long pale pillared building in a haze of more steam; then terraces, honey-coloured, curving round the hill like the rings of a sawn log; and on the top of the hill, against the sky, a castle that is, when you look at it properly, only a wall with holes in it. The air smells of warm iron.

**To the foot of Lim Bridge**

The foot of Lim Bridge: fish-baskets, a tar-pot, washing strung between upper windows, and the river going by, brown and quick. A board on the toll-house says WELCOME TO HESSEL, THE QUEEN OF HEALING, and somebody has added in chalk AND OF HEALERS.

**To Quay Lane**

Quay Lane: narrow houses, a milliner’s with three bonnets in the window on wooden heads, a lodging-house with a card in its window, and at the end the steam of the laundries rolling across the lane like weather. Number Four is locked, with a card in the window: TO LET, A GOOD LODGING-HOUSE, APPLY MR GILMOUR, DAYTIMES.

**To Stall Street, by the chairmen's stand**

Stall Street: the chairmen’s stand, a long bench under a penthouse roof, and the chairs themselves, black, glossy, like great beetles with their poles laid by them; men in blue coats sitting on the bench with their arms folded, every one of them built like a door. Big Lanyon, the chairmen’s spokesman, a huge mild man with a voice like a bell under water, is here.

**Into the Hospital of the Waters**

The Hospital of the Waters: a long whitewashed ward, twenty beds, twenty patients, a smell of vinegar and the river. The poor of three counties come to Hessel for the waters, the same as the rich, and here is where they lie. A bench by the door has a doctor’s bag on it, packed. Dr Jonas Ottery, an untidy old physician in a coat that has been slept in, is here.

**Ask Dr Ottery why he is packing**

‘Because I’m turned off.’ Ottery stuffs a roll of lint into the bag as if it had insulted him. ‘Twenty years I’ve physicked the poor of three counties on this ward for nothing, and last month the governors turned me off for frightening the patients.’ He snorts. ‘Mrs Cherrett asked me, in the Pump Room, in front of everybody, was she dying. I said yes, madam, of dropsy, by the end of the Season, and you’d do well to stop drinking that muck. Dr Hartopp is a governor.’ He shuts the bag. ‘And now the College at Orm wants me tomorrow noon, for practising without its licence, which I never needed while I was the Hospital’s. I need a witness of good character. There’s not a soul in Hessel would be seen walking up the Orm road with me.’ He looks at you. ‘Seven o’clock, the Beacon. If anybody wanted to know.’

**Tell Dr Ottery you will be at the Beacon at seven, and walk to Orm with him**

Ottery looks at you for a long moment over his spectacles. ‘You,’ he says. ‘A stranger. Of good character.’ He considers it. ‘Well, nobody knows you’re not. Seven. Don’t be late. The College hangs about for nobody, and it’ll strike me off for the pleasure of not having to read my letters.’
