# The Calf in the Tail-Race: the story, before the code

*Pass 1. Fiction first; the Kyr that follows is a reading of it.*

## 1. The wheel that stopped

Tolt lies at the head of a sea-loch where the hills let go of the land. Its mill is a tide-mill: the sea comes in over a sill
into a pond, and goes out again through the wheel, and Agnel Thord has ground oats on that going-out for thirty-one years.
Eleven days ago the wheel stopped. Something is wedged in the tail-race under it.

"Don't tell me what it is," says Agnel, white to the elbows. "Everyone tells me what it is. I'd like to hear it from someone
who's looked."

It is a whale calf, grey and glossy as a wet slate roof, twenty feet of it jammed nose-up in a channel built for water.
It has an eye the colour of a plum. A girl of nine is sitting on its flipper reading it a list of vegetables.

"Parsnip," says Mabet, without turning round. "He's heard all the others. He hasn't heard parsnip."

## 2. The gauger

The Rì's oil-gauger comes to the Ladle at noon on the second day, with four boilers and a copper the size of a church font on
a cart. Waric Holle is thin, damp, and exact, and he is not a villain. A great fish in the Rì's water is the Rì's, to be
boiled for the lamps of the Star-Readers' towers, three barrels a calf, at dawn on the third day, before it spoils.

"I trimmed wicks in the lower tower of Dun Rìerdan for nine years," he says, putting his folding scales on the table, one pan
at a time. "I can tell you what a tower costs to keep lit. Every lamp in it is a small decision somebody made not to go
dark. I don't enjoy it. I would like it to be someone else's calf."

## 3. The man on the head

Every dusk Maric Selle rows to the bar-head with a ram's horn and a harpoon he has forged himself, and sounds the horn
across the loch mouth. Something out beyond the bar sounds back. It is long, low, and the windows in Tolt hum with it.

"That's her," says Maric. "She's been out there eleven nights. Sixty barrels, if she's a pint. She'll come in to the calf.
I'll be here."

Last March the *Good Intent* went up out of the water on a whale's back, and Joss Selle, his brother, went with her. Maric
knows it was not malice. He has known for a year. This does not help.

On the calf's left flank, if you look, is a rope-scar, and in the scar a splinter of tarred oak with a brass nail through it.

## 4. Ulkin's geese

Hobin Ulkin keeps the salt-meadow below the mill-pond, a hundred geese, and a theory that the sea is polite. If the sill is
drawn at the top of the spring tide the sea will come over his hay. He knows the loch burns on dark nights; he has seen it
since he was six. "Put your oar in," he says, "when nobody's looking at you."

## 5. The tide

The third night. The sill is drawn with the mill's own mallet, the hand that lent it knowing exactly what it will cost.
The sea comes over the sill. The race fills; the calf shifts, and lifts, and lows, and the stones in the mill ring.
Out on the bar-head a man who has carried a horn for a year raises it, and does not sound the kill.
She answers. The whole loch, stirred by two backs, lights from beneath, green as the inside of a wave, and Tolt stands on the
hard with its lamps out and does not say a word.

Or the boilers' copper is lit at dawn and the Ladle smells for a month of something sweet and terrible, and the loch is shut
by an old whale who will not leave the bar. Or a calf swims out alone. Or a harpoon.

## What I want the player to carry away

A calf on a mill-wheel is funny until you have fed it. Everyone with a claim on it is right about something. The best
ending is not the only one a decent person can reach, and a decent person can reach all of them.
