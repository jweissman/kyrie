# Left Alone

Played by Tamsin, a bard; seed `sennet`; 0 steps given, 171 taken; ended at watch 1014, at the ending "The Second Whelming". Written by `bun scripts/the-grand-tour-transcripts.ts` from the journey as the page told it.

*(The first page)*

The coast road comes over the last of the downs, and there below you, all at once, is the sea, and a city going down to it in glass. Above the city, over the bay, the cloud lies on the water like a fleece; and standing up out of the cloud, in sunlight, is a hill. It has houses on it, and gardens hanging over its edges, and a chain coming down out of the bottom of it to the city like a plumb-line, as if somebody had lifted the hill up out of the bay by it and forgotten to put it back. Under the bay, where the hill came out, there is nothing to see at all. A milestone at the roadside says SENNET 1, and under it someone has scratched: AND THEN UP, OR DOWN.

**Wait › An hour**

The bells of the House of Doors ring eight across the bay, and the gulls go up off the Quay all at once, and over all of it, over the cloud, a hill of houses catches the sun.

*(17 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

An hour passes. It is a long time since you slept. You are weary, and will fight the worse for it until you sleep.

*(3 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Sennet wakes to the winch-gang’s horn at the foot of the Ascent and the smell of the glass-houses lighting their furnaces from the Fire Door.

*(23 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

It is the day Cloudgate lets Perrin Halyard’s coffin down off the Balustrade. All morning the Quay looks up.

*(23 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Ascension Day. Cloudgate has hung out its bunting; you can see it from the Quay, very small, in the sun above the cloud. On the Quay the harbour is buying candles.

*(5 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At noon a long horn blows from the winch-house at the foot of the Ascent, and goes on blowing, and then stops; and the chain, which has hummed all your time in Sennet, is silent. The winch-gang has sat down.

*(5 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

At six in the evening the Halyards’ steward comes down the chain on a bosun’s chair, which nobody has seen a steward do before, with a purse. Cloudgate has run out of clean shirts for the Lanterns. The horn blows again, short, and the chain starts to hum.

*(11 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

The morning after the Lanterns. Paper lanterns that went up from Cloudgate last night are coming down all over Sennet, burnt out, on roofs and in gutters and on the water.

*(23 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

A grey morning, with the wind off the sea. The fishing boats stay in.

*(23 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

The seventh day of the Tour since you came. In the Glass Market a man is selling a print of the Door of Doors, opened, with a Rector going through it in a halo.

*(16 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

All over Sennet the windows rattle, and the dogs begin. Over the bay, in the sun above the cloud, Cloudgate has begun, very slowly, to tilt.

**Wait › An hour**

Cloudgate comes down into the bay. It comes down slowly, slower than you would believe, into the hole it came out of two hundred years ago; and the sea goes up round it in a wall, white, higher than the Quay, and comes in over the Quay and up the Haul and into the Glass Market to the knees, and goes out again, taking a great deal with it. When it has gone, Halyard Hill is standing in the bay where it always stood, wet to the first floor, with its gardens dripping, a little island, a hundred yards off the end of the Fish Stairs.

*(8 waits in which nothing is told)*

**Wait › An hour**

Of the Rector: she went through the Door of Doors with the four petals of the Rose set in it, alone, and has not come back. Mr. Tench has written in his ledger: MAGISTRA I. QUANCE. OUT. Of the Halyards: the Dowager was carried off the hill on a door by four of her own footmen, upright, saying nothing; Aubrey Halyard was found in the wine cellar of Halyard House, up to his knees in the sea, holding a bottle, laughing. Of Thursday, the House’s cat: Mr. Tench saw him go through the cat-flap of the tower door yesterday, as he always does, and come out an hour later with sand in his fur from a beach nobody in Sennet has ever seen. The first through the Door of Doors in two hundred years, Mr. Tench says, may not have been a person. Of the Air petal: it is in the Rose, in the boss of the Door of Doors, with the other three, and the North Wind has not been told.

**Wait › An hour**

In the morning Halyard Hill is standing in the bay where it stood two hundred years ago, wet to its first floors, its gardens hanging off it in ropes of weed, a hundred yards off the end of the Fish Stairs; and under it is the Whelm. Chandlers’ Row, and Lir’s church with its bell, and the Guildhall with the arms of the Glovers’ Company, and the merfolk who lived in them, who had never in their lives been told that anything was coming. Some got through the Water Door. Not many. All morning things come up on the tide at the Fish Stairs: a teapot, a drowned armchair, a basket of cockles. The harbour stands on the Quay and watches the water and says nothing, not even the thing it says when the sea is up. Cloudgate’s people come across on the fishermen’s boats, in their pale clothes, wet, and stand on the Quay among them, and for once nobody in Sennet can tell which is which.
