# Found Out

Played by Ama, a cleric (an acolyte); seed `sennet`; 16 steps given, 187 taken; ended at watch 1014, at the ending "Found Out". Written by `bun scripts/the-grand-tour-transcripts.ts` from the journey as the page told it.

*(The first page)*

The coast road comes over the last of the downs, and there below you, all at once, is the sea, and a city going down to it in glass. Above the city, over the bay, the cloud lies on the water like a fleece; and standing up out of the cloud, in sunlight, is a hill. It has houses on it, and gardens hanging over its edges, and a chain coming down out of the bottom of it to the city like a plumb-line, as if somebody had lifted the hill up out of the bay by it and forgotten to put it back. Under the bay, where the hill came out, there is nothing to see at all. A milestone at the roadside says SENNET 1, and under it someone has scratched: AND THEN UP, OR DOWN.

**>sennet__landward_gate**

You set out from the top of the coast road early, with your shadow long in front of you on the road. The bells of the House of Doors ring eight across the bay, and the gulls go up off the Quay all at once, and over all of it, over the cloud, a hill of houses catches the sun. The Landward Gate is a gate in no wall: the wall came down a century ago and the gate was kept because the city liked it. Beyond it Sennet goes down to the water in roofs of green copper and glass, glass everywhere, glass-house chimneys smoking, and the bay beyond, very blue. Under everything, so low you feel it in your feet before you hear it, the city hums: that is the chain.

**>sennet__glass_market**

The Glass Market: a square of stalls under awnings, and everything on them glass. Glass floats for nets, glass eyes for dolls, glass bottles with the light of the Fire Door still moving in them, sold as night-lights. A crier walks the square with a board on a pole. The crier’s board says, in red: THE GRAND TOUR IS PROCLAIMED. Underneath, smaller, in black: PERRIN HALYARD, DOORWARD, FELL 12 DAYS SINCE. LET DOWN ON THE 3RD DAY AT NOON. Someone has chalked beneath both of them: MIND YOUR HEADS.

**>sennet__ascent_yard**

The anchor chain comes down out of the cloud into the middle of the yard, each link the size of a door, and goes into the ground through an iron collar on a granite block the size of a house, and the whole yard hums with it, very low, the way a church does after the organ has stopped. Beside it the winch-house, all doors and chimneys, smoking. Looking straight up the chain you lose it in the cloud a long way up, and above the cloud, very small, very sunlit, the underside of Cloudgate.

**>sennet__winch_floor**

The winch-house: four capstans as big as merry-go-rounds, each with fifteen bars and fifteen people on them, walking round and round in a thunder of boots and chain, singing. In the middle of the floor the basket waits on its hook, a wicker thing the size of a cart with a bench in it and a rail round it, and a slate hanging off the rail: UP 5 COIN. DOWN FREE. NO DOGS. Mother Kittiwake, the master of the winch-gang, with a voice like a foghorn, is here.

**Talk to Mother Kittiwake**

A woman built like a capstan herself, in a leather apron with a bosun’s whistle on a chain, looks you up and down. ‘UP OR DOWN?’ she says, at a volume meant for the far side of the bay, and then, at a normal one, seeing you flinch: ‘Sorry, love. Twenty years in here. Up’s five. Down’s free. Nobody’s ever been charged for coming down to earth.’

**Ask Mother Kittiwake how the gang gets on with Cloudgate**

‘Cloudgate pays by the basket. The Halyards pay, being the first family, and the rest pay the Halyards.’ She spits, accurately, into a bucket. ‘Since the boy fell, the Dowager’s docked us. A third. Mourning economies, says her steward. They’ve stopped the laundry going up twice a week and the coal going up and our pay going down, and the wine still goes up every morning, I notice.’ She folds her arms. ‘Ascension Day’s the fourth day. They’ll want every basket running for their lanterns. If we’ve not had our third by noon on Ascension Day, the gang sits down.’

**Put your shoulder to a capstan bar for a shift**

Mother Kittiwake looks at you as if you had offered to marry her, and then puts you on number three capstan between a boy of fourteen and a woman of seventy who walks you off your feet. You walk round. You walk round. The chain goes up and comes down; baskets of Tourists go up, baskets of laundry come down; the gang sings, the same song, which is about a girl in Cloudgate who dropped her garter and what became of the man it hit. At the end of the shift your legs have stopped belonging to you, and Mother Kittiwake slaps you on the back hard enough to loosen a tooth. ‘You’ll go up free,’ she says. ‘Whenever you like. You’ve earned it the hard way, which is the only way up that’s honest.’ Ama gains 25 experience: walked a shift on the winch-gang’s capstans.

**Ask Mother Kittiwake who came down the night Perrin Halyard fell**

She stops the capstan with a blast of the whistle and comes close, and for once her voice is not much more than an ordinary voice. ‘The Rector,’ she says. ‘Magistra Quance, in her gown, with her chain on. She went up on the seven o’clock with all the House for the Halyards’ do, and the House came down at nine, all of them, except her. She came down on the eleven o’clock. Alone. That was after the bells, and the shouting up there, and the fog.’ She looks at her own hands. ‘She gave me a crown. A crown, for the basket, that’s free going down. And her hand was shaking so, she dropped it, and I picked it up for her.’ She straightens. ‘I put it down to the height. Everybody shakes, coming down off that rock after a death.’ A pause. ‘I’d not say it to anybody that hadn’t walked my bars.’

**>sennet__quay**

The Quay: granite, wet, smelling of weed and tar and frying. Boats tied two deep. Eel barrows, lobster pots, a gull standing on a bollard with the air of having been appointed to it. Looking up from here, Cloudgate is directly overhead, and its underside is rock, brown and hanging, like the bottom of a turnip pulled out of the ground. No ferry runs between the Quay and Halyard Hill while the hill is in the sky.

**>sennet__taproom**

The Sounding Line is low, and dark, and full of fishermen. A sounding-lead the size of a child’s head hangs over the bar on its line, and every so often somebody pats it for luck without looking. The window looks straight out across the bay at nothing but water and, above the water, the underside of Cloudgate. In the corner of the Sounding Line, with a pint he has not touched, Mr. Tolley is writing in a notebook that is not his shorthand pad, fast, with his tongue between his teeth, and covering the page with his arm whenever anyone goes by. Old Bethan’s eyes are red at the rims, and twice while you watch she starts to nod over the eels and jerks awake. Here are Peg Arkle, the landlady of the Sounding Line; Mr. Josiah Pumphrey, the Harbourmaster of Sennet, with a brass telescope and the Bylaws under his arm; Prue Quickly, a glass-blower, with forearms like a stevedore’s and no eyebrows; and Mr. Edwin Tolley, the Rector’s amanuensis, a young man with a shorthand pad and no chin.

**Ask Mr. Tolley what he is writing, here in the corner**

He covers the page with both arms, and then, slowly, uncovers it. ‘A novel.’ He says it as if it were a disease. ‘A romance. Of the sea. About a lighthouse keeper’s daughter who.’ He stops. ‘It doesn’t matter.’ He turns his pint round. ‘The Rector says I’ve no ear. She takes the bits she likes for Thresholds. That line over the gate of the House, everyone thinks is hers: Every door is a promise to someone on the other side of it. She had it off me in March. It was the first line of my chapter four.’ He drinks, finally. ‘She says the House’s sentences are the House’s.’

**Tell Mr. Tolley to put his own name to his own novel, and take it to the Courant himself**

He looks at you as if you had suggested he take it to the moon. ‘Under my name.’ He looks at the notebook. ‘Edwin Tolley. A Romance of the Sea.’ He says it again, quietly, trying it. ‘She’d sack me.’ He thinks about that for longer than you expect, and then he laughs, out loud, for the first time since you’ve known him. ‘She would. She’d sack me. And then I’d have to.’ He closes the notebook. ‘The Courant’s on Fish Street. They open at nine.’ Ama gains 50 experience: gave Edwin Tolley his own sentences back.

**Take a bed and supper**

Sennet wakes to the winch-gang’s horn at the foot of the Ascent and the smell of the glass-houses lighting their furnaces from the Fire Door. You eat what is put in front of you, and sleep long, under a roof.

**>sennet__lamp_street**

Lamp Street climbs toward the headland between glass-houses, every one of them with its doors open and its furnace roaring, so that walking up it is like walking past a row of opened ovens. Over each furnace a copper pipe comes down out of the street’s great main, which runs up the hill on iron legs to the House of Doors, and hums. Between the glass-houses, two shops: MURREY’S, under a sign of a gilt key, with a window full of the same red book; and PARGETER’S, OUTFITTERS TO THE GRAND TOUR, with a dummy in the window wearing a fire-cloak, a brine-glass helm and an expression of great confidence. Sergeant Bassett, a sergeant of the Watch, in a leather hat, is here.

**>sennet__college_gate**

The House of Doors stands on the headland over the bay, a long grey building with a short grey tower, much less grand than you expected, with lichen on it and students sitting on the wall eating pies. Over the gate, in old letters, a line cut deep: EVERY DOOR IS A PROMISE TO SOMEONE ON THE OTHER SIDE OF IT.

**>sennet__quad**

The Quad: grass worn to earth along the paths, four doors in four walls, and students crossing it in gowns of four colours, red, brown, blue and white, arguing. A board by the Hall’s doors says THE GRAND TOUR: TOURISTS TO THE RECTOR’S DOOR, and somebody has added in chalk, AND GOD HELP YOU.

**>sennet__rector_study**

The Rector’s study: books to the ceiling and on the floor and on the chairs, a desk the size of a boat, and on the desk among the books a case of black wood lined with velvet with four empty hollows in it, each the shape of a petal. Through the window, the bay, and Cloudgate. Here are Magistra Isadora Quance, the Rector of the House of Doors, in a gown of four colours and a chain of keys; and Mr. Edwin Tolley, the Rector’s amanuensis, a young man with a shorthand pad and no chin.

**Ask Mr. Tolley what the Rector has dictated about the night Perrin Halyard fell**

He looks round, though there is nobody to see. ‘She’s done it four times.’ He gets out his pad and turns back through it. ‘Chapter Thirty-One, The Halyards’ Reception. The first time, the morning after: I came down on the eleven o’clock basket, shaken by the height, and could not sleep. The second time she struck out shaken by the height. The third time it was the ten o’clock. The fourth time, and that’s the one going to the Courant, it was the nine o’clock, with the rest of the House, and she was in her bed by ten.’ He shuts the pad. ‘I don’t know what it means. I know she doesn’t change things. She never changes anything. She says it’s vulgar to revise.’ You put it together: the Rector came down alone on the eleven o’clock basket after the fall, shaking; and she has dictated that night four times, until she came down at nine and was in bed by ten.

**Lay before Sergeant Bassett what the winch-gang, the footman and the amanuensis know about the night Perrin Halyard fell**

The sergeant hears it all without a word, writing in his notebook on the crown of his hat. When you have finished he reads it back to you, slowly, with his finger under the line, and asks you twice whether that is what was said, and twice you tell him it is. Then he puts the notebook away. ‘I’ve known the winch-gang thirty years,’ he says, ‘and the Halyards’ people, and most of the House. They’re not all honest. But they’re frightened, and frightened people are mostly telling the truth.’ He puts his hat on. ‘I’m going up the headland,’ says Sergeant Bassett, ‘to have a word.’

**Wait › An hour**

Sergeant Bassett of the Watch walks up the Headland Steps with his hat in his hand, and into the House of Doors, and is in the Rector’s study for a quarter of an hour; and, in the hour after, the whole of Lamp Street sees him come down again with the Rector of the House of Doors walking beside him in her gown of four colours, her chain of keys in his other hand, talking to him pleasantly about the weather in Cloudgate. Ama gains 150 experience: put the Rector of the House of Doors before the Watch.

**Wait › An hour**

Of the Rector: she is in the Watch-house on Fish Street, and the Courant has stopped printing Thresholds in the middle of Chapter Thirty-One. Of Mr. Tolley: the Courant on Fish Street has bought the first three chapters of A Romance of the Sea, by Edwin Tolley, for four coin, and asked for more.

**Wait › An hour**

The Rector of the House of Doors is in the Watch-house on Fish Street, in a cell with a window on the bay, dictating to nobody. Sergeant Bassett brings her tea. She is perfectly charming to him. The Door of Doors stands in its round room at the top of the tower, shut, with the cat-flap of the tower door swinging, and the House has no Rector, and the Grand Tour is suspended, pending. Nobody has opened anything. Nobody has fallen out of the sky. The courts keep their petals, or you do, or a hill does. In Sennet they say it is the strangest Tour there has ever been, because nobody won it, and everybody seems to have come home.
