# The Grand Tour: the story

*Synopsis, acts, characters, conflicts, and the eleven ends. Spoilers throughout.*

## Synopsis

Sennet is a port in three storeys. On the bay: quays, eel-wives, glass-houses fed with heat from the Fire Door, and on
the headland the House of Doors, a shabby college whose doors open on the elemental planes. Over the cloud-line,
**Cloudgate**: the top of the old city, lifted into the sky two hundred years ago by Ysabeau Halyard,
where it is always a fine afternoon and the servants never see the view. Under the bay, **the Whelm**: the lower town,
drowned when the hill went up, its lamps still lit, where the merfolk came through the Water Door two lifetimes later
and live in the shops.

Twelve days before the company comes down the coast road, the Rector of the House of Doors, Magistra Isadora Quance,
proclaimed the first Grand Tour in two hundred years: whoever brings her the four petals of the Rose of the Winds,
each asked of its plane's court in its own fashion, will be first through the Door of Doors, which opens on anywhere.
The same night Perrin Halyard, her best student and the Dowager's younger son, went over the Balustrade of Cloudgate
in the fog. His coffin, empty, goes off the rail at noon on the third day.

What the city does not know, and the company can learn: Perrin was pushed, by the Rector, because he had found out
that the fourth petal, the Air, is not in any court. It is set in the keel of Cloudgate, a love-gift from the North
Wind to Ysabeau, and it is what holds the hill up. Taken out, the hill comes down where it came from, on the Whelm.
Perrin was going to give it back. The Rector means to take it, and go first through the Door, and does not much mind
what is under the hill when it lands. Mercy, a merfolk girl, caught Perrin in the water; he is alive, in an air-bell,
with a broken leg, reading drowned Aenean books, and would like to stay dead, because if he is alive somebody pushed
him and he believes it was his brother.

## The acts

The design was revised (E-series, `01-editorial.md`) into four acts over seven days; play does not keep to them,
which is the point of a city.

- **I. Arrival** (day 1): the coast road; the crier's board; the House, the Rector, the fee or her commission; the
  Handbook and its author's grandson; the Third Step. A knowing player can be in the Fire by ten and the Whelm by
  supper.
- **II. The Tour** (days 1–4): the Fire, the Earth and the Whelm, in any order, against the rivals' schedule:
  Florian Mottram, a Tourist from Cloudgate, and his bear-leader Miss Sone, who will be crowned in the Hour Court at noon
  on the second day and dig in the Earth on the fourth, and lay what they have on the Rector's desk at five.
- **III. The fall** (in parallel, days 1–4): the three witnesses and their wants; Aubrey on the sofa; the funeral at
  noon on the third day, where the Rector's voice does not shake once (an insightful company sees her hand on the
  rail); the Night of Lanterns on the fourth, with Mercy in a water-butt on the Promenade.
- **IV. The keel and the Door** (whenever three petals are in one pair of hands): the keel, the tower, the Rose or
  the engine; or the seventh night, when the Rector, if she has three, goes down the keel ladder herself.

## The conflicts

- **The Tour against the Rector's plot.** The Tour is real and the Rector means it; she also means to be second
  through, and if the company is slow, first. Every petal the company brings her is a petal toward the keel.
- **The city against its storeys.** Cloudgate's comfort is the Whelm's drowning; the Fish Stairs' brass plate and the
  winch-gang's docked pay are the same arrangement seen from below. The satire is that nobody in Sennet is wicked
  except the Rector, and the arrangement does the rest.
- **Truth against kindness.** Perrin's secret is a promise the company makes; the Lanterns break it; the sylphs carry
  everything; telling the truth endangers the man it would clear.
- **Gift against lever.** The Air petal was given for love and used to lift a family out of the damp. Every ending
  answers what it is now: returned, renewed, replaced, taken, or bought silence.

## The eleven ends

The settlements are reached by what was done, not chosen. Each is told at nine on the closing morning, after the
news, which tells the fates of some forty people and things by what is true (`aftermath.kyr`).

| Ending | How | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **A Present from Sennet** | Prue's made petal set in the keel, the true Air petal taken, the Rose made | Cloudgate held up by Lamp Street glass stamped A PRESENT FROM SENNET, and in trade |
| **The Keepsake** | The Dowager, sorry, to the North Wind with the company | Snow in June; the Dowager on the Quay with a candle she cannot light, and Bethan lights it |
| **The Letter** | Ysabeau's letter stolen from the escritoire and given to the North Wind | Snow in June; an empty box and a green ribbon; the sylphs telling everyone, and nobody believing them |
| **The Undertow** | The tide-engine, by the Whelm's leave | The Door opened from below; the Rose never made; the Tour declared won by a House that doesn't know what else to declare |
| **The Coming Down** | The keel taken, the Whelm warned | Halyard Hill an island; the merfolk gone home through the Water Door; a penny ferry; the Halyards cheated on the Quay like anybody |
| **The Second Whelming** | The keel taken (by anyone), the Whelm not warned | The hill in the bay on top of the Whelm; things coming up on the tide all morning; nobody can tell harbour from Cloudgate |
| **First Through** | The company stands aside at the keel and goes with the Rector | First through, as promised; the Door knows you; there are a great many places you would rather not see again |
| **The Rector's Door** | She is given four petals, or gets them, and goes through first | A nervous new Rector; *Thresholds* ends at the door; some say it opens on everywhere, which you cannot come back from |
| **Found Out** | Two witnesses to Bassett, or Perrin face to face, or the Promenade | The Rector in the Watch-house, charming; nobody opened anything; everybody seems to have come home |
| **Kept Above** | The Dowager's hundred coin taken to keep the secret, nothing opened | Received at Halyard House whenever you go up; you find that you do not go up |
| **The Tour Unfinished** | Nothing decisive by the eighth morning | Sennet does not wait to see. It has eels to sell |

The treatment's seven settlements became eleven in play. The Letter was added for a thief's approach to the Air
(the burglar's version of the Keepsake). First Through was split from the Second Whelming so that an accomplice is told
as one. The design's "Rose made from three petals and the North Wind's leave" was not built: in the Keepsake and the
Letter the Air petal stays in the keel, and the Door stays shut; the Tour is unwon and the city is mended instead. That
reads better than it plans, and is recorded in `11-changes.md`.
