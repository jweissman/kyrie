# The Grand Tour: the small adventures

*Every thread in Sennet, what it is made of, the ways through it, and what it changes later. Spoilers throughout.*

The commission's test for a small adventure was: a person with a motive; a place or an object; an uncertainty; more
than one way through; a complication; a resolution with consequences; and later effects. Each entry below gives
those, and the file where it lives. Seven of the twenty threads touch nothing cosmic and change only a person; they
are marked **local**.

## The local stories

**1. The Bell** (`harbour.kyr`) — **local**. *Old Bethan*, an eel-wife on the Quay, has not slept since her husband
Ned went down last winter, because twice a day, at the turn of the tide, a bell rings under the bay. *Uncertainty:*
who rings it, and why it rings at all. It is Ha'penny and the merfolk children, swinging on the rope of the drowned
church for the joy of the noise. *Ways through:* ask Ha'penny to stop (the children lose their best game); ask her to
ring it once, at the evening tide, for Ned, and then not again; tie up the clapper yourself in the church (breath,
and the children's anger); or, for a cleric or anyone of a faith background, say the words for the drowned with
Bethan at the edge of the Quay (`bethan_rite`). *Complication:* the words cut on the drowned church door (`mercy_meaning`)
are the same words. *Later:* on the Night of Lanterns she sets one candle on the water for Ned and goes home to bed;
in the Keepsake she lights the Dowager's candle without a word; the news tells which way it went.

**2. The Third Step** (`harbour.kyr`, `lamp.kyr`) — **local**, and a hinge. *Ha'penny*, a merfolk child, wants to buy
Mercy a present in the Glass Market with her own pearl. *Object:* the brass plate on the third step, BY ORDER: NO
PERSON OF THE SEA ABOVE THIS STEP. MDCIX. *Mr. Pumphrey*, the Harbourmaster, will fine anyone who brings her up five
coin. *Ways through:* pay; pay in advance as a "deposit" (he finds a form for it); argue in front of the Quay that a
child shopping is not a person of the sea (persuasion, an even chance); have Old Bethan tell him, loud enough for the Quay,
about his own grandmother, who sold cockles to the sea-folk from the top step and let him pull their hair; or simply lift her into a fish-basket and carry her.
*Resolution:* Prue gives her a mirror for a ha'penny and a glass fish for change. *Later:* Ha'penny gives the company
breath and asks it to supper, which is the kindest way into the Whelm; No Admittance hears that a dry one asked a dry
one for one of hers, and it moves her toward the Water petal; the news reports No Admittance and Mr. Pumphrey meeting at
the third step.

**3. Prue's petals** (`lamp.kyr`, `collegium.kyr`). *Prue Quickly*, a glass-blower, sells red glass petals at a coin
each, stamped A PRESENT FROM SENNET on the back. Florian Mottram laid one on the Rector's desk as the true Fire petal,
and the Bursar has charged her with forgery: on the third day at noon the House takes back her heat off the main.
*Ways through:* ask the Rector (who thinks it the best joke of the year, and wants a paperweight: you now owe her);
have Florian confess to Sergeant Bassett it was his own mistake; or let it go. *Complication:* the furnace is the
only one in the world that can make a petal. *Later:* saved, she can make the Made Petal; not saved, her furnace is
cold for the rest of the week, and the best ending is shut.

**4. Jem's place** (`cloudgate.kyr`, `harbour.kyr`, `lamp.kyr`) — **local**, and a witness. *Jem Tuttle*, the
Halyards' footman, came up at fourteen from Fish Street, is terrified of heights, and has not been let down in eleven
years. *Ways through:* find him a place below, as potman at the Sounding Line (Peg Arkle) or hand at Prue's furnace;
or take him by the collar and frighten it out of him (intimidation). *Resolution:* given a place, he takes off the
green coat, folds it on a gilt chair, and goes down on the four o'clock with his eyes shut, and tells you on the way
what he saw at the Balustrade (`bishop_sleeves`). *Later:* he is a potman; Halyard House's door is unkept.

**5. The Capstan** (`ascent.kyr`). *Mother Kittiwake* and the winch-gang have had their pay docked for the Halyards'
mourning. On Ascension Day at noon they sit down, and the chain stops, with all Cloudgate's guests for the Lanterns
below. *Ways through:* pay the gang's third yourself (twenty coin); do a shift on number three capstan (three hours;
no skill, no charm: it makes you a friend of the gang and you go up free for ever); or wait. *Resolution:* at six the
Halyards' steward comes down on a bosun's chair with a purse, because Cloudgate has run out of clean shirts. *Later:*
a friend of the gang hears from Kittiwake who came down alone on the eleven o'clock basket the night Perrin fell
(`late_basket`), and can borrow the winch-house fire-butt for a mermaid.

**6. Tolley's line** (`collegium.kyr`) — **local**, and a witness. *Edwin Tolley*, the Rector's amanuensis, wrote
the line cut over the House's gate, EVERY DOOR IS A PROMISE TO SOMEONE ON THE OTHER SIDE OF IT; she had it off him
for her memoirs. In the evenings he writes a romance in the Sounding Line. *Ways through:* ask the Rector to put his
name to the line (she does, charmingly, and he never forgives you for asking her); or tell him to put his own name
to his own novel and take it to the Courant. *Later:* either way he stops passing everything he hears to the Rector,
and he tells you she has rewritten the chapter about that night four times (`chapter_rewritten`); the Courant buys
the first three chapters of *A Romance of the Sea*.

**7. The ember in Cabinet Fourteen** (`collegium.kyr`, `fire.kyr`, `rivals.kyr`) — **local**. In the Museum of the
Tour, an ember brought back by Florian's great-grandfather is still alive after ninety years, and asks to go home.
*Dr. Cole*, the curator, would let it go if he could; *the Rector* will not ("if the Museum gives back one thing, every
court of every plane will want its things back"). *Ways through:* persuade Cole; tell Florian, who can claim it as
family property and carry it home; pick the cabinet (lockwork) or break the glass (a crowbar). *Resolution:* taken
home to the Hour Court, the ember is the newest thing the court has ever seen, being ninety years old, and is crowned,
and reigns. *Later:* it gives the company the Fire petal, and the news tells how it rules.

**8. Fabian's Handbook** (`murrey.kyr`, `handbook.kyr`, `lanterns.kyr`). *Fabian Murrey*, whose grandmother wrote
the Handbook and told him at twelve he had the soul of an index, has never been through a door. *Ways through:* go
through one yourself and come back and tell him the truth; promise to bring him home (`fabian_home`); defend him to
Florian, who mocks him in his own shop on the first afternoon. *Complication:* he is a level-2 commoner; he can die in
the Fire, in a fight, on the landing. *Resolution:* he writes the seventh edition as you go, one line per place, in his
own words (the page reads them out), and on the Night of Lanterns, if he is fond enough, dances. *Later:* home, he
takes his grandmother's portrait down and hangs it lower; told what he is to you, the seventh edition has a dedication;
lost, the shop stays shut. The collection's one romance for the protagonist (E10).

**9. Chert** (`earth.kyr`). The youngest delegate of the Slow Assembly, six hundred and forty, wants to see the sky.
*Ways through:* take him. *Complication:* he will fetch the petal out of the floor in the middle of a motion if asked
("It's only a motion"). *Later:* on your boot in the Quad he sees the sky and says nothing for an hour; he opens the
tower door through the cat-flap; at the end he is "on your boot, looking at a gull".

**10. Aubrey** (`cloudgate.kyr`, `whelm.kyr`). *Aubrey Halyard* believes he pushed his brother; he remembers the rail,
the fog, and nothing. *Ways through:* drink with him and listen; tell him a man who meant to say sorry is not a man who
pushes his brother; bring him a letter from Perrin; bring him down to the air-bell. *Later:* he gives you the cellar
key and the keel ladder; the brothers meet; or he is still on the sofa in the news.

## The planes, each played by its own rule (E4)

**11. The Hour Court** (`fire.kyr`). The Fire crowns the newest thing in the court every hour. *Ways:* show it a
hand-mirror (it has never seen itself still), a geode (a million years old), a jar of the North Wind (cold), the
Handbook (old; Fabian has to let it go), sing something new (persuasion), perform (a stage background); win the Lists
against a salamander (a fight); snatch the petal (stealth, hard, and you burn); bring the ember home. Crowned, you
decree: the petal goes home with you; the court cools; a holiday. *Complication:* Florian Mottram is crowned at noon
on day 2 with a block of ice, unless the court has seen something newer; reigns reset hourly.

**12. The Slow Assembly** (`earth.kyr`). The Earth decides by procedure, at the speed of stone. *Ways:* sit through two
days of the second reading as petitioner (no skill, two days); move the question (lore, or a learned background);
dig the petal out (digging, or an earth background); ask Chert. *Complication:* Florian digs on day 4 at ten, and is
two words from being granted it.

**13. No Admittance** (`whelm.kyr`). The Water keeps. The matriarch, called No Admittance after the sign over the
Guildhall's back door, which the merfolk took for a name, keeps the green petal in her crown. *Ways:* eat what you are
given; tell her what her name means in Sennet (she laughs until the fish leave by the windows); tell her the
Harbourmaster let Ha'penny up the stairs; ask again. *Complication:* the House's divers, ordered on day 4, raid the
Whelm on day 6 at five unless she is warned or you are there.

**14. The Gallery of Winds** (`air.kyr`). The Air trades in talk: the North Wind receives only the most talked-of.
*Ways:* tell the sylphs a true thing about yourself; that Perrin is alive; that the Rector pushed him; that the petal
is in the keel; break the Halyards' seal; do something in Cloudgate. *Complication:* everything told is down in
Cloudgate within the hour and in the House by the next: telling the sylphs Perrin is alive tells the Rector.

**15. Tramontane** (`air.kyr`) — the second romance, two hundred and thirty years old. The North Wind gave the court's
petal to a red-haired girl who came up through the arch on a dare. *Ways:* take him his petal back (and the hill comes
down), bring Ysabeau's last letter (two lines, never sent), or bring a Halyard to say sorry. *Later:* it snows on
Sennet in June.

## The large story

**16. Perrin's fall** (`whelm.kyr`, `fall.kyr`). Perrin Halyard, the Rector's best student, went over the Balustrade
in the fog on the night of the proclamation. He did not die: Mercy caught him. He was pushed; he heard keys. *The
uncertainty* is distributed among three witnesses, each with their own want (4, 5, 6), and Perrin's own testimony.
Any two witnesses make the case (`quance_pushed`). *Ways to resolve:* lay it before Sergeant Bassett; say it aloud on
the Promenade on the Night of Lanterns (with Perrin there, or two witnesses: else Cloudgate laughs); bring Perrin up
to face her; tell the sylphs.

**17. Mercy and Perrin** (`whelm.kyr`, `lanterns.kyr`) — the third romance, and a comic one: a merfolk girl who
caught a man out of the sky and keeps him in an air-bell, and a dead man who would like to stay dead. *Ways:* take
Mercy up the chain in a water-butt to see the lanterns go up alive (Peg's rain-butt or the winch-house fire-butt);
set Perrin's leg (mending, or a healing background); take his letters up. *Complication:* Mercy will tell the Dowager,
in front of all Cloudgate, and the Rector will hear, and go for her cloak.

**18. The keel** (`door.kyr`, `rector.kyr`). The Air petal holds Cloudgate up. Taken, the hill comes down in an hour
on the Whelm. *Ways:* take it (and warn the Whelm, or not); swap it for Prue's made petal; leave it, and let the gift
be renewed; or stand at the keel on the seventh night when the Rector comes for it, and go with her, or refuse her.

**19. The Undertow** (`undertow.kyr`). Under the Guildhall, the Aenean tide-engine opens the Door of Doors from below
without the Rose, and empties every air-bell for a day. *Ways:* with No Admittance's leave and the whole Whelm on the
wheel (move Perrin first); alone, by lockwork, mending, or a mechanical background, without leave (and the Whelm is
shut to you after).

**20. The Rector** (`collegium.kyr`, `rector.kyr`). The antagonist is a person with a plan, a schedule, and a
weakness for being first. She enters Tourists; she lends helms; she wants Perrin's notebook (whose last pages are
dated after he fell); she buys petals; she sends divers; she hunts Perrin at two in the morning after she hears; she
goes to the keel on the seventh night; given the four petals she climbs the tower at once.

```
 Fabian, Chert ──companions; the cat-flap; the Earth petal ─┤
 Aubrey ──key──► the keel ──► take / swap / leave ──────────┤
 Mercy ──the water-butt──► the Lanterns ──► the Dowager kneels ──► the North Wind ──► the Keepsake
 Perrin ──the engine──► No Admittance's leave ──► the Undertow
 The Rector ──hears──► the air-bell at two; ──three petals──► the keel on the seventh night
```

The design rule from the editorial (E1) held: no witness is an exhibit. Every one of the three who make the case
against the Rector is a small story about that person first, and speaks only once that story is served.
