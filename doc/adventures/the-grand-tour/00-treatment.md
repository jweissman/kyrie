# The Grand Tour: the treatment

*Stage 1, written before looking at what the engine can say. Ambitious on purpose; Stage 2
([01-editorial.md](01-editorial.md)) cuts it.*

## The pitch, in a paragraph

Sennet is a port city in three storeys. On the bay stands the city proper, with its quays and glass-houses and, on
the headland, the Collegium of Veyra, which everyone calls **the House of Doors**: four doors in its Hall open on the
four elemental planes, and the trade that comes through them is why Sennet is rich. Above the city, over the
cloud-line, floats **Cloudgate**, the hill the founding families lifted into the sky two hundred years ago, where it is
always a fine afternoon. Under the bay lies **the Whelm**, the lower town the sea poured into when the hill went up,
where a colony of merfolk now lives among the drowned shop-signs, and, deeper, older ruins whose Aenean machinery
still turns. The Rector of the House of Doors has proclaimed the first **Grand Tour** in two hundred years: whoever
brings her the four petals of the **Rose of the Winds** from the elemental planes will be the first through the
**Door of Doors**, the Nexus at the top of her tower, which opens on anywhere. Twelve days ago, the night she proclaimed
it, a young Doorward of the House, Perrin Halyard, went over the balustrade of Cloudgate in the fog. They buried an
empty coffin. He is not dead. He is under the sea, in love.

## The kind of thing it is

A **romance** in the old and the new senses: an adventure-romance (doors, planes, a floating city, a drowned one), and
a love story, several of them. A **comedy of manners** with **satire** in it: of tourism and collecting, of a
hereditary aristocracy that is literally above everyone, of an academy that measures knowledge by what it brings home.
And a **murder mystery** with a human culprit who chose to do harm, and a victim who is alive and does not want to be
found.

Its register should be the one the collection has least of: bright, quick, sensuous and funny; joy rather than
dryness; desire; terror in places; tenderness between equals. Its counter-voice is a guidebook: *Murrey's Handbook
for Travellers through the Doors*, sixth edition, forty years out of date, confident, snobbish and often wrong, whose
author's grandson is too frightened to go through a door himself.

Its subject is **what a guest takes from the places they visit**. Souvenirs, petals, lovers, a hill. The goddess of the
House of Doors is Veyra, Keeper of the Returning Path, whose observance is to share food, remember absent travellers,
and promise a path home. Every resolution of the work is an answer to what should be given back.

## The city in three storeys

### Sennet, on the bay

A city of glass and salt. Its trades: glass-blowing (the Fire door's heat, sold by the hour to the glass-houses of
Lamp Street), salt and pearls (traded with the merfolk at the Fish Stairs), wind (Cloudgate's mills, sold down the
Ascent), stone and gems (through the Earth door). Its people: harbour folk descended from the survivors of the
Whelming, who still put candles on the water on the night the hill went up; craftsmen; students; and Tourists, the
young rich of Cloudgate and of every country round, who come to Sennet to go through the doors with a bear-leader and a
guidebook and bring back things to put in cabinets.

Districts:

- **The Fish Stairs and the Quay.** Where the merfolk come up to trade, to the third step and no further, by the
  Harbour Bylaw of 1109. The eel market. The Harbourmaster's office. *The Sounding Line*, the harbour tavern, where the
  bell under the water is heard at the turn of the tide.
- **Lamp Street.** The glass-houses. Nance Quickly, who blows "genuine petals of the Rose" for tourists at a shilling
  each. Murrey's, the guidebook shop, *established in the year of the Lifting*. Outfitters: fire-cloaks, brine-glass
  helmets, wind-jars, "a Tourist's complete necessaire, eleven guineas".
- **The Ascent.** The winch-house at the foot of the great chain, worked by Mother Gannet's gang, sixty men and women
  on four capstans. A basket up costs five coin, unless you go up at dawn with the laundry.
- **The House of Doors.** The Gate; the Quad, where students argue across the faculties of the four doors; the Hall of
  Doors, where the Fire and Earth doors stand (the Air door is in Cloudgate, the Water door in the Whelm, which is why
  Sennet was built here); the Museum of the Tour, cabinets of two hundred years of things brought back; the Library;
  the Chapel of Veyra; the Rector's Lodging; and up the tower, the Nexus, a round room with a door in it that has not
  been opened since the Treaty of the Four Doors.

### Cloudgate, above

Above the cloud-line, so always in sun. A hill of the old city, with its great houses on it, lifted into the sky on the
night of the Lifting by Ysolde Halyard, who had been given something by someone in the Plane of Air. It moors over the
bay at the top of the chain. It has a Promenade along its rim and a balustrade along the Promenade. Its people say
"down there" of Sennet the way other people say "in trade". Ascension Day, the anniversary of the Lifting, is their
great day; the Ascension Ball is on its night. The harbour calls the same night the Whelming.

- **The Landing Stage**, where the basket comes up through the cloud like a bucket out of milk.
- **The Promenade and the Balustrade**, where Perrin fell.
- **Halyard House**: the drawing room of the Dowager Lady Imogen Halyard; the rooms of her elder son Rafe; the long
  gallery of portraits of Halyards looking down on things.
- **The Ascension Chapel**, where the empty coffin lies in state.
- **The Wind Court**, the Air door, kept by the Halyards since the Lifting.
- **The Keel**: under Cloudgate, the hanging rock of the hill's underside, where nobody goes. Reached by a ladder down
  through the wine cellars of Halyard House. In the keel, set in the rock, is the thing Ysolde was given.

### The Whelm, below

The lower town of old Sennet, two hundred years under the bay: streets, shopfronts, a church with its bell, a
guildhall, all in green water, lit by Aenean lamps the merfolk found working and use. The merfolk (undines; they came
through the Water door, which is down here, three generations ago) live in the drowned houses and take their names
from the signs over them, which they think are old words of power: the matriarch is **No Admittance**, a child is
**Twopence**, and the girl who caught a man falling out of the sky is called **Mercy**, from MERCY ON US cut into the
church door by someone on the night the water came.

Under the drowned town, older: the **Aenean Undercroft**, ruins of the city before Sennet, whose machinery still turns:
air-bells that hold a room of breathable air under the sea, lamps that burn on nothing, and a great tide-engine that
the merfolk have never dared start. Perrin Halyard lives in one of the air-bells, with a broken leg mending, a stack of
drowned books drying, and Mercy.

### The planes

The Grand Tour goes through the doors.

- **Fire: the Hour Court.** In the Plane of Fire nothing lasts more than an hour, so the court of the ifrit values only
  what is new. A thing seen twice is ash. They crown a new Monarch every hour, of whatever is newest in the court; the
  Fire petal is held by whoever is Monarch. Satire of fashion and celebrity.
- **Earth: the Slow Assembly.** The earth-folk deliberate at the pace of stone. The Earth petal lies in the floor of
  their chamber under a motion "that the Assembly consider whether to consider the return of the petal to the House of
  Doors", tabled in the year of the Lifting; the second reading is under way, at a word a year. The youngest delegate,
  a pebble called Chert, is six hundred and forty and cannot bear it. Satire of committees.
- **Air: the Gallery of Winds.** In the Plane of Air every word said is carried everywhere at once; nothing is secret,
  and nothing is believed. The sylph court will tell you anything. What it tells you is that its petal was given away
  two hundred and thirty years ago by Prince Tramontane, the north wind, to a mortal woman he loved, called Ysolde
  Halyard, who used it to lift a hill. Satire of gossip and reputation.
- **Water**: the Water petal is not in the Plane of Water. The Water court gave it to the merfolk to keep when they
  came through. No Admittance wears it.

## The people

- **Magistra Honoria Quance**, Rector of the House of Doors, sixty-one. Brilliant, generous, vain, very funny, and a
  murderer. She is dictating her memoirs (*Thresholds*, in instalments) and means their last chapter to be her walking
  first through the Door of Doors. Perrin, her favourite student, found what is in Cloudgate's keel and told her, and
  told her he would tell the Air court so that it could have its petal back; and that would have put the fourth petal
  where no Rector could reach it. She followed him to the balustrade.
- **Perrin Halyard**, twenty-six, younger son of Cloudgate's first family, Doorward of the House. Pushed, in the fog,
  by hands he did not see. He believes it was his brother, with whom he had quarrelled ten minutes before. He would
  rather stay dead than accuse him. He is happy for the first time in his life.
- **Mercy**, a merfolk girl of the Whelm, who caught him. Curious about everything dry. She wants to see the sun on
  Cloudgate, which she has seen only as a light on the surface. He wants to stay under; she wants to go up.
- **No Admittance**, the merfolk matriarch: grand, grumpy, the keeper of who comes and goes in the Whelm, and of the
  Water petal, which she wears. Disapproves of dry men.
- **The Dowager Lady Imogen Halyard**, Perrin's mother, who keeps the family's secret (she knows what is in the keel)
  and who is grieving. Every guest at the funeral thinks she has a motive.
- **Rafe Halyard**, thirty-one, the heir: drinks, gambles, quarrelled with his brother, cannot remember the end of the
  night, and has begun to think he may have done it.
- **Ambrose Murrey**, thirty-four, grandson of Dame Philippa Murrey of the Handbook, who has written four hundred pages
  of the seventh edition from the sixth and has never been through a door. Frightened, funny, warm, possibly in love
  with whoever takes him.
- **The Honourable Florian Mottram**, nineteen, a rival Tourist from Cloudgate, an idiot of great sweetness, and his
  bear-leader **Miss Agatha Sone**, a retired sergeant of fifty who has done the Tour eleven times with eleven idiots and
  would like to do something else before she dies.
- **Mother Gannet**, master of the winch-gang. **Old Bethan** of the eel market. **Nance Quickly**, glass-blower and
  forger. **Mr. Tolley**, the Rector's amanuensis. **Dr. Rook**, curator of the Museum. **The Harbourmaster**, Mr.
  Pumphrey. **Thursday**, the House's cat, who goes through doors. **Prince Tramontane** of the Air. **Chert** of the
  Assembly. **Whoever is Monarch of Fire this hour.**

## The small adventures

Each should have a person, a place or thing, an uncertainty, more than one approach, a turn, a resolution, and an
after.

1. **The Bear-Leader.** Florian Mottram's family wants a second bear-leader to help Miss Sone. Taking the job puts the
   protagonist inside the rivals' party; refusing makes them rivals.
2. **The Seventh Edition.** Ambrose needs someone to check his entries against the planes. His grandmother's book is
   wrong in ways that are funny, and in one way that is dangerous (it says the keel of Cloudgate is "solid rock and of
   no interest").
3. **The Ember in the Cabinet.** In the Museum of the Tour, in a glass case, an ember that has burned for eighty years.
   It is a child of the Hour Court, taken as a souvenir. Dr. Rook knows; the Rector forbids its return ("the collection
   is not a lending library"). Steal it, buy it, argue it out, or take it home through the door.
4. **Fake Petals.** Nance Quickly sells glass petals to tourists. One customer has been caught trying to pass one off
   at the House. Nance is in trouble with the Watch; she is also the only person in Sennet who could make a petal good
   enough to fool anyone.
5. **The Third Step.** Merfolk may not come above the third step of the Fish Stairs. Twopence, a merfolk child, wants
   to see the eel market. The Harbourmaster will fine anyone who helps.
6. **The Bell Under the Water.** At the turn of the tide a bell rings under the bay. The harbour says it is the drowned
   ringing for help; the merfolk ring it because they like the noise. Old Bethan wants it to stop.
7. **The Laundry Basket.** How to get up to Cloudgate without five coin: up with the laundry at dawn, up the chain by
   hand (a climber), up by the winch-gang's favour, up on a forged invitation, up in a Halyard's company.
8. **The Empty Coffin.** The lying-in-state in the Ascension Chapel. Who comes, who weeps, who does not.
9. **The Brother.** Rafe drinking at the card-tables, sure he killed his brother.
10. **The Footman.** Pollard, who saw a gown at the balustrade, and will say so to nobody who matters.
11. **The Winch Log.** Mother Gannet remembers who came down late that night, and who tipped too well.
12. **The Amanuensis.** Mr. Tolley has written down everything the Rector has dictated since that night, and has
   noticed that the chapter about the reception has been rewritten four times.
13. **Mercy's Question.** What does MERCY ON US mean? Who will tell her?
14. **The Admittance's Table.** To be admitted to the Whelm you must eat with No Admittance, and the Whelm eats
   things a dry stomach does not like.
15. **The Air-Bell.** Finding Perrin. Not frightening him. What to tell him.
16. **The Hour Court.** Be the newest thing in Fire for an hour, and be crowned.
17. **The Slow Assembly.** Wait two hundred years, or take Chert.
18. **The Gallery of Winds.** Be talked of enough to be heard. Learn whose the fourth petal was.
19. **The Ascension Ball.** Dance; be seen; be talked of; slip away to the keel; be followed.
20. **The Keel.** Down the ladder below the wine cellar, out onto the underside of a hill in the sky.
21. **The Student Lovers.** Minna (Air faculty) and Dunstan (Fire faculty) want to elope through a door, and have
   chosen the wrong one.
22. **Thursday.** The cat comes back from the doors with things in its mouth.

## The large story

**Act I, the city and the proclamation.** The protagonist arrives in Sennet with the Tour proclaimed and the
funeral in Cloudgate. The House of Doors admits Tourists; the Rector receives them herself, charmingly. The small
adventures of the city.

**Act II, the Tour.** Fire, Earth, the Whelm; the rivals; the Air court's revelation. The protagonist learns where the
fourth petal is: in the keel of Cloudgate.

**Act III, the fall.** Investigating Perrin's fall: the empty coffin, the brother, the footman, the winch log, the
amanuensis. Finding him alive. Learning who pushed him.

**Act IV, Ascension Day.** The ball; the keel; the Rector's move. She has three petals (from her rivals, or yours); she
goes down to the keel herself.

**Act V, the Door of Doors.** What is done with the Rose, with Cloudgate, with the Rector, with Perrin.

## The two ways to the Door of Doors

1. **The Grand Tour.** The petals gathered by each court's leave or by craft; the Rose assembled; the door opened
   from the Nexus.
2. **The Undertow.** The Aenean tide-engine under the Whelm started, which opens the Nexus from beneath without the
   Rose, up the Aenean shaft that the House of Doors was built over. It needs the merfolk's leave (starting it puts out
   their lamps and empties their air-bells for a day), and someone who can start a machine nobody has started in a
   thousand years.

## How it can end

- **The Rose Made**: the petals assembled and the Door of Doors opened; the fourth petal from the keel; Cloudgate
  comes down.
- **The Coming Down**: the Air petal returned to Tramontane; Cloudgate sinks through the cloud and settles in the bay
  where it came from, on top of the Whelm unless the merfolk are warned and gone.
- **The Gift Renewed**: Tramontane, given an apology from a Halyard and told that Ysolde loved him, lets Cloudgate keep
  it.
- **The Made Petal**: Nance makes a petal out of a jar of the Plane of Air's own wind, and it goes into the keel; the
  real one goes home; Cloudgate stays up, on a thing made down in Lamp Street, and has to pay for its upkeep.
- **The Rector's Door**: Quance through the Door of Doors and not come back.
- **The Second Whelming**: left alone. The rivals bring the Rector three petals; she goes down the keel ladder on
  Ascension night and takes the fourth; Cloudgate drops into its hole on top of the merfolk.

## Rewards

- The Door of Doors: from the Nexus, a door to anywhere the protagonist has been.
- An hour as Monarch of Fire, and whatever the Monarch may take away.
- Chert, as a companion.
- The breath of the Whelm.
- A Chair at the House of Doors.
- The seventh edition of the Handbook.
