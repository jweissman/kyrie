# The Grand Tour: a severe reading, and the design as revised

*Stage 2. The treatment ([00-treatment.md](00-treatment.md)) read as an editor would read it, against the brief and
against the collection's own survey ([repertoire-2026-10-26.md](../repertoire-2026-10-26.md)). Each finding is
numbered (E1…) so that later documents can say what became of it.*

## What is right, and should be kept

- **The three storeys are a social fact and a physical one.** The rich live above the clouds on a hill lifted out of
  the city, and the poor quarter drowned in the hole it left. The satire is built into the geography, so nobody has to
  state it. The plot (the theft that holds the hill up) grows out of the same fact.
- **The victim is alive.** That turns a whodunnit into a romance, and the question changes from *who did it* to *will
  he come back, and what will it cost him*. The change of interpretation is in the middle of the work, where it can be
  played with, not at the end.
- **The subject is the guest's ethic: what a visitor takes and what should be given back.** It is not another misread
  inheritance. Nobody in Sennet misunderstands the Rose, the keel or the doors. The Dowager knows what holds Cloudgate
  up. The merfolk know what their machines are. The Rector knows exactly what she is doing. The work is about wanting
  and taking, not about finding out.
- **The register.** Bright, sensuous, funny; joy, desire, hilarity and some terror. The collection is short of all of
  them.

## The findings

**E1. The city is not yet alive apart from its mystery.** Of the twenty-two small adventures, twelve exist to deliver a
clue or a petal. *The Footman*, *The Winch Log* and *The Amanuensis* are each "talk to a witness, learn a fact": three
exhibits wearing coats. *Revision:* every witness gets a want of their own, and the clue comes as a by-product of
serving it. Pollard the footman wants to leave Cloudgate, where he is afraid of the edge every day of his life, and he
speaks once he has a place below. Mother Gannet remembers only for someone who has put a shoulder to her capstan, in
a week when the Halyards have docked the winchers' pay for mourning. Mr. Tolley wants credit for the sentences the
Rector takes from him. Each of them is a small story about that person, and the clue is what they let slip after.

**E2. There are not enough stories that stay small.** *Revision:* at least six threads touch nothing cosmic and change
only a person:

- *The Bell*: Old Bethan, whose husband went down last winter, cannot sleep for the drowned bell.
- *The Third Step*: a merfolk child who wants to buy a present.
- *Nance's petals*: a glass-blower with the Watch at her door.
- *Pollard's place*: a footman afraid of heights.
- *The Capstan*: a strike.
- *Florian's cabinet*: a silly boy who has never seen a real thing.

Two of them turn out to bear on the large story, Nance and Pollard. That is allowed, and it is better than
manufacturing their relevance.

**E3. The comedy is texture, not structure.** The merfolk names are a good joke, and a joke is not a structure.
*Revision:* one comic set piece is built out of compounding complications. The lovers want opposite things: Perrin
wants to stay under the sea, and Mercy wants to see the sun on Cloudgate. Reconciling them means getting a mermaid
up the Ascent and onto the Promenade on the night of the Ascension Ball, in a water-butt in the laundry basket. Nobody
in Cloudgate has seen one. Everybody will be talking of it by morning, the sylphs included. The Dowager comes face to
face with the girl who saved her son. And the Rector learns, from the talk, that someone has been in the Whelm. Each
complication is a consequence of the one before. It is funny, and it moves the plot.

**E4. The satire must be a game, not a description.** Each plane's society has to be played by its own rule:

- **Fire**: *novelty is currency.* The Hour Court crowns whoever shows it the newest thing, every hour. The
  protagonist's curiosities (a pearl, a fish in a jar, a book forty years old, a glass petal "A PRESENT FROM SENNET")
  are each shown once and are then ash to the court. The thing the court has never seen in its existence is something
  *old*: the sixth edition of the Handbook, forty years old, is the most astonishing object ever carried through the
  Fire door. It burns in an hour, which Ambrose has to choose to let happen. Crowned, the protagonist reigns until the
  next hour, and may do as a Monarch does, which includes giving the Fire petal away to themselves.
- **Earth**: *patience is currency.* The Assembly's reading advances a word for every day the company sits through it.
  The reading needs three words more, so the company can sit three days, while the rivals race. Or it can find the
  youngest delegate, who cannot bear it, or dig, or argue procedure.
- **Air**: *being talked of is currency.* The Gallery of Winds receives the most talked-of person in it, and every word
  said there is heard everywhere within the hour. To be heard by Tramontane the company must have done something worth
  talking of, or say something worth repeating. A secret said in the Gallery is down in Cloudgate before the company
  is, and that includes the secret that Perrin Halyard is alive.
- **The Whelm**: *hospitality is currency.* To be admitted, the company eats at No Admittance's table. The Whelm eats
  raw things in brine; a weak stomach shows, and that is remarked on. It brings a gift, and it names things truly.

**E5. The two approaches to the Door of Doors did not yet differ in what they cost.** The Grand Tour and the Undertow
both reached the Nexus, but neither changed the strategic picture, which made the second a mere alternative.
*Revision:* the routes differ in whom they cost.

- **The Grand Tour** needs the fourth petal from the keel of Cloudgate, so it forces the question of Cloudgate's fall.
  It is diplomatic, planar, social and slow, and it is open to anyone who can win four courts in four different ways.
- **The Undertow** opens the Nexus from below without the Rose, so the Rector's whole purpose (to be first) is lost,
  and Cloudgate need never come down. It costs the Whelm instead: for the day the tide-engine runs, the lamps go out and
  the air-bells empty, Perrin's among them, so he must be moved. It needs the merfolk's leave, which is earned only by
  someone they trust. It is mechanical, aquatic and intimate.

So the player who takes the Tour is the one who will have to decide about the keel. The one who takes the Undertow has
to answer to the merfolk.

**E6. The Aenean machine at the bottom is the escalation reflex.** The repertoire names it: every work digs down to an
ancient engine. *Revision, partial, because the brief asks for Aenean ruins with working machinery:* the Aenean things
are lived in and domestic. The merfolk use the lamps for light, the air-bells as guest rooms and a hot vent as a
kitchen. They know what the tide-engine is; their grandmothers simply never needed it. Nothing Aenean is misunderstood,
and nothing Aenean is the answer: starting the engine is a question of the merfolk's consent and someone's skill, and
it costs them. The work's climax is in the city, among people: at the Ball, at the balustrade, in the Nexus. It never
goes further down than the Undercroft, nor further out than the three planes. It names no Kiritarun and no Varakiiri.

**E7. The murder is too easy to solve.** Players who know the genre suspect the charming patron. *Revision:* three
suspects with real motives, and a victim whose own belief is wrong.

- **The Dowager** keeps the family's secret, the theft that holds Cloudgate up. Perrin was going to give it away, and
  she was at the balustrade that night. She wears the key of the Wind Court on a chatelaine.
- **Rafe** quarrelled with his brother ten minutes before the fall. He was drunk and remembers nothing after, and he
  has come to believe he did it.
- **The Rector** wears the House's ring of door-keys on her chain of office. Perrin had told her about the keel.

What Perrin remembers is hands in the fog and *keys*. That points at his mother or the Rector, and he will not believe
it of his mother. So he tells himself it was Rafe, whom he can forgive, and stays dead rather than accuse him. The
truth comes from people:

- the winch log in Mother Gannet's memory (the Rector came down on the eleven o'clock basket, not the nine, and tipped
  a crown with a shaking hand);
- Pollard's "sleeves like a bishop's";
- Tolley's four rewritings of one chapter.

It is not one exhibit. It is three people, each of whom has to be served first.

**E8. Minds must change for reasons other than evidence.** Every persuasion in the design is listed here, with what
moves it. Being shown something is allowed in at most a third of them.

| Who | Moved by |
|---|---|
| Rafe, believing himself a murderer | His brother alive, and his brother's word that he forgives him (people) |
| Perrin, staying dead | Rafe's grief, carried to him; or Mercy's wish to see the sun (people); the truth about the keys (evidence) |
| No Admittance, refusing dry men | A meal eaten at her table; being made to laugh (what her name means); a gift (hospitality) |
| Mercy, wanting to go up | Nothing: she goes up, if someone takes her |
| Tramontane, wanting his petal back | An apology from a Halyard; Ysolde's last letter, which says she loved him (love); or being out-talked (reputation) |
| The Dowager, keeping the secret | Her son alive; meeting Mercy; the harbour's candles on Whelming night (shame, gratitude) |
| Mother Gannet | A shift at the capstan (labour) |
| Pollard | A place below (service) |
| Tolley | Credit (vanity, justice) |
| Florian | Seeing one real thing (wonder); Miss Sone's resignation (loss) |
| Miss Sone | Being asked her opinion (respect) |
| Chert | Being taken along (adventure) |
| The Hour Court | Novelty |
| The Rector | Very little. Perrin alive, before witnesses, takes her composure; Tolley's defection takes her story; she is not converted |

**E9. The protagonist must want something, and be able to do harm.** *Revision:* the Grand Tour is a contest, and the
prize is real: to be first through the Door of Doors in two hundred years, and a door from the Nexus to anywhere one
has been. The protagonist can want it, compete for it, and beat the rivals to it. The protagonist can also do harm
through the obvious honest act. The Rector is their employer, and she asks them, kindly, to look in the Whelm for
"poor Perrin's effects". Tell her he is alive and she goes down that night with a brine-glass helmet to finish it. If
nobody gets there first, Perrin dies, and the death is the protagonist's doing. The design signals this (she asks
twice, and she asks who else knows), but does not prevent it.

**E10. A romance for the protagonist.** The collection has none. *Revision:* Ambrose Murrey is the companion. He is
frightened, funny and warm, and he has never been anywhere. His fondness for the protagonist can grow by what they do
together. It grows when he is defended rather than mocked, when the hour in Fire is spent letting his grandmother's
book burn, and when there is a dance at the Ball. It is chaste and optional, and it is a relation between two people
in front of the player, not a reward. At the end he writes the seventh edition, and what it says about the protagonist
depends on what they were to him.

**E11. Too much lore.** The treatment carries the Treaty of the Four Doors, the Lifting, the Aeneans, the Water
court's gift and the history of the Air petal. *Revision:* one sentence each, said by someone with an interest in
saying it. The Rose is "the key of the Door of Doors, broken in four and given to the four courts so that nobody could
open it alone": that is the Rector's proclamation, and it is all anyone needs. The Lifting and the Whelming are two
names for one night, said by two sides of the city. Ysolde's love story is told by the Gallery of Winds, which tells
everything. The guidebook carries the rest as comedy, wrong.

**E12. Cut.** *The Student Lovers* (a third romance, too thin to earn its place) and Rafe's card debts. Thursday the
cat stays, but small: a cat that goes through doors, and comes back from the Nexus. The Nexus has a cat-flap, and that
is all Thursday is for.

**E13. The left-alone outcome must come from people, not a calendar.** *Revision:*

- The rivals take the Fire petal on the third day (Miss Sone is competent) and the Earth petal on the sixth (Florian
  digs, being incapable of patience).
- The Rector takes the Water petal on the eighth, with hired divers and harpoons, from merfolk who do not expect
  violence.
- She goes down the keel on the ninth night.

Cloudgate falls into its hole on the Whelm. Every step is someone's own act, and each can be prevented: the rivals
befriended or beaten, the merfolk warned, the Rector exposed. The Ascension Ball is fixed (the fifth night), since
festivals are, but nothing depends on its date except the stunt and the dance.

**E14. A narrator that is not the house's.** *Revision:* the work's prose is second person and present tense, as the
game requires, but quicker, fonder and more ornamented than the collection's. It tolerates an adjective. Where the
company carries the sixth edition, the Handbook speaks at about twenty places, in its own voice: confident, snobbish,
forty years out of date. Where Ambrose has written the seventh, the page quotes him instead. The two voices are the
book's argument about what a traveller owes the place they visit.

**E15. Tone check against the collection's habits.**

| Habit | Kept off |
|---|---|
| A ledger as the decisive exhibit | There is no ledger. The winch log is in Mother Gannet's head |
| A vote, council or hearing | The Earth Assembly is a satire of one, and nobody wins by its vote except by sitting through it |
| A grey clerk who cannot lie | Tolley lies constantly, for his employer, and stops |
| A creditor beneath the city | Nobody owes anybody anything below |
| An old woman with the key | No Admittance is old and has the petal, and she is funny, and she has a temper. Kept, knowingly |
| Ending on a custom | At most one ending closes in the iterative present |

## The design as revised

**Sennet**, a port city in three storeys, nine days.

- **Act I, arrival** (days 1–2): the city, the proclamation, the funeral, the Tour entered.
- **Act II, the Tour** (days 2–8): Fire, Earth and the Whelm, in any order, against the rivals' race.
- **Act III, the fall** (in parallel): the witnesses, the brother, the air-bell. The Ball is on the fifth night.
- **Act IV, the keel and the Door**: when the company or the Rector holds three petals.

**Two approaches** to the Door of Doors: the Grand Tour (the Rose) and the Undertow (the tide-engine).

**Settlements**, the ending reached by what was done:

1. **The Rose Made.** Four petals assembled; Cloudgate comes down, the Whelm warned or not.
2. **The Gift Renewed.** Tramontane lets Cloudgate keep the petal. The Rose is made from three petals and his leave.
3. **The Made Petal.** Nance's petal in the keel, the true one returned, Cloudgate up on the labour of Lamp Street.
4. **The Undertow.** The Nexus opened from below, the Rose never made, Cloudgate untouched, the Rector forestalled.
5. **The Second Whelming.** Left alone, or the Rector served.
6. **The Rector's Door.** Exposed at the last, she goes through the Door of Doors and is not seen again.
7. **Kept Above.** The company takes the Dowager's money and keeps her secret; nothing is opened; Perrin stays drowned.

Each settlement is crossed with the fates of Perrin, the Rector and Ambrose, which are told in the epilogue by what is
true.

**Builds**: every petal has at least one approach that needs no charm and no learning. The Tour and the Undertow each
have at least one step only a skill opens, and at least one anyone can take.
