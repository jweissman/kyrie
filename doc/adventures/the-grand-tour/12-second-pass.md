# The Grand Tour: the second pass

*Read again as literature, against the hardest standard I could hold it to: what it is about, whether it means it,
where it is only exuberant, and whether a player would feel the shape of it. Then what was done, what is still not
right, and where ten times the ambition would begin. Spoilers throughout.*

## The diagnosis

### Is there depth?

There is a spine, and in the first build it was stated more often than it was dramatised. The spine is a single
question asked at three heights: **what is a gift once it has been used as a lever?** Ysabeau's keepsake lifted a
family out of the damp and drowned the street below; the brass plate on the third step is the same arrangement
written in law; the winch-gang's docked pay is the same arrangement written in wages. Around it are three themes
the work had found without knowing it had:

- **Keeping.** No Admittance keeps the green petal for a court that never came to see; the Museum keeps an ember
  that wants to go home; the Halyards keep a secret; Old Bethan keeps her grief; the House keeps Tolley's sentences.
  The best of the city's people keep things. The worst of it has kept them so long it has forgotten what they were
  for.
- **Being let up.** Who is allowed up the chain, through which door, above which step. The Rector, the Dowager,
  Jem, Ha'penny, Mercy and Fabian are each defined by a threshold they were or were not let over.
- **Being seen.** Cloudgate's windows all face outward; Jem cannot look at the view; Mercy wants to be seen; the Hour
  Court crowns itself in a mirror; the North Wind receives only the most talked-of; the Rector wants to be first,
  which is to say seen first.

The first build had these as texture. The antagonist, where they should all have met, was hollow: a jovial
enchanter with a chain of keys who wanted to be first through a door, for no reason the page gave. The Lifting was a
date. Cloudgate was one drawing room. The deepest thing in the work, MERCY ON US cut on the drowned church door by a
sexton's girl as the water came up the nave, was two sentences off the main road.

### Where it reads as pulp

Not pulp of the lurid kind: pulp of the whimsical kind, which is worse because it is pleased with itself. The
measurable tics in the first build's 2,650 lines:

| | |
|---|---|
| "like a …" | 81 |
| "as if …" | 55 |
| "for a long time" / "a long time" | 51 |
| someone laughs | 30 |
| "the size of a …" | 23 |
| "all at once" | 14 |

Everyone in Sennet was delighted. The ifrit screamed with joy, the sylphs screamed with joy, the merfolk laughed with
No Admittance because she was laughing, the Quay went silent as one man, Cloudgate turned as one head. Every
character's beat was the same beat: a pause "for a long time", a dry line, a laugh. A city where everyone has the same
timing is not a city; it is a narrator doing voices. And the planes, the work's most imaginative inventions, had no
menace at all: the Fire was a parlour game with a heat penalty, the Whelm a sea-floor tea-party.

### Tone

One key, major and bright, from the coast road to the closing news. The only dark notes were in the endings (the
Second Whelming's teapot coming up on the tide is the best sentence in the work) and in one consequence (Perrin
drowned at two in the morning). There was nothing in the middle of the week to be afraid of, and so nothing for the
comedy to be brave against.

### Foreshadowing

Almost none was planted on purpose. Perrin says he heard keys; the Rector wears a chain of keys in her role line and
nobody ever hears them. The petal in the keel makes Cloudgate weatherless and pours cold wind into the cellar; the
first build never let a player notice either before being told. The tide-engine opens the Door from below, and the
Nexus floor had nothing in it to wonder at. The last Grand Tour, two hundred years ago, which is when the Air petal
went missing, did not exist.

### Dramaturgy

The acts were a calendar. Day 3's funeral, which should be the midpoint, was something that happened at noon. The
antagonist's moves (Florian crowned, the dig, the divers, the raid, the Rector's delivery) were invisible unless the
company stood in the room. The race had no scoreboard; the mystery had no wrong answer; the Rector had no scene in
which she was more than charming.

### Playability

It was playable and it was generous: every gate had a way for anyone, no wrong turn killed. That generosity was a
fault as much as a virtue. There was no fight a player would choose to avoid, and nothing a player could get badly,
irretrievably wrong except the notebook.

### And the history

Two internal contradictions were found by reading: the North Wind gave his gift "two hundred and thirty years ago"
but the Lifting was "two hundred years ago", and Ysabeau "married Lord Halyard the year after the Lifting and had
nine children" after a summer that was thirty years earlier; and the brass plate on the Fish Stairs was dated
MDCIX, 1609, three centuries in the world's future (the calendar is 1290).

## What was done

### The antagonist given a history, in her own voice

The Sennet Courant on the bar of the Sounding Line prints the Rector's memoirs, *Thresholds*, a chapter a morning
(`courant.kyr`). Chapter Twenty-Nine: *Under the Plate*: she was born on Fish Street, the eel-wife's daughter, and
watched the lanterns go up from the third step, "and it seemed to me that the plate had been put there for me as much
as for the sea." Thirty: *Second*: through every door of her life after a Mottram, after a Halyard. Thirty-One,
printed on the funeral morning with a black border, is the fourth draft of the night Perrin fell: "I came down on the
nine o'clock basket." Set beside Mother Kittiwake's memory of the eleven o'clock, it is a witness in itself
(`printed_lie`): **the first deduction in the work the player makes rather than the module.** Thirty-Two:
*Lanterns*: when Cloudgate at last asked her up, she found she wanted only to have been able to refuse.

Old Bethan knew her as Isa Quance, the clever girl on the next barrow, who used to stand in the Ascent yard with her
arms round the chain and her eyes shut to feel it hum, and never went up it. On Ascension Eve, every year, the Rector
comes down in her gown of four colours and sits on Bethan's crate an hour and says nothing (`isa_visit`; her
schedule changed for it).

At the keel on the seventh night the player can now ask her two questions before choosing (`rector.kyr`). Why she
pushed him: "I didn't push him. I put my hands on his back… I was not going to be second to weather." What is under
the hill: "Fish… The sea-folk. Yes. I suppose so. I was born under that hill, you know, on Fish Street. Nobody up here
ever came down to tell us anything. I don't see why I should go down to tell them." **She has become Cloudgate.**
That is the satire's last turn, and it belongs to its villain.

### The Dowager made her mirror

Lady Imogen Halyard married the hill (`"I married it"`). Mr. Pargeter, the outfitter on Lamp Street, is her nephew:
she was born Imogen Pargeter over his shop, went up the chain at nineteen as a lady's maid, came down a Halyard, and
has been in the shop once since, to buy her son a fire-cloak without looking at the man who sold it (`lamp.kyr`). On
the Night of Lanterns, told what the harbour is setting on the water, she says "I was born on Lamp Street. Over a
shop. I have never once in my life gone down to the Quay on this night." The two women at the centre of the work both
climbed out of the lower town: one by marriage, who learned to look outward; one by work, who learned to look at
nothing but the door.

### Danger, with the ways round it

**The Sexton** (`sexton.kyr`). The harbour says the drowned sexton of Lir's has been at his rope two hundred years.
The merfolk say the Sexton is a conger as long as a boat, coiled round the bell-beam since the water came in; the bell
rung at the turn of the tide keeps it there. The grandmothers knew; the children ring it for the noise, and have
forgotten why: *that is what keeping is, when it works: nobody knows what it is for.* It changes the work's gentlest
local story into its hardest choice. Old Bethan cannot sleep for the bell; every way of stopping it (the children
stop, the bell rung once for Ned and then not again, the clapper muffled) lets the Sexton down into Chandlers' Row at
the next turn, and the Row shuts its doors.

Ways through, all played (`the-grand-tour-second.test.ts`):

- **Fight it.** Level 5, ambush: a level-1 warrior who climbs the tower at the wrong hour falls in one blow.
- **Time it.** At the turn, while the bell rings, it lies with its head under its tail; the belfry is safe for the
  hour.
- **Feed it.** A creel of Old Bethan's eels: "it lies looking at you with one round eye the size of a plate, as a dog
  looks at whoever has fed it."
- **Ring it again.** Ha'penny and the others, asked, ring at the turn; or cut your own muffle in the belfry. It goes
  back up.
- **Understand it.** Tell Old Bethan why the merfolk ring: "Then it was never ringing for Ned. It's ringing so
  nobody else's goes. … He'd want it rung." She sleeps through the next turn. This needs no skill and no trip under
  the water, only having listened to a child.
- **Kill it**, if you can, and No Admittance looks at it on the cobbles: "It was old. It kept the tower. We kept it.
  Now nobody keeps anything."

### The race told

The crier's board in the Glass Market is chalked as the Tour goes (`courant.kyr`): MR. F. MOTTRAM OF CLOUDGATE
CROWNED IN THE HOUR COURT; A TOURIST CROWNED IN THE FIRE, NAME NOT KNOWN; THE HOUSE OF DOORS REQUIRES DIVERS (and
under it, in chalk, WHAT FOR); THE WATER PETAL RECOVERED FOR THE HOUSE (FROM WHO); THE GANG HAS SAT DOWN; the
Rector's violet cards, RECEIVED AT THE RECTOR'S DOOR, THE FIRE, with a dead fish pinned beside the Water. The
antagonist's moves are on the page now, in the city's own voice.

### A wrong answer

Aubrey's own story, carried to Sergeant Bassett, is believed, because Aubrey believes it (`fall.kyr`). Within the
hour he comes down the chain beside the sergeant, not in irons, looking at the cloud as if he had never seen it, and
thanks him for the walk. The Rector, when you next go to her study, "looks, for the first time since you have known
her, entirely at rest." Perrin, told, puts his book down open so as not to lose his place: "He'll say yes to all of
it, because he thinks it's true. The idiot. The great kind idiot. Get me up those stairs." Only the dead man coming
up, or the Rector found out, frees him; otherwise the closing news says the Assize sits at Michaelmas.

### Foreshadowing planted

- **The keys.** The first time in her study: "Whenever the Rector moves, the keys on her chain of office chime, a
  small bright sound, like a purse shaken." At the eulogy, against the stone of the rail. After Perrin has said he
  heard keys: "The Rector's keys chime when she moves. You find that you are listening to them."
- **The petal in the keel.** "There is never any weather on Cloudgate… In Cloudgate they say it is breeding. Down on
  the Quay they say it is not natural, and spit." In the cellar, before anyone has told you: the bottles nearest the
  rock are frosted, in June.
- **The Undertow.** In the Nexus floor, a square of black metal with no handle, which hums like the chain.
- **The last Tour.** The Roll of the Grand Tour in the Hall of Doors: 1012, won by Odo Sare, who came back and would
  not say where; 1047, not won; 1088, not won, and among its Tourists *Ysabeau Halyard, aged seventeen, through the Air
  Door, DECLARED NOTHING*; and under it, in newer gilt, the Court of the Air reporting its petal lost the same year.
- **Fabian's heart.** His notebook stops saying "the traveller" and starts saying "we"; later, your name, crossed out
  and written again. His fondness rises with the two things at the centre of his story, being told the truth and
  being promised, and the main route now reaches the first sign of it.

### The illusion of a larger world

The Museum's labels: the first Tourist's salamander skin (1012); A SIGH OF THE AIR, IN A STOPPERED BOTTLE. DO NOT
UNSTOP; a minute of the Slow Assembly, RESOLVED, THAT THE ASSEMBLY IS AWARE (1146); Dame Philippa's snowball jar,
MELTED, 1231; and a green shell comb, A PERSON OF THE SEA'S, TAKEN ON THE FISH STAIRS, MCCXI, which is the year on the
brass plate, and nobody has written down from whom, or why. The register on the drowned lectern: forty-one names
written as the water came, and the last, in letters the size of a child's, JINNY SALTASH; and Bethan: "They put a
plaque up in Cloudgate for the Lifting. To the Glory of God and the House of Halyard. Nobody put one up for the
church." Where the merfolk learned their English: off the drowned shop-signs (so the old woman is No Admittance and
Ha'penny's mother is Rooms To Let) and out of Lir's hymn-books, "so they all talk like the Book of Common Prayer in a
hurry." A nursemaid from the Sennet side wheeling a Cloudgate baby along the inside of the Promenade, who does not
look at the view, and lets the rattle lie when it is thrown at the Balustrade.

### Shape

The week's chapters are named as acts now: *The Coast Road*, *The Courts*, *The Letting-Down*, *Ascension Day*,
*The Morning After*, *The Divers*, *The Keel*. The funeral is a midpoint with a scene in it: Aubrey at the rail,
"I did it, you know. Ask anyone," and the Rector's kind hand on his arm, which he lets stay; or, freed, Aubrey letting
the empty box down himself, hand under hand. And on the evening before Ascension Day, the Rector on Bethan's crate.

### The prose

The worst tics were cut by hand where they flattened a person into the narrator: Aubrey turns the bottle until its
label faces him; Perrin picks at his splint; No Admittance takes off her crown and holds it, "which in the Whelm is how
you think"; the Dowager looks at your boots, which have been on the Quay. Fabian drops a book instead of laughing; Prue
snorts. "For a long time" is down from 51 to 24. The history was made consistent: the gift in 1088, the Lifting in
1090, Ysabeau married her cousin the year after "so as not to change her name"; the plate is MCCXI.

### Smaller

Perrin and Aubrey join and leave in their own words; the Sexton's news is in the closing account; four new
milestones (the bell kept and Bethan asleep, the Sexton fed, the Sexton killed, the printed lie caught).

## What I am still not happy with

1. **Everyone is still witty.** Fewer people laugh, but nearly everyone is quick, and the quickness is the narrator's.
   The merfolk's hymn-book English is explained now, by Perrin, more than it is heard. The work would be better if
   No Admittance spoke only in collects and shop-signs, and if half the city were simply slow.
2. **The planes are still the least alive part.** They are good satires and thin places: three rooms each, one game
   each, no weather, no history, no one who wants anything but the game. The Earth has Chert and the Air has
   Tramontane; the Fire has a hawker.
3. **The Rector's depth arrives in print.** Her best material is in the Courant and in two answers at the keel, which
   a player who never reads the bar or never stands at the keel will not see. The woman on Bethan's crate is the right
   kind of scene: wordless, seen in passing. There should be more like it and fewer chapters.
4. **The mystery still deduces itself.** The printed lie is the one deduction the player makes, and the module still
   announces it ("One of them is lying in the Courant"). Kyr cannot hold an accusation the player composes; it can
   only offer topics the author wrote.
5. **The Undertow still skips the heart.** Now at least the Nexus floor, the humming chain, the Roll and the Sexton
   give a warrior who never goes up the chain some of the city's history. It is still the shortest and thinnest road.
6. **Waiting is still empty.** The left-alone week is chapter lines and the crier's board if you stand in the Glass
   Market. A city should come to you; this one waits to be visited.
7. **The two romances that matter most are both optional and both late.** Mercy at the Lanterns and Fabian's "we".
   The work has three love stories and no one who will be hurt by the player.

## If the ambition were ten times this

Start with the **Whelm**, because it is where the work's best images already are and where it has done least.

1. **A drowned town with a calendar of its own.** The merfolk keep the old town's feast days off the hymn-books,
   wrongly; Lir's has a congregation of fish and a sexton that is an eel; the Guildhall keeps the Glovers' Company's
   ordinances as law. A week in the Whelm should be as full as a week on the Quay.
2. **The Aeneans as a presence, not a machine.** The tide-engine is the only Aenean thing; it should be the last of
   many, each a door the Aeneans never needed keys for, each with its price in the Whelm's light.
3. **The Lifting as a remembered night.** Forty-one names in a register is a beginning. Each should be findable:
   a house in the Row still laid for supper; a Saltash descendant on the Quay; the nurse of Ysabeau's on Chandlers'
   Row whose house is under the Guildhall's roof; the merchant whose ledger is still open.
4. **Cloudgate as a society.** Twenty houses, not one; the hill's own bylaws (no washing to be hung on the rim side);
   a Cloudgate girl who wants to go down; servants who have not seen the sea in thirty years; the ice-house.
5. **The planes as countries.** The Hour Court has a history that lasts an hour and a capital that is ash by
   evening; the Slow Assembly's Minutes are a library a scholar could live in; the Gallery of Winds trades rumours as
   currency, and a lie told there comes back as weather.
6. **The race as people.** Four rival Tourists, not one, each with a bear-leader and a reason: a scholarship boy from
   Fish Street; a widow who wants to see her husband's place again; a Mottram; a clerk of the House with a notebook.
7. **A Rector met on the stairs.** Scenes, not chapters: Isa Quance on Bethan's crate; at the Ascent with her arms
   round the chain at night; in the chapel at Veyra's table laid for one, sitting in the absent traveller's chair.
8. **A city that comes to you.** Criers, broadsheets, gossip in the taproom keyed to the week's state, the Courant
   answering the company's own deeds, the sylphs' rumours arriving as weather.
9. **The Door of Doors as a sequel's first page.** In a world that goes on, the Doorward's door is a way into every
   other adventure Kyrie has; Sennet could be the hub the collection has not had.

## Did the second pass find its form?

Better than the first: the villain is now the satire's victim turned its instrument; the Dowager is her mirror; the
gentlest local story is now a dilemma with five resolutions and a monster in it; the race is visible; the mystery has
a wrong answer that costs a man; the history is consistent and has holes in it that feel like rooms. The tone has a
floor to stand on. It is still a bright work, and it should be. What it is not yet is a work in which the city's
people would go on without the player; that is where the next ten times would go.
