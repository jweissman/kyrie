# Quittance — Stage 1: the original design

*An author-builder commission. Branch `adventure/quittance`. Written before the engine was consulted for anything but its
general shape; Stage 2 is the editorial review of this document, Stage 3 what the engine will bear.*

## 1. The pitch

**Quittance** is a river-port metropolis of eighty thousand that burned to the ground ninety-four years ago and was
rebuilt on credit. Every house in it wears a **fire-mark** — a cast plate over the door, a gilt bird, a fish, an open
hand — which says which **Office of Assurance** will save it if it burns. Each Office keeps a **brigade**, and a brigade
saves the houses of its own clients. Ask at the gate what the city is for, and a clerk will say: *the management of loss*.

Under the comedy of a city that has priced everything, something is wrong with the arithmetic. Fire losses have been
*falling* for forty years, which is the best news the Offices ever had, and every year the **Receipt** — the white sheet
that the city's Founders' Policy is renewed with, hung on the Cinderwall at Reckoning — comes out a little paler. This
year it is nearly blank. Reckoning is nine days off.

You arrive as an outsider with a little money. The city, which cannot bear anything uninsured, will insure you before
you have taken three steps; and by the end of the week you will have adjusted a dozen losses, saved or burned a few
houses, courted or disappointed some people, and been asked — by the Chief Underwriter of the Phoenix Fire Office, by an
old woman who keeps a coal in a tin, and by something at the bottom of the city that has been waiting ninety-four years
to be paid — what a city owes for not having burned.

**Tone.** Dry civic comedy, claims-desk noir, a slow courtship conducted in the margins of surveys, and — underneath, kept
quiet until it is not — a patient grandeur. The register is Dickens's *Our Mutual Friend* crossed with Calvino and a
clerk's sense of humour. No quest is *about* insurance's being wicked; the Offices keep the city standing, and the
satire's edge is that *they are not wrong*. What they mistake is what they are selling.

How it differs from the existing adventures: Quittance is a **metropolis** — many districts with their own trades,
hours and idioms, not a village, a spa or a single-issue city — and it is organised as an **ecology of claims**, a
freelance adjuster's board of small cases, not a siege, a trial or a countdown to one event. It is an adventure about
*liability*: who must pay for what, and who counts as a person to whom something is owed.

## 2. History: what really happened

Ninety-four years ago the city of Cindermouth, a glass-and-iron town, burned for three days. The cause is told as an
accident of the glassworks. It was not. In the cellars of the Old Exchange a **great fire** — a plane-born thing that had
lodged in the furnaces for two centuries on the heat of the town's hearths and was spited by its landlords — walked up
into the streets.

**Dame Honora Quill**, a notary, found it in the Exchange vault, in the middle of her own burning city, and did the only
thing a notary can do. She wrote it a contract.

The **Cinder Treaty** (called by the city *the Founders' Policy* and by the Fire *the Hospitality*):

1. The Fire will spare Quittance, and every hearth in it that keeps a *Lent coal*.
2. A **Lent coal** is a coal of the Founding Hearth, kept alive in a household's hearth, never let out. It is the Fire's
   foot in the door of the house: where a coal is kept, the Fire is a *guest*, and a guest does not burn the house.
3. Each year, at **Reckoning**, the Hearthkeeper carries the coals *home* to the Hearth Below in the cellars of the
   Exchange, and gives each household back a lit one. The Fire is *paid* by being kept — attended, fed, sat with — and by
   the Return, the one night when it is a city's whole company.
4. The Fire gives a **Receipt** for each year: a leaf of white fire, *quittance*, which says the account is square.

Honora Quill made her cousins the first Offices so that the coals would have carriers; the brigades are the
carriers' descendants; the fire-mark is a coal-in-a-nest. The ceremonies thinned. A hundred years of honest
people turned the Hospitality into a **premium**, the coal into a **mark**, the carrier into a **brigade**, and the
Return into a banquet. They forgot because nobody had lost anything for a long time. That is what insurance is: a
machine for forgetting what the premium was for.

**Why it is failing.** Forty years ago the **Gas Company** and the Phoenix Office, in a perfectly reasonable alliance,
began the **Smokeless Ordinance**: no open hearth in the inner wards; gas lamps, then cold-lamps; stoves with doors. Fewer
fires, lower claims, higher profits. But each hearth that goes out is a coal that does not come home at Reckoning, and
each year the Fire is a smaller guest in a larger house. A guest not fed does not leave. It starves *politely*, and gets
smaller, and its patience and the Receipt go pale together.

## 3. The Appointed Losses

Six years ago **Sedley Prewitt**, Chief Underwriter of the Phoenix, found the Founders' papers in the Exchange
strongroom while looking for something else, and read them with the cold terror of a man who understood his own Tables.
His wife Margery had burned in a marked house in the third year of the Ordinance; it was the only fire in Mark Street
that decade, and it should not have happened. Now he knew why.

He did not restore the hearths — the Gas Company, his own Office's reserves and the entire economy of the inner wards
stood in the way, and the Ordinance was his own work. He concluded, as an actuary must, that the Fire is owed a **Tithe**,
and that a tithe paid in a controlled form is cheaper than the arrears. Each year since, a derelict block in the Bare
Wards — the uninsured warrens beyond the Cinderwall — has burned on an *Appointed Night*, its tenants given notice by a
rent-collector who is paid to be early. A few die. They are *Lapsers*; they were unmarked, and unmarked lives are not
in the Tables. It is, in his arithmetic, merciful: four hundred a year against eighty thousand.

He is wrong, and the thing wrong in his arithmetic is the whole of the book. The Fire never asked for a burnt offering; it
was promised *company*. The Bare Wards are where the Lent coals are still kept — the poor never stopped, because they
had no stoves — and the Appointed Losses burn the Fire's own *hosts*. A spirit bound by oath to guard the hearths that
keep it is made, by every Appointed Night, an oath-breaker's accomplice. It weakens faster. This year Prewitt has
decided that the Great Tithe — the Cinder Ward, three thousand people, the ward where the First Hearth's flue still
rises — must be burned at Reckoning, to keep the other seventy-seven thousand safe. His daughter does not know.

The Fire, for its part, is **not a villain**. It is an old, lonely, courteous creditor who has been defrauded for
decades by honest, foolish and cynical people, and is now being asked to help in burning its own friends. It is very
patient; it is nearly out of patience.

## 4. The region

Quittance is a walled river-city on the south bank of a wide tidal river, entered by one road, one water-stair and a
fence of other expedients. Eight districts, each a settlement of its own, plus the buried city.

| District | Character | Hours | Sites |

| **Policy Row & the Exchange** | The Offices' houses; the **Bell & Slip** coffee-house where freelance adjusters take claims off the Slate; the Hall of the Five; the Old Exchange above the Sunk City | 9 to 4; coffee-house till late | Bell & Slip, Phoenix House, Providence House, the Hall of the Five, Tables Room, the Exchange cellars door |

The Sunk City is reached by at least four ways down, each tied to a small adventure or a skill: the Exchange cellars (a
key, or lockwork), the Mortmain catacomb stair (a rite, or the Teague directions), the Lightering Reach diving-bell
(brawn and a salvor's favour), and the Cinder Ward's First Flue (the one the Fire itself opens, if it likes you).

## 5. The city's own life, apart from the plot

The city should be playable as a place before any mystery is touched.

- **Daily bulletin.** The Slate at the Bell & Slip is rewritten each morning: claims posted with rewards, brigade
  fixtures, deaths, marriages, premiums moved. The company reads it as a board of jobs, not a quest list; some claims
  expire, some are taken by rival adjusters.
- **Small fires.** There is a minor fire in some marked house most mornings, and the two-brigade rivalry over it — who
  has the right to the hose, whose mark is whose, what is salvaged — is the city's spectator sport.
- **Hours.** The Exchange bell rings at nine and four; the Arch closes at ten; the lamplighter's round runs at dusk;
  the stokers change shift; the Night Market opens in the Bare Wards when the Exchange bell rings for close.
- **Trades and prices.** An adjuster's licence and fee; a salvor's share; the chandler's mark-insured candles; a
  Penny-Mutual burial fee; a pie that costs more in Policy Row than at the Wharves.
- **Things people say.** Everybody has a premium opinion. "He's a bad risk." "She'll never lapse." "Fully comprehensive."
  The city's idiom is the idiom of the policy.

## 6. The small adventures

Twelve claims and cases, each with a person, a place or object, something misunderstood, more than one approach, a
complication, a resolution and a consequence. Six are *purely local* (their significance is personal, comic or practical);
six touch the larger matter. Each can be done, ignored, lost to someone else, or done badly.

1. **Mark Street** *(Brickfields; comic emergency)*. At about ten on the first morning a house on Mark Street burns with
   the Phoenix's mark on the door and the Lamprey's brigade on the scene: a drunk surveyor nailed the wrong plate. Two
   captains argue the mark while the house burns, and a family stands in the street. Approaches: a bucket-line (brawn,
   and whoever will pass a bucket), a *rule* (the Mark Street Rule: salvage may be shared if a third party certifies the
   mark — the company can be that third party if licensed), moving or pulling the mark by night before the argument
   (stealth), bribing, or intimidation. Complication: the Lamprey captain's argument is *right*. Resolution: house
   saved, half-saved or lost; the family's fate; the captains' regard. Effect: the Mark Street Rule exists afterwards and
   can be invoked in the Brigade's Hearing; Tobias Vane, a young Phoenix fireman, is introduced.

2. **The Annuitant** *(Mortmain; claims investigation, family comedy)*. The Sovereign Mutual has paid an annuity to Mrs.
   Jessamy Oakes, "aged 131", for ninety-four years. An adjuster, Pennington Cray, paid by the denied claim, will stop it
   Monday; seven people in the almshouse live on it. Truth: "Mrs. Oakes" is a *succession* of women of one family,
   each taking the name when the last died. The current one is nineteen and plays a ruin behind veils and an ear-trumpet.
   Approaches: expose, forge a testimonial, find the contract wording ("*so long as Jessamy Oakes shall be living in her
   name*" — which the family has honoured to the letter), win Cray over, or shame him. Cray does not change his mind
   from evidence; he changes it when someone asks him about his mother. Effect: a friend in the Sovereign (access to the
   Life Office's records, which show the Quill family annuity) or an enemy.

3. **The Scuttled Barge** *(Lightering Reach; salvage and fraud)*. The *Prudent Margaret* has sunk at the Mere Wharf with
   a cargo of cold-lamps. Two salvor crews claim her; the Lamprey adjuster must call it Total Loss or Salvage; the
   owner is suspiciously calm. Approaches: dive (brawn, the bell, a favour), search the rival's yard (stealth), read the
   manifest against the wreck (lore), confront the owner, fight the rival crew. Complication: the barge was scuttled
   to hide a *passenger*, a Bare Ward girl smuggled out of the Appointed list. Resolution: the girl, the fraud, the
   claim, the cargo — each can be handled differently. Effect: Lamprey Wharf regard; the diving-bell is available to the
   Descent.

4. **The Lamplighter's Round** *(Glass Quarter; purely local, intimate)*. Hob Tansy has lit the Foundry's gas-lamps for
   forty-one years and is to be retired the night the cold-lamps come in. His eyes have gone; he asks the company to
   walk his last round with him. Seven lamps, seven neighbours, seven small stories, an old man's pride. The way each
   is handled decides how he retires. The pilot-flames, he notes, have been going out one by one and coming back *in
   step*. Effect: a lamp that answers; a Foundry that remembers.

5. **Mr. Teague's Wake** *(Lantern Hill; comic, a funeral)*. Ambrose Teague, a cellar-diver, died insured for a large
   sum "to my beloved Rosalind". There are two Rosalinds, a wife and a lodger. His parrot, Fusby, repeats his last words;
   what the parrot says may decide who is paid. Approaches: coax the parrot (performance), plant words in it
   (deception), bribe the keeper, find which Rosalind he meant, or propose a settlement. Complication: the parrot's
   last words are a *route* — directions to a way down. Effect: a Sunk City route; a widow's good opinion.

6. **The Cold-Lamp Demonstration** *(Glass Quarter; engineering, theatre, sabotage)*. Perpetua Wynne's cold-lamps are to
   be shown to the Offices; the Gas Company needs them to fail. Approaches: help her demonstration, rig it to fail,
   rig it to succeed (it will not on its own), expose its flaw, persuade the chairman. Complication: the lamps work by
   trapping something that does not like being trapped. Effect: Wynne's salamander-glass, or the Gas Company's thanks.

7. **The Penny Mutual** *(Bare Wards; communal, practical)*. The burial-club's treasurer has run with the funeral fund and
   nine of the club's members are dying slowly. Approaches: find the treasurer (tracking, the Night Market), pay it
   yourself, talk to the landlord, expose. Complication: she was paid by a rent-collector *to stay quiet* about the
   Appointed notice. Effect: first hard evidence of the Appointed Losses; the Wards' trust.

8. **Pip's Tin** *(Bare Wards; local, tender)*. A nine-year-old keeps a Lent coal in a tin because her grandmother said to,
   and the coal is dying. Nobody in the Wards can relight it, because nobody has one that is not from the same line.
   Approaches: carry it somewhere (the Wynne lamp-house's salamander-glass, the Gasworks furnace, Quill's own hearth),
   or sit with it all night. Complication: the coal wants a *name*. Effect: the Wards' coals; a child's opinion; and an
   early sign that the Fire hears.

9. **The Surveyor's Daughter** *(Lantern Hill/Brickfields; romance)*. Ianthe Marl surveys risk and climbs roofs. Tobias Vane
   puts out fires and is of the wrong brigade. Her professional judgment is the one thing that would ruin his family
   (their house must be Declined), and she has not written it. Approaches: help them meet (an ordinary comedy of
   assignations), forge a survey, speak to the families, help them elope, or let it fail. Complication: it is
   *her* integrity she is protecting, not his. Effect: Ianthe's access to the Surveyors' Roll; Tobias as companion.

10. **The Mark-Forger** *(Brickfields; skulduggery)*. Somebody sells counterfeit Phoenix marks to the unmarked poor, who nail
    them up so a brigade will at least *look*. The Phoenix wants the forger. Ma Gedge is the forger. Approaches: report
    her, join her, improve her marks, extort her, protect her by turning the Phoenix's hunter. Complication: the
    marks *work*, sometimes. Effect: a network of marked doors in the Wards, which matters on the Night.

11. **The Queue** *(Exchange cellars; ghost comedy, pathos)*. In the cellars of the Old Exchange the dead of the Burning
    queue to file their claims, ninety-four years late. A junior clerk, Dobbin Fisk, has been taking their claims in secret
    and has a ledger of eleven hundred. They will not leave until paid, and they are polite about it. Approaches: pay a
    penny on the claim, find the descendants, say a rite, adjust the claims fairly, or break the queue. Complication: one
    claimant is not dead, and one claim is for *the fire*. Effect: the Ledger of the Unpaid is the single best document
    in the book; the dead are on the Night's side.

12. **The Bakery Inspection** *(Brickfields; purely local, practical)*. Gwen Tolliver's oven is to be surveyed for cover, and
    it does not meet the Ordinance; without cover she loses the bakery. Her oven draws, she says, because it was never
    taken out. Approaches: fix the flue (trapwork/brawn), help her cook for the surveyor, borrow a surveyor, bribe, take
    the loaf to the committee. Complication: the oven is the only one in the Brickfields with a Lent coal in it, and
    Gwen does not know. Effect: bread; a bakery that does not burn; an unmarked hearth in the marked wards.

## 7. The overarching drama

### Acts

**Act I — Assessment (days 1–3).** Cross the Arch; take a licence; read the Slate; adjust. The city's life shows itself.
Three clues, from three directions, say the Tables are wrong: Cornelia Ashby's paradox (the unmarked Wards burn *less*);
the Penny Mutual's rent-collector; the lamps going out in step. On the night of day 3 the **Candlewick Court** burns — an
Appointed Loss — and the company can be there.

**Act II — Adjustment (days 4–7).** The claims board fills out; the Offices and the Gas Company take sides. The company
learns what the Receipt is, who Honora Quill was, what the Queue knows, and that the Fire is *real* and below. Prewitt
learns the company has learned. The Surveyors' Roll shows the Cinder Ward Declined. A second Appointed Loss burns on
night 6 unless it is stopped.

**Act III — Settlement (days 8–9).** Reckoning Eve and Reckoning. Two principal ways to go at it, which can be combined.

### The two principal approaches

**The Hearth Night (civic).** Light the city. Bring coals to hearths — the Brickfields, the Wharves, the Foundry,
Lantern Hill, Mortmain and the Wards — and so *pay* the Fire as it was always meant to be paid, in public, in a single
night, against the will of the Gas Company and the Smokeless Ordinance. Needs the Offices, the brigades and the
wards; every ward you lit through a small adventure counts. Needs a coal-bearer: the Hearthkeeper (Auberon Quill) is
senile, and the Return must be said aloud. The climax is the **Hearing** in the Hall of the Five, where Prewitt's
Appointed Losses are put to the Five and he is brought to a choice. This is a game of *standing, logistics and nerve*.

**The Descent (private).** Go down. Find the Hearth Below and talk to the Fire. Needs a way down (four), a way across
the Slag Galleries (heat, glass, the Queue), and an audience. The Fire is not hostile: its *mood* is the game, its
terms are negotiable, and what it will take is a person, a promise or an apology — or a fight, if you insist. This is a
game of *attrition, discovery and conversation*.

Both can be chosen; the best outcome needs a piece of each. Neither is "do all the quests, then talk."

### Endings

1. **Quittance** — the Hearth Night is kept, the Receipt is issued, the Appointed Losses are exposed, Prewitt resigns or
   dies. The Smokeless Ordinance is amended. The Wards get a mark of their own. (Civic best.)
2. **The Hospitality Renewed** — the Fire is given terms: a Hearth in the Hall, a Hearthkeeper, an annual night. Someone
   stays. (Descent best; costs a person.)
3. **General Average** — the Five Offices agree to bear a *shared* loss: the Cinder Ward is evacuated and burned on
   purpose, in daylight, with no one in it, and the Offices pay. The Fire takes what it is owed; no one dies. A
   cold-eyed bargain; the city is poorer and alive. (Reached by hybrid.)
4. **The Controlled Loss** — Prewitt's plan goes ahead. The Cinder Ward burns on Reckoning night. The company is paid.
   (The cynic's ending.)
5. **The Lapse** — nobody pays; the Receipt is not issued; the Burning begins. (The failure ending; it has gradations,
   by how many were warned.)
6. **The Slaying** — the Fire is killed or bound by force. The city is safe from fire for ever and its hearths do not
   light for a year. (The martial ending.)

### Characters

- **Sedley Prewitt**, Chief Underwriter of the Phoenix. Urbane, bereaved, exact; believes arithmetic is mercy. His arc:
  he is not moved by evidence (he *wrote* the Tables); he is moved when someone asks him to stand in the ward on the Night,
  when his daughter sees it, or when a better sum is shown him. Three roads to him: *witness*, *kin*, *arithmetic*.
- **Ottoline Prewitt**, a Phoenix loss-adjuster, his daughter; the company's first patron. She has a high opinion of her
  father and a lower one of herself.
- **Cornelia Ashby**, Keeper of the Tables. Shy to the point of speechlessness except about numbers. She found the
  paradox and has told no one. A possible companion. Her arc: finding her voice in the Hall of the Five.
- **Auberon Quill**, 83, last Hearthkeeper. Deaf, kind, mostly lost. In the middle of one sentence in three he is
  entirely lucid. He cannot say the Return alone.
- **Captain Moll Garrity** (Phoenix brigade) and **Captain Josiah Dree** (Lamprey): rivals who hate each other with the
  intimacy of colleagues. Garrity changes her mind when her crew is risked; Dree when he is out-bargained.
- **Lord Gaunt-Halloran**, Chairman of the Gas Company. A buffoon with a real interest. Changes his mind when it is
  made profitable and flattering.
- **Perpetua Wynne**, inventor, fearful of her own lamps. **Ma Gedge**, forger. **Hob Tansy**, lamplighter. **Pip**,
  who keeps the coal. **Dobbin Fisk**, clerk to the dead. **Ianthe Marl** and **Tobias Vane**. **Pennington Cray**.
- **The Fire**, the Name Beneath. Speaks rarely and exactly. Its moods are *owed*, *lonely*, *wary*, *tender*, *wrathful*.

### Strategic conflicts

Phoenix v. Lamprey (brigades); Phoenix v. Providence (rates); the Gas Company v. the Lamp-house; the Offices v. the
Lapsers; Prewitt v. the Treaty; Garrity v. her orders; Ianthe v. her own pen.

## 8. What changes, and what is remembered

The city keeps accounts. Each small adventure deposits something: a **regard** with an Office or a ward, a **coal lit**,
a **writ** (a document), a **companion**, a **route down**, a **fact known**. The Hearth Night counts lit wards; the
Descent counts routes and nerve; the Hearing counts regard and witnesses. A quest ignored leaves its person at the
mercy of the clock.

## 9. Rewards

- **The Adjuster's Licence** (entry): reduced prices at Office-owned counters; admission to the Slate; the right to
  certify a mark.
- **A mark of your own**: a cast plate that turns any one door in the company's name to *marked* — mechanically, a
  carried item that grants the company the right to claim brigade rescue.
- **A Lent coal in a tin**: warms every rest, grants `flame`; kept lit through the book; the single most important
  possession.
- **Salamander-glass lantern** (Wynne): a light that does not go out, resisting the Slag Galleries' heat.
- **The Ledger of the Unpaid**, **the Surveyors' Roll**, **the Founders' Policy**: documents.
- **Companions**: Tobias Vane (warrior), Cornelia Ashby (lore/insight), possibly Ottoline.
- **Standing**: a persistent record in the world's chronicle. A company that comes back to the first country is
  recognised by name in the places where news of Quittance travels.
- **Experience**: for each adventure resolved, by Kyrie's own schedule.
