# Into Quittance: through the gap in the Cinderwall

The same company, declaring nothing: along the outer wall, a copper to the gap boys, and into the Bare Wards without a name on any Roll.

Seed `arch-gap`. Played by the labels a player sees; each block is the page's telling of the step.

**1. To the foot of the outer wall** _(watch 2, gap_path)_

> The wall, on this side, is another wall: black, old, not brick but fused glass and slag laid in courses like loaves, and without a single plate on it. This is the Cinderwall. A gap the width of a handcart has been left in it, and through the gap you can smell cooking, and woodsmoke, which the signs on the other wall forbid. A rope hitched between two nails bars the gap in the Cinderwall, at about the height of a child’s idea of dignity. The gap boys are here.

**2. Pay the gap boys a copper a head, to be remembered** _(watch 2, wards_gate)_

> The eldest drops the rope, solemnly, and writes something with a nail on the wall. ‘That’s you remembered,’ she says. ‘Down Salt Lane for the pie, and don’t give anyone your real name before the second night.’ Inside the gap in the Cinderwall, the Bare Wards begin without ceremony: timber houses leaning over alleys, lines of laundry, the sound of a hundred conversations in a hundred upper rooms. There are no plates on any door. There are, on a surprising number of them, plates; they are the wrong colour. The rope lies on the ground across the gap, in a loop.

**3. To Salt Lane** _(watch 2, salt_lane)_

> Salt Lane runs down the middle of the Wards like a cut: crooked, crowded, loud, smelling of smoke and frying and people. Hearths glow in every window. In the rest of the city the sign says fire is forbidden; here there is a fire in every room.
