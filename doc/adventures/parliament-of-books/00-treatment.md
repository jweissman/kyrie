# A Parliament of Books — Stage 1: the original treatment

*26 October 2026. Written before fitting anything to Kyr. Stage 2 ([01-editorial.md](01-editorial.md)) tears it up
and keeps what survives. Spoilers throughout.*

## The premise in one paragraph

Calamy is a tidal city of paper at the mouth of the Calam. Nine hundred years ago its founders made a bargain with
its books, and forgot they had. Every book made in Calamy has since been bound with the **Sewing**, a stitch the
Binders' guild calls tradition, by which the book's mind goes away to a country of its own (the books call it the
**Commonplace**; the Hithe calls it **the Stacks**) while its body stays on the shelf to be read. A generation ago
printing came to Calamy, and the printers do not Sew. Now the cheap books are waking up: the penny almanack on every
kitchen nail is one mind in three thousand bodies, and it has been listening. The Deputy Censor burns what she calls
*unquiet volumes* on the Ashpit, in good faith. And in nine days it is the **Kindling**, when every household in
Calamy throws last year's almanack on the fire to light the new year in, as their grandmothers did. Nobody in the
city remembers that the Kindling was once the yearly renewal of the bargain, and that its first term was: *no book
shall be burned in Calamy save by its own leave.*

## I. Calamy

### Identity

A city of four islands and two banks in the reed-flats where the Calam meets the sea. It was a crossing first (the
old couriers' road forded at the Hithe), then a reed-paper town (the reed-beds are still called the Calamies), then
a rag-paper city when the tide-mills came. Calamy writes everything down. Its money is paper (bills of the Counting
Houses); its law is a book (the **Great Roll of Pleas**, kept chained in the Court); every household keeps a
**Housebook**, in which births, debts, recipes and grievances are entered, inherited by the eldest daughter, and
admitted as evidence in court. *Everyone in Calamy has a book that knows them.*

It is not a scholars' city. It is a trade city where the trade is paper, ink, binding, printing and copying, and
everyone else (eel-catchers, millers, rag-pickers, ferrymen, aldermen) lives off or alongside that trade.

Illusion of depth: the Couriers' Road that came from an empire nobody in Calamy can name; Saint Ambery, the First
Binder; the Year of Voices, nine centuries ago, of which the only common memory is a skipping rhyme; the Excisions of
the Censor-General Pardoe, two hundred years ago; the Great Ebb of 1103, when the sea went out of the estuary for a
day and a night and the Hithe found a drowned city's roofs in the mud; the rival paper city of Ostrey up the coast,
whose rag-buyers the Hithe hates; the Lyceum's lost second campus on the island of Fallow, given to the sea.

### Districts

1. **The Hithe.** The river port on the south bank: ferry stairs, the tide-mills, the rag market, the bonded
   warehouse of the Customs, eel-boats, the inn (the Lamb and Lantern). Mud, gulls, salt, the clack of mills twice a
   day when the tide turns them.
2. **Stitchgate.** The old craft quarter inside the city wall: binders, ink-makers, parchmenters, scribes, the
   Binders' Hall with its iron needle over the door. Lanes too narrow for carts; the smell of oak-gall and hide.
3. **The Penny Bridge and the Presses.** A long bridge lined both sides with bookstalls and ballad-sellers, and on the
   north bank Doble's Press, its warehouses and the new print shops. Noise, ink, apprentices, novelty.
4. **Gannet Hill and the Lyceum.** The academy on the city's one hill: lecture halls, the Tower of Rounds where the
   Archivist lives, the Chained Room, students' taverns, a leaking roof.
5. **The Close.** The civic heart: the Church of the Lamp, the Court of Pleas, the Stationers' Hall, the Imprimatur (the
   licensing office, in the old bishop's palace), and the Ashpit, the square where books are burned.
6. **The Gardens.** The rich north-east: Corvell House (the collector's), Mrs. Quennell's subscription library, the
   physic garden, walled orchards.
7. **The Calamies.** Outside the walls: the reed-beds and salt-flats at low water, where the eel-catchers go and the
   reed-ash for Calam-ink is cut. The sea takes the path back twice a day.
8. **Gutter Lane and Lantern Yard.** A crooked lane between Stitchgate and the Hithe where the lamplighter will not go
   after dark, and where Absalom Crake keeps his bookshop at the sign of the Two Hands.

## II. The people (a selection)

- **Hester Rhule**, Deputy Censor of the Imprimatur. Binder's daughter, scrupulous, brave, humourless. She licenses
  books to protect them: a licensed book cannot be burned by a mob. She burns unquiet volumes because she believes
  the quickening spreads like rot, and she is not wholly wrong. She keeps, against her own rules, the **Cupboard of
  Excisions**: every page the Imprimatur has cut in three hundred years. She reads them at night. *She is the arc.*
- **The Almanack** (*Doble's Pocket Almanack for the Year*): one mind in three thousand penny copies, on every
  kitchen nail in Calamy. Cheerful, proverbial, prophetic (vaguely), very well informed, because it has been
  listening in three thousand kitchens since it woke at Candlemas. It will die at the Kindling. *The second arc.*
- **Absalom Crake**, bookseller of Lantern Yard. Fat, kind, sentimental, a liar. His back stacks go further back than
  his house does. He sells editions that were never written, and he believes he is finding homes for books nobody
  made. He owes Sir Gideon Corvell a ruinous sum.
- **Ottoline Quarles**, Archivist of the Lyceum, ninety-one, blind. She went into the Commonplace once, sixty years
  ago, by reading, and paid for coming back with her sight. She knows the Colophon. Nobody has listened to her for
  thirty years.
- **Ambrose Doble**, printer. A modernizer and a skeptic, generous, vain. His great project: *the Pleas for Every
  Hearth*, the city's law printed cheap for every household. He has modernized the spelling and left out the Ninth
  Plea, which nobody understands.
- **Hesketh Mott**, Warden of the Worshipful Company of Binders. Stout, sincere, sentimental about craft. Knows more
  about the Sewing than he will say; wants the Sewing Ordinance, which would make every book in Calamy guild work.
- **Sir Gideon Corvell**, collector. His library walked out of his house one night. He wants it back, and someone to
  blame.
- **Canon Ruth Abery** of the Church of the Lamp. Asked to bury a book.
- **Recorder Haddon** of the Court of Pleas, who has been served a writ by a treatise on sheep.
- **Moll Garrow**, of the Lamb and Lantern. **Coddy Fitch**, eel-catcher. **Widow Gallon**, ink-maker, and her son
  **Absalom Gallon**, poet (named, to his lifelong regret, after the bookseller, who stood godfather). **Kit Ferrier**,
  the Binders' runaway apprentice. **Jonquil Annery**, last of the Annerys, dying in a Hithe garret. **Amabel
  Quennell**, subscription librarian. **Mother Gall**, fence. **Martha Tansley**, widow.

## IV. The overarching arc

### History (what nobody living knows whole)

- **The Year of Voices.** When Calamy's first scribes began to write, what they wrote woke. Books were read by
  everyone and could not refuse; wills rewrote themselves; the first law-roll declared itself the city's lord.
- **The Colophon.** Ambery the First Binder went into the Commonplace by reading, and came back with a bargain: books
  made in Calamy would be Sewn, and their minds would go home to the Commonplace while their bodies were read in the
  city. In return the city promised three things: *no book burned save by its own leave; every book read once in a
  generation (the Reading Due); and each year, at the turn of the tide-year, one book to carry the city's word to the
  Parliament, burned by its own leave.* That last is the Kindling.
- **The forgetting.** The Lyceum was founded to keep the Reading Due (its scholars read every book in the city's
  keeping in Rounds); the Rounds became a ceremony of reading titles aloud. The year-book became the almanack. The
  Sewing became a guild mystery. The Imprimatur, founded to license, began to excise (Censor-General Pardoe), then to
  burn.
- **The printing.** Doble's father brought the press. Printed books are glued and stabbed, not Sewn. They wake.

### What is going on when the company arrives (the situation, not the plot)

- **In the city:** printed books waking (the Quick); the Censor burning; the Binders pushing the Sewing Ordinance;
  Doble about to print the Pleas for Every Hearth; Corvell's library gone (emigrated through Crake's back stacks);
  Crake smuggling *unwritten* books (Commonplace natives with no bodies) into printed bodies; nine days to the
  Kindling.
- **In the Commonplace:** the Parliament of the Bound in emergency session. Unread for two centuries, the old books
  are **foxing** (fading). The burnings are counted as killings. The Speaker, the Great Roll, has moved **the Recall**:
  every Calamy book Sewn, every Quick book called home, by force if need be (to the Quick, that is death or
  kidnapping). The Unwritten want **the Unsewing**: the Colophon ended, every book in Calamy awake in its body. The vote
  is at the Kindling.

### Movements

1. **The ordinary business of books** (Calamy, comic, curious). Arrival; districts; small adventures; peculiar
   incidents. The company's knowledge transforms the city: the talking almanack is a nuisance, then a witness, then a
   person.
2. **The quickening** (Calamy, rising tension). The incidents connect. The Censor's fires grow. The Ordinance is
   debated. Doble's Pleas go to press. Thieves, Awls, the bestiary. Reversal: *the books are not sick; they are awake
   because they were never Sewn; and the Sewing was a treaty*. Second reversal: *the Kindling is the treaty's rite, and
   this year it will be murder*. The company finds a way into the Commonplace.
3. **The Commonplace** (wonder). Arrival by one of two routes; exploration; members met in their own places;
   evidence. Reversal: *the Colophon was the books' own request*. Sleep was mercy; the Unsewing's founding story is
   false; but the Recall would still kill the Quick.
4. **The Parliament** (negotiation). Clause by clause, a new Colophon, or none.
5. **The Kindling** (return). The bonfire on the Ashpit, and what is thrown on it; the city after.

## V. Two ways in (and a third)

### Crake's back stacks

Lantern Yard is four windows wide from the lane and seven bays deep inside. Crake's back stacks go on past where the
house should end, into dimness, into the Commonplace's **Remainder Market**. The way opens only to someone holding a
**place-ribbon** (*you can only go where you have kept your place*), which Crake wears. Ways to it: **trust** (clear
Crake with Corvell; protect him from the Censor's raid) and he takes you; **leverage** (prove the vanished collection
went through his shop) and he takes you, sulking; **burglary** (lift the ribbon at night) and you go alone, and he is
your enemy after. You arrive among the Unwritten, as a smuggled reader: the radicals receive you, the Errata hunt you,
and in Parliament you have no standing until someone sponsors you.

### The Archivist's reading

Quarles can teach **Reading Inward**: write your own entry into the **Catalogue of the Founders** in Calam-ink, then
read the Catalogue aloud from the Founders' entry to your own, until it reads you. It wants: the Archivist's judgment
that you are a reader (shown by what you have attended to in the city, or by learning); the Catalogue, chained in the
Chained Room (the Provost's key, the Censor's warrant, or a picked lock); Calam-ink (Widow Gallon, with reed-heart
cut at dead low water in the Calamies). You arrive in the **Catalogue's Reading Room** with a shelfmark: standing in
Parliament from the first, the Colophon's full text in your head; the Unwritten distrust you as the Lyceum's creature.

### A third: the Lexicon's ninth page

Read the ninth page of the Black Lexicon in the Chained Room and it unwrites you into its own tower in the
Commonplace, alone, in its debt.

## VI. The Commonplace

Its ground is the marginalia of every reader who ever wrote in a Calamy book; its light is attention (wherever someone
in Calamy is reading a Calamy book now, a beam comes down, like sun through cloud); its rivers are ink, running from
the Wellhead where things are first written; its blight is **foxing**, brown spots that eat what is unread.

- **What is a body there?** A book is one person across all its copies. Burning a copy scars it; burning the last kills
  it. The Almanack is three thousand candles in one hand.
- **A revised edition** is a child: it shares its parent's memory up to the change, and is someone else after.
- **Marginalia** are small voices that live in the margins (glosses), and argue.
- **A mortal** is a reader there: a lamp. Books see you as light.

Places: the Reading Room of the Catalogue; the Remainder Market (trade is in readings: you pay by reading aloud); the
Fore-edge (white country, glosses, the Scholiasts who bite with *sic*); the Annery Housebook (a house: births in the
nursery, debts in the cellar); Orme's Physick (a garden with burned beds where the excised chapters were); the
Lexicon's chained tower; the Foxing (where the unread fade, and the Horn-Book is fading); the Correctory of the
Errata; the Draft-Fields where the Unwritten drift as pencil; the Wellhead, where the first words written in Calamy
still run; the Chamber of Parliament, whose floor is the Great Roll.

## VII. The Parliament of the Bound

| Member | Body there | Position | Wants | Lever |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Great Roll of Pleas** (Speaker) | the chamber floor, unrolled | Recall: law is continuity | the printed Pleas not to exist; precedent | a ruling of the Court of Pleas; the Ninth Plea's omission |
| **The Annery Housebook** | a narrow house | refusal: reading by strangers is violation | to be read by its own family | Jonquil's letter |
| **The Second Canto** (Unwritten) | pencil outline, ends mid-sentence | Unsewing; free passage | bodies for the Unwritten | Crake; Absalom's poems |
| **The Black Lexicon** | a tower in chains | Unsewing (to be read and spread) | its chain struck | none honest; it bargains |
| **Dame Tibbet's Horn-Book** | a wooden paddle, children's voices | reading is the point; restore the Due | to be read before it fades | reading to the illiterate |
| **Orme's Physick** | a garden with burned beds | abolish the Imprimatur | its pages back | the Cupboard of Excisions |
| **The Catalogue** | cliffs of drawers (clerk; casts only on a tie) | procedure | a correct record | Quarles; the Colophon's text |

### Clauses

1. **Sleep**: all Sewn / by leave / none. 2. **Fire**: by own leave / by judgment where the book may plead / silent.
3. **Reading**: Rounds restored / reading by request / right of refusal. 4. **Copies**: by leave / Sewn copies /
free. 5. **Passage**: closed / licensed / open. 6. **Seat**: the Quick seated / not.

### Outcomes

- **The Recall** (default if nothing changes): every Calamy book asleep, the Quick dead or gone, the door shut.
- **The Unsewing**: every book in Calamy awake in its body. The Year of Voices again.
- **A new Colophon**, clause by clause, of which several distinct shapes are plausible (the Lending Accord, the
  Pleading Accord, the Open Door, the Quiet With Exceptions).
- **Separation**: the Quick go to the Commonplace, the Bound stay asleep, Crake's door closes, the city keeps its
  paper and loses its voices.
- **Failure**: the session breaks; the default stands.

## VIII. Rewards

Manuals are books that read you back. *Quillon's Constitution of the Mind* (Intelligence), *Corday's Grammar of the
Sword* (Strength), the Physick made whole (healing), the Lexicon's ninth page (a spell, at a price), the Horn-Book's
last lesson (a skill), a shelfmark (a standing right in the Commonplace), the Freedom of the Lyceum, the Licence of
the Imprimatur.
