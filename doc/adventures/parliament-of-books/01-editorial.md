# Stage 2 — a severe reading of the treatment, and the design as revised

*Questions the commission sets, answered against [00-treatment.md](00-treatment.md), each with what changes.*

## Is the city alive without its mystery?

**Not yet.** Of the twenty-two situations in the treatment, fifteen turn on books and twelve on *intelligent* books.
That is the commission's warning exactly: "every small incident secretly about intelligent books". A city of paper may
be forgiven a majority of book business, but not a majority of *talking*-book business. A miller does not care whether
an almanack has a soul.

**Revision.** At least eight situations owe nothing to the quickening, and several about books are about *books as
objects*: debts, fines, variant readings, a leaking roof. The revised inventory (below) has nine that ignore the
Commonplace entirely, six about books as books, and the rest about the Quick. Two of the non-book stories have an
effect on the arc *only through the city's material life*: the mills make the paper for Doble's Pleas; the eels feed
the Kindling fair. That is the right kind of connection: not "secretly about books" but "the city is one place".

## Are the small quests substantial?

Mostly. Thin ones: *the gull thief* (fine as a curiosity, kept), *the Awls* (a fight with no question in it; revised:
the Awls are Mott's apprentices, not thugs, and the question is whether Mott sent them; he did not, his journeyman
did, and the player can find out which), *the reed-ash* (a fetch; revised: the reed-beds are where Coddy Fitch goes,
and the tide decides how long you have, and Coddy knows the way if he owes you).

Each kept situation has a person with a motive, a place or object that matters, something misunderstood, two or more
approaches, a complication, and a consequence that is visible later (the table's last column).

## Does the plot grow from the setting?

Yes, and this is the treatment's best idea: the festival is the treaty, degraded. The Kindling is ordinary city life
(a fair, eels, a bonfire, a skipping rhyme) whose forgotten meaning is the whole metaphysical stake. The danger is
**exposition**: an Archivist who explains the Colophon in one conversation would spend it. **Revision:** the Colophon
is never told whole by any one person in the city. It is assembled from: the skipping rhyme (children on the Bridge);
the Ninth Plea that Doble left out (the Roll, or the Court's chained copy); the variant in the Life of Ambery (the two
scribes); the Ambery Codex (the Lyceum); the Almanack's own account of waking; Quarles, who knows the whole but will
tell only someone who has found two of the pieces. The journal assembles it as the player does.

## Does the Commonplace astonish?

Its ideas do (light as attention, foxing as the blight of the unread, editions as children, paying in readings). Its
list of eleven places does not: a list of eleven is how one makes a theme park. **Revision:** nine places, each with a
purpose in play, each with its own wonder, danger or joke, and each reachable for a reason:

| Place | Purpose in play |
|---|---|
| The Reading Room (Catalogue) | arrival by reading; the shelfmark; procedure |
| The Remainder Market | arrival by the back stacks; trading in readings; the Unwritten |
| The Fore-edge | the wild between; the Scholiasts (danger); the Grammar of the Sword |
| The Annery Housebook | a house to enter; Jonquil's letter |
| Orme's Garden | the burned beds; the excised pages; the cure |
| The Foxing | the unread fading; the Horn-Book; the strongest argument for the Reading Due |
| The Wellhead | the first words written in Calamy: the reversal |
| The Lexicon's tower | the bargain; the chain; the worst idea in the adventure |
| The Chamber | the Parliament, on the Roll |

The Correctory and the Draft-Fields are folded in (the Errata take a smuggled reader to the Reading Room to be
*catalogued*, not to a prison; the Draft-Fields are the far side of the Remainder Market).

## Are the books persons, or mouthpieces?

The treatment gave each a position. A position is a mouthpiece until it has a contradiction it cannot resolve alone.
**Revision:**

- **The Roll** moves the Recall in the name of continuity, and its own printed child (the Pleas for Every Hearth)
  would be one of the Quick it recalls. It does not know whether it is protecting the city's law or its own
  authorship. It respects a precedent more than an argument.
- **The Housebook** demands the right to refuse reading, and the one thing it wants is to be read by Jonquil Annery,
  who sold it.
- **The Second Canto** wants bodies for the Unwritten. A body is a finished text. Given one, it would have to *end*,
  and it has spent four hundred years being the most interesting thing in the Commonplace precisely because it never
  has. It does not know this about itself; Absalom Gallon's poems (which want to be burned *because* they are finished
  and bad) can show it.
- **The Lexicon** is the only member who wants nothing it does not say. It is the most honest voice in the chamber and
  the most dangerous.
- **The Horn-Book** speaks in words of one syllable. It is fading. It wants the Reading Due restored, and it would vote
  for the Recall tomorrow if the Recall came with readers.
- **Orme's Physick** wants the Imprimatur abolished and its excised chapters back. Some of those chapters are a cure
  for the Hithe ague. One of them is a poison, and Censor-General Pardoe was right to cut it. The Physick knows.

## Is the council a game, or a long passage of dialogue?

The treatment's six clauses, three options each, before six voices with a tie-breaking clerk, is a game on paper and
a spreadsheet on the page. **Revision:**

- **Four articles**: *Sleep* (folding in copies), *Fire*, *Reading*, *the Door*. Each has three readings.
- **Each member's position on each article is discoverable** by questioning them (in their own places, or in the
  chamber) and is shown in the journal once known.
- **A position moves only for a reason the fiction states**, and each reason is a thing the player can find,
  bring, know, be, or have with them: evidence (a court ruling, the excised pages, Jonquil's letter, the first words at
  the Wellhead), knowledge (the variant reading, the Ninth Plea, the Principle of the Text), a companion who speaks
  (Rhule, Crake, the Almanack, Quarles' message), or who the protagonist is (a scholar can name the eye-skip; a
  merchant can price a prohibition; a priest can speak of a soul; someone who has been a prisoner can speak to the
  Quick about the Recall; a court officer can move the question). Persuasion checks exist, but as one lever among
  many and never the only one for any member.
- **An article passes with four of six voices.** A failed reading can be put again in another form; the chamber
  makes counter-proposals of its own (the Roll offers *by judgment* when *by leave* fails).
- **Offending a member** (a lie found out, the Lexicon's chain touched) can make it leave the chamber, which changes
  the arithmetic.
- **The Colophon is sealed** when the player calls the question with all four articles settled; or at the Kindling hour
  regardless, with what is settled; unsettled articles fall to the Roll's motion.

## Are there real alternatives?

The outcomes in the treatment were three dominant ones plus a vague "new Colophon". **Revision:** the settlement is
the combination of four readings; the epilogue is written from each article separately, and a few combinations have
names (the **Recall**, the **Unsewing**, the **Lending Accord**, the **Pleading Accord**, the **Separation**). Each
article changes something durable in the city: who is on the Ashpit at the Kindling, whether the almanack on the nail
at the Lamb speaks, whether Crake's back stacks end at the wall, whether the Lyceum's Rounds are read for real, what
Rhule does next.

## Does it reward different builds?

The treatment gestures. The revised design gives each of the fourteen crafts at least one thing it can do that others
cannot, without any of them being *needed*, and keeps an unlettered path through every gate (see the build matrix in
the final report). Three gates were rewritten because only a scholar could pass them:

- *The Archivist's judgment*: she does not test learning. She asks what you have *noticed*. Two of the Colophon's
  pieces found in the city is enough.
- *The selective codex*: it opens for someone who has read aloud to someone who could not read: Martha Tansley, Jonquil
  Annery, or the children at the dame school. An unlettered protagonist can have the Almanack read for them.
- *The variant reading*: a scholar names the eye-skip at a glance; anyone can lay the two copies beside the Church's
  exemplar and see which line is missing.

## Too much exposition? Comedy earned? Does anyone change?

- Every member's first words in the chamber are cut to two sentences. Their history comes from their places.
- The comedy is situational (a treatise suing over a misprint that is really killing sheep; a fine forty years
  overdue; a poet's poems begging to die) and every joke has a cost or a use later.
- **Hester Rhule** changes by evidence, not revelation: she hears the Almanack scream through a copy she is burning;
  she reads in the Cupboard what Pardoe cut; she is shown the Colophon's first term; she watches the Court rule a book
  has standing. Each moves her, and the player chooses what she is shown and when. Three ends: **the Advocate** (she
  pleads for books under the new Colophon), **the Burner** (she lights the Kindling herself), **the Binder** (she hands
  in her seal and goes back to her father's trade). Which one depends on what she has seen and whether she has been
  lied to.
- **The Almanack** changes too: from a cheerful gossip to someone who knows what it means to have heard three thousand
  kitchens, and must decide what to do with one thing it heard.

## What the revision cuts

The ballad (kept, small); the eel-pie wedding (folded into the night sluice); the Hithe ague and Martha Tansley's
funeral (kept); the Lexicon's ninth page as a third way in (cut to a temptation inside the Commonplace: it is the one
bargain there that costs the company something permanent, and it was too strong as a door); the Seat article (folded
into *Sleep*: the Quick seated if they wake by leave).
