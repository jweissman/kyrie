# The Stacks: the Infinite Library

*The third pass, at the commission's request: the Library Plane made deep. Behind the Reading Room and beyond the
Remainder Market the Commonplace stops being a country and becomes what it always also was: a library without end,
hexagon after hexagon. Files: [`stacks.kyr`](../../../data/adventures/parliament-of-books/stacks.kyr) (the place,
its law, its books), [`concordance.kyr`](../../../data/adventures/parliament-of-books/concordance.kyr) (its people,
the murder, the threads in Calamy), [`beyond.kyr`](../../../data/adventures/parliament-of-books/beyond.kyr) (the two
realms its doors open on). Journeys: `test/parliament-of-books-stacks.test.ts`.*

## The idea, and its form

The Commonplace of the first build was a pastoral: beams of attention, the Fore-edge, the Foxing, members in their
houses. It lacked the thing the commission asked for most directly, a Library that is *deep*: winding, crowded,
ambiguous, full of books one can actually read, with the vertigo of a place that goes on past every edge. The Stacks
are that, and they are built on one idea that answers the question the whole adventure asks (*why do Calamy's books
wake?*):

> **Nobody wakes a book. A book wakes because there is another book near enough to wake up for.**

The Library was not made. It *thickened*: two books shelved together by a clerk who never came back, until they
began to finish each other's sentences; then four; then a shelf, a hexagon, an infinity of hexagons. There is no
centre, only density; no Author, only nearness. Calamy's almanacks woke because three thousand copies of one book
stand on three thousand nails within earshot of each other every year. The Two Books say so; the brown history says
so; and Tobias Hask, the binder who came looking for the Author, has to be told so, or he steps into the shaft.

## The law of the Stacks

**What is written does not stay written.** A letter, a register, a book of one's own, carried in the Stacks for two
hours, comes back saying something else, in the same hand and with the same confidence. Only the Library's own inks
hold: Calam-ink, and the ink that wells up at the Wellhead. The law is warned of three times before it bites (the
chained guide-book, Crake, the Catalogue; a fourth time in Fenwick's report in the Provost's study), and it has teeth:

| Carried in | Becomes | And so |
|---|---|---|
| Hester Rhule's register of the burned | titles of books never burned or never written | the burned cannot be released by its names until Rhule writes it out again from memory ("I have never forgotten one") |
| Absalom Gallon's *Effusions* | good poems: very good | they no longer consent to the Kindling fire; the year-book is lost (comedy, and a real cost) |
| Jonquil's letter, Orme's chapters, the Canto's lines | something else | (felt, and a warning) |
| **your own name**, if you sign the lending slip | somebody else's | the lent way opens straight to the Rotunda, and the company loses its standing in the Parliament until the Catalogue hears its name *said aloud* (the Stacks cannot alter what is said, only what is written) |

Sealed passages, shown and never opened, carry the illusion of depth that in the Library is not an illusion: a hot
iron stair TO THE LIBRARY OF ASH; a gallery roped off with black silk whose every empty case is labelled TESSALY; a door
ajar on THE LIBRARY IN WHICH EVERY BOOK IS THE SAME BOOK, where somebody far in is weeping with happiness; a gallery
chained shut since 1104 whose books read their readers, and whose shelves lean inward, listening.

The Borgesian ambiguity is deliberate and unresolved: the *Chronology, Corrected* contradicts Pardoe's slab and the
Index's own account of its burning; the two true histories contradict each other; the Cyclopaedia is describing a
world into existence. The adventure never says which history is true. (The Librarian of the First Terrace says the
true history of every library is "the dullest reading in creation, and the most consoling".)

## Who is there

- **Dr. Aurelian Fenwick**, Fellow of the Lyceum (the Academy), went through Crake's door eleven years ago with a
  pencil and two sandwiches to map the Stacks, and believes it has been nine weeks; his hexagon is papered with
  plans that move as you look. Tell him the years (*Fridays*: the bedmaker still airs his room); give him ink that
  holds (Calam-ink, or Wellhead ink fetched from the spring) and he draws *one true plan* in eleven years, with a
  short way to the Hexagon of Doors and the spiral's step-count; he signed the lending slip in his first week and is
  not sure of his own name; he joins as a companion and walks home to the quad, where a student drops her book.
- **Tobias Hask**, master binder, expelled by Warden Mott for Sewing books with the words backward so they would talk
  to him; certain that every book is a page of One Book with a living Author at the centre. He murdered the
  Concordance of Every Word by opening it at the spine, because it answered AUTHOR: *not found*, and he could not
  believe it. He stands at the white lectern in the Rotunda, the false heart of the Library, reading the blank book
  that always writes what he wants. His **ends**: told *with proof* (the Two Books, the brown history) that there is
  no Author, only nearness, he sits down on the stolen quires, a big man suddenly old ("I could have just put two on
  a shelf"), and Sews the Concordance back himself; told *flatly*, he steps into the shaft ("they always hide the
  important books at the bottom"); accused before the Catalogue, he is *corrected* by the Errata; beaten (his Bound
  Man, a golem of glued pages, cut down), he gives up the quires and is taken.
- **Signature XVII** (AUSPICE to AUTUMN), cut out of the Concordance and sold on the Remainderman's stall, the quire
  that holds the entry AUTHOR: *no occurrences*. Bought for an hour of your reading, it becomes a **companion**:
  acerbic, precise, pocket-sized ("Not with anything written. I'll correct it."), and it knows a way between any two
  hexagons that hold the same word, because the Library is folded. When the Concordance is whole it can go home into
  the column, or stay with you, "looking at one thing at a time", to see AUTUMN: not the word, the thing.
- **The Two Books**, a grammar and a book of tides shelved together for an afternoon in the reign of somebody, who
  now finish each other's sentences and say the same word at once and laugh at it.
- **The library ladder**, which remembers everyone who climbed it and that nobody listens to the ladder; **the
  reading-lamp of the Concordance**, still lit, with nobody to read by it, the murder's witness.
- **The Concordance of Every Word**, restored: "AUTHOR: no occurrences. READER: every page." It gives every
  occurrence of PARDOE, R., in order, which is what one needs to read the Index whole: the Stacks' answer to the Ash.

## The murder of the Concordance

A detective thread with five clues, a red herring and four resolutions. The **red herring** is the Index's Penknife
(a censor's margin-cut); the **body** shows instead a binder's cut, at the fold. **Clues**: the cut (examine the body);
a length of waxed linen with the Stitchgate twist (search the hexagon); the lamp's testimony (a heavy man with a
binder's hands, a bone folder, and a paper man carrying the quires); the Remainderman's admission (bought from a
binder in a hurry who said they had lied to him); and Signature XVII's own entry. Any thread, a witness or a fence, and
a cut or the entry, with the binder's name from anywhere (the ladder, Crake, Mott, the prospectus) makes the case.
**Resolutions**: accuse him to the Catalogue; confront and convince him; confront and break him; fight his Bound Man.
Sewn back by Hask (redemption) or by **Hester Rhule**, the binder's daughter, if she is with you ("He taught me on a
psalter, when they wouldn't let girls in the Company").

## Puzzles, traps, enemies

- **The spiral stair** adds a step for anyone who goes down without counting aloud, and the added step goes to the
  Hexagon of the Untitled, among the **silverfish** (forearm-long, scaled, mouths full of half-digested letters).
- **The Hexagon of Mirrors**: every way out is a reflection; read the gilt inscription over your shoulder (*THE
  CENTRE IS WHERE THE READER STANDS*), or work it out letter by letter if you are a scholar; the mirror's lesson
  (Mirror Image) stays with you.
- **The lending desk**: a real shortcut, for your name.
- **The Bound Man** at the Rotunda (fight, or let Hask call it off by asking him who he is).
- **The shaft**: no bottom; voices of a library reading itself aloud, and under that, something burning.

## The doors: deep planar magic

- **The Book of the Damned** (LIBER DAMNATORUM): a list of names signed in bad faith, struck out and written again;
  its last line is a door to **Cthelll**, the furnace-city of the damned, which is *administered*: iron counters, grey
  clerks with pens lit like tapers, a bell every hour at which every clerk turns a page. The way back is to sign their
  ledger (your name will be written in Cthelll every night, and struck out, and written again; their receipt teaches
  Doom Spiral, a corridor that turns back on itself) or to find the red stair nobody mentions and climb it saying the
  Lamp prayer (faith, rites, or the Lamp's taper). The clerks know of **the Last Index**: Pardoe corresponds with three
  censors of other cities under their Commonplaces, and "we are rather looking forward to the rest".
- **The Book of the Exalted** (LIBER EXALTATORUM): it reads you first, and opens only for the good, as the Mount
  reckons good: by deeds (a cure, a bestiary quieted, the burned released, children read to, a house sent home, a
  widow's book heard) and by the lies you did not tell (Rhule) and the harms you did not do (the Lexicon freed, Hask
  sent into the shaft). It opens on **the lowest terrace of the Mount**, where every book is true and nobody reads
  them, and seven terraces go up to a library with no books, "because there is nothing there that needs to be
  written down"; the Librarian of the First Terrace blesses you ("Keep your place. That is all a blessing is.";
  Anoint), and says of the libraries under yours: "Every library has a basement."

## Threads that point beyond

The Last Index; Tessaly's empty gallery and its refugees in the Fore-edge; the Commonplaces of Hallam (brass, ledgers,
silent), Orsk (under ice, never called to Parliament) and the Salt Towns (charts, mostly water); the Library of the
Same Book; the gallery that reads its readers, closed since 1104; the Lamp, which the *Chronology, Corrected* says
Ambery brought out already lit from someone on the far side "who had kept it since the first book"; the Great
Erratum, of which the Errata are larvae; Pardoe, who may never have been burned at all.
