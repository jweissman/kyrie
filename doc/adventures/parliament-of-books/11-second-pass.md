# The second pass: a harder reading, and what it changed

*Written after rereading every file and every transcript with one question: is there genuine depth here, or a
pleasant surface of invention? The first critique ([09](09-critique.md)) was about craft. This one is about form.*

## The verdict on the first version

**It was a chamber piece wearing a supermodule's coat.** The invention was dense and mostly well-judged; the prose
was restrained; the central conceit (the festival is the treaty, degraded) had real shape. But:

1. **There was no adversary with a will.** The Recall was a motion, the Imprimatur an institution, Rhule a person to
   be persuaded. Nothing in the world *wanted* anything badly enough to come and get it. The supermodules this
   commission invokes (the slavers' trail from a hill-giant's steading down to the Abyss) are built on a hidden
   enemy whose plan the players uncover a layer at a time. Calamy had a misunderstanding, not an enemy.
2. **The cosmology had a heaven and no underworld.** The Commonplace was a country of attention, beams of light,
   gentle fading. Every image was benign. A cosmology without a shadow has no pressure in it; wonder without dread
   reads as whimsy, and whimsy was the pulp risk.
3. **The darkest theme was introduced and dropped.** The *knife* (excision as mercy and violence: Pardoe was right
   about the seventeenth chapter) was the most morally interesting idea in the adventure, and it lived in two topics.
4. **Rhule's arc was evidential but not tragic.** She changed by being shown things; nothing she had *done* was
   implicated in the danger. Her secret night reading of the excisions was a mood, not a mechanism.
5. **The session was a tally.** Its outcome was known by the time it was called.
6. **Two readings were straw** (the Censors' discretion, no reading owed): nobody wanted them.

### Where the prose was pulp

Specific, not general: a reflexive *very* (125 uses: "very old", "very small", "very quiet"), now 68, the rest kept
where they mean something ("very straight", the year-book going up into the smoke and Rhule at the brazier, is a
deliberate echo). "Tangerine and church" used three times; now twice, and the two are the same plant on purpose (the
calamus root and the Physick's leaf are the same cure). The whimsical registers (the floor that says "Briefly";
fifty bad poems that say "tell him heart and tart was the best thing in us") are kept, because each has a cost
attached; the decorative ones without a cost were the problem, and the cure was not to cut them but to give the
world a weight they could float on.

## What changed: the Ash

The second pass adds one thing, grown out of what was already there, and threads it through everything.

**Books burned without their leave do not go home.** For three hundred years of Thursdays, and every Kindling since
Doble's father's press, they have gone *down*: under the Foxing, past a scorched edge where the paper stops, to the
**Cinderfield**, a plain of ash where the burned stand in ranks the size of books, whispering *not read, not asked*.

**Something is drilling them.** In 1140 Censor-General Robert Pardoe, the best reader the Lyceum ever had (he could
see what every line might do, to whom, in a hundred years, and could not bear it), had himself burned at the
Kindling with his Index, the register of everything he had cut: the only burning by leave in three hundred years.
What came down was **the Index of Pardoe**: a reasonable, grieving, terrible book that wants to *finish*. A text that
can be trusted is one that says what it said the day before. On Kindling night three thousand almanacks will go into
the fire without their leave, and come to it, and it will have enough: it goes up the smoke and cuts Calamy clean.

**It lives on one reader.** One beam of attention falls on the Cinderfield, nightly, from the Close: Hester Rhule,
reading the excised pages in her kitchen because somebody should read once what was cut. She is the Index's only
light. And among the burned is a book of hours with her father's Needle Street knot on it, which says *Hessie* in his
voice: the book that woke on his bench when she was fourteen, and that a frightened street burned with him. Every
Thursday since, she has been sending them to it.

### The strategic adversary, and the ways to answer it

The Index is an enemy with a plan (the Kindling), a regiment that grows with every Thursday (after the second
burning its pickets come up out of the char into the Foxing, where the Primer is), an agent it does not know it has
(Rhule's candle), a political move (on Kindling eve the burned march up the gangway and the Index **claims a seat in
the Parliament** for those who were never asked; the Roll, bound by procedure, rules the claim in order), and a
devil's bargain (carry the Censors' discretion and it will keep its ranks in the field; the straw reading now has a
patron and a price). Every answer has more than one form:

| To | By force | By craft or courage | By understanding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pass the burned** | fight the pickets and the ranks (a lone level-8 soldier wins two times in three) | carry a taper lit at the Lamp, the one fire every book consented to, from Canon Abery | (the burned are not the enemy; see below) |
| **Release the burned** | | read them their names, aloud, from Rhule's register of every title she burned (she lends it if she trusts you; it is in her room if she does not) | ask Rhule to say them herself; she never forgot one |
| **Answer the claim in the chamber** | a soldier drives the burned out of the gangway | carry the Lamp's flame down among them | read the burned their names there; or Rhule, beside you, turns her back on the Index and says them |
| **End the Index** | past the Penknife (a censor's knife grown arm-long), into its own furnace (it says, going in, "Without its leave") | read it aloud whole, every cut and every reason, until it is a history and not a list; it is *entered* by the Catalogue | tell Rhule her reading is its only light: she pinches out her candle ("Let it see how it likes being unread"), and a day and a half later it foxes like any unread book |
| **Buy it off** | | carry *that the Censors may burn as they judge*: no Year of Ash, and burnings on Thursdays for ever | |

If nothing is done and the almanacks burn, the new worst ending is **the Year of Ash**: the smoke does not go up;
something goes through Calamy with a knife, a little every night; words missing from wills, a gap where the Sixth
Plea was, children who cannot remember the third line of the rhyme.

### The themes, now carried through

- **Consent.** Sleep by leave; burning by leave; the year-book that gives itself; the Lamp, the one fire every book
  agreed to; Pardoe's own burning, the only one by leave; the burned, never asked. The adversary is the theme's
  shadow: an absolute refusal of consent in the name of safety.
- **Attention as life.** Beams are readers; the unread fox; the burned are healed by being *read*, even only their
  names; the Index dies of inattention. Rhule's reading is mercy and fuel at once.
- **The knife.** Orme's seventeenth chapter (rightly cut); her fifteenth (wrongly); Rhule's cut book with its ladder
  of stubs; the Penknife; the Year of Ash as the knife without a hand. The adventure now asks whether any cut can be
  justified and answers *some*, which is harder than *none*.
- **Copies and children.** Last year's almanack in the front rank, trying to find "half past"; the Almanack: "Every
  year Calamy throws me on the fire and I come here and forget the tides. I'd like to be wrong about one whole spring."

## The acts, as they now move

1. **The ordinary business of books** (days 1–3; the Hithe, the bridge, Stitchgate). Arrival, the Almanack, the mills,
   the bond. *Plant*: the first Thursday burning (day 2), the shriek in the pocket, the ash in lines, the pepper verse.
2. **The quickening** (days 2–6; the Close, Newmarket, the hill). The Court, the Ordinance, the Awls, the Pleas.
   Reversals: the Sewing was a treaty; the Kindling is its rite. *Plant*: Pardoe's slab, Quarles on Pardoe, the
   register, Rhule's kitchen.
3. **The Commonplace** (any time once a way is found). Wonder: the Reading Room, the Market, the Fore-edge, the members
   in their places, the Wellhead's reversal (*WE ASK TO SLEEP*).
4. **The Cinderfield** (the descent). Dread: the burned edge, the ranks, last year's almanack, the father's book, the
   one beam, the hearth and the Index. Reversal: the danger to Calamy is not the waking books but what its fear has
   made of the ones it burned, and Rhule has been feeding it.
5. **The Parliament** (Kindling eve). The session now has a turn: the burned come up the gangway and the Index claims
   a seat, in order. What the company did below decides what it can do here.
6. **The Kindling, and the morning after.**

## Is it comparable to the supermodules?

In *shape*, now yes: an urban investigation that opens onto another plane, which opens onto that plane's underworld,
where a strategic enemy with a plan and an army waits, with a climax that brings the enemy up into the political
heart of the story. In *scale*, no, and honestly so: one city of about eighty rooms, a Plane of twenty-one, nine days,
one adversary. The giants-to-drow-to-Abyss arc is three regions, three enemies and a dozen sessions per region.

## Is it playable and compelling as written?

**Playable**, yes: every ending is reached by journeys a player could take, each answer to the Ash has a test, and the
autoplay laboratory keeps the whole sound. **Compelling** in its best scenes (the first burning, the Wellhead, the
father's book, the Almanack at the fire, the chamber's turn), and thinner in its middle errands, as [09](09-critique.md)
says. What I am still not happy with:

- The Cinderfield is three rooms. It should be a country: the ranks by century, the Tessaly refugees' camp, the
  furnace that is also a door upward.
- The Index's bargain is offered but there is no scene in which a member argues *for* it (the Roll, which loves
  procedure, should be tempted).
- The Almanack still has no choice in act IV; it witnesses. It should be able to *go down* in place of the three
  thousand, as a year-book, and the game should let it.
- Combat remains one roll of the auto-resolver. The Penknife deserves a fight with a shape (it cuts *words*: a
  spell-caster loses a prepared spell per hit).

## If I had to ten-times the ambition

I would start with the **sealed boxes in the Imprimatur**. Pardoe wrote to the censors of four cities for eight years;
three answered. That is the campaign: every city that writes has a Commonplace (the Catalogue says so: Hallam's of
ledgers, brass-lit and silent; Orsk's under ice, where no Parliament has ever been called; the Salt Towns' of
sea-charts, mostly water; Tessaly's, burned four hundred years ago, whose refugees camped in Calamy's Fore-edge for a
century). Each has its own censor, its own fire, its own field of ash. **The Index is not one book.** It is a
correspondence: four registers that have been writing to each other under the world for two hundred years, and the
Cinderfields are connected below the Commonplaces like cellars under a street. The supermodule is a descent through
four cities' underworlds to the place where the four Indexes meet: **the Last Index**, a furnace at the
bottom of all writing where everything ever cut is kept, and something is drafting a clean text of the whole world.
Calamy is module one: the slavers' trail. Tessaly, which was cut entirely, is the drow city: a whole Commonplace that
survives only as ash and refugees. The final module is the furnace: the only way to end it is to read it, all of it,
aloud, and someone has to stay down there to do it. Rhule would.

That is out of scope for this branch; the hooks for it are in.
