# Three Nights at Sallowfield

*Adventure VI of [the anthology](README.md). A defence. Module `data/adventures/sallowfield/` (province "the vale of the Brimble", west of the first country), campaign `data/campaigns/sallowfield.campaign.json`, spec `test/sallowfield.test.ts`, friction in [friction-sallowfield.md](friction-sallowfield.md). 7 November 2026. Form and ending rules: [forms.md](forms.md); self-check: [craft-checklist.md](craft-checklist.md).*

## What it is

A coach has cracked its axle at the ford of a village called Sallowfield, and the smith says three days. The passengers are three strangers who are not investigators, not soldiers, and not anybody's heroes: they are, for three days, the only people in the vale who have seen how a company of soldiers is put together. Forty of the Margrave's border-men, unpaid since spring and led by a captain named Tobin Wyre, are coming down the east road to the only granary in the vale. Sallowfield has sixty people, a mill, a chapel with a bell, a tithe granary, a reeve who writes things down, and one child with a sling.

The party's time is spent on the village. Nobody is asked to save it. The reeve will tell them, plainly, that they may walk on south.

## Why it is not the others

| | The usual shape | This |
|---|---|---|
| **Who asks** | A quest-giver, a case, a contract | Nobody. The party is stranded, and the village is the thing in front of them |
| **What the clock is** | A deadline to beat | A visitor who arrives on schedule and **reads** what the party has done |
| **What the time is spent on** | Travelling to a place of interest | Fortifying real places, recruiting named villagers, scouting, and deciding what to give up |
| **What the opposition is** | A monster that attacks, or a puzzle that waits | A captain with a plan, a reason and a weakness, who is told things by his scouts and changes his thrust |
| **What a fight is** | One die at the climax | Four posts, each a different engagement, each decided by what stands there; and then three armoured men who can be talked to, or not |
| **What losing is** | A game over | Most of the sixteen endings cost something; one is the company's own death; none is a dead end |

## The map

Twenty-two rooms in four areas. The village is the middle; the river is the front; the marsh and the Scar are the edge of the world.

- **East bank** — the east road (start), the ford stones, the parley stone (a standing stone, a hundred yards up the road).
- **Sallowfield** — the green (well, geese, a chapel at one end), the Ploughshare taproom, Kersey's forge, the tithe granary, the chapel of Saint Eadred (nave, bell loft, tower roof), Quarmby's mill yard, loft and race, the packhorse bridge, the ford meadow, the well.
- **Upvale** — the high track, the sheep fold (Crowther), the Scar lookout.
- **Lowvale** — Greenhalgh's farm, the reed marsh (a causeway of hurdles, the one way a stranger comes in unseen), the south road (the way out).

Four posts are fought over on the third night: the **ford stones**, the **bridge**, the **mill yard** and the **nave**. Every other room is something to do before then, or somewhere to stand.

## What the party's time is spent on

Two days and three nights: the clock runs from watch 0 (08:00 on day 1) to watch 366 (21:00 on day 3), about sixty-one hours, of which perhaps forty-nine are the company's. The works below, with the walking between them, come to about eighty-one (a full run in the explorer finished at watch 489, and a spec plays most of the list and arrives after he has come). Nothing is enough for all of it.

A villager can be asked to **hold a post** (Ashworth, Wadsworth, Quarmby, Greenhalgh, Kersey, Elland...). Posted people are in the clash by name and **die by name**.

## How Wyre reads the party

Wyre is never on the map. He is what the party's works *look like* from the east road:

- Night one, his riders look at the ford. If someone is there to be looked at (a posted villager, the company), they report a watch and he comes on day 3. If no one is, he comes **a day early**, on the second night, with less time for everything.
- What the riders see (a barricade, a cut, a flood, a man in the tower) becomes **thrust** at each post: a barricade draws him to the bridge, a cut to the ford, both to the mill. The flood he does not see; the pits he does not see; the grain he sees only if the boy tells him.
- The boy in the reeds on night two is Lowes. If he walks out of the reeds loose, Wyre knows what stood **at that moment** (a wall built after is not known to him). If the party takes him they choose what he carries: the truth (Wyre knows the same), a lie (that the ford is the soft place: Wyre leans on the ford, and the mill and bridge are left alone), or nothing (he stays in the cellar and Wyre plans blind). Measured: wall + cut, boy loose → thrust ford 6, bridge 2, mill 12; boy sent back with a lie → ford 14, bridge 2, mill 0; boy never reports → the base plan 12/7/3.
- At noon on the third day he comes to the standing stone and says what he wants. The party can pay him (the reeve's sixty, Mowbray's plate, the grain), refuse, lie, or bribe Fothergill.
- The lieutenant wants the village burned, and wants Wyre's job. That is the weakness.

## The third night

Two waves, and an hour between them that is the player's.

1. **The wear** (dusk): each post loses a quarter of what Wyre sent at it.
2. **The first wave** (+1 h): at each of the four posts, defence + two for each posted villager still able + the drill + (13 if the company stands there, 4 if not; in the second wave 11 and 3) is set against that post's thrust and a die. A post that holds loses a few people; one that falls loses many, by name.
3. **The company's hour**: the reports come in, some late (the nave's), some wrong (a horn at a bridge that has not fallen). The company may *hold a post to the last* (+7, and its people do not come back), *give a post up* (the enemy's, without a second fight and without its dead), *burn the bridge behind the men on it* (needs the one pot of pitch, which is also what fouls the grain; spares the nave the road a fallen bridge opened), *light the causeway* (the second wave's horse goes into the marsh, but the company is not at a post), *ring the bell* (+2 everywhere), *give out Ellen's arrows* (+3 at one post), or simply *walk to another post*.
4. **The second wave** (+2 h) goes where the first opened a road: a held ford sends it to the mill, a fallen bridge to the nave, a fallen mill to the nave; a post that fell the first time is passed, and a post that stood is fought for again with what is left of it.
5. **Fire and the push inward**; **the picked men** (+3 h) come to the company wherever it is. They are negotiators with their hands on their hilts: argue them off (a fair check), yield them the granary, draw on them (a hard check, and a lost one is the end of the company), or do nothing, and they take the granary and go. All three end differently on the page the next morning.

The player chooses *where the company stands*, *whom to post where*, *what to build*, and then, between the waves, *what to give up*.

## The hours

Works take hours (the wall eighteen, the pits fourteen, the caltrops nine, the drill ten each, the helpless six, the bucket line two, the sixty sixteen, the cut and the flood fewer) and the page says, in bands, how many hours there are before the night he is expected. ## The faces

- **Davy Pinn**, twelve, with a sling. Asks if he is too young. Joins; sits on the tower roof. Lives or does not.
- **Ellen Cragg**, widow, counting arrows in a church. Joins; fetches her husband's bow from Greenhalgh's barn; allocates the arrows at the battle window.
- **Father Mowbray**, priest, swears. Would he kill? *No, come and keep the hurt alive.* Gives the plate or keeps it. Raises one of the fallen if the rite takes.
- **Gil Quarmby**, miller, will not leave. Holds the mill; agrees to the flood only with a key and a grudge.
- **Aldred Stansfield**, reeve, writes everything down. Knows what the village can pay.
- **Hob Wadsworth**, boaster; breaks on the night unless steadied.
- **Peg Kersey**, smith. **Nan Ashworth**, the Ploughshare. **Ned Greenhalgh** (the coachman is Amos Teasdale), who will not leave his cows. **Tib Elland**, tithe-clerk, holds a door with a tally-stick. **Crowther**, the shepherd, who sees things.
- **Captain Tobin Wyre**, **Lieutenant Fothergill**, **Sergeant Voll**, and **Kit Lowes**, nineteen, in a coat that is too big.

## 2. Authored content present but not reachable (or not specced)

- The early assault is played by a spec; a posted villager who is at the ford or the bridge counts as watching, which one `Ask X to hold the ford` (an hour) satisfies, and that is still open.
- **Cragg's bow** (`fetch_bow` from Greenhalgh's barn; the arrow allocation); **Pinn on the roof**; **Mowbray's rite** over a fallen villager; **Wadsworth breaking** unless steadied; the **bell**; **Elland's door**; the **night-2 raid on the farm** and Greenhalgh's evacuation; **`bluff_wyre`**; **`let_voll_look`**; the **hard interrogation**; the **picked men parleyed with** (the spec argues them off, but does not exercise the failed check); the **bribe's failed check**.
- Three companions' ending variants are Observes at dawn; only one combination was ever seen in a played run.

## 3. Actions or consequences the engine cannot yet represent

- **Dynamic counts in prose.** "Thirty-odd" in the evacuation is fixed; the page cannot say "eleven of them".
- **A battlefield.** The four posts are a table, not a map. The clash is one comparison per post, not a back-and-forth; nothing the player does *during* it changes it.
- **Wyre moving.** He has no location: the "adversary" is a set of measures and flags. A captain who could be seen on the road, or intercepted, needs a pursuit with a start time (see friction in the Wrong Boat), and the shape of this adventure (he reads, he does not move) did not fit one.
- **A wounded survivor as a state** that outlives the night and changes an ending (Cragg alive but blind). Only dead or able.
- **Companions who can be killed by the engine** and then *raised* by Mowbray: the rite exists as an action on the fallen; no persisted "raised" body, only a flag.
- **A group that can be defeated without the book closing** (the company fell is an Ending; there is no way to continue as the survivors).

## 4. Existing assets whose integration is incomplete

- No new species, ancestries or artwork. The party is the stock elf, human and dwarf from the campaign scaffold.
- The scenes are text; there is no ambience (`Observe … as sound`) for the mill, the river or the night.
- The country marker is `wyre`, who is not on the map until the parley; the skyline line describes the vale, not the captain.
- `Pursuit` is not used; Wyre's riders and raiders are `Arrives` persons. The pursuit that would hunt the party through the vale is unbuilt.
- Companions have three beats each but no authored *leave* scene.

## After the hostile reviews

First review: the boy's report is a snapshot, a held boy carries what the player says, `felled` records the dead (companions read as `dead` from the hour they join), the fates and the ending texts are read before the book closes, the night's reports are gated on who is posted, the peace costs ten coin, the axle is two days.

Second review: the night is two waves with a company's hour between them (above); works are longer and the hours are shown; the riders ride the valley as `Pursuit`s (the Scar road in the morning, the standing stone at noon, the east road in the afternoon, the ford at dusk) and Crowther's count of them changes; the picked men are negotiators, so a wait no longer kills the company by walking past their warning; the Scar count can be wrong (a failed count is sixty armed men and a levy that loses heart); the bell, the helpless lost, the bell silenced, the grain taken and the plate kept all read at dawn; the voices of Kersey, Davy, Quarmby, Mowbray and Greenhalgh differ; Wadsworth speaks on the bridge; Cragg's arrows are forty-one; Wyre no longer explains the moral.

Still open: the engine cannot stop a wait for a *report* (friction 8), so the long waits are survivable rather than interrupted; the company's hour is five actions and a walk, not a map; the casualty lottery is still dice; the first-night watch is satisfied by one posted villager standing at the ford or bridge (posting now costs an hour and puts them there), not by the company.

Third review (round 2): the sixty is real and must be collected; a peace with the lieutenant unbought ends with the mill burned anyway, one with him bought holds; fouling the grain raises Wyre's thrust, and he withdraws only if the boy told him *and* Voll was walked through it; the wall takes eighteen hours, the pits fourteen, the drill ten each, the farm raid and the boy come in the same watch; the Scar lights are talked about by Crowther and Greenhalgh from the second morning; a failed seizure sends the boy off with what he saw; a failed kind word allows a hard one; Wyre's weight shows from the tower, the Scar and the east road; the dawn says whether he guessed right; the bribe costs its ten coin whether or not it takes; yielding after a failed argument costs the bell; the roof can fall; Mowbray's arms add a point of drill; five voices were rewritten.

Fourth review: weariness (the engine's, eighteen hours awake) now bites: a point off every post, a point of drill, and the nerve of the villagers who would be posted; a night in camp mends it. Fouling the grain needs the reeve told, costs the villagers' nerve and sends Tib Elland up the fold, raises thrust, withdraws Wyre only from a village that has built something, and ends in the mill's burning unless the lieutenant is bought. Posting takes an hour and puts the person at the post. The Scar count, failed, is a false count that costs defence. The bell falls with the nave in the first wave, so its cost lands before the second. Giving the picked men the granary is its own ending. The cows take an afternoon. Hob Wadsworth's line is on Hob. The Observes of Wyre's weight include the nave and keep off the east road in the hour of the parley.

Fifth review: the raiders at the farm are takers (`Peaceable`), not a wall across the south road; before this a company that walked south after the raid was killed with no ending. Walking on is now specced on day 2, day 3 and with the night begun, and any fall of the whole company is an ending. A failed look from Voll no longer counts as a look and the boy alone no longer withdraws Wyre. Withdrawal now needs the wall as well as the evacuation or the bucket line; the sixty costs sixteen hours and a failed collection cannot pay. The flood costs Greenhalgh's hay, not the granary. The Scar count is one try, and a false one is paid at the clash. Weariness is paid at the clash, so a sleep before it avoids the bill and nothing is restored for free. The Captain's Own needs the wall or the cut.

Sixth review: the doc and the code agree on the endings (sixteen, all specced), the flood (+3, −4 at the bridge, drowns the pits), the sixty (sixteen hours), the Next Granary gate, and the Scar count (even odds, +2 or −1). The company's weight at a post is 13 in the first wave and 11 in the second; in an eight-seed sweep with the skill checks forced and one decision in the company's hour (Ellen's arrows at the ford), the best-prepared company took "Held, at a Cost" seven times in eight and "What Was Left" once; without the arrows, six in eight. With the checks unforced the route is much less likely, because it needs many of them to pass. Arguing the picked men off is a fair, not a hard, check, so the best ending is not a coin flip at the end; drawing on them is still a gamble that can end the company.

Seventh review: asking Wyre what he is owed (`wyre_owed`) now costs a point of drill and takes two off his thrust at the ford, the bridge and the mill; refusing him ("Nothing. We will not pay") adds a point to his thrust at all four posts and two to the drill. Neither dominates (owed is better at the three posts and one worse at the nave), and a spec compares them. The flood also sends the second wave round by the high ground: +3 thrust at the mill and the nave in the second wave, on any route. Drawing on the raiders is not offered once the farm has burned. The fates close on different beats. "Draw on the four with the sacks" is a hard brawn check; the page's odds label is generated from the check, and I did not alter it.
