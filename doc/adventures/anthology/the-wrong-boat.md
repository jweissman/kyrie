# The Wrong Boat

*Adventure V of [the anthology](README.md). A pursuit. Module `data/adventures/wrongboat/` (province "the Aldwater", south of the first country), campaign `data/campaigns/wrongboat.campaign.json`, spec `test/wrongboat.test.ts`. 7 November 2026. Form and ending rules: [forms.md](forms.md).*

## What it is

The company has paid for passage on the *Patient Maud*. The Maud has gone. A landlady with a kitchen on fire presses a hamper on them for the Maud's cook, and they step aboard the *Patient Mary*, which looks the same. Under the seed-cakes in the hamper is a music box. The Mary's owner, Hob Garrod, took it from a dead bargeman's widow against a debt, left it at the Heron to be carried to a buyer, and went off to be locked up for eleven shillings. The widow, Bess Lowther, a toll-clerk owed three months' wages, has followed it to the quay; two of the Trust's lads, sent by a sergeant who has spotted that it is worth money, are coming by another road. Nobody is asking the company to find out anything. The company has to get down a river, over a hill and out of the story, with a thing they cannot put down, and a clock that is not theirs.

## Why it is not the others

| | The usual shape | This |
|---|---|---|
| The party | licensed, competent, welcomed | passengers who got on the wrong boat |
| The time | spent asking and gathering | spent *waiting* (a barge keeps a timetable), *moving* and *being seen* |
| The pressure | a deadline set by the author's festival | **a chaser with a clock of her own** (Bess, by the road, with Plans 24 steps) and a second one the party can sweep, pay or fool (the lads) |
| What makes the climax possible | evidence laid before a body | a place and a moment: the fen mist, the fair crowd, the relief sluice, the ferry |
| The end | a settlement | one of nine private endings: a kitchen, a fiasco, a theft that cannot be proved, a friend left on a stage |

The Mary's four rooms are carried from landing to landing by a timetable (`stageNow`): at Staithe to +9 watches, afloat, at the fen +36–50, at Pelham +90–120, at the race-head +150–170, at Highfoot for good from +200. Each plank opens only where she lies. A company that steps ashore at the fen is a company the barge will leave.

## Pressure and play

- **Bess** walks the chain of pursuits `bess_fen` → `bess_fair` → `bess_mill` → `bess_stage` → `bess_foot` (priorities 1–5; at each, the highest whose time has come). She is always somewhere, she is told of in rumours (some false: Jack Parr, at the Heron, is sure she went up the Maud), and when she is in the company's room she **sees** them unless they are hiding, disguised or in the fen mist. Hiding is a roll of the dice once per visit. Meeting her is a conversation: a question put to the player, three answers (give it, name Hob and bring her along, tell her it is not hers to ask), and two actions with rolls (swear it is cakes; run).
- **The lads** take the back road to the weir. A territorial pair: a real fight, with four ways round it: the relief sluice (sweeps them off the apron), fifteen coin, surrendering the box, or not being at the weir when they are.
- **Information that changes:** the quay-warden's report, Parr's mistake, Noll's "behind you now, though the mist is a great liar".
- **False trail:** a ration-cake in a dishcloth in the eel-skiff sends Bess to the eel-stage.
- **Disguise** (the slop-shop: a bargeman, or a bridegroom in lavender), **hiding** (hold, bell-cot loft, puppet booth, the miller's kitchen), **the fen mist** (thick from +30 to +90 on the causeway).

## The box
A music box in the shape of a loaf, brass-cornered, which plays *The Miller's Daughter* — a song the whole river sings with a hundred verses added, and which Tom Lowther wrote for his wife. Wind it twice; the third time, a drawer: an iron ring, a wage-slip (seven months unpaid on the Mary), and scratched in the back-plate *B.L. from T.L., the day we were wed.* It can be kept, sold (fifty coin to the dealer, forty to Mr Fosse at the Last Pint who is the buyer Hob meant it for), dropped in the river, traded (to a young gentleman for a horse), surrendered, given back, or carried to the gap. It is never a plot coupon: no door opens with it, and nobody needs to know what it is, which is part of the pleasure.

## Faces
Dolly Prout (skipper, boisterous), Mrs Brabant the cook (cries into the soup and means it), Shim Garrod the mate (nineteen, shouts the news of the river to nobody), Mr Peregrine Dally (a young gentleman on the wrong barge for the right reason), Mother Cobbold, Jack Parr, Una Marchbank (the ferrywoman who speaks in few words), Joel Sunderland the dealer, Orpha Tunney the slop-seller, Gabriel Hoby the puppet-man (his villain is whoever you name), Noll Fairley the bellman, Tabitha and Wilfred Auster at the mill, Barnaby Fosse at the Last Pint.
**Companions with arcs:** *Shim* (interview: whose side are you on when your uncle is wrong; at Pelham: what do you do about the man in the lock-up; at the hill: he stops, because of what he has done to his uncle, or goes on) and *Mr Dally* (a question about love; the church bell at the fair; the choice to go or to bolt; the horse).
**Conversations that are interactions:** Cobbold's *which barge*, Dolly's fare, Brabant's *have you lost a thing*, Shim's side, Shim's uncle, Dally's love, Una's *what are you carrying*, Tunney's *what do you want to be taken for*, Hoby's *name me a villain*, Noll's *how long would you wait*, Fosse's *who are you to sell it*, Bess's *where is it*.
**Side episodes that do not connect to the main line:** Hoby's play and the villain the player names; Noll's bell and the question about waiting; the miller's boy's sum (forty thousand sacks); the greased fair itself.

## Endings (and what each is)
*The Lowther Kitchen* (given back; a latch in the music). *Bess at the Gap* (she walks with you, and takes it back down alone). *Down the Weir* (the lads left to the water). *Without Shim* (the boy on the stage with his hand up). *Clean Water* (box in your pack, nobody behind you). *Fifty Coin and No Questions*. *Empty Hands*. *A Glorious Bad Bargain* (a horse that will not go forwards, a wedding that goes off anyway). *Forty Coin and a Back-plate* (you sold it, and read the words after). *Taken at the Stage*. None settles a society.

## 1. Content that works in play
The mistake and the hamper; the barge timetable and gangplanks; the chase clock (Bess's five pursuits, the lads' two); being seen, hiding, disguise, the mist, the false trail; Bess's conversation; the sluice; the ferry; the nine endings; Shim's interview and Dally's.

## 2. Authored content present but not reachable (or not specced)
Hiding in the hold, the bell-cot or the puppet booth *with Bess in the room* (the rules are written and linted; the die is not forced in any journey); `swear_false` and `flee_bess` as rolls; Dolly's sing; Mrs Brabant's confession (played by hand); Dally's church (`dally_go`) and the wedding; Shim's visit to his uncle; Hoby's play; Tabitha's pie; the miller's sum; pay_lads; `drop_box`; the `Empty Hands`, `Bess at the Gap` and `Clean Water` endings (reachable, not specced); a fight with the lads.

## 3. Actions or consequences the engine cannot yet represent
- A **moving place**: the Mary is four rooms and five gangplanks and a timetable, not a thing that goes. (`ferry` passages open by a measure; the circus's friction 1 applies.)
- A **pursuer who is sighted at a distance**: rumours are Topics keyed on where Bess is; a company cannot look down a road and see her coming.
- A **search of a room**: hiding is one die per visit, not a contest of what is moved.
- **Following people across a ferry and a hill road** is `following` and works, but nothing says how the ferrywoman feels about three people and a woman with a scale.
- No **map** shows the Mary's course; a company that is aboard has no sense of the hour except waiting.
- Chaser's **clock is invisible** to the player unless they ask: there is no "she is two hours behind" line.

## 4. Existing assets whose integration is incomplete
- The music box is an Orsino valuable (`music_box`, 50): selling it works as any valuable does, but **its tune is nothing the engine can play**, and the page can only say it plays.
- Eleven kinds of Orsino gear named by the draper (clergyman's bands, bargeman's smock) are told as prose, not as items: a disguise is a fact, not a garment.
- The fen's plants and eels are told in prose only; no plant stand was written (the river country has no herbs the catalog lists for it).
- No **new species**: the river's animals are a heron, geese, a bear on a chain and an unseen eel, all prose.
