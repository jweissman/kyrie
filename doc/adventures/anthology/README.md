# Three places of adventure — creative treatments

*Written 7 November 2026, before any of it was authored. Each treatment says what the place is for, how it is built,
what lives in it and why, and what a company takes out of it; then what the engine is expected to carry and what it
is not. The record of what was built and what was found missing is in each adventure's own page
([tallow-deep.md](tallow-deep.md), [the-long-muster.md](the-long-muster.md), [the-fourth-act.md](the-fourth-act.md)),
under "What the engine did not carry".*

The collection (see [../repertoire-2026-11-03.md](../repertoire-2026-11-03.md)) is rich in towns, courts and quarrels
and thin in **country that does not care who walks through it**. These three are of that kind, each with a different
way of being explored:

| | Structure | The player's chief verb | What brings them back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I. Tallow Deep** | a *graph*: four levels, four ways in, loops and shortcuts | choose a way, and a way home | each expedition opens a route, so the next begins deeper or safer |
| **II. The Long Muster's Road** | a *line with braids*: five days of road, each day a choice of three ways | decide what to carry, when to stop, when to turn | a different company can cross a different way; the far end opens a second road |
| **III. The Fourth Act** | a *field*: an island of six regions with no order | what to look at, and what to leave alone | what was taken, and what was left, changes the circus |

## I. Tallow Deep

*This treatment was written before anything was authored and was brought into line with the module after the hostile review of 7 November (the Company is sixty years gone, two custodians, one Settling Cistern, the Dial and not a Star Room, prices as Mistress Orpe pays them). Where it and the module differ, the module and [tallow-deep.md](tallow-deep.md) are right.*

### The place

**Tallow Edge** is a long pale limestone ridge, and the river **Wend** is lost in it. The river goes in at the
**Swallow**, a sink-hole in the upland at the ridge's north end, and comes out nine miles round at the **Rising**,
a cave mouth above the village of **Swallet**. What happens to the Wend in between is the whole adventure: three
peoples have made a place there for three different reasons, and each has left it for the next.

1. **The Mathematical Age** (older than anyone's writing). A vertical bore, half a mile deep and four paces across,
   dead straight, cut through the ridge by something that did not use picks, with a gear-hall at its foot. It was an
   instrument for *looking up*: from the bottom of the bore, at noon, a star shows. The makers' remains are the
   **Long Bore**, the **Gear-Hall**, and two **custodians** that have kept it since.
2. **The Aenean Empire** (about eleven hundred years ago). Engineers of the legions found the Wend's swallow and
   made the river a *conduit* — a tunnel-aqueduct from the Swallow down through a settling cistern to the Rising —
   to water a legionary road-station at Swallet. They also cut the **Calx Way**, a road-tunnel under the ridge, to
   spare the legions the climb. They found the Bore and did not understand it: they built the Cistern Hall *around*
   its head, to cap it, and left a tally-wall in Aenean saying so.
3. **The Brasiers' Company** (sixty years ago, the winter of 'fifty-two). Miners sinking for lead and the black ore the old
   workings showed followed the Aenean galleries down. Their **Day-Level** adit, **winding-house** and
   **Blind Shaft** are the dungeon's upper storeys. In the winter of the **Breakthrough** they holed through the
   Aenean cap into the Bore's head. The Wend came into the works at once; fourteen men were left below; the Company
   abandoned the mine in a season and the village has not spoken of it since except as "the water-trouble".
4. **Now.** The **Tallymen**, a company of river-smugglers, squat in the Day-Level; they use the sound of the
   Aenean road tunnel to bring salt and spirits under the ridge past the tolls of two counties, and they would like
   to be left alone. Below them the ecology has arranged itself around what the Breakthrough left.

### Structure

```
                       THE SWALLOW (north, upland, rope)                        SWALLET (south)
                              |                                                  |
   L0  surface    [Swallow rim]---(owlbear scrub)---[Tallow Edge top]---[village]---[Day-Level portal]  [The Rising cave]
                              |                                      \                  |                       |
   L1  the Day-Level   [Chimney]              (the Calx Way's west portal, buried)  [Adit]-[Tally-Room]-[Smithy]   [Rising Gallery]
                              |                                                    |         |   \                    |
   L2  the Conduit    [Upper Conduit]-----[Spider Gallery]------[Settling Cistern]-----[Winding-House]--[Blind Shaft]----+
                              |                                    |    (rats)         |
   L3  the Cistern    [Cistern Hall (flooded, ooze)]----[Foreman's Office]----[Ossuary (ghouls)]----[Aenean Tally-Wall]
                              |                                                      |
   L4  the Bore       [Head of the Long Bore]--[Gear-Hall (custodians)]--[Quiet Gallery (hollow-stalkers)]--[The Dial]
```

Four ways in, each a different expedition:

| Way | Cost | Lands | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Day-Level** (south, an adit) | six coin to the Tallymen (and again each day), a lie, a game of cards, or a fight; nobody slips past them | L1 Tally-Room | the easy way, and the way the Tallymen watch; the way out is never barred |
| **The Rising** (east, the river cave) | wade up the Wend; closed when it is in spate (after rain) | L1 Rising Gallery, then up | bats, a spider's ledge; a swimmer's way |
| **The Swallow** (north, the sink) | rope, a two-hour climb, an owlbear in the scrub (bait it with two rations) | L2 Upper Conduit | quickest route to the depth; nothing to hold the rope but yourself |
| **The Calx Way** (west, buried) | lamp oil for the pawl and a shovel for the fall at the west end; the ghouls between | L4 beside the Ossuary | **the shortcut**: from the Ossuary to a portal two miles from Swallet; a second home road |

Loops: Day-Level → Winding-House → Blind Shaft down → Cistern → up the Calx Way is a circuit; so is Rising →
Spider Gallery → Settling Cistern → Winding-House → Adit. A company never has to retrace.

### Who lives where and why

| Population | Where | Does what | Telegraphed by | The ways other than a fight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bats** | the Rising, the Swallow chimney | roost; the river's draught moves them | guano, and fluttering in and out at dusk; their *direction* is the airflow | follow them to a way out |
| **Cave spiders** | Spider Gallery | hunt bats and the Tallymen's cats | webs, bat-wings in them, a cat's collar | burn the webs, go round by the Settling Cistern |
| **Great rats** | the Day-Level stores, the Settling Cistern | scavenge the Tallymen | droppings, a gnawed boot, the Tallymen's poison dishes | ratsbane; or the rats are the ghouls' fear |
| **Tallymen** (human smugglers) | Tally-Room | hold the adit; buy and sell | a lantern in the adit, a boy on watch | pay the toll, trade, hire a guide, or report them |
| **Slump / ooze** | Cistern Hall | feeds on guano and the drowned | a clean-scoured floor, no rats | heap guano on the shore; it eats it |
| **Ghouls** | Ossuary | feed on the Breakthrough's dead; raid the rats | a charnel smell, scratch-marks high on the walls | read the fourteen names from the Foreman's ledger, or say the Company's burial words (a rites check, easier for one who knows how the Foreman laid them) |
| **Hollow-stalkers** (three) | the Quiet Gallery | hunt by breath, take ghouls and lone Tallymen | an absence of every other creature | blankets over boots and wet cloth over mouths: two hours of passage, one blanket each time |
| **Clockwork custodians** | Gear-Hall | keep the instrument; attack what is not measured | slow ticking, a stopped step | set the escapement to the beat cut in the Tally-Wall (or read it off the chart) |
| **An owlbear** | upland scrub at the Swallow | feeds in the heather; will not go below | torn turf, a calf's remains | bait, go round, or the rope in the dark |

### What a company takes out and why it matters

*Treasure drawn from Orsino's inventory and the catalog, never "a chest of gold":* an **ancient orrery** (Mistress Orpe pays 250, the
Dial) and a **celestial chart** (150; and it is also the key to the custodians),
a **lensing crystal** (30) and **antique compass** (90) from the Dial, **bell-teeth** (30, from the Aenean wheel),
a **dowsing rod** (20) the Brasiers' Company left in its counting-house, **steel coin** and **toll silver** in the Tally-Room,
a **zoological manual** (20) in the Foreman's office, **grave rings** from the Ossuary, a **climbing harness**,
**piton**s and **rope** left by a failed party, **ratsbane** from the Tallymen's stores, **lamp oil** and **oil flasks**
in the smithy, and — by chance, as Orsino's loot is drawn — whatever the engine's own tables add below. Stones: the
assay-office holds a cased **rose quartz** and an uncut **jet**.

*Objects that change a plan:* the Foreman's **sealed letter** (a local document) opens the Calx Way and explains the
Breakthrough; the **tally-book** (a document) says who owes whom among the dead and living; the **assay-office
collection** (specimens in drawers) lets a company *identify* the black ore. The **wind chime** and the **signal whistle**
are the lures.

*Herbs:* **feverwort** (limestone) and **gentian** and **alhuren** (rocky ground; silver leaves, blue flowers that
glow faintly) grow at the Rising and on the Swallow rim; the Brasiers' bothy garden has run to seed — **foxglove**,
**tansy**, **rue**, **poppy** — and the garden is the reason the Tallymen are healthy. **Kingsfoil** grows in one place
in the whole hill (the Swallow rim, where the spray keeps a green fringe), and a company that notices it can cure the
poison of the cave spiders and the ghouls' rot on the way home. **Poppy** and **cinquefoil** (sleep)
are used, by an authored action, to dose a pack animal or a guard-dog out of the stalkers' hearing. Where their
"uses" (a plant good against fear, a plant that glows) are not read by any rule, the page says so (the engine
section below).

### Why return

- *First expedition:* the Day-Level and the Rising, a short look below, a retreat with ore and an old book.
- *Second:* by the Swallow, to the Cistern; a letter found; the Calx Way opened.
- *Third:* by the Calx Way straight into the Ossuary's far side; the Bore at last; the Dial.
- Everything has a cost that persists: poison dishes thin the rats and send the spiders down to the water; selling
  the Tallymen out ends the toll and the market; burning the Spider Gallery's webs sends the spiders into their vault
  for a week; taking a grey thing off the Dial's shelf and not setting it back makes the Wend rise more often; and
  a company that sets the escapement wrong learns what a custodian's "measured" means.

## II. The Long Muster's Road

### The place

When the Aenean legions were recalled, **eleven thousand men of the Long Muster** marched out of the eastern provinces
by a road their engineers had cut over the **Hesper Fells**, in one season. Ten thousand arrived. The road they built
runs north to south for sixty miles, from **Kell Ford** (the last inhabited village of the first country) to
the **Mansio of the Last Cohort**, and no ordinary party crosses it: its bridges are down, its galleries
collapsed, its mansios (day-stations) roofless. The road is there; the *crossing* is the adventure.

### Structure: five days, three ways each

```
 Day 1 KELL FORD ── the Low Road (river gorge, ford, flood) ──────────┐
         │   ── the Old Causeway (dry, long, wolves)  ────────────────┤  MANSIO I  "Septimus"
         │   ── Goatherds' track (steep, needs a guide) ──────────────┘
 Day 2   ── the High Road (open, fast, the wyvern's hunting ground at noon)
         ── the Gallery (tunnel; dark; bad air; a fall at the Third Arch)
         ── the Ledge (local-knowledge path; exposed; avalanche after snow)  ──► MANSIO II  "Octavius"
 Day 3   the Great Viaduct: broken at the fifth arch.  Cross by: rope-and-piton traverse; a lowering and a ford
         beneath; the surviving pier-top; or turn back.                           ──► MANSIO III "the Eagle"
 Day 4   the Giant's Pass: an old giant has made a house in the road. Go round, bargain, wait, sneak.
 Day 5   the Descent into the Last Cohort's valley: sentries who still challenge in Aenean.
```

Each stage has: *what it costs* (hours, rations, fuel for fire, wear), *what it risks* (weather, fall, creatures),
*what you can learn* (milestones in Aenean give distance and day-names; a goatherd; the mansios' graffiti — soldiers
cut their cohort and the number of days since the last pay), *how you can prepare* (rope, pitons, climbing harness,
spyglass, antique compass, ratsbane, a pack mule — and its limits: the mule will not cross the Gallery), and *where you
can turn back* (every mansio has a roof of some kind; none has food).

**Weather** is a scenery entity (the Varrow's pattern, in the guide): fair, wind, snow, thaw. It changes by the die
and by season; it closes the Ledge in snow, floods the Low Road in thaw, grounds the wyvern in wind.

**Threats too great to confront:** the **wyvern** (a monster of heights) which hunts the High Road at noon and is
*only* a hazard to those who walk in the open; the **giant** (a behemoth of the fells) in the road-house at Day 4, who
asks a toll in stories and in iron; **avalanche**; the **Third Arch**. None can be killed by a first company; each can
be passed.

**Companions** matter: the goatherd **Marra Tellis** at Kell Ford (hire; she knows the Ledge, will not enter the
Gallery or go within sight of the giant); a **pack mule**; a hired **surveyor** whose antique compass is the only
reason anyone found the Third Arch. A company may lose a companion.

### Where it leads

The **Last Cohort** is a valley of about two thousand people, descended from the Muster's rearguard, who never heard
the empire fell. Their law is the Muster's law, their calendar its last emperor's reign (year 1,104 of his rule),
their language a conservative Aenean; they keep the road's far gate and have sent a courier north every spring for
eleven centuries, with no reply. The courier's pouches are stacked in the Mansio of the Eagle, undelivered, because
the northern end no longer exists. **That is the discovery**: a living memory of an empire, a community that
believes itself loyal to a throne that is dust, and a question put to the company: *what do you say to them?* Telling
is acting (the engine has "telling" as a mechanism): the company may tell them; may carry their dispatch to a lowland
archive; may trade (they are rich in iron and in salted trout, and short of salt-pans, women's names and news); may
leave. The thing they hold that the world wants is the **Itinerarium**, the road-book of the Muster, with a
sealed bundle of the General's letters.

### Treasure and objects

*Orsino items:* **toll silver** (a season of the Toll, in the Mansio of the Eagle), **steel coin**, **antique
compass**, **ancient map** and **faded map** (the Itinerarium's extracts), **celestial chart**, **signal whistle**,
**climbing harness**, **piton**, **rope**, **spyglass**, **tent**, **bedroll**, **blanket**, **cloak**, **boots**, **lamp oil**,
**firewood**. A **wardstone** and the **dun_rierdan_waystone** (a waystone, as Orsino has it) lie in the General's
mansio. A local document: **the Muster-roll**. *Herbs:* **cloudberry** on the bogs of Day 3, **heather** and
**gentian** on Day 2, **kingsfoil** in the sheltered hollow behind Mansio III, **bruisewort** and **comfrey**
on the Low Road gorge. All sold at the Last Cohort's apothecary, which pays more than any lowland herb-wife.

## III. Wapshott's Fourth Act — The Island of Monsters

### The continuation

Phases I and II ended with a thigh-bone and a claw: the Leg of the Great Worm under the marl-pit, **the Inventory of
the Menagerie Royal** unfilled, **Sir Gervase Fenwick** with money and a contract, and the Colonel holding (or not) the
Warrant. The **Fourth Act** takes those threads and pulls: **the Great Worm was not a worm.** The Keepers' Book lists
"thunder-lizards of the Southern Preserve, 37 head, delivered at the Rest, 1,100 years since the last Progress"; it
states that the Progress kept *a preserve on an island*. The claw is from there.

### The act, in three scales

1. **The tent** (personal, theatrical). A crate arrives at the circus, consigned to "the Keeper of the Menagerie Royal"
   by a sea-captain who died on the voyage: it holds a clutch of **eggs**, which hatch the night before a show. By
   morning there are eleven small, quick, feathered, extremely clever things loose in the wagon-yard, one of them
   in Mumchance's trunk; the tortoise and the owlbear have opinions; Billie has a plan; the Colonel sees a *show*.
   They are **not monsters**; they are young of something, and they are frightened and very good at doors.
2. **The town** (escalation). The hatchlings go for the geese of the Fair town and for the marl-pit; the Fair
   jury, Fenwick's dinner, the baker's buns. The Colonel's first mistakes: he lets the first one out for the house,
   because the house *loved* it; and he names it. Their mother is somewhere, and she sends no letter: the
   mother's call, in the third town's marl, is what the claw was. Sir Gervase reads the Keepers' Book properly for
   the first time and says the word *Southern*.
3. **The island.** Fenwick charters the *Sundial*, a brig, and a map from a printer in the port; the circus goes
   with a naturalist, a sceptic, a Steward's clerk and no sensible reason. The **Preserve** — "Isle Sarn" on the
   Keepers' map, "the Island of Monsters" on the sailors' — is a drowned volcano rim, a cycad forest, a fern plain,
   a tar-and-steam basin, a mangrove delta, a cliff of nesting ledges and a central lake, with the Progress's
   quay and pavilion still standing in the jungle, crowned wheel on every post.

### The island is a field

Six regions; no order; each with its own ecology and several ways to be useful.

| Region | Ecology | What is there | Danger | What is worth seeing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Quay and the Pavilion** | ruin overgrown | the Progress's Preserve-house, a **keeper's journal** (a document), cages, a boat | scavengers (small, quick) | a map; the proof of *intention* |
| **Mangrove delta** | brackish; nesting banks | egg-layers, crocodilians, waders, shell-diggers | the **delta-lurker** in the channels | the **egg-beds** (the Colonel's crate came from here) |
| **Cycad forest** | shade, ferns, leaf litter | **hook-claw** pack hunters; small browsers | the pack at dusk | the forest's **small things** — fern-runners, glide-lizards |
| **The Fern Plain** | open, migrating herds | **three-horns**, **lantern-necks** (the great browsers), the herd's calves | stampede; the **tyrant** hunting the herd's edge | **the herd's dawn crossing** — see below |
| **The Steam Basin** | geysers, tar, bone beds | carrion-eaters, a **tar-trapped** old animal | gas, hot ground | bone beds: *what happened to the others*; rare specimens |
| **The Nesting Cliffs** | ledges; sea-wind | **sail-wings** colony, nesting | sail-wings strike intruders | the colony's launch at the dawn thermals |

**The most memorable sight** is the **Lantern-necks at dawn**: a herd of ten that comes down out of the crater mist
and wades the lake, necks up, feeding on the cycad canopy, with their calves between. They are not hunted by
anything, they are indifferent to everyone, and they wreck whatever they walk through by mistake. A company can sit
and watch, can be trampled, or can try to take a calf. Nothing in the adventure tells them which.

### Objectives (overlapping; none required)

1. **Recover the Keeper's Journal** (and what it says about the preserve's founding).
2. **Understand the catastrophe**: the crate was a *clearing*; the Preserve's wardens, Sallow's agent, and the
   Steward's order each did something. Evidence is scattered; the truth is that the preserve was **abandoned in
   the Progress's last year** and the first sailor who found it fled with a crate. 
3. **Recover the hatchlings' mother's egg**, or bring back a hatchling for the Rest's Inventory — or return
   what was taken. *What should or should not be taken away* is the adventure's last question: Fenwick wants a
   specimen for the Royal Society; Billie wants the clutch returned; the Steward wants the preserve declared;
   Sallow's lion-tamer wants a hook-claw; the Colonel wants to be loved.
4. **Specimens and plants**: *cycad, bamboo, dulse, cinnamon, hazel, staggerweed, dragonwort*; the island's one
   **kingsfoil** hollow; the steam basin's tar-and-sulphur which is a *mineral* with uses.
5. **Another expedition** is on the island: **Captain Dace Orrery**, formerly of Sallow's Hippodrome, with six men and
   a net, taking calves for the Hippodrome. Fight, parley, or sabotage.

### Wapshott

He is a showman who loves an audience more than any animal and an animal more than most people; he will do something
generous, something vain and something unforgivable; and these can be the same act. His arc:

- *He makes the first mistake with love*: he lets one out because the town wanted to see it.
- *He is brave without being competent*: on the island he walks toward the great herd with a trumpet.
- *He wants to bring the calf home*: for the Rest, for the Warrant, for the crowd. He is persuadable, and what
  persuades him is **Mumchance's silence**, **Billie's standards**, and the tortoise.
- *What he does at the end* depends on the company and on what he has done: he can take the calf and lose Billie;
  leave it, and the Warrant; or sell the Inventory to Fenwick. None is wrong. Each is his.

### Creatures: what is canonical and what is missing

The bestiary has no dinosaurs. Existing kinds used: **crocodile** (delta), **snake**, **lizard**, **turtle**, **eel**,
**frog**, **crow**/**raven** (scavengers), **owl**, **bat**, **wyvern** (as the lowest echo of the great hunters, or
none), **rhino** and **elephant** for scale comparisons, and the circus's own **owlbear**, **tortoise**, **horse**.
New canonical records, written as catalog records so that the whole game's pipeline carries them, and each noting
the fact that its painting is still to be made: **fern-runner**, **hook-claw**, **three-horn**, **lantern-neck**,
**tyrant**, **sail-wing**, **mire-lurker**. See [the-fourth-act.md](the-fourth-act.md).

## What each is expected to prove about the engine

| Adventure | Tests |
|---|---|
| I. Tallow Deep | many entrances, loops, one-way passages, hidden ways, vertical ways needing equipment, creatures with temperaments and lairs, tolerance bargains, items from the catalog, plant stands, a sale of finds, a shortcut that persists across a save |
| II. The Long Muster's Road | a dated journey with consumed supplies, weather, a hired companion who refuses some ways, passages needing items, one-way descents, a creature too strong to fight, a destination with a population and a speech |
| III. The Fourth Act | a continuation across two phases of an installed standing scenario; new species through the catalog; a province reached by an action rather than a road; overlapping objectives; a persistent specimen |

**Reading order:** this page; then the adventure page of whichever is being played or tested.
