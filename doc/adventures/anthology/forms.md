# Forms, positions, voices and endings

*A deliberate counterweight to the corpus's recurring shape: a licensed outsider enters an unfamiliar society, investigates its contradictions, gathers testimony, and brings about recognition, judgment, reconciliation or reform (see doc/adventures/repertoire-2026-11-03.md, §3). 7 November 2026. This page governs the anthology; the individual pages say how.*

## The audit (what the party mostly does, what presses, what makes the climax)

| Piece | As first built | Verdict | What it becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tallow Deep | explore a graph, bargain with creatures, learn history; climax: reading the dead, opening the road | mostly **exploration/survival**, but two payoffs (the names read aloud, the road reported to Hester/Joss) are recognition beats | Keep as **delve**; the recognition beats stay optional; add endings that are escapes and losses (stranded on the Cap, the sluice, a companion lost) |
| The Long Muster's Road | five days' gauntlet, then *tell the Cohort the empire fell* | the crossing is **survival**; the arrival is the old formula (a revelation put to an institution) | Keep the crossing as the climax; the Cohort becomes a place you may **reach and leave**, not a verdict; the telling is a private act with a cost to *you* (a companion stays; a boy follows you); endings: arrival, turning back, losing the one you hired |
| Fourth Act (Sarn) | circus crate → voyage → island → Colonel's verdict (the Warrant, Fenwick's contract) | the island is **awe and survival**, but the frame resolves in a ruling | Sarn is **expedition/survival/horror at night**: getting everyone home is the question; the climax is physical (the night of the tyrant, the storm off the reef); the Colonel's arc is **character comedy-tragedy**, not a hearing; endings: home with nothing, home with a loss, a comic fiasco at the quay |
| The King's Players | arrival, Hamlet, Antigone, **the debt (a lawsuit)**, command performance | Act IV was the formula | Act I **farce**; II **suspense** (a ghost that hunts; the play is a trap and a pursuit); III **tragedy** (Antigone, no hearing); IV a **heist/caper** (the forged note taken back during a performance); V a **glorious fiasco**; romance threaded; no act ends in a civic settlement |
| New: The Wrong Boat | — | — | **Pursuit and fugitives** (see below) |

## The forms chosen (and what the party spends its time on)

1. **Delve / survival (Tallow Deep)** — time on: choosing a way, reading signs, rationing light and rope. Pressure: the sluice's four hours, torches, the Cap. Climax: the Bore, or the shaft, or the road home. Unlike others: no one is judged.
2. **Crossing / endurance (Long Muster's Road)** — time on: days, weather, load, who walks and who is carried. Pressure: supplies and season. Climax: the Viaduct or the Pass, not the Cohort. Ending may be a turning back.
3. **Expedition / awe / night horror (Sarn)** — time on: looking, tracking, camping, avoiding; one night of being hunted. Pressure: the tide, the tyrant at dusk, fever, the hold of the Sundial. Climax: getting off the reef alive. Not a hearing.
4. **Pursuit and suspense (The Wrong Boat)** — fugitives who made a terrible mistake; distance, concealment, changing information; the engine is the chaser's clock, not evidence. Ending: escape, caught, or a glorious bad bargain.
5. **Farce (King's Players I, V)** — time on: lies maintained, doubles, doors; climax: all the lies in one room. Ending: a fiasco.
6. **Tragedy (King's Players III, Antigone)** — incompatible obligations; loss is on the table; "save everyone" is a hard, costly, resented path.
7. **Heist / caper (King's Players IV)** — preparation, disguise, timing, competing plans; success changes nothing civic.
8. **Romance / intimate drama (threaded through companions)** — Sib and Joss; Billie and the Colonel's trumpet; Mumchance and Honoria; a player-companion's courtship that costs something.

## The protagonist's position
Not always the licensed investigator. Positions in the anthology: delvers with no standing (Tallow); hired hands crossing a country on a goatherd's say-so (Muster); stranded passengers and a wrongheaded patron's staff (Sarn); **fugitives** (The Wrong Boat); **players** with a patent they may lose (King's Players); **lovers and rivals** inside the companies.

## Voices
The corpus's dry, exact, self-aware register stays for some. Add, on purpose: the boisterous (Harrow's net-men, the Tallymen's cardsharps, a bargeman); the provincial and inarticulate (Nib, the goatherds, a deserter who can say only three things); the sentimental (Honoria, Hester at the pinned page); the foolish and vain (Fipps, Fenwick, Corin Fallow in a hat); the taciturn (Mumchance, Sib, Usk); the impulsive (Pip); the unreflective (the Colonel with a trumpet). **Rule:** no character explains the philosophical significance of what has happened to them unless it is a joke.

## Endings (a table to keep us honest)
Tallow: stranded by the sluice and walking out by the road; home with a dagger; home with a friend; home with nothing. Muster: turned back at the Viaduct; the Cohort reached and left; reached with one fewer; a boy follows you south. Sarn: home with nothing; home with a loss; a comic fiasco at the quay; the Colonel stays behind. King's Players: a fiasco; a grave; a theft that cannot be proved; everyone saved and the playwright furious. The Wrong Boat: clean escape; caught; escaped with the thing and without a friend. **No two end in a civic settlement.**
