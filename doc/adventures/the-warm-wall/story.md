# The Warm Wall: the story first

*Written before any Kyr. The adventure follows it; where play changed it, friction.md says how.*

## The place

Seethe is one long bath-hall at the foot of the Brynach fells, where the drove road comes down in sleet. A hot spring
comes out of the gill behind it. Thirty-one winters ago the hall was cut in two by a brick wall. Maud Veale keeps the
Hot Side. Nesta Thaxter keeps the Cold Side. They have not spoken since. They are, between them, the only roof for
nine miles, and the spring that feeds them both is going cold.

Nobody knows why. Each says it is the other's doing, and each is right.

## Scenes

**The step.** Pen, eleven, takes you in out of the sleet and asks which door. The Hot Side costs four pence and a
towel is a towel. The Cold Side is free, if you can stand it. 'Nobody crosses the yard in their shirt twice,' says Pen,
'so choose proper.' There is a hatch in the wall where the linen goes through. Pen is the only one who uses it.

**Two wheels.** Each keeper has a wheel that lets the spring's water into her own pool. Each wheel has a pointer, and
both pointers are at nine. Each started at four. Every winter one of them opened a notch, because she heard the other's
pipe run louder, and the other heard that. Neither wheel can be eased alone: whatever one gives up the other draws.
'If I close a notch and she does not,' says Maud, 'I have made her a present of my heat.' 'Let her go first,' says
Nesta, 'I'll go second, I'm not proud.' She is entirely proud.

**The warm wall.** Lay a hand on the Hot Side's wall: warm, a flue runs through it. On the Cold Side, Nesta's cot stands
against the same brick. 'The draught,' she says, when asked, and goes to swim in the ice.

**The stone.** On the drying green behind the Hot Side the snow will not lie on one flat stone with no name. On it, a
dried pear core, set down like a coin.

**The step again.** The second evening a man comes down the drove road and stops at the yard gate and does not
knock. He never knocks. He is Osric Sallis, who was the Rì's headsman in the south for thirty years, with a bad back
and hands like two loaves, and a string of dried pears he offers to anyone. He has come every winter. Nobody has ever
asked him in. The drove (eleven dwarves, drovers of Brynach, whose lord will not pay the Rì's toll and whose Madoc
Hafren was hanged at Dun Rìerdan for turning back the toll-men with a pike) is in the Hot Side's hall, singing.

Thirty-one winters ago the man on the step was his uncle Ammet. Maud had eleven drovers in the hall that night too, and
a rule: *guests are the house.* Nesta said, open the door. Maud said no. In the morning there was an old man on the step.
Nesta built the wall that week. She has never known what Maud did at dawn: she went out in her shift and carried him in
with her own arms, and washed him, and sent a boy for his nephew, and buried him at her own cost on the drying green
under a stone she could not put a name on, because the Rì does not let the hangman's dead be named in hallowed ground.
Since that winter she has let Osric in by the stoke door, after the guests sleep, and bathed him herself in the dark. She
has told no one. She is not ashamed of the wrong she did. She is ashamed of the first answer.

**The hatch.** There is a way to bring him in that neither woman could do alone. The Cold Side will take him and has no
heat. The Hot Side has the heat and will not take him. Between them, buckets go through the hatch, four, and the women
stand either side of it and do not look.

> 'Mind. It's hot.'
> 'I know what hot water is, Veale.'
> 'Then you will know to fill the left-hand side first.'
> A pause. 'Thirty-one years, and she still tells me which side of a tub.'

**The dark.** The drove takes the steam before dawn, in the dark, with the lamp out, singing. If the headsman is in
the steam room with them, nobody sees whose voice it is. 'I sat with your brother the last night,' says a voice in the
dark. 'He asked that somebody tell you he was not afraid and that the pike was his own idea.' Gethin says nothing for as
long as it takes a stone to cool.

**The song.** Ottoline Brightwell, who was once the finest contralto south of the Ford and is now seventy-one and merely
excellent, taught two girls here a duet in three parts. Sung in the steam room, it goes through the wall. It has no
third part now.

## What can happen

- The spring is saved by two women each closing a notch on the same breath. They can be brought to it honestly, by what
  each does not know of the other, or by the song, or by the hatch, or by a lie.
- The wall can stand, or be breached. Maud will say *mind the pipe behind the third course.*
- The headsman can die on the step again, if nobody brings him in.
- The drove can walk out. Gethin can hear the truth in the dark. These are not the same.
- The water can simply go cold, and the baths close, and two women carry the same man's chair down the drove road.

## Why it is not a ledger

Nobody is owed anything. Two women made the same mistake from opposite sides of a wall and have been warming each other
through it for thirty-one winters. The question is who says it first.
