Brottor reads it by the door, where the light is, and then reads it again. 'Brother Anselm's hand,' Brottor says. 'I did not know he was still living.' A pause. You carry out the action.

YOU ASK GIMBLE WHAT BRINGS HER TO BARAZNOF.

'I walk from shrine to shrine. The gods have not said where to stop.' Gimble looks you over. 'If you are going anywhere, I would come along. 3 coin, and my keep.'

YOU ASK GIMBLE TO JOIN THE COMPANY.

You carry out the action.

YOU ASK GIMBLE ABOUT HOME.

'Over at Barzelavor. Hawk is there, my godparent, and keeps the wayside shrine of Lugh there. The rest went away, or into the ground.'

YOU ASK GIMBLE ABOUT THE GODS OF THIS COUNTRY.

'This country keeps Lugh, mostly: Lord of Light. But every shrine keeps its own god too, and they do not all agree with one another.' Gimble speaks more quietly. 'The oldest holy place is not a shrine at all. Leohtcnyll, in Sataretz. Folk say it was holy before there were folk.'

THE GATE-YARD OF GOTHNEKUDA

TALK TO THE PRIOR

You carry out the action.

YOU ASK THE PRIOR ABOUT THE OLD WORKINGS.

'The brothers of Ísenheald kept that place once, and blessed it, and could not keep what was in it down,' says the prior. 'What the makers died for should not be sold in a market or carried before a lord. Bring the heart of the vein to us and it will be put where it can do no more harm. We are not rich; we can give 1000 coin, which is all the house has. If no one will go, the brothers will go themselves.'

YOU ASK THE PRIOR FOR SOMETHING TO GO DOWN WITH.

The prior gives you ten coin out of a box with a broken lock. 'For light. It comes off what the house can give you after.'

THE CRAG

YOU CLIMB THE CRAG AND LOOK OUT OVER MUZGHAR.

You know the lie of Muzghar: its paths, its water, and which way the edges lie.

From the top of the crag the whole of Muzghar is laid out: the paths, the becks, the folds, and the valley road below.

Rails of iron, black and flaking, run back into the hill on sleepers of oak. The walls are cut square and smooth; the Delvers did not hurry.

Later miners worked the upper galleries for tin, and their rough pit-props stand among the Delvers' oak: the kingdom of Ísenheald took what it could reach. A crowbar lies where one of them left it.

THE FOOT OF THE SHAFT
THE DRESSING-HALL

YOU READ THE TALLY ON THE WALL.

The way to a great pit in the fellside with a wheel over it: to the foot of Muzghar by Muzggiba, and on to the crag, then the tarn, where the track to it starts. Before the folk came, the Delvers held this country. They followed the veins of a pale ore they called star-iron into the roots of the fells, timbered their galleries with oak that has not rotted yet, and raised their ore in iron cages on counterweights. They held that the earth's heart is a forge, and that whoever goes deep enough may stand beside the smith. How the Delvers ended: The star-iron gave out, and they turned on one another for what was left; the galleries fell silent in a single winter.

They followed the veins of a pale ore they called star-iron into the roots of the fells, timbered their galleries with oak that has not rotted yet, and raised their ore in iron cages on counterweights.

The star-iron gave out, and they turned on one another for what was left; the galleries fell silent in a single winter.

With the story of the Delvers whole, one thing in it stands out that no single stone says: where they went at the end. Every inscription that speaks of it points the same way, to Muzghar.

A tally of star-iron, cart by cart, for a hundred years; then a gap; then, cut deep and crooked: WE HAVE SHUT IT IN. DO NOT LET IT OUT.
