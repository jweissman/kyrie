# Reviewer's brief: a blind editorial comparison and a read playthrough

You are an independent editor of a literary fantasy role-playing game. You have not worked on it. Read everything in
this folder and nothing else of the project; do not search the repository for the names here. Write your report as
plain prose with short headed sections, quoting the material to support every judgement. Be exact and unsparing: the
owner wants to know what is true, not what is encouraging.

## Part 1: six countries, unnamed

`countries/country-A.md` to `country-F.md` each describe one generated country: what it lives by, its peoples, faith
and makers, the works of those makers, the creatures in it, its merchants, and what its module says of its settlements
and people. Every country's and settlement's name is replaced by a letter (a settlement is its country's letter and a
number). Some of the six were drawn under a hand-written strategic description of three neighbouring countries; some
were drawn without one. You are not told which.

1. Group the six into those you believe belong to one composed world of neighbours and those that do not, and say why.
2. For each pair you think are neighbours: does crossing from one to the other change the experience in a way a player
   would understand? Name the differences that are more than description (different things to do, buy, fear or learn)
   and those that are only words.
3. Does a settlement's prosperity or poverty follow from where it is and what it lives by?
4. Do the makers' works and ruins seem to belong to a larger history, or to be scattered decoration?
5. Does any chief town read as a real centre of people, trade, knowledge and power, or as an oversized village?
6. Say plainly where the differences are mainly descriptive. That would mean the experiment has not succeeded.

## Part 2: three playthroughs

`transcripts/` holds three headless playthroughs of a game's first hours, written as the game told them: what the
player chose, in [brackets], then what the game said. Two begin in the same country (one per life the player was
given); one, the control, begins in a different country under the game's older opening. Read them as a player would.

1. Within the first ten turns of each: what does the player know about who they are, where they are and what they
   could do next? Is there more than one credible thing to become involved in, and do they differ in what you would
   actually do (not only in their names)?
2. Which moments made you want to see what happens next, and which were dead, repetitive or confusing? Quote them.
3. Did any expedition open something genuinely new: a new place, a new thing the character can do, a new reason to
   go somewhere?
4. Does the world remember anything the player did? Where?
5. Compare the two countries' openings to the control's: is either better, and why?
6. The one question the owner cares about most: does this world repay the player's attention and make them want to
   continue? Answer it for each transcript, yes or no, and why.

## What not to do

Do not suggest new features in general terms. Where you would change something, say what, where, and what it would
change for a player in the turn it happens.
