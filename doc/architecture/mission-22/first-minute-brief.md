# The first minute and the presentation of choices (the owner, 17 November 2026, late)

Verbatim:

> Another cold playthrough has exposed several problems with the first minute and the fundamental presentation of choices.
>
> Please treat these as observed player-experience defects, not merely aesthetic preferences.
>
> ### A. Investigate the ancestry-screen fade and flash
>
> During ancestry selection, the entire page appeared to fade out and then flash back into view.
>
> Record the actual transition frame by frame on the deployed build. Investigate component remounting, opacity animations, background-image loading, and competing transitions.
>
> Nothing should cause the controls and scene to vanish and reappear unexpectedly.
>
> Review the title's remaining aesthetic weaknesses separately, with an emphasis on subtle refinement rather than another wholesale redesign.
>
> ### B. Show all twelve birth signs
>
> Please prototype a birth-sign page that presents all twelve signs simultaneously, rather than requiring repeated pagination through three at a time.
>
> Treat this as an explicit exception for a fixed-set selection instrument, not as a relaxation of the four-action rule for ordinary gameplay.
>
> The visual zodiac and its textual options should remain synchronized and directly operable. Consideration should reveal the relevant information; commitment should choose the sign through the existing authoritative selection path.
>
> Conduct an independent aesthetic review of the constellation artwork itself, not merely the layout and interaction.
>
> ### C. Rewrite and verify the opening roadside vignette
>
> The first scene involving a boy, a rabbit, and a man or keeper on the road is insufficiently clear.
>
> The situation, identities, relationships, risks, and offered intervention need to be intelligible before the player chooses.
>
> Review the opening's actual scene text, action labels, consequences, and pacing as a complete literary unit.
>
> Avoid ellipses that conceal what the offered action means. The player should understand what they are attempting and why.
>
> ### D. Simplify the narration after consequential actions
>
> After this initial interaction, the page presented multiple competing headings and reports: "Coming home," a restatement of the chosen action, an expandable dice result, a bright green note, and a gold "Your first goal."
>
> Audit this composition.
>
> The player should receive one clear fictional account of the action and its outcome. Make roll details available for inspection where useful, but do not present inspection as a fictional action.
>
> Do not display redundant system notifications merely because a fact was recorded.
>
> Enforce the Color Atlas contract: gold must signify an actual followed undertaking or its valid next step, not an unaccepted suggestion or editorial heading. Green must likewise represent a genuine gain rather than arbitrary positive feedback.
>
> ### E. Repair lens focus and opportunity indicators
>
> The bottom-right lens rail does not clearly indicate focus or selection.
>
> More importantly, I observed a companion/person-related pip that did not reveal a corresponding opportunity when the lens was focused.
>
> Investigate the full authoritative chain from world fact to character perception, opportunity classification, lens indicator, lens contents, and executable choice.
>
> Test both the appearance and disappearance of opportunities following world changes.
>
> An icon indicating something available must correspond to something the player can actually find, understand, and act upon, unless its state is explicitly identified as merely sensed or uncertain.
>
> ### Evidence required
>
> Please provide short deployed recordings of the affected transitions, before-and-after captures, and semantic assertions for the gold and green labels and lens indicators.
>
> Prioritize visible correctness and coherence over adding more interface features.
>
> **The objective is that the player encounters one understandable scene, makes one understandable choice, and receives one intelligible account of what happened—with every visual signal corresponding to something true in the world.**
