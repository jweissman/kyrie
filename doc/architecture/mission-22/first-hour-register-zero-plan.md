# A first-hour register-zero plan

20 November 2026. This report turns the parallel maturity and sprint tracks into one bounded programme for reaching
zero live P0/P1 failures in the first hour of play. It is a planning document: no code, data, register state or sprint
state is changed by it.

Counts were taken from `bun scripts/register-state.ts`, `bun scripts/register-tickets.ts --json`, and
`bun scripts/m22-status.ts` on 20 November. They agree: 938 rows, 437 live; 3 live P0s and 132 live P1s. Of those,
3 P0s and 94 P1s are assigned to features a player meets. The live P0s are FH-01, OR-91 and PP-05.

This report should be read beside:

- `current-sprint.md`, the operative Sprint 3 board;
- `maturity-track.md`, especially its revised order;
- `maturity-next-steps.md`, the counted close-out and track slices;
- `reviews/sprint-audit-2026-11-19.md`, the evidence audit and proposed Sprint 3 sequence;
- `open-register.md`, the only authoritative ledger.

---

## 1. Executive conclusion

Westernesse has many local proofs and too little integrated proof. Individual specs establish that a warning can be
honest, a preparation can matter, a decision can persist, a signal can have a witness, and a route can be found. They
do not yet establish that all those things are true together for one ordinary player in one shipped first hour.

The highest-yield strategy is therefore not a new subsystem. It is to make one played first hour the common proving
ground for several construction invariants, then close or split the register against that evidence.

The programme has six approaches:

1. **One authoritative first-hour acceptance trace.** Two complete, shipped-world routes become the evidence spine
   for story, danger, opportunity, prose, paintings, persistence and return.
2. **A register closure tribunal.** Close proved scope, split exact remainders, mark duplicates and demand a current
   reproducer before historical observations remain live.
3. **A lead is not an aim.** Repair the undertown notice and establish the semantic boundary between hearing of work
   and undertaking it.
4. **Semantic trust contracts.** Make every signal, label and container derive from an accessible executable choice,
   and make the row's words name what the choice actually does.
5. **Perception-bound narration.** Require prose and paintings to have a valid witness in location, time, light and
   life state, first over the golden traces.
6. **Three explicit P0 exit lanes.** Performance, independent human acceptance and owner prose approval are separate
   gates and must be scheduled as such.

These approaches are not six sequential projects. The maturity track supplies the construction invariants; the sprint
track supplies the played route and repairs. They meet at recorded gates.

## 2. The product judgment behind the plan

Westernesse's distinctive proposition is a literary RPG whose world keeps accounts. A company sets out into a world
that does not wait, learns enough to choose, prepares, accepts danger, changes people and places, returns, and finds
that what it did remains done. The page should feel like a book, but the book is backed by one committed simulation.

That proposition depends on trust more than breadth. A player can forgive a small world. They cannot reliably play a
world in which:

- a gold or coloured mark does not lead to what it promises;
- the words on a row do not name the act it performs;
- a journey hides hours and discoveries inside one jump;
- prose says a dead person speaks or daylight falls underground;
- preparation is mentioned but does not alter the later situation;
- a consequence is proved in one isolated spec but absent from the return;
- an input appears unanswered long enough to invite a second commitment.

The immediate product task is therefore **coherence under play**, not another increase in the number of possible
systems. Iscoed's drove and weir routes are the deliberately bounded place to prove that coherence.

## 3. Define the zero precisely

### 3.1 Why the global count is not the mission gate

The 135 live P0/P1 rows include future redesigns, authoring architecture, broad world ambitions, terminal work,
deferred M23 systems and observations outside any supported first hour. Requiring every one to close before Mission
22 closes would turn the mission into the whole product roadmap. Conversely, demoting or deferring rows merely to
claim zero would make the register untrustworthy.

The mission needs a derived **first-hour gate set**, not a second ledger.

### 3.2 Membership in the first-hour gate set

A live P0/P1 row belongs to the gate set when all three conditions hold:

1. It can occur in the first hour on a supported start, including creation, opening, Iscoed's two acceptance routes,
   ordinary travel, a first fight, return, save and resume.
2. It can materially damage comprehension, control, trust, reasonable survival, progress or the visibility of a
   consequence.
3. Its acceptance can be exercised on the deployed build or in a trace known to be semantically identical to it.

A row excluded from the gate set remains live at its existing priority. Its status should name why it is outside the
gate and where it belongs; exclusion is not closure.

### 3.3 Exit condition

The first-hour zero is reached only when:

- no gate-set P0 or P1 remains open, partly, unverified or deferred;
- the last deployed robot cold play discovers no new P0/P1 gate-set failure;
- the independent human session either accepts the hour or produces a new, registered blocker;
- the owner has ruled on the reference prose corpus;
- the register, current sprint, STATUS and close-out name the same result.

The numerator and denominator must be printed at every gate: for example, `0 / 37 first-hour P0/P1 rows live`, not
merely `zero`.

### 3.4 Initial classification method

Do not classify all 437 live rows by hand. Start with the 135 live P0/P1 rows, grouped by feature. For each row record
four small fields in a generated report or planning table:

- `firstHour`: yes, no, or needs reproduction;
- `route`: creation, drove, weir, general generated start, or outside;
- `proof`: existing spec/trace, missing, or owner/person gate;
- `next`: close, split, reproduce, repair, decide, or later home.

The open register remains the source; this is a disposable view generated from it.

## 4. Approach A — one authoritative first-hour acceptance trace

### 4.1 Objective

Build two complete, reproducible first-hour traces through the world as shipped:

- a human warrior on Iscoed's drove route;
- an elf mage on Iscoed's weir route.

Each trace must demonstrate the eight beats already chosen in the sprint: desire, first step, preparation, departure,
danger, discovery, choice and consequence, and return. It must not set newly arrived foes aside with `BeforeRooms`,
teleport over the middle of a site, or substitute a generic character for the character on the acceptance route.

### 4.2 Why this clears a class rather than a ticket

The same trace can answer many questions at the exact state where they matter:

- Was the next act visible and honestly labelled?
- Did preparation change odds, access or outcome?
- Were danger and help stated before commitment?
- Did narration fit location, light, time and life state?
- Did the painting fit the scene?
- Did a decision change a person, place or opportunity?
- Was that change still true after save and resume?
- Did the return acknowledge the deed?
- Did a dead or absent person remain unavailable?

Without a shared trace, each question grows its own fixture and the project can pass every fixture while the composed
hour still fails.

### 4.3 Rows expected to close or advance

The exact disposition must follow evidence, but the primary candidates are:

- Sprint rows S3-0, S3-4 and S3-5;
- CB-03 and the weak evidence behind S2-4 and S2-6;
- FH-08, CL-09, CL-46 and CP-04 on the golden route;
- the golden-route scope of NP-01, NP-02, OA-13 and CO-17;
- the route-specific remainder of CL-52;
- OB-17 if the resulting cold runs become positive, though not FH-01 itself;
- AP-01 and AP-07 where the trace is also consumed by the trust-contract checks.

No omnibus row should close for world-wide scope when only Iscoed was proved. Close the completed scope and split or
retain a specifically worded remainder.

### 4.4 Construction guide

#### Step A1 — freeze the route contract, not the prose

Write a small route manifest for each character containing:

- character identity and past;
- the eight beats;
- the authoritative facts that witness each beat;
- the acceptable alternate acts that reach the same beat;
- the save/resume point;
- the required changed reception.

Facts and roles are the contract. Exact line text is evidence to review, not the route's identity. This keeps the
trace alive through editorial revisions.

#### Step A2 — drive by semantic labels

Use the journey-review machinery and the existing label-regex style used by the route specs. A step may identify an
act by operator, target, role or a stable phrase, but must fail clearly if several choices match. Do not identify acts
by row number.

#### Step A3 — play every meaningful middle

The warrior must traverse the halls between the barrow's entrance and end; the preparation must change what happens
there rather than merely add an aim. The mage must discover, afford and recruit the actual route's helper, travel,
meet the forecast danger and survive, avoid or retreat. A proxy `Gauntlet` mage is supplementary evidence only.

#### Step A4 — assert each beat at the moment it occurs

At each witness step retain:

- the chosen act and its authoritative target;
- relevant world facts before and after;
- the offered choices before and after;
- narration and painting identifiers;
- player location, time, light, living/dead participants and active aim;
- the persistent digest where consequence matters.

This creates a small chronological evidence record rather than one final assertion that cannot locate a regression.

#### Step A5 — cross save/resume

At beat seven, serialize and begin a fresh session. Beat eight must occur after resumption. Compare the facts that
matter, not the whole incidental world, unless exact replay equality is itself the contract under test.

#### Step A6 — retain human-readable renders

Keep `bin/kyrie review record` output under `mission-22/review/golden-warrior/` and `golden-mage/`, together with a
short rendered chronology. The trace is both a spec fixture and an artifact a writer or owner can inspect.

### 4.5 Acceptance

This approach is complete when both routes:

- reach all eight beats in order without a test-only teleport or removed content;
- prove one preparation changes the later situation;
- prove a consequential choice through a fresh resumed session;
- produce no broken semantic promise under Approach D;
- produce no golden-trace narration contradiction under Approach E;
- complete on the current shipped content revision.

### 4.6 Stop rules and risks

- Do not make exact prose a route key.
- Do not remove difficult content merely to make the trace green; repair warning, affordance, preparation or balance.
- Do not turn the trace into an exhaustive tour of Iscoed.
- Do not treat two scripted successes as human acceptance.
- If the route requires a large new career, faction or quest system, stop and repair the local story with existing
  Kyr forms.

## 5. Approach B — a register closure tribunal

### 5.1 Objective

Make every live P0/P1 row describe a current, independently closable obligation. This is a read-mostly evidence pass,
not a bulk edit that declares success.

### 5.2 The four legitimate dispositions

Every reviewed row receives exactly one of these outcomes:

1. **Close:** its entire stated obligation is met by current evidence.
2. **Split:** the proved scope closes and the exact unproved remainder becomes a new or existing row with its proper
   priority and mission home.
3. **Duplicate/supersede:** another row owns the same remaining obligation, or the product decision removed it.
4. **Retain:** a current reproducer, missing decision or missing evidence is named.

`Partly` is acceptable only when the row is still a coherent unit. It must not be a tomb containing years of completed
work and one unrelated clause.

### 5.3 Review order

Review in this order:

1. the three P0s;
2. P1s in the first-hour gate set;
3. live P1s not checked since 17 November;
4. rows whose status begins `partly` but whose open clause already has its own row;
5. deferred P1s that are cheap and first-hour-facing;
6. umbrella rows that repeat several children.

### 5.4 Review packet for each row

The reviewer should see, on one screen:

- the row's full words and current disposition;
- last verified date;
- linked specs, traces and commits named by the row;
- any child or duplicate rows;
- first-hour gate classification;
- proposed disposition and exact replacement wording.

The reviewer should not infer closure from a commit title or a test name. Run or inspect the evidence named in the
acceptance clause.

### 5.5 Guide for splitting an omnibus row

When only part remains:

1. Quote the original clauses as a checklist.
2. Attach evidence to each met clause.
3. Close the original row if its central promise is complete.
4. Move each genuine remainder to the most specific existing row.
5. If no row owns it, create one row whose words describe only that remainder.
6. Re-evaluate priority. A browser measurement remainder need not inherit the P0 of a headless correctness failure.
7. Record the relationship in both statuses so history remains legible.

### 5.6 Likely early candidates

The existing maturity and sprint audits already identify useful patterns:

- completed maturity steps whose later ambitions should not keep the step open;
- browser-only and headless portions of responsiveness mixed together;
- golden-route completion mixed with world-wide system ambitions;
- old layout observations needing one current screenshot;
- duplicate balance and onboarding rows;
- rows whose one remainder is already carried by a named M23 item.

These are candidates for verification, not assumed closures.

### 5.7 Acceptance

The tribunal is complete when:

- both register readers still agree;
- there are no duplicate IDs, unknown dispositions or closed rows still saying open;
- every gate-set P0/P1 has a current reproducer, decision gate or passing acceptance artifact;
- every split remainder has one owner and one mission home;
- the Sprint 3 board names only live obligations;
- a second reader can reproduce the close/split decisions from the cited evidence.

### 5.8 Stop rules and risks

- Closure count is not an outcome measure.
- Do not lower priority because a row is inconvenient.
- Do not let `not reproduced` mean `not attempted`; name the stronger invariant or matrix that makes recurrence
  unlikely.
- Do not edit prose and code during the tribunal except for an obviously stale planning statement. Repairs go into
  the appropriate track.
- Time-box the first pass to half a day; unresolved rows become a short evidence queue.

## 6. Approach C — a lead is not an aim

### 6.1 Objective

Separate awareness of possible work from commitment to pursue it. Reading a notice should teach what work exists; it
should not silently create several held aims. An aim arises when the company deliberately takes up the work or asks
its giver for it.

### 6.2 Why this is a bulk move

The undertown notice is one concrete defect, but it exposes a semantic ambiguity shared by objectives, Notes, gold,
promotion and onboarding. Repairing only the displayed count would leave every consumer free to decide for itself
what counts as an aim.

The invariant is:

> Knowledge of a possible undertaking is a lead. A held aim requires an explicit act of acceptance or pursuit.

### 6.3 Rows expected to close or advance

Primary closures or near-closures:

- HP-01;
- the notice portion of FH-05;
- MT-11 if its remaining drawn-world findings are landed and verified with the same compatibility run.

Likely advances:

- OR-74;
- CP-13;
- US-07;
- FH-04;
- S3-3;
- the onboarding remainder of OR-60.

### 6.4 Construction guide

#### Step C1 — preserve discovery

Reading the board must still teach the notice fact and list each job by name in Notes or the appropriate lead view.
Each giver and question must remain reachable without already holding the job's aim.

#### Step C2 — remove automatic job objectives

Remove or change the five undertown `*_job_sought` objectives that arise solely from knowing the notice. Use the
existing job-given objective when the giver is asked. Prefer this bounded data change to introducing a generalized
lead record in this mission.

#### Step C3 — decide whether the board offers explicit acceptance

The smallest version needs no new action: the player asks the giver. If playtesting shows a board needs a deliberate
commitment, add one authored action such as “Take up the notice's work,” yielding one chosen aim. Do not add all jobs.

#### Step C4 — make one projection authoritative

`AimWords.held` and `Undertakings.held` must not independently infer held work. Choose the authoritative fold and make
the other a view over it. Name any remaining lexical convention such as `_sought`; do not let it remain an invisible
protocol.

#### Step C5 — test the state transition

On a drawn first town, assert:

- before reading: no job aim;
- after reading: job leads visible, no job held;
- after asking one giver: exactly that job held;
- after completion/payment: it is no longer held, while historical knowledge remains appropriately recorded;
- the other jobs remain discoverable.

Run the same slice on Iscoed's mage opening and a generated a1 first town.

#### Step C6 — classify world movement

The change alters aims and choices in every drawn world with an undertown notice. Run full compatibility from the
chosen base, classify expected movements, regenerate affected bundles, then run the golden routes. Land MT-11's
compatible layout corrections in the same classified batch only if doing so does not obscure the cause of a failure.

### 6.5 Acceptance

- Reading the notice adds zero held job objectives.
- Asking a giver adds exactly one corresponding aim.
- All advertised jobs remain reachable.
- Completion removes the held aim without erasing appropriate history.
- The first scene's principal aims and prospects stay within the chosen onboarding bound.
- Compatibility differences are entirely explained.

### 6.6 Stop rules and risks

- Do not build a general quest journal.
- Do not hide the jobs to reduce the count.
- Do not make job givers unreachable because their questions depended on the old objective.
- Do not key the presentation to English words such as “job” or “notice.”
- If a broader lead record is needed, register it for M23 after the data slice proves its shape.

## 7. Approach D — semantic trust contracts

### 7.1 Objective

Eliminate the family in which the Atlas, a lens, a menu, a colour or a label promises something that the executable
choice beneath it does not support.

The target contract is:

> Every visible semantic promise has an accessible executable witness, and the witness's words identify its actual
> target or effect.

### 7.2 What is already available

Recent work has established useful foundations: indicators built from witnesses, containers folded from descendant
leaves, executable `Doable` choices, and tests for promises and signal resolution. The next move should exploit these
constructions, not replace them with the full M23 Offering tree.

### 7.3 Failure classes to cover

The corpus should distinguish:

1. **Orphan signal:** a mark appears, but no supporting row can be reached.
2. **False label:** the row is executable, but its words do not name its destination or effect.
3. **Blank or note-like row:** the row has no operative wording or begins as incidental prose.
4. **Lost witness:** paging, promotion or grouping drops the reason that justified the mark.
5. **False colour:** an aspect implies knowledge, gain, danger or aim continuation that the act does not deliver.
6. **Suppressed marked act:** an eligible marked choice loses to unmarked filler without an urgent reason.
7. **Stale promise:** the mark persists after the supported opportunity has gone.

### 7.4 Construction guide

#### Step D1 — build the false-promise corpus

Read cold plays 2–8 and record each candidate as:

- state;
- visible signal or wording;
- accessible path through containers;
- chosen act;
- actual result;
- violated contract;
- causal layer.

Include the former blank/landing row as case zero even if it is now fixed; it is a regression archetype.

#### Step D2 — strengthen the row/target invariant

For both golden routes and the paged places lens at its first cursor position, require:

- every travel or `go` row names its destination;
- every talk row identifies whom it opens or the exchange it performs;
- every row has operative words and a valid target;
- activation reaches the named target;
- the same choice means the same thing in page, runner and terminal projections.

#### Step D3 — validate promise kinds

For each Atlas aspect, assert the result:

- blue teaches or exposes something genuinely new;
- green yields a real gain, not merely bookkeeping;
- gold advances or reaches the followed undertaking;
- red names actual danger or a peaceful act toward a threat under the owner's ruling;
- violet comes from a typed marvel;
- silver/idle/spent conditions do not dim a repeatable useful act.

#### Step D4 — repair causes at the projection boundary

Fix false promises in `Doable`, affordance construction, aspects, promotion or folding. Do not conceal them with CSS,
remove their colour while leaving the false semantic classification, or special-case a displayed string.

#### Step D5 — solve AT-11 with subject preservation

The attempted “marked before unmarked” rank emptied the four because the one-row-per-person rule discarded useful
siblings. Change ranking and subject allocation together. The invariant should say:

- no non-urgent unmarked row occupies the four while an eligible marked row remains hidden;
- promoting one marked act does not erase every other meaningful act concerning that subject;
- the four do not become artificially sparse where meaningful distinct choices exist.

Prove this first on the failed kite road case, then on drawn walks.

#### Step D6 — run continuously over Approach A

At every golden-trace step, ask `Promises.broken` and retain any failure with the frame. This makes the contract part
of the integration spine rather than a separate census.

### 7.5 Rows expected to close or advance

The most direct candidates are AP-01, AP-07, AT-11 and related CL/OR false-promise rows. It also supplies stronger
evidence for golden-route scope in S3-2 and S3-3. AT-10 remains M23 unless this work proves a bounded piece necessary;
do not pull the entire Offering-tree programme into Mission 22.

### 7.6 Acceptance

- The false-promise corpus is explicit and replayable.
- Both golden traces contain no broken promise.
- The row/target invariant covers page, runner and terminal meaning.
- Marked eligible actions outrank unmarked non-urgent fillers without collapsing subject variety.
- Every fix is at a semantic producer, not a visual mask.
- A synthetic tiny world covers each failure class cheaply.

### 7.7 Stop rules and risks

- Do not add another hue.
- Do not redesign the Atlas.
- Do not require the full Offering tree or Known facade to close M22.
- Do not equate “has a target” with “words truthfully describe the target.”
- Do not use large random walks as the only pin; keep one tiny fixture per causal class.

## 8. Approach E — perception-bound narration and paintings

### 8.1 Objective

Prevent the page from telling what the company could not perceive or depicting a scene that contradicts the committed
world. Begin with the golden routes, then promote repeated causes into construction invariants.

### 8.2 The contract

Every narrated event or selected painting should be justifiable from:

- the company's location and perceptual reach;
- time and weather where relevant;
- light and enclosure;
- whether named participants are alive, present and able to act;
- the fact, action, rule or clock event that produced the telling;
- what the company knows at that moment.

This is not a demand that all prose become mechanical. It is a demand that literary surface not contradict state.

### 8.3 Construction guide

#### Step E1 — consume the golden chronological record

For every step in Approach A, read narration, painting and state together. Produce failures with:

- exact line or painting ID;
- producing rule/source;
- expected perceptual witness;
- actual state;
- causal category.

#### Step E2 — repair high-confidence local contradictions

Correct or remove cases such as:

- speech by someone dead or absent;
- daylight, sky or weather in sealed underground places;
- a locked-door description after the company passed through it;
- a lit object in an unlit sealed room without a source;
- an outside gesture narrated indoors;
- a painting whose time, light or environment conflicts with the scene.

If a painting cannot honestly be selected, omit it rather than add a brittle condition solely to keep an asset visible.

#### Step E3 — anchor rule narration

Use the existing anchored-hearing mechanism where an event has a place or participant. Audit `unheard` lint findings
on the two routes first. A line that is genuinely global history may remain unanchored by decision; a situated event
should not.

#### Step E4 — require living and present speakers

No talk row or spontaneous speech should be projected for a dead, removed or imperceptible person. Pin this once in a
tiny world and once over the golden traces, including Joss Hatherly and post-vault deaths.

#### Step E5 — separate historical knowledge from current availability

Notes may remember that a person said something. The scene must not infer from that history that the person is still
available. Preserve the distinction through save/resume.

#### Step E6 — promote repeated causes

When two or more golden failures share a cause, add the smallest typed carrier or lint that prevents the class. Do not
add a new general narration architecture for one stray authored line.

### 8.4 Rows expected to close or advance

- golden-route scope of NP-01 and NP-02;
- OA-13 and CO-17 where paintings or narration contradict state;
- CL-09, CL-46 and CP-04 where consequence and availability intersect;
- S3-4 and S3-5;
- WM-01 on the proved route, with off-route deserter repetition retained separately if still live.

### 8.5 Acceptance

- No golden-trace line lacks its required perceptual witness.
- No dead or absent person is offered or speaks as present.
- No selected golden-trace painting contradicts location, light, time, weather or life state.
- The same claims remain true after save/resume.
- Each off-route remainder is split rather than silently claimed closed.

### 8.6 Stop rules and risks

- Do not rewrite all prose in the game.
- Do not make the narrator omniscient by default to legalize contradictions.
- Do not reject evocative prose merely because it is not a literal state dump.
- Do not close world-wide narration rows from two routes; close or split the route scope.

## 9. Approach F — three explicit P0 exit lanes

The three live P0s do not form one engineering queue. Scheduling them as if they do guarantees that one owner or human
gate remains invisible until the end.

### 9.1 OR-91 — responsiveness and secure commitment

#### Outcome

Meet the owner's current targets:

- pure simulation under 2 ms for the routine slice;
- headless-session median under 20 ms;
- p95 and worst reported, with install/check outliers identified;
- incremental projection equal to full recomputation;
- browser input acknowledged immediately even when the resulting act takes longer.

#### Guide

1. Measure pure simulation, headless session and browser separately.
2. Partition by act class: talk, go, take, fight round and travel leg.
3. Record median, p95, maximum and the slowest named phase.
4. Take the largest repeatable cost first, not the most convenient function.
5. Improve dependency picks so a changed fact invalidates only readers of the relevant arguments.
6. Keep worded choices, leaf/glance and creature readout by what each actually read.
7. Add causal-extent counts per act class; a timing ceiling alone is too noisy to preserve architecture.
8. Maintain incremental-versus-full equality over the representative route states.
9. Measure the browser acknowledgement path separately from completion. The row should react visually at once even if
   computation continues.
10. Split any remaining browser-only concern into its existing browser lane rather than keeping the headless P0 open.

#### Acceptance

Publish the hardware/load context, three repeated runs, per-class table, profile of the worst ordinary act, equality
spec results and browser acknowledgement result. Close only the scope actually meeting the target.

### 9.2 FH-01 — independent human acceptance

#### Prerequisites

- the worst semantic trust failures are repaired;
- both golden traces pass on shipped content;
- the deployed build includes those repairs;
- cold play 8 does not reveal a new blocker;
- the session protocol and consent/recording method are ready.

#### Guide

1. Recruit someone who has not read the register, code or route.
2. Give only the existing neutral prompt.
3. Observe without rescuing unless asked.
4. Record time to usable title, first confident choice, requests for help, stated purpose, preparation, consequence,
   voluntary continuation and trust in words/marks/pictures.
5. Ask the standard post-play questions without teaching the intended interpretation.
6. Register concrete failures before discussing fixes.
7. Score acceptance against behavior and the player's own account, not against successful completion alone.

#### Acceptance

FH-01 closes only if the independent session supports acceptance of the first hour. If it does not, retain FH-01 with
the sharper diagnosis and add only the concrete blockers found. One human session closes the current missing gate; it
does not establish broad usability.

### 9.3 PP-05 — owner approval of the reference corpus

#### Prerequisites

- the 30 passages are the actual common first-hour corpus on the candidate build;
- golden-trace contradiction repairs have landed;
- the passages are presented with source and moment, not as disembodied excerpts.

#### Guide

1. Prepare the 30 passages in encounter order and group by function: opening, travel, greeting, transaction, danger,
   consequence and return.
2. Include one sentence of state context for each.
3. Read them aloud; mark approve, revise or cut.
4. For revision, record the failure category: vague, ornamental, repetitive, tonally false, mechanically false or
   hard to speak.
5. Make one bounded editorial pass without changing facts or consequences.
6. Re-run prose lint, filled-text lint and golden traces.
7. Freeze the approved corpus: subsequent polishing requires an explicit owner decision, while correctness fixes remain
   allowed and must be re-approved where they change a passage.

#### Acceptance

All 30 are approved or deliberately cut/replaced, the corpus is versioned with the candidate build, and its protection
rule is documented. PP-05 is an owner decision, not a code task.

## 10. The two tracks, coordinated

### 10.1 Maturity track responsibilities

The maturity track should deliver reusable prevention mechanisms:

- OR-91 causal-extent and performance work;
- one authoritative held-aim projection;
- semantic promise and row/target contracts;
- perception/life-state construction invariants where repeated causes justify them;
- compatibility and replay stability for world-moving changes;
- register verification and splitting.

It should not take on broad faction, career, placement, Offering-tree or narration redesigns merely because they are
architecturally adjacent.

### 10.2 Sprint track responsibilities

The sprint should deliver the composed player outcome:

- local repair of blockers on the two routes;
- the full golden traces;
- truth of prose, paintings, danger and consequences on those traces;
- deployed robot cold play;
- independent human play;
- owner prose approval.

### 10.3 Integration gates

| Gate | Maturity evidence | Sprint evidence | May proceed when |
|---|---|---|---|
| G0 — ledger | Gate-set view and closure tribunal | Board names only live obligations | Counts agree and every P0 has a lane |

## 14. Decision rules for discoveries

When a new issue is found:

1. Reproduce it at the smallest faithful layer.
2. Ask whether it is a gate-set P0/P1, a lower-priority route defect, or outside the first hour.
3. Identify whether its cause is local content, projection semantics, runtime state, presentation, or evidence.
4. If an active construction invariant should have prevented it, extend that invariant rather than open an unrelated
   implementation track.
5. If it is a one-off authored contradiction, repair locally and add a general rule only after recurrence.
6. If it requires a large new system, apply the stop rule and find the smallest honest route repair.
7. Add it to the sprint only if it blocks the goal; registration does not imply commitment.

Priority rules remain strict:

- P0: the primary outcome cannot be obtained, destructive failure, or a required acceptance gate cannot be run;
- P1: material first-hour trust, control, progress, survival or consequence failure;
- later: desirable breadth, redesign or architecture not needed for the candidate hour.

## 15. Principal risks and mitigations

### Risk: scripted routes become theatre

**Mitigation:** assert world facts and semantic choices, retain alternate legitimate acts, then follow with a blind
deployed robot and an independent person.

### Risk: the gate set becomes a way to hide work

**Mitigation:** excluded P1s remain live with an explicit later home. Publish the global counts beside the gate-set
count.

### Risk: register closure becomes administrative success

**Mitigation:** require current evidence and a second-reader check. Track player outcomes separately.

### Risk: compatibility work consumes the sprint

**Mitigation:** batch only closely related world-moving changes, one at a time, before canonical traces are recorded.

### Risk: invariants grow into M23 redesigns

**Mitigation:** use the smallest proving slice and stop at the current `Doable`/fold/aim architecture. Record broader
Offering, Known, faction, career and placement work for its existing later home.

### Risk: human or owner gates are scheduled last and cannot happen

**Mitigation:** recruit the participant and book the prose review during Phase 0, while their prerequisites are being
built. Treat dates as dependencies visible on the board.

### Risk: performance work optimizes medians while the player still double-commits

**Mitigation:** measure acknowledgement and completion separately; report p95 and worst as well as median; preserve
the hold/commit browser proof.

## 17. Final recommendation

Begin with Z0, then land the lead/aim and semantic-trust batch before recording anything canonical. Build Z2 as the
shared evidence spine. Consume it for truth and consequence while OR-91 proceeds independently. Only then deploy,
run the blind player, and spend the scarce human and owner attention.

The plan deliberately avoids a generalized quest system, a new interaction framework, a faction simulation, a combat
redesign and the M23 Offering tree. None is necessary to answer Mission 22's question.

That question is narrower and more valuable: can a new player spend an hour in this book-like world, understand what
it offers, trust what it says, prepare for danger, make a consequential choice, return to a world that remembers, and
want to continue? The maturity track should make those truths hard to break. The sprint should prove that they are all
true at once.
