# Story sheets: the golden route's scenarios (ST-01)

The owner's critique of 18 November (doc/architecture/reviews/scenario-structural-literary-critique-2026-11-18.md)
asks, before any prose is touched, seven sentences of each scenario presented as a story. A sentence that can only be
filled with a place, an object, a check or a predicate marks the scenario not ready. Each sheet below is written
against the scenario's source as it stands, then gives what would make the sentence true.

## The held drove (data/goings-on/iscoed-drove/situation.kyr)

| Sentence | As it stands | Ready? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Apparent story | At first the player thinks this is about getting their drover out of a customs-house over four missing head. | yes |
| 2. Moving actor | If ignored, nobody does anything for four days; then `sent_north` sends Bran to the Judges and sells the drove. Mardle, Bran and Owain wait. | **no**: a deadline, not an actor |
| 3. Opposing claim | Mardle "cannot allow" nothing: he skims four head off every drove and is simply wrong; the customs-man has no stake; the reeve "has the sea-gate to think of". | **no**: obstruction, not a claim |
| 4. Turn | Bran's "it is not the Rì's tenth" and the altered ink turn a miscount into a fraud. | partly: it revises who is wrong, not what the ask was |
| 5. Crisis | The evidence is shown to Mardle at his counter, alone (`show_drowned`, `show_altered`); the reeve hears it later, as a report. | **no**: a delivery, not a collision |
| 6. Choice | Pay twelve crowns, show the drowned four, or show the ink. All three free Bran; only the ink turns the clerk. | **no**: methods, not meanings |
| 7. Aftermath | Bran out; the drove sold with him (since S2-5), the keeper's loaf, the road west; the clerk "turned" only if the ink was shown; the reeve writes something down. | partly: coin and a loaf; the town is not visibly different |

### What would make it a story (ST-02), in the critique's terms and the module's own material

- **A moving actor (2).** Mardle acts on the clock in three beats, each a Kyr rule with a line where it is met:
  - *warning* (day 1, evening): he moves the drove's sale forward to the morning, "for the Rì's toll";
  - *deterioration* (day 2): a buyer from Wrackham comes to the pens, and Owain is told to give the drove up;
  - *crisis* (day 3): the sale begins at the pens, and Bran's beasts go one by one unless someone stops it.
  Delay then changes who has what, not only whether a flag flips.
- **An opposing claim (3).** Mardle's fear is legitimate. He answers to the toll-farmer in Dun Rìerdan, who docks him
  for every drove that comes in short, and the four head are the difference between his place and the street. Give
  him one line that says so, met where the ink is shown, and a family on the waterfront who depends on the place.
- **A collision (5).** Showing the evidence happens before the reeve, at the sale or the counter, with Mardle and the
  customs-man present. The scene is one exchange in which each reacts: the reeve's "third drove this year", the
  customs-man choosing a side, Mardle's fear said aloud.
- **A choice with a cost (6).** Three endings that mean different things:
  - *expose him* (the ink, before the reeve): Bran freed, Mardle dismissed, his family turned out; the Brynach men sell at
    Porthalen again; the waterfront owes the company, and the customs-men do not;
  - *pay and keep quiet* (twelve crowns): Bran freed, Mardle keeps his place and does it to the next drove; Bran knows it;
  - *the drowned four only*: Bran freed, Mardle shamed but kept; the reeve is not told unless the company tells her.
- **An aftermath (7).** Daily life differs in three ways the company meets later: who keeps the customs counter; whether
  the Brynach men's droves come down to Porthalen (the reeve's line); how the customs-men and the waterfront greet the
  company. The keeper's loaf stays as the smallest of these, not the whole of it.
- **The handoff (ST-04).** Which ending was taken is read at the sea-gate: the reeve's trust (exposure earns it; paying
  costs it), and at the Tower, where the toll-farmer's man reckons the quarter.

## The last sea-gate (data/goings-on/iscoed-gate/situation.kyr)

| Sentence | As it stands | Ready? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Apparent story | At first the player thinks this is about shutting an old sea-gate before the spring tide drowns the fen. | yes |
| 2. Moving actor | If ignored, the tide comes on the sixth day (`floods`). Nobody else acts: the smugglers who use the sluice-cut, and want the gate open, do nothing in the six days. | **no**: the tide is the only actor |
| 3. Opposing claim | The smugglers' interest is real (an open gate lets their boats up the cut, and they pay the customs clerk one sack in ten) but is found only as a store, a rope and a tally behind the chain. | partly: a claim with nobody to make it |
| 4. Turn | The pawl's pin "sawn through, not worn" turns old machinery failing into someone wanting the gate open. | **yes**: the best turn on the route |
| 5. Crisis | The gate is wound shut by the company at the windlass, alone (`wind_gate`, `wind_at_ebb`). | **no**: maintenance, not a collision |
| 6. Choice | Wind with help or at low water; show the reeve the tally or not. The first is a method; the second is a real choice, but optional and after the fact. | partly |
| 7. Aftermath | The hamlet thanks the company (a wave chalked on the well-post, an old man's hands); the reeve sends her tally north; with the tally shown, "a new clerk by the quarter-day". | partly: thanks and a report; the smugglers and the eel-men unchanged |

### What would make it a story (ST-03)

- **A moving actor (2).** The smugglers act inside the six days, each beat met where it happens:
  - *warning* (night 2): the windlass's chain is found unpinned again, or the socket packed with salt;
  - *deterioration* (day 4): the eel-men are told the gate is cursed and will not go near the gatehouse; Nest, who
    keeps the weir, is offered coin to say so too;
  - *crisis* (the night before the tide): the smugglers' boat comes up the cut for the last load, and finds the gate
    shut or being shut.
- **An opposing claim (3).** The smugglers are named people with a stake: an eel-man of the hamlet whose family lives
  on the run up the cut, and who sees the reeve's gate as the Rì's toll by another name. One line from him, said at
  the gate, makes the claim.
- **A collision (5).** The crisis is at the gate, with the eel-man, Nest and (if sent for) the reeve present: winding
  the gate shut is done in front of them.
  Built in part (18–19 November): Ifan's boat comes up the cut against the shut gate, and letting it through a tide or
  turning it back are chosen in the scene's four, about the boat (test/sea-gate-claim); Nest and the reeve are not there.
- **A choice with a cost (6).** Shut it and expose them (the hamlet's eel-men lose the cut's money; the reeve trusts
  the company); shut it and let the last boat through first (the eel-man's family keeps its winter; the reeve is not
  told); or bargain, as the Wardens' wall says the sea is bargained with (a tide a month left open, by agreement).
- **An aftermath (7).** Who keeps the gate after; whether the eel-men greet the company at the lane; what the reeve
  sends north on her tally.
- **The handoff (ST-04).** The smugglers pay Mardle. If the drove exposed him, they have lost their protection and act
  sooner and more desperately; if Bran was bought out, Mardle warns them the company is coming.

## The cold pans (data/goings-on/iscoed-pans/situation.kyr)

| Sentence | As it stands | Ready? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Apparent story | At first the player thinks this is about fetching fuel for a salter whose pans go cold in three days. | yes |
| 2. Moving actor | If ignored, the brine spoils on the third day (`spoils`). Ianto sits on the bank with his arm strapped; the salter waits; nobody else acts. | **no**: a deadline, not an actor |
| 3. Opposing claim | Nobody wants anything else: the salter, the boatman, the village and the workers all want the fires lit. "Nobody here will take her through the cut since the water went wrong" names a reason and no one who holds it. | **no**: no claim at all |
| 4. Turn | None. Once Ianto has said his arm is broken, what to do is known: pole the boat or carry two sacks. | **no** |
| 5. Crisis | The boat grounded on the silt where "the old sluice has let the sea into the cut", or two sacks carried a day's walk; delivered to the salter alone. | **no**: a delivery |
| 6. Choice | Pole (a check of brawn: a month's fuel, fifteen coin) or carry (two sacks: a day or two, eight coin). | **no**: methods, not meanings |
| 7. Aftermath | The fires lit, coin, three a shift for work after; spoiled, two men tipping brine into the ditch. | partly: a wage, and the town otherwise unchanged |

### What would make it a story, in the critique's terms and the route's own material

- **The cut is the sea-gate's cut.** "Since the water went wrong" is the sawn pawl's doing (ST-03): the gate open, the
  sea up the sluice-cut, the silt that grounds Ianto's boat. The opposing claim is already on the route: the eel-men of
  Gatesend, Ifan Rhys's family, run their boats up that cut at night, and a fuel boat poled down it in daylight is a
  witness on their water. Nobody at the staithe will take her through because they have been told not to.
- **A moving actor (2)**, in three beats: *warning* (day 1): the salter lets one hearth go out to keep the other two;
  *deterioration* (day 2): the salt-rakers are laid off, and stand on the quay wanting their arrears before anyone's
  fuel is paid for; *crisis* (day 3): the salter offers the boat's load to whichever pans pay most, and Holgar's are
  not the only pans.
- **A collision (5).** The boat comes to the quay with the rakers there and the salter: who is paid first, the rakers'
  arrears or the fuel; which pans the load goes to.
- **A choice with a cost (6).** The whole load to Holgar (his season saved, the other salter's lost and the price up);
  shared between the pans (both kept a fortnight, neither a season); or the rakers' arrears first out of the company's
  own coin (the rakers back at the pans, the company poor). Poled down the cut in daylight, Ifan's people know the
  company has been on their water.
- **An aftermath (7).** Whose chimneys smoke on the second morning; whether the rakers greet the company on the quay;
  the salt price under Iscoed's jar at the Tower (ST-04).
- **The handoff (ST-04).** In from the gate: shut, the cut is passable at ebb and the boat comes down; open, it grounds.
  Out to the Tower: which pans burned is the salt in the reeve's tally.
  Built (19 November): Ianto names who keeps the boat off the cut (Ifan Rhys, on the staithe at the new moon); the gate
  wound shut teaches the cut clear, and the boat comes down it with no check; poled down it by day, Ifan says so at his
  door; and the reeve writes which pans burned into her tally, at the claim or after it (test/pans-cut). The gate's
  module is installed after the pans', so the gate reads the pans and teaches what the pans read (an import runs one
  way only). What the Exchange reads of that line past the march is the Tower's sheet's open half (WD-16).

## The Tower of the Scales (data/goings-on/rierdan-scales/situation.kyr)

| Sentence | As it stands | Ready? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Apparent story | At first the player thinks this is about carrying the reeve's tally to the Exchange in Dun Rìerdan. | yes |
| 2. Moving actor | Nobody: the Exchange takes tallies every quarter (the manifest's own "unclocked": a standing institution). | **no**, and rightly: not a story |
| 3. Opposing claim | None. Iolo is glad of the tally. | **no** |
| 4. Turn | None. | **no** |
| 5. Crisis | The tally handed over (`tally`). | **no**: a transaction |
| 6. Choice | None. | **no** |
| 7. Aftermath | Twelve crowns, the company's name in the red ink, the Tower of Doors told of: an introduction to the city. | yes, as an institution's |

### What it is, and what would make the route's choices reach it

- **Reclassified, as the critique says: a civic institution, not a story (ST-05).** Its payload is standing: the
  company vouched for in the city ("the colleges and the courts of the city ask the Exchange who a stranger is"). A
  story attaches to it only through a contested tally: two tallies for one march, a price vote bought by the sea-gate's
  saboteur, a tally whose acceptance ruins the people who saved the crop.
- **The reckoning (ST-04).** The Tower should read the chain the company actually made. It cannot yet: Dun Rìerdan is
  drawn as its own country, and the Exchange's module imports nothing from Iscoed's (only the reeve's scroll-case, by
  its kind). What Iolo read off the tally had claimed one chain for every company ("Gatesend's gate shut, and a clerk
  turned out"), even one that paid Mardle his twelve crowns and kept him at his counter; since 19 November he says only
  what every tally that reaches him holds: the gate shut, and the reeve writing well of the company. To say more, the
  reeve's tally wants to carry what she wrote (a tally of its own kind for each chain, or a fact a country beyond a march
  can be handed), which is the generalization the plan's stop rule asks to be recorded rather than built.
