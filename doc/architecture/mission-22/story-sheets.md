# Story sheets: the golden route's scenarios (ST-01)

The owner's critique of 18 November (doc/architecture/reviews/scenario-structural-literary-critique-2026-11-18.md)
asks, before any prose is touched, seven sentences of each scenario presented as a story. A sentence that can only be
filled with a place, an object, a check or a predicate marks the scenario not ready. Each sheet below is written
against the scenario's source as it stands, then gives what would make the sentence true.

## The held drove (data/goings-on/iscoed-drove/situation.kyr)

| Sentence | As it stands | Ready? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Apparent story | At first the player thinks this is about getting their drover out of a customs-house over four missing head. | yes |
| 2. Moving actor | If ignored, nobody does anything for four days; then `sent_north` sends Bran to the Judges and sells the drove. Mardle, Bran and Owain wait. | **no**: a deadline, not an actor |
| 3. Opposing claim | Mardle "cannot allow" nothing: he skims four head off every drove and is simply wrong; the customs-man has no stake; the reeve "has the sea-gate to think of". | **no**: obstruction, not a claim |
| 4. Turn | Bran's "it is not the Rì's tenth" and the altered ink turn a miscount into a fraud. | partly: it revises who is wrong, not what the ask was |
| 5. Crisis | The evidence is shown to Mardle at his counter, alone (`show_drowned`, `show_altered`); the reeve hears it later, as a report. | **no**: a delivery, not a collision |
| 6. Choice | Pay twelve crowns, show the drowned four, or show the ink. All three free Bran; only the ink turns the clerk. | **no**: methods, not meanings |
| 7. Aftermath | Bran out; the drove sold with him (since S2-5), the keeper's loaf, the road west; the clerk "turned" only if the ink was shown; the reeve writes something down. | partly: coin and a loaf; the town is not visibly different |

### What would make it a story (ST-02), in the critique's terms and the module's own material

- **A moving actor (2).** Mardle acts on the clock in three beats, each a Kyr rule with a line where it is met:
  - *warning* (day 1, evening): he moves the drove's sale forward to the morning, "for the Rì's toll";
  - *deterioration* (day 2): a buyer from Wrackham comes to the pens, and Owain is told to give the drove up;
  - *crisis* (day 3): the sale begins at the pens, and Bran's beasts go one by one unless someone stops it.
  Delay then changes who has what, not only whether a flag flips.
- **An opposing claim (3).** Mardle's fear is legitimate. He answers to the toll-farmer in Dun Rìerdan, who docks him
  for every drove that comes in short, and the four head are the difference between his place and the street. Give
  him one line that says so, met where the ink is shown, and a family on the waterfront who depends on the place.
- **A collision (5).** Showing the evidence happens before the reeve, at the sale or the counter, with Mardle and the
  customs-man present. The scene is one exchange in which each reacts: the reeve's "third drove this year", the
  customs-man choosing a side, Mardle's fear said aloud.
- **A choice with a cost (6).** Three endings that mean different things:
  - *expose him* (the ink, before the reeve): Bran freed, Mardle dismissed, his family turned out; the Brynach men sell at
    Porthalen again; the waterfront owes the company, and the customs-men do not;
  - *pay and keep quiet* (twelve crowns): Bran freed, Mardle keeps his place and does it to the next drove; Bran knows it;
  - *the drowned four only*: Bran freed, Mardle shamed but kept; the reeve is not told unless the company tells her.
- **An aftermath (7).** Daily life differs in three ways the company meets later: who keeps the customs counter; whether
  the Brynach men's droves come down to Porthalen (the reeve's line); how the customs-men and the waterfront greet the
  company. The keeper's loaf stays as the smallest of these, not the whole of it.
- **The handoff (ST-04).** Which ending was taken is read at the sea-gate: the reeve's trust (exposure earns it; paying
  costs it), and at the Tower, where the toll-farmer's man reckons the quarter.
