**Score: 6.5 / 10**

## Why
The first twenty minutes are the best opening I've had in a text game for a while. A broken sea-gate, a missing pawl, a pawl found in the silt with the wardens' wave cut in it, a pin "sawn through, not worn", a failed wind that tells me it "wants more weight than yours", a hired lizardfolk who makes it "an even chance", and then "something enormous seats itself in stone, and the mill-race sound stops." That was a whole small adventure I solved by looking and preparing, not by fighting. The Hall is full of the best-written people in the game (Wystan Hale, Orla Pennick, the steward who counts fourteen spoons). It loses points because the world does not answer what I did: the sabotage leads nowhere, nobody notices the town is saved, a promised payment never arrives, the guidance marks loop or point at dead acts, and the menu is ~60 entries long every turn.

## Did a weak caster find a way through?
Partly. The gate was solved by search, light and hiring, with no fight. Gek offered ratsbane "if you'd sooner not be bitten", but no ratsbane was ever for sale or found, so both rat jobs were fights, which Gesk won while I barely mattered (one Siphon Life, one staff blow). I backed off from a great centipede (T44), and that was my choice, not one the game offered. Preparation did change outcomes: hiring Gesk changed the gate odds and made the fights safe; buying an antidote was never tested; preparing a spell a second time (T66) did nothing until a rest.
