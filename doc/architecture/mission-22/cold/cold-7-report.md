# Cold play 7 — Bryn, dwarf cleric, report
**Score: 6 / 10** (a strong, clear opening that I wanted to continue, ended by a sudden death I could not have read coming)
## Why
The first twenty turns are a real small story: a drover arrested over four missing cattle, a clerk who is plainly fiddling the count, a tally-stick, drowned bullocks found by searching, Bran freed. Every step was led by an aim, the prose was concrete, and the consequence (Bran's thanks, 150 xp) landed. The fuel-boat errand was smaller but fine. Then I made camp while Weary, was ambushed by hobs, chose "Press on to the end" expecting a fight I could steer, and my 10-hp cleric was dead in two rounds with no real decision made. I would replay, but the death left a sour taste.
## What I wanted
1. Free Bran / find the four beasts: done, well.
2. Fuel for Holgar's pans: done by carrying two sacks (safer than the even-chance poling); paid 8 coin.
3. Learn why the sea-gate at Gatesend will not shut: set out, never arrived (died on the road).
4. Sleep off being Weary: attempting it killed me.
## Defects
1. T24: Camping at a bridge on the road offered no warning that ambush was likely, and the fight offered no hint of odds. Death from one camp action felt arbitrary.
2. T24-25: "Press on to the end" resolves the whole fight in one move. Nothing said it would end the game in two rounds; I lost the chance to flee or defend. Needs a warning or a safer default.
3. T25: In the combat, Bryn cast Shadow Mote on an ally, "Nettle" (a party member, "Nettle takes 2 necrotic damage... is frozen stiff"). The auto-fighter hurt my own companion.
4. T23: Weary arrived with no hint of the cost. The only sleeping option was "Make camp and sleep" in the open, and no inn/safe bed was named.
5. T21: The Gatesend trip is 811 minutes at night with 1 ration; the game did not say the road was dangerous.
6. T25: The change log on death is a huge wall of "- brings / + relieves" lines (unreadable noise).
## What worked
1. The opening: Owain and Bran and Mardle give consistent, contradicting evidence that you can test (tally-stick, the ink, the Rhyne).
2. Aims and the "follow" command made every next step clear.
3. Offered choices carried honest odds ("an even chance · one try").
4. Prose such as the gulls on the quay and the smell of the Rhyne.
5. Tidy ending screen that says what happened.
## Surprise
A cattle-drover's tale about a rigged customs count, solved by searching a ditch rather than fighting, was not what I expected from a fantasy game. The unexpected part was how sudden the permanent-feeling death was.
