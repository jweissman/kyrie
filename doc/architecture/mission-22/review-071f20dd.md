# Review: Westernesse opening, build 071f20dd

> Third independent review, 17 November 2026, by an agent that saw only the frames, stills and timings of the unedited deployed recordings (r4-desktop, r4-phone), blind to the code, the documents and both earlier reviews. Its findings are carried as OR-47..OR-53 and in OR-25, OR-30, OR-31, OR-43, OR-44. The lead's notes: creation's rows are held as play's are (the recorder holds every row); a page-turn row (More…, Something else…) is navigation, not one of the four, by the owner's ruling of 16 November, so a menu of four and a page-turn is four.

The material is the r4-desktop recording (mage, 1280×800) and the r4-phone recording (warrior, 390×844, touch). Frame `fNNN` is `frames/fNNN.0.png`. Frame 0 is the blank tab, so fN falls about N s after navigation.

## 1. The first fifteen seconds

A studio splash appears first: seven candles and a star, "Seventh Star" (f001–f003). The title blurs in at f004–f005 and the menu is up by f006. The title page (03-title.png) is the best-composed screen in the recording: spaced capitals, a thin rule, an italic subtitle, three rows and a few drifting motes. It is quiet and confident. The pointer works 3.4 s after navigation, but keys do not respond until 6.8 s, so a keyboard player waits through a title they can already read. New Game (f008) goes to "Drawing a region…" on black (f009). Creation then fades in over an empty dark field (f010), and its background arrives a second later (f011). That background is a heavily blurred grey-green painting. At rest it reads as mud, or as an image that failed to load, not as atmosphere. Fifteen seconds in, the player is on the second questionnaire page and has not yet seen the world.

## 2. The first minute

Creation has seven pages: why, ancestry, stars, calling, before, pronouns, name. Every page uses the same frame: a header top-left, an italic gloss top-right, rows under the gloss, and a back arrow plus an icon strip at the bottom. The layout is consistent, but there is too much of it before the world appears. The stars page (06-create-sign.png) is the one page that is beautiful: a drawn crown constellation on a real night sky. On the other pages the gloss and the rows describe a character the player has not chosen. The bottom-right icons show an elf and a sword on the first page (04) before any choice is made. Ancestry opens with Elf selected and described (f012–f013), and on the phone with Dwarf. The desktop "before" page arrives with no row selected and a gloss about "Brawn… Intimidation" (f020), but the options listed are the mage's. Setting out is the only labelled hold ("hold", 11-setting-out.png), and the narrative repeats it: "Hold to set out." "Setting out…" then shows on mud for two seconds (f027–f028). Next comes a crossfade in which the shrine painting is laid over the village, under a Continue button with no text above it (f029). The prologue's two pages (f030–f040) are handsome: a pastoral painting, centred prose and a gold "Your first goal". Their Continue is a centred button, a component not used anywhere else.

## 3. Entering play

The first place (f041, 16-act-1.png) finally pays off. It has a full-bleed painting, a large header, good prose ("A slab of the old stone, worn hollow…") and four rows. Some details undercut it:
- A ghost line, "MORNING, SUNNY, 3 BLOSSOM", appears in the header and then vanishes.
- No row is selected, unlike every creation page.
- The fourth row, "To the market-place of Calentîr…", has no bar and no number, and an extra gap above it.
- "Follow: The brothers want the old writing" is a mechanical label. Holding it produces only "You set your mind to it" and nothing in the world changes.
- "Talk to Soveliss — give the brothers' letter" promises the letter, but giving it takes a second hold inside the conversation.

The talk page (17–19) is striking: a half-screen Renaissance portrait with the name in large type. But the portrait is a bare-chested St Jerome with a skull, set beside a marble temple, after an English village of sheep and thatch. These are three visual worlds in three pages. "YOU KNOW NOW: The altar… the first ferryman set it up" is attached to a greeting that never mentions it. The prose of the replies is good.

## 4. The act of choosing

There is not one way to choose. Creation rows are tapped and the first row is pre-selected. Play rows are held, nothing is pre-selected, and the hold is never labelled. Continue is a third control, centred and boxed (f030) or bare with no box (15-beat-2, phone). The fill colour of a hold follows the row's left bar: grey for Follow (f043), gold for the letter (f048), blue for a question (phone f049). Bars appear, disappear and change colour with no legend. Things that look pressable but are not menu rows:
- the bottom-left reload and back glyphs and the unexplained curved arrow in creation;
- the creation icon strip, which reads as buttons;
- a greyed magnifier in the play rail (16);
- small numerals on rows, absent on More/Something else.

Menus run to five rows: four plus More… in creation, five on the name page, and four plus "Something else… 1 of 4" in talk. That breaks the four-row rule. Row spacing is irregular: on the desktop talk page the gap before "Take your leave" is about 70 px against 52 px elsewhere. Worst of all, the menu moves while it is being held. When a hold starts, an echo line ("◆ Follow: …") or a blank band is inserted above the narrative and pushes every row down about 32 px (f041→f043, f047→f048).

## 5. Findings

1. **High.** The menu jumps about 32 px when a hold begins, so the target slides out from under the pointer or finger (desktop f047→f048, f041→f043). Reserve the echo line's space in advance, or put it below the menu.
2. **High.** On the phone talk page, text and rows are clipped. "Thoradin" is cut by the top edge. Rows sit half under a fade mask ("Ask about the stones", phone f046). The directions to Stonehold stop mid-sentence ("come back with", phone f047, 18-act-3). One label is truncated with a trailing comma (19-act-4). Lay this page out as a column that never masks a row or a sentence.
3. **High.** In desktop 19-act-4, "NOTED, FURTHER OFF" is sliced through by the menu divider. Let the narrative scroll above the divider, or shorten what it holds.
4. **High.** The interaction grammar is inconsistent: tap in creation and hold in play, a cursor in one and none in the other, three different Continue treatments, unlabelled holds. Choose one model, mark held rows the same way everywhere, and select row 1 on every page.
5. **Medium.** Menus exceed four rows (04, 10, 17, 19). Fold More…/Something else into the rail or into the fourth row.
6. **Medium.** Creation shows a pre-rolled character as if it had been chosen: an elf in the strip on page 1 (04), the Elf cursor (f012) and the warrior gloss on the mage's page (f020). Show nothing until the player chooses.
7. **Medium.** The wrong painting flashes under an empty Continue (desktop f029, phone f028). Wait until the prologue text and painting are ready before fading in.
8. **Medium.** The creation background is blurred mud (04–11). Use a legible, dimmed painting, or the night sky of the stars page.
9. **Medium.** Following the goal does nothing visible (f043→16), and Talk promises the letter but needs a second hold. Make Follow move the player, and make "Talk — give the letter" give the letter.
10. **Medium.** The first line of each reply is faded as if it had scrolled away (18, 19). Begin new text at full opacity.
11. **Low.** The weather ghost line in the header appears and then vanishes (f041, phone f039–f040).
12. **Low.** The greyed magnifier sits in the rail (16). Hide unavailable tools.
13. **Low.** Seven creation pages plus a splash and two interstitials pass before the world appears (about 34 s at a reader's pace). Merge pronouns and name, and drop the studio splash on repeat visits.
14. **Low.** The art is stylistically incoherent: an English pastoral, then a marble temple, then St Jerome. Draw the paintings of one town from one register.

## 6. Scores

- **Coherence as one game: 5/10.** Each page is well made, but creation, the prologue, place and talk each use their own grammar and their own painting tradition.
- **Legibility: 4/10.** The desktop is mostly readable, but clipped and masked text on talk pages and on every phone page hides the very sentences the player needs.
- **Beauty at rest: 6/10.** The title, the stars page, the shrine and the portrait are lovely, while the creation backdrop and the transitional states are not.
- **Restraint: 5/10.** The foreground is calm until coloured bars, numerals, five-row menus, knowledge boxes and icon strips crowd in around the prose.
- **Responsiveness: 6/10.** Input is acknowledged within milliseconds and acts take 223–698 ms, but the menu jumps during holds, keys are dead for 6.8 s and two loading interstitials add delay.

## 7. Desktop vs phone

- On desktop, the place and talk pages have room to breathe. On the phone, the talk portrait shrinks to a banner and the name is clipped.
- Phone rows truncate ("give the brothers' let…", "ask Br…") and wrap at odd widths ("Climb the tower and / look out over Harir"), so row heights vary down the list.
- Phone narratives are cut off without any sign that they scroll (04 phone "one has ever copied", act-1, act-3).
- The phone's creation strip splits into two bars, and the crown constellation is pushed down against it.
- The phone prologue's pale painting gives weak contrast under white and italic text (phone 13, 15).
- The phone shows its hold fill clearly (f049). On desktop, the hold fill and the menu jump are the only feedback.
