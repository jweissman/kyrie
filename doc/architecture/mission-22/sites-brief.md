# M22 side commission: substantial early sites (the owner, 16 November 2026, verbatim)

If we have bandwidth, please delegate a bounded investigation to an independent generation/content subagent, ideally working initially in read-only mode, so it does not displace the mission's primary first-touch work.

**The objective is that early explorable sites should feel like substantial places with their own dangers, discoveries, rewards, and histories—not a few connected rooms containing interchangeable encounters.**

### 1. Orient and measure before implementing

Review the existing generation and expedition material, especially:

- `src/world/deep/` — layout, pacing, room contents, purposes, and rewards.
- `src/world/region-works.ts`, the generated works and marvels.
- `doc/history/missions/14/orsino-dungeon.md` — the earlier behavioral audit of Orsino dungeon generation.
- `doc/history/missions/18/review.md` — actual expedition experience, including the field-economy collapse.
- `doc/history/missions/19/items.md` — loot, merchandise, resale, and item capabilities.
- The relevant dungeon, economy, discovery, and authorability rows in the consolidated register.

Generate a representative sample of starting-world sites. Audit both their static contents and what an ordinary starting party can actually reach, recognize, interact with, and resolve.

Classify substantial dungeons separately from minor roadside sites, settlements, and single-purpose encounters.

### 2. Establish a richer expedition baseline

For most substantial early explorable dungeons, aim for:

**Combat and danger**

- At least three distinct opportunities for combat, preferably with different enemies, tactical circumstances, or stakes.
- At least one confrontation that an ordinary direct expedition reliably encounters, rather than every danger being an optional side room.
- Real possibilities of retreat, preparation, and alternative resolution where appropriate. Do not confuse unavoidable danger with compulsory victory through violence.
- Interesting consequences after combat: enemies remain defeated or wounded, resources are spent, people respond, and the site changes.

**Treasure and discovery**

- A meaningful cache or treasury, containing a varied selection of arms, armor, jewelry, valuables, useful equipment, or historically interesting objects.
- Sometimes a concealed chamber, false wall, trapped coffer, or secret passage, with evidence a careful explorer can discover.
- Several ways to investigate the secret where fiction and tools allow. Avoid blind pixel-hunting or simply clicking every wall.
- Rewards worth keeping or using, not just objects whose only function is to become coin.

**Puzzles and obstacles**

- At least one modest but genuine puzzle or problem requiring observation and reasoning.
- Go beyond conventional multiple-choice riddles: mechanisms, missing components, keyfinding, spatial relationships, inscriptions, timing, environmental manipulation, and combinations of equipment and skills.
- Allow useful alternate approaches where the world supports them.
- Every solution must be executable through the authoritative Kyr rules and normal interaction grammar.

**Wonder and historical depth**

- A real possibility of encountering something extraordinary, rather than a guaranteed magical spectacle in every dungeon.
- Every such wonder should carry at least one meaningful fragment of the larger world's history or mystery: perhaps evidence concerning the Aeneans, Mathematicians, their works, their relationship, or their successors.
- These fragments should be persistent world knowledge, not decorative mystery prose. Multiple discoveries ought eventually to support genuine inference about something the player could not deduce from any one fragment alone.
- Preserve uncertainty and the possibility of missing a discovery. Do not make every site an obligatory metacampaign quest.

### 3. Improve the structure, not merely the counts

Investigate a more deliberate grammar of dungeon composition:

- Entrance and initial impression.
- Recognizable inhabitants or factions.
- A route that introduces the site's primary danger.
- Branches, loops, shortcuts, and meaningful obstacles.
- Secrets, rewards, and optional depths.
- A memorable heart or central situation.
- Withdrawal, return, and persistent change.

Different types of sites should realize this grammar differently. A tomb, mine, temple, abandoned observatory, fortification, and inhabited ruin should not share the same encounter sequence with different nouns.

Look for additional opportunities involving conversation, stealth, exploration, supplies, light, hazards, companions, environmental manipulation, and the distinctive abilities of different callings.

More rooms and more fights are not sufficient. We want a richer *expedition*.

### 4. Balance the economy alongside greater rewards

Do not increase loot density without measuring its effects on the surrounding towns and progression.

Assess early expedition earnings against supplies, lodging, training, companions, tools, equipment, repairs, and other meaningful expenditures.

Expand what towns can sell where useful, emphasizing purchases that **unlock new adventuring verbs**, not merely better statistics.

Examples might include tools for climbing, surveying, detecting, illuminating, breaching, repairing, extracting, preserving, transporting, and exploring difficult environments.

A useful purchase should sometimes let the character solve a problem they could not previously solve.

Preserve distinctions between everyday expenses, expedition investments, and larger ambitions such as workshops, property, research, or patronage.

Avoid making the optimal choice universally to sell every interesting artifact. Valuable discoveries might also provide knowledge, relationships, crafting possibilities, or new undertakings.

### 5. Deliver a small playable proof

Do not begin with a general dungeon-generation rewrite.

First provide:

1. A measured baseline across representative seeds and site types.
2. A diagnosis separating missing generated content, inaccessible content, poor perception, deficient authoring capabilities, and weak presentation.
3. A proposed structural grammar for substantial dungeons, identifying what the existing generator already supports.
4. One or two improved early sites or generation templates, with real playthroughs demonstrating combat, a secret cache, a puzzle, a possible wonder, and meaningful return to town.
5. A before/after economic comparison, including whether newly obtainable tools unlock actual gameplay capabilities.
6. A short list of reusable engine or Kyr improvements required, each with a minimal executable test.

Test the result with several character builds, including a fragile novice, and ensure that the site can be approached, explored, abandoned, and revisited without losing continuity.

**Success is not three fights, one chest, one puzzle, and one violet item mechanically inserted into every site.**

Success is that a starting character can venture into a distinctive, dangerous, layered location; discover more than they expected; use intelligence and equipment; return with consequential gains; and find that the wider world has become more interesting because of what they learned.

Keep the work bounded and report it to the M22 lead as an independent proposal and demonstrated improvement. It should inform subsequent world-generation and first-hour design without delaying the principal interaction mission.
