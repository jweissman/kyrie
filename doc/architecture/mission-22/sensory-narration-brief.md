## Where it stands

- Observations carry no sense. They are typed only by their role in the scene's budget: lead, standing, life, changed
  and bearing (SceneBudget).
- The turn's budget (OR-69) is three sentences, with the rest held for a deliberate look. Nothing yet says which three.

## A proof slice, in order

1. **Census before rule** (SN-01). A lexicon classifier guesses each told sentence's sense: sight, sound, smell,
   touch, taste, or *lore* (known rather than perceived). Over the first hour in five worlds it measures:
   - sentences per sense;
   - repeated senses within one telling;
   - sentences with no sense at all;
   - grounded sentences: the share whose subject is an entity the company can act on or look at closely.
2. **Composition rules** (SN-02). The budget picks its three sentences by:
   - one sense per sentence;
   - no sense twice in one telling;
   - sight first, then one other sense where there is one;
   - grounded before ungrounded.

   The census is re-read after.
3. **The tag in Kyr** (SN-03). Observations declare their sense, an authored tag overrides the classifier, and the
   lint gate reports untagged lines. This happens once the census says where the voice drifts.
4. **The lens** (SN-04). The glance keeps sight and one other sense. Sound, smell and touch are offered when the
   company attends: the Look lens, and a general look around.
5. **Who perceives** (SN-05).
   - Perception splits into subskills: sight, hearing, scent.
   - The company's members perceive differently: an elf hears what a human misses; a hound smells what no one sees.
   - The hook is the perception range the sensed companion already uses (Perception.range).
