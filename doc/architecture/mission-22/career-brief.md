# Commission: a hunter's career (the owner, 17 November 2026)

Verbatim, as the owner gave it.

Please commission a deliberately simple, optional undertaking chain that gives a character a reason to travel progressively farther through Westernesse, encounter its dangerous creatures, and earn lasting recognition from its institutions.

The proposed arc is:

**Royal posting → Dragon venture → Knighthood → Royal game-warden → Continuing creature bounties and honors.**

This should be a comprehensible career within the existing world, not a new main quest or a new quest framework.

**The opening promise.** A posting found in ordinary early play offers a royal commission: slay a particular dangerous dragon, prove the deed, and receive knighthood and a substantial reward. The posting should give a real lead, not an automatically revealed route to the lair. The dragon must be a genuine world inhabitant with its own location, capabilities, activities, and fate.

This is an ambition for a developing character, not an encounter made trivial for a first-level novice. Lesser local bounties can provide nearer opportunities while the royal commission remains a distant possibility.

**A consequential deed.** Defeating the dragon must have an authoritative, persistent result. Proof can involve a trophy, credible witnesses, and the world's record of what actually happened. Do not allow a repeatable inventory interaction to manufacture dragon-slaying credit. Recognize the contributions of companions appropriately.

Receiving knighthood should establish a persistent social identity, not merely display an achievement notification. It might change forms of address, access to royal offices, available commissions, relations with certain people, or eligibility for other honors. It need not change the character's combat class.

**The royal game-warden.** Establish an official in Dun Rierdan who maintains a register of dangerous quarry, pays authorized rewards, and awards distinctive trophies or honors for notable hunts.

First examine the existing Dun Rierdan material in `data/adventures/gosses-company/` and its royal court. The city must become appropriately accessible for this career through ordinary world travel, without breaking the adventure-specific story or requiring the player to complete it.

**Continuing bounties.** The office should periodically issue new commissions based on actual dangers developing in the world: a creature raiding livestock, a predator troubling a road, an extraordinary beast appearing near a settlement, or a notorious monster whose territory has become dangerous.

These opportunities should arise from authoritative populations, locations, incidents, and time—not from spawning arbitrary targets because the player requested a quest.

A bounty has an issuer, quarry or problem, affected location, reward, evidence requirements, and a meaningful status. It can expire, be resolved by others, or become irrelevant when the underlying threat changes. Different creatures and habitats should create different investigative and tactical problems.

Some commissions may require killing. Others might legitimately permit driving off, capturing, relocating, or otherwise neutralizing a threat if the issuer accepts that outcome.

**Trophies and the Record of Deeds.** Track completed hunts, exceptional creatures, locations, honors, and royal recognition through authoritative events. Give the player a modest, attractive record of their career. A trophy should have a history and meaning, not merely be a stackable currency.

Repeated hunting can become a legitimate paragon activity: from local pest and predator commissions to exceptional quarry and royal distinction. More advanced commissions should present new capabilities, investigative problems, and social consequences rather than simply larger enemy statistics.

**Economic and gameplay value.** Rewards must fit the broader expedition economy. The hunter should have meaningful expenses and investments: maps, guides, specialist gear, retainers, tracking equipment, traps, preservation of trophies, transport, and preparation for different habitats.

Where new equipment is needed, prioritize things that unlock actual actions—tracking, laying bait, trapping, surveying, researching, or safely approaching dangerous creatures—over another series of numerical upgrades.

**Smallest proof.** Begin by reviewing existing bounty authoring, monster placement, death and proof events, time-based incident generation, and recognition mechanics. Then implement or demonstrate one early posting, one real dragon commission, a credible knighthood outcome, one game-warden interaction, and one smaller bounty that can arise and be resolved dynamically over time.

Test that the world continues changing while the commission is ignored, that claims cannot be duplicated, that the correct creature's fate is recognized, and that a completed bounty has an enduring consequence.

If substantial generic engine work is required, report the missing primitive and a minimal executable proof before building a large system.

**The intended experience:** a novice sees a royal proclamation and thinks, *one day*. Much later, perhaps after many entirely unrelated adventures, that character returns with the proof of an extraordinary deed, is recognized by the Crown, and discovers that this accomplishment has opened a continuing life of dangerous, prestigious service.

The world should remember what the character has actually earned. The player should remain entirely free to pursue something else.
