## The week's largest finding: the clients are not the same

The owner's rule is that every client is the same and the Atlas is entirely the engine's. It does not hold, and
nothing verifies it:

- **The runner re-derives the offer.** About 1,170 lines under src/cli/runner*.ts turn the engine's rows (Doable) into
  their own: rows named by command instead of Doable's id, talks grouped by RunnerTalks, journeys folded, leads matched
  by RunnerLed. The lead names the engine's row; the runner offers its own, so `going` names rows no runner turn offers
  (AT-13: `pans__ianto#talk` for `talk:pans__ianto`, `company/1:Search here` for `search(gate__gatehouse)`).
- **The page keeps rules the engine should.** The cross-hex rule lived in MenuModel.stays until TR-20 put it in the
  engine; the wayfinder and the at-hand lens still decide parts of what a row bears.
- **A fifth presenter.** `bin/kyrie serve` builds its own led acts from the leaf's glance (TB-12, the closure review).
- **What verifies parity today:** test/small/one-projection compares the four led rows across clients, and only them.

The remedy is MT-18: one projection, every client a renderer. First a parity measure over drawn walks (every
client's whole offer, ids, words, marks and the lead's step against the engine's Doable projection), its divergences
kept as a counted baseline that may only shrink, as the layer ratchet is; then each divergence moved into the engine,
the runner offering Doable's rows by Doable's ids (taking the old command ids as aliases, so the immutable cold-play
captures still replay). This is the Atlas's foundation as much as the maturity track's: a mark is only trustworthy if
every client draws the same mark from the same row.

## The lead's read

- **What went well.** Agents with narrow briefs and a proof standard did strong work: per-act cost down by half with
  equality held by mutation-checked specs, the cold start halved, cross-hex travel enforced by the engine, keyed draws
  with every moved spec explained. Scratch-branch full runs before main kept main green all week.
- **What went badly.** Integration was the weak point, and it was the lead's. AT-11's first form shrank the four and
  was landed into a full run before it was understood; the night batch carried two world-moving changes at once and
  failed nineteen specs; a stray `git checkout` reverted edits; a commit went in on a failed gate through a `;`. Each was
  caught by a full run, but they cost the night two runs and the owner's attention.
- **The register grows as fast as it closes.** About thirty rows were added on 19 November alone, many directions
  rather than defects. Register zero, literally, is weeks away at this rate; the burn-down that matters is the first
  hour's 3 P0 and 96 P1, and even those will grow with each honest walk (S3-0 found four in 34 turns).
- **What the order should be next week.** MT-18's parity measure first (Monday), because every Atlas row closed
  without it can drift back; AT-12 and AT-13 inside it; then the maturity steps that serve it (MT-16's player layer,
  the runner and serve reading Doable alone); the sprint's stories after, each checked across clients by the parity
  measure rather than in one.
