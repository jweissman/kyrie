# Play 13: what is owed, as a dwarf cleric, on an upright tablet

Played on 28 October on the build after 53d847f (the place by salience), at 820 by 1180 by touch, in a world drawn
at random (Mosscarr, fen country): the debt origin, a cleric, a hired bard. About thirty taps, an afternoon of the
game's time. What was the game's fault is fixed in the commit after this one, and is in the register as M17-111 to
M17-114.

## The game

Brann, a dwarf cleric (Wisdom 18, Dexterity 4, a greatclub), owing Gilbert the salter fifty coin by market day for a
cart of salt lost at the ford last spring.

1. **Creation.** The three peoples now say what they are and what they are born with (Keen Senses, Enduring); the
   cleric's card what it wears, wields and knows. Five reasons to have come; a debt chosen.
2. **Gilbert's salt-house.** Gilbert, the man the aim names, leads the page, larger and marked. "Fifty coin, by market
   day. You'll have heard I said so." Promised him; asked what is buried in the king-barrow: "Bring me what the old
   folk buried and I will pay what it is worth." A new aim, the crown of the first king. Two torches and a coil of
   rope from his stock.
3. **The landing and the inn.** Five people of the landing each a line, two to a row; the ways (nine) a touch away.
   At the Weeping Unicorn the innkeeper tells the way to Lacrimerecs ("Go in daylight, and take more light than you
   think"); Cedric, a pedlar who is a bard, comes along for three coin.
4. **The road.** Five hours by the Graegex road; the journey stops at the lord's toll bridge, the bar down, the reeve
   and his men by it; the track to the barrow goes off before the bar, and the company goes on.
5. **Lacrimerecs.** "No bird sits on the mound." Grave-robbers in the forecourt; talked to, they "draw back and
   watch. Nobody speaks first," and there is nothing to ask them; asked to let the company by, Cedric rolls a 1. Into
   the long passage regardless, pitch dark: a torch lit from the foot of the page. Four bats, fought and won (Cedric
   bitten to 2 of 7). A hidden switch at the narrowest place, with five ways to deal with it; read and passed. The
   cross-chamber: a whetstone scraping in a side recess, "whoever is in there is in no hurry", and a stone casket
   with a riddle ("has a mouth but never talks"); River; a bronze mirror.

## What pulled, and what lost

**Pulled:** a debt that turns into a barrow and a buyer; the barrow's signs before it is entered; a trap with many
answers; the riddle; a person who matters set first on the page.

**Lost:** grave-robbers who could be "talked to" and had nothing to say; in the barrow, the aim on the page was the
debt, not the crown the barrow held, and the barrow had been neither first nor marked among the ways though an aim
named it; the hidden switch and its five answers in a column a third of the page wide; a fight with the bats that
opened on "Brann lights a torch."; a fen salt-house opened on sheep in a wooded lane.

## Fixed after this play

A danger is offered to be talked to only where something is written to be asked of it (its parley stays); the aim
that names where the company stands is the one on the page, and an aim that names a site takes in its places, so the
way to its mouth leads and is marked; a way with something on it has the whole row; a fight opens on what began it,
never on what was told before; a fen town opens on a mill or boats drawn up.

**Left:** creation still reads as a page of rules more than as a choice of a life; the forecourt's robbers are a
danger to get past rather than people, which wants their authors' words.
