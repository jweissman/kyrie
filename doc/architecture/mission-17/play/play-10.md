# Play 10: coming home, as a mage, at tablet size

Played on 28 October on the build of 83ce490 (tending, greetings by mood, spells on the caster's page), at 820 by 1180
by touch, with no world named: a fresh game, a different calling and origin from plays 8 and 9. About thirty taps, an
afternoon of the game's time, the origin's thread seen through. The findings that were the game's fault are fixed in
the commit after this one, and are in the register as M17-90 to M17-101.

## The game

Maud, a human mage (Strength 5, Intelligence 17, five hit points, a sling), coming home to Merinael in Greenweald,
forest country, three years after she left: her father, a drover, went out with the cart and did not come home.

1. **Creation.** The defaults were an elf warrior coming home; I made her a human mage. Five pages of cards, quick.
2. **The landing at Merinael.** Boats on the shingle, a shepherd's boy's rumour of the sink, a lame grey dog by the
   inn door. Her brother on a rail with their father's old spear: "taller by a head than when you went away". On a
   tablet held upright the first paragraph and six people come first; the rumour, a hook, is far below, behind the ways
   at the foot. The brother was painted as an old man in a window.
3. **The brother.** "Looks at you as if counting the years you have been away, and then just holds on to you a
   moment." He will go after their father whether she goes or not, and joins at once (and his face changed, to a young
   shepherd). Asked about the road: "He had the left wheel out of true all spring ... If the cart went off the road,
   look for where a wheel like that would take it." About home: the flood year, the eels the size of oxen. He was an
   elf ranger, though she is human.
4. **The inn.** Her mother at the hearth, the woman cutting a fish, younger than her daughter looked; greeted in the
   brother's very words; named "Jassin Galanodel" by the telling, while a charcoal-burner looking for work at the same
   inn was Jassin too. "The dog came home on the fourth, lame in the off foot": the dog on the landing. I hired the
   charcoal-burner for three coin, and the brother said "Father would have liked Jassin ... and then given Jassin the
   worst ox."
5. **The road.** The aim said "Your father went out on the ford of the Nûrnellë". Two roads out were both "to the ford
   of the Nûrnellë", one of them "by Merinael". The road page set its ends' words together ("Merinaelthe Nûrnellë road").
6. **The ruts.** At the ford the brother stopped: "That is the left wheel. That is his." Followed over the edge: the
   cart in the ditch and a man under it, "a day or more dead ... You know the coat first, and the hands, before you will
   let yourself know the face." The brother knelt by the cart a long time. I buried him by the road (two hours), and
   his knife lay on the cairn: "nobody could bring themselves to bury it, or to take it." I took it.
7. **Home.** "'I knew,' she says. 'The dog knew.' ... 'You buried him. By the road?' ... 'He would like that. He could
   never stand to be under a roof.'" Seen through, 300 experience each. Shown the knife: "Keep it. He would want it
   used, not hung on a wall." After that, nothing: no aim, though the oxen had been "dragged off toward the hills".
8. **The innkeeper.** Sixteen things to choose among, two asks for each of two caves, each "the north-western cave in
   the hillside in the heart of Rhawtaur". Told "Something has made its den there", the aim said "The spiders have a den".

## What pulled, and what lost

**Pulled:** the best thread I have played in this game: the brother's hint and the wheel, the cart, the burying, the
mother's "The dog knew", the knife. Every beat answered what I did (the knife taken had its scene).

**Lost:** the family told wrong at every turn: of another people, two faces for one brother, a mother younger than
her child, her full name in the telling, one greeting for both, a name shared with a hired hand. "Went out on the
ford". A road page's words run together. An aim that knew what nobody had said. Nothing to do once the thread ended,
though the brother had the next one in him if asked.

## Fixed after this play

The family an origin makes the protagonist's own are of the protagonist's people; each person keeps one painting
wherever they are met, chosen among the people of their own town; a son is painted young, and a parent never by a
young sitter (and the paintings' own records corrected: the fish-cutter young, the mason old); one's own mother or
father is "your mother" in what is said of them, greeted in one of four ways, and shown the knife as their own;
"out by" the place on the road; after the news she has stopped looking at the door; a road's ends with their ways
beneath them; a den is "something's" in the aims until it has been seen; the rumour's ewe taken from the flock by its
place; and the brother, with the company and the cart behind it, says unasked what he means to do about the oxen.

**Left:** twin sites' names, now "the north-western cave in the hillside in Rhawtaur", still with two asks each; two
roads to fords of one river; the choices that still name the mother ("Talk to Jassin Galanodel"), and her given name
shared with a hired hand (drawn apart by her family name, as the country's people are); the rumour a long scroll
below six people at tablet size.
