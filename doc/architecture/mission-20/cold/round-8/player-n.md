Character: Wren, a birdfolk woman. Bard, once a rhetorician. Origin: "The brothers' errand" (scribe of an order, sent
to copy the old writing at Lapisrecs). Played socially, with about 140 actions.

## 1. Summary
- I made Wren in about two minutes. She started at the shrine in Thornhaven with a letter for the priestess Indira, who
  gave me the way to Lapisrecs and four more leads.
- On my own initiative I fixed the travelling players' trouble. I found Lem Coyle at the lime-kiln, paid his wages out
  of my own purse and brought the hat back to Gosse, which unchained the wagon. Then I hired Xyn, a soldier, for 6 coin.
- I went into the barrow Lapisrecs, talked my way past a robbers' captain and solved a riddle casket. In the long
  gallery a mummy and a spirit knocked Wren out (6 to 0 in one claw).
- Xyn carried her back to town. An inn bed and a system-shock save brought her back on 1 HP. I reloaded the page to
  check saving, and it had kept my progress.
- I went back in, talked past a band of grave-robbers, solved a second riddle and took the jar of noonlight Indira had
  told me about, from under a bone hound's nose. At the king's door I read the inscription. The errand is still open: I
  found no way to "copy" the writing, and a hound guards the door.

## 2. The first five minutes
It was very clear. The origin cards tell you who you are and why you are here in one sentence each. "Details" shows
what each one means in play ("Makes you a scribe, who knows lore"). The opening prose put the letter in my coat and
named Indira. Indira's first conversation was immediately interesting. She knew Brother Anselm's hand, and the sealed
king-barrow made her angry ("Open it, and I will not pray for you, nor heal you, nor sit up with your dead"). I knew
who I was, where I was, and who to talk to. What I could *do* was less clear: there are six small HUD icons with pips,
and I had to try each one to learn it.

## 3. What I pursued, and why
- **The brothers' errand (Lapisrecs):** this was my own reason for coming, so I chose "Follow this" deliberately.
- **The players' hat:** nobody gave me this. I pieced it together from Indira ("the man who went off with the hat"),
  then Jem Pudding, then Gosse. Gosse never asks for help ("I do not ask for money. I mention that it exists."), so it
  felt like my own choice. It was the best part of the session.
- **Hiring Xyn:** my own caution. A 6-HP bard should not go into a barrow alone. That turned out right: without him
  Wren would have died.
- I felt I had real choices: Gosse's offer of a season with the company, the errand, the crypt, Arborturris, Lucstur.

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
- **Conversation:** the strongest part. People have voices: Gosse, Pudding and his bear "duchess", Indira's dry humour
  about the Colonel's Hippodrama. The "You know now" and "A new aim" boxes are useful. Arguments with odds ("an even
  chance · one try") felt like real decisions, and my Charisma 20 visibly mattered. Weaknesses: the generated topics are
  awkward ("Ask about the silk from the sink at the south-western cave in the hillside on Stonecrag"), and the "X they
  speak of is called Y" line repeats. Danger "talk" is a single dice line ("You talk it over… and in the end you are let
  be"), with no conversation.
- **Fights:** clear layout, a lovely bird portrait, resistances shown, an honest "could be lost" forecast. My own
  abilities mattered little. Inspire is a buff, Charm Person never appeared (undead, jars), and the crossbow does half
  damage to spirits. The fight is mostly "press T and watch Xyn". Wren dropped from full to 0 in one hit. Defend has a
  cooldown that isn't obvious. Silly details: spirits and mummies "bleed", and "cannot be infected" repeats every line.
- **Inventory:** a clean grid with pictures. Some items have blank squares: glossary, bone flute, cloak-pin. Items can't
  be used: no flute, and I couldn't find a way to use the jar.
- **Map:** a beautiful 3D hex island in fog. I could tell which town I was in, and "Set out" just worked. The journey
  panel's "The way +" lists the legs, but no route was drawn on the map. Travel was eventless except when the bats
  blocked my way home.
- **Notes/Book:** excellent. Aims are sorted into "What you mean to do / To hand / Further off", with "Follow this" and
  "On the map". "Told you, not yet seen" lists who told me what and when. I noticed Arborturris's open graves go from
  five to six on day 2, so the world moves.

## 8. Danger
Yes, real danger. Each threat came with a forecast ("Should be won", "A hard fight… Wren could fall", "Could be lost")
and "will give you N minutes". That let me prepare: I hired Xyn, bought torches, backed away from the revenants and took
the jar while the hound waited. The mummy and spirit fight surprised me because the only warning was "Something is in
there". I fled nothing, but Flee was offered ("sure, now" or "1 in 2"). Coming out of the dark with Wren senseless and
having to reach an inn bed was genuinely tense, and the system-shock save was a good moment.

## 9. Did I want to keep playing?
Yes, moderately. I want to bring the noonlight to the king's door and see whether it does what Indira promised, and I'm
curious about Gosse's company at Pentrice. What dampens it: my bard's contribution to fights is close to nothing, and
the dungeon kept changing its single "next" exit. Also, my errand is "copy the writing", and I have *read* it but no
action says "copy". I'm not sure what would finish it.

## 10. Boredom and interest
Bored: auto-pressing T through 7-round fights against things my weapon barely scratches. Re-walking the barrow room by
room on the second visit. The repeated "Since you were last here" blocks.

Most interesting: the hat. It was a small human story I put together myself from three people, and it was resolved by
spending my own money. A close second was finding the jar of noonlight exactly where Indira's lore said the embalmers
kept it.

## 11. Decisions
1. Arguing with Indira about the sealed door. It was "one try", so a fail would have closed the door on her goodwill. I
   won and got the "day in a jar" lore that later mattered.
2. Paying Lem 4 of my 18 coin instead of taking the hat by force. This led to the wagon being freed, 100 xp and Gosse's
   job offer.
3. Spending 6 coin to hire Xyn. This saved Wren's life.
4. Declining Gosse's season with the company, for now.

Tactical: Inspire first, then let Xyn tank; backing off from the revenants ("could be lost"); grabbing the jar from the
Look panel before the hound's minutes ran out. The argument with Indira and paying Lem both changed what happened
later.

## 17. Who I made
Yes, Wren was recognisable: a birdfolk scribe-bard with a bird portrait I liked. The choices mattered: Charisma 20 and

## 18. Getting about
The map journeys went where they said, but no route was drawn, so I couldn't judge the way. Inside Thornhaven the Go
list is long (about 18 places) but clear, with travel times. Entering Lapisrecs from the map was obvious ("Where you

had to fight before "travel" continued.

## 19. Waiting
Building the world was effectively instant. Between "Set out" and the first playable scene there were only the two
prose screens, and I spent the time reading. Reload went to a title screen with Continue and took about 5 s. The page
never froze. A few of my clicks seemed to do nothing the first time (the "Back to the mourners' hall" button, and the
Book icon at 137b) and worked on the second try.

## 21. Top five problems

2. **The gold next step is unstable in the dungeon:** it changed target between visits, led into dead ends (the

   sheet. "Hits for 10" becomes "takes 8".
