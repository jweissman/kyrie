# Cold play 6: Westernesse, dwarf cleric, "Gone under"

Notes: /var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold6/i/notes.md · Shots: /var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold6/i/shots/ (00–144)

## 1. Summary
- I made Gunnvor, a dwarf cleric of Light who used to be a prospector, and chose "Gone under": my sister went to Seolforfyrd for silver and never came back.
- I ignored that goal at first. I spent Day 1 in Thornhaven: I talked to the players' troupe, found Lem and paid him his four coin, hired Xyn, laid the raised dead in the crypt with the cleric's rite, and went on down through the charnel passage, undercroft, drain, nest and rat-runs. I came up through the corn-store.
- I collected the crypt reward from Indira, shopped at the chandlery (lantern, oil, crowbar), then travelled to Seolforfyrd.
- There I parleyed with the hobs, forced the strongroom with my crowbar, killed a wolf and took a jeweled box. It was evening on Day 1 when I stopped.
- I won 6 fights and never lost a hit point. I found no trace of my sister.

## 2. The first five minutes
- Creation (00–21) is beautiful and quick. The "Gone under" letter card (22) is a strong hook.
- The wagoner on the road (24–25) is a small, real choice.
- At the landing (30), the prose ties straight to my reason: hobs out of Seolforfyrd stole the town bell. That was immediately interesting, because my sister went there.
- I understood who I was and where I was. What I could do was less clear: 11 portraits, a blue option, a notice, Go…, Look… and an hourglass all appear at once, and it's busy.

## 3. What I pursued, and why
- I found the crypt job myself, from the well-post notice (41). I was drawn to it because I was a Light cleric whose Pure Beam does extra harm to undead.
- The Lem thread came from asking around (Hepzibah, then Indira's "under his coat like a hen"), and that felt like my own find.
- Underground, the game kept offering more: "worth a look", the undercroft mystery, rats. It never felt forced.
- I chose Seolforfyrd at the end because my own reason and the bell both pointed there. I always felt I had a choice. Later aims piled up faster than I could follow them.

## 6. How each part felt to use
- **Conversation:** The writing is the best part: Gosse, Pudding, Hepzibah, Lem, and Indira naming the dead. But topics re-order after every question, some labels are machine-made ("the silk from the sink at the south-western cave in the hillside on Stonecrag"), and many threads can't go further (I couldn't ask anyone about my sister).
- **Fights:** The layout is clear: company on the left, foes on the right, "Vulnerable to radiant", and a sensible default attack. The T key ("Let them fight this turn") makes fights quick. All six were trivial, with zero damage taken. By the third I was on autopilot.
- **Inventory:** The Pack screen has a nice grid of icons. Lantern and oil went to Xyn automatically. The shop's numbered grid is fast. The hat that should have been there wasn't.
- **Map:** The 3D hex island is attractive. The numbered route with times and "set out" button made travel easy. The journey itself was one click with nothing happening on the road.
- **Notes:** Thorough: aims grouped as "To hand / Further off", and facts listed with who told me and when. Some facts I saw myself are filed under "told you, not yet seen", and the times don't match (Lem "told you 9:10", but I arrived 9:17).

## 8. Danger
- The ossuary (72) worked well: a red danger card, a "hard fight" warning and a 9-minute grace period. Persuasion (19 against 13) and the rite of rest got me through without a fight.
- Light running out was the only real pressure. Xyn's torch guttered on entering, then my candle died mid-search (105). That made me buy a lantern.
- The arrival at Seolforfyrd stated my escape odds plainly (134), which is good.
- But every fight the game called "easily won" was easy, and I was never hurt. A failed rite cost nothing: I just clicked it again and passed. Prying at the flag did cost an hour.

## 9. Did I want to keep playing?
Mildly, yes. The bells, the undercroft flag and my sister are open hooks. But Seolforfyrd gave me no trace of my sister, and nobody would let me ask. The fights are a formality, and the Lem/hat thread ended in nothing. I'd play another 20 minutes for the writing, not for the challenge.

## 10. Boredom and interest
- **Bored:** the four fights in a row underground (drain, nest, rat-runs, wolf), all pressing T with no damage. The walk back out of the corn-store, re-reading "Look…" entries, and "Something else…" dead ends were also dull.
- **Most interesting:** the black-candle scene (75–77). It offered three approaches, and the one made for my calling was "Say the rite of rest · likely · Gunnvor". The candle went out and the man fled. It was the moment my character mattered.
- **Runner-up:** the undercroft (92), "dug into from three sides by people who did not know it was here".

## 11. Decisions
- **Paying Lem from my own purse** rather than the coin-flip options. It cost me 4 coin and nothing came of it afterwards.
- **Hiring Xyn for 6 coin.** It mattered: his torch, and his brawn being used on the flag.
- **Breaking the town's padlock** rather than walking 1½ hours. No consequence appeared.
- **Buying a crowbar on a whim.** It later opened the strongroom: the best payoff of the session.
- **Persuading the man rather than fighting him, and the rite of rest.**
- **In fights:** Pure Beam on a zombie because it was "vulnerable to radiant", then the at-will Sunmark.

What I chose did change later play: the clue sent me to the kiln, the crowbar opened the strongroom, the cleric unlocked the rite. What I did not see was any later effect of paying Lem or of breaking the padlock.

## 18. Top five problems
1. Fights are trivially easy and failed checks can be retried for free, so danger cards stop meaning anything. I fought six fights unhurt and was clicking through by the third.
2. Threads end silently or contradict what was said:
   - Lem's hat never exists.
   - The players never react.
   - Tuck's "rest of the pay" appears without a first payment.
   - Locked doors I came through are still described as locked.
3. The paintings often contradict the text: a Roman temple for a slab altar, an oast house for a kiln, a window in a windowless vault. Portraits are inconsistent (Xyn's two faces) or carry raw museum captions (Lem). This keeps breaking the spell the writing builds.
4. Arriving at my own reason, Seolforfyrd, gives no trace of my sister and no way to ask about her. The personal hook goes quiet exactly when I follow it.
5. Navigation and text clutter:
   - Topics re-order after every question, and key threads hide under "Something else…".
   - "Another aim" labels say nothing.
   - Look… repeats text or lists actions.
   - Some places appear only in Go… and not among the scene's own options.
   - Clicks on visible words sometimes hit hidden page text.
