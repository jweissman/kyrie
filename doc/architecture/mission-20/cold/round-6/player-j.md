# Cold play report: Westernesse on a phone (390×844). Onyx, catfolk thief, "A Dead Man's Map"

Screenshots: /var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold6/j/shots · running log: /var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold6/j/notes.md · about 160 actions.

## 1. Summary
- I made Onyx (catfolk thief, once a dungeon delver), stole a sack off a wagoner on the road, picked the corn-store lock in Thornhaven and cleared the rats, the buck rat and a centipede under it.
- I went into the undercroft and lost my only candle there to a failed attempt to lever up a flagstone, then walked back out in the dark.
- Tuck paid me with a lantern for the rats. I hired Xyn (a soldier), slept at the inn, and travelled 3 hours at night to Seolforfyrd.
- There I slipped past the hob guards and killed them, survived an ambush by a raiding party, freed a shepherd's boy, robbed the plunder heap and the Gaffer's chest, and killed the Gaffer. I dug up the dead man's box at the door ("Seen through") and picked the strongroom and the toll-chest.
- I came back at dawn, hung the stolen bell back in its frame for a 10-coin purse, and sold the loot (140 coin). Day 2 morning, level 1, "ready for level 2". A reload kept everything.

## 2. The first five minutes
The creation cards are beautiful on a phone and very clear. The "Details" panels say exactly what each choice does in play. "A catfolk thief. A dead man's map. Once a dungeon delver. A dart in hand." made a recognisable person.

The opening text cards are excellent: the map inside my shirt, and a drover dead in the straw. The wagoner scene gave a real thief choice straight away. Then "YOUR FIRST GOAL: … Search there." The Notes page told me where Seolforfyrd is, the route to it (via the charcoal-burners' clearing), and that a soldier at the inn was for hire.

Immediately interesting:
- the hobs took the town's bell to Seolforfyrd, which tied the town to my map;
- a blue "Pick the corn-store padlock" sat right in front of a thief.

## 3. What I pursued and why
- **The corn-store lock and the cellar under it.** I found this myself: it was a thief action shown in blue, so I took it.
- **The undercroft and "the old dead under Thornhaven".** These were offered as aims after the rat fight, and they excited me as a treasure-seeker.
- **The map's Seolforfyrd.** This was the game's aim and also my own.

Tuck turned one talk into star-iron (30 coin a lump), a crown in Lapisrecs and the heart of the vein. I had plenty of choice, perhaps too much: about 7 aims within an hour. I never felt pushed. "Following" was just a quiet marker.

## 5. Misleading or useless controls and text
- **"Easily won: It is no match for you" (46).** This was on the great centipede, which halved my HP and poisoned me in one round (47).
- **"A hard fight … Onyx could fall" (91).** The hobs needed 16–22 to hit me and never landed a blow.
- **"Ring the bell — worth a look" (128, 133).** It only said "The bell's voice goes out over everything." It then stayed offered in every room of the cave.
- **"You would come there after dark" (143).** This was shown in blue at midnight.
- **Plunder heap and chest actions (115, 126).** "Prod it with the pole" and "Listen at it" were offered on a plunder heap and a chest. There was no "check for traps" for a thief with trapwork +9.
- **"Onyx shrugs off the poison. Onyx is poisoned." (47).** The two sentences contradict each other.
- **"telling blow … for 1" (35).** A critical hit for 1 damage reads oddly.
- **Lighting the lantern gave three near-identical lines (100).**
- **The taproom title overlaps the map/menu icons on a phone (74).**
- **On the trade screen the grid runs under the Continue button (07).** The trade description needed scrolling to see.

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
**Conversation** has short numbered topics and clear rewards (coin, items, "YOU KNOW NOW"). Tuck's talk was rich. Hiring Xyn showed his full stats first, which was good. But generic merchant lines leak into the wrong mouths. The tied, crying captive boy greets me with "'Now then! … A new face. What can I do for you?'" Later he says "The very one! I was just telling someone about you", and on leaving he uses Baldric's farewell.

**Fights** fit the phone well: company on the left, foes on the right, a clear turn panel, and a T key / "Let Onyx fight" button. Choosing a target by tapping the foe card works, but the target chips row did nothing. After the centipede, every fight was me pressing T.

**Inventory** is a lovely icon grid. Both treasures were the same item, "a small empty box encrusted with jewels".

**Map** is pretty, and the route panel is excellent: legs, total time, and arrival "after dark". But on a phone, a dozen "way into…" labels pile up and the panel covers half the screen.

**Notes** are the best part of the interface: aims grouped by place, and who told you what and when.

## 7. Writing
Memorable lines:

- bells hung on the hold "every one with its clapper torn out";
- "People look at what you are carrying, and then at the frame, and nobody says anything yet";

Repetitive: every monster "stands its ground and watches you. It will not have you here for long. … will give you a few minutes, no more." "Onyx looks away solemnly" / "does not look pleased" also repeats.

Things I could not have seen, or that contradict the scene:
- "Up the smoke-hole there is daylight" and "the river's light comes through" at 11 p.m. and 3 a.m.;
- "Hobs cannot abide the sound of a bell", noted as told by the boy, though I never saw him say it;
- after he died, "The Gaffer comes off his fleeces with a knife in each hand, cackling" was repeated;
- a wolf "denned" in a windowless strongroom locked from outside;
- Seolforfyrd is described as both a tall stone toll-house and a cave mouth behind brambles, and the painting is a sunlit classical arch at night;
- the painting of the bonded cellar shows sacks where the text says barrels;
- the shepherd's boy's portrait is a woman in a headscarf.

## 8. Danger
- **Rats, buck rat, Gaffer, wolf:** trivial.
- **Hob gate (2) and the raiding party ambush (3):** labelled hard, but harmless. My catfolk in leather was hit on 19–22 only.
- **The centipede:** the one real threat. Its venom bypasses armour through a saving throw. I went from 8 to 4 HP and was poisoned. Flee was shown honestly ("1 in 2"). I gambled on one dart and won.

The danger I could see coming was signalled wrongly: the "easily won" label is the problem. The best signal was on the map: "SOMETHING HERE IS DANGEROUS. Getting away is an even chance, then three in four, then sure". It was clear, honest and useful.

The real danger I felt came from my own choice: levering the flag burned out my only candle, two storeys down at 4 HP. That was the tensest moment.

## 9. Did I want to keep playing?
Yes, moderately. The undercroft's lettered flag, "the old dead under Thornhaven", star-iron and the crown in Lapisrecs pull at a treasure-seeker.

But Seolforfyrd's payoffs were flat: an "empty" jewelled box for the dead man's map, another identical box as the princes' tolls, and picks that showed no roll. Fights stopped mattering after the first hour, because nothing can hit me. I'd continue for the undercroft, not for the fighting.

Runner-up: the candle burning out after my reckless lever attempt.

## 11. Decisions
- **Levering the "unlikely" flag.** Real cost: an hour and my light, stranding me in the dark at 4 HP.
- **Going to Seolforfyrd at night.** The route panel told me I would arrive after dark. I went anyway, though nothing seemed different for it.
- **Slipping past the hobs, then attacking them from behind.** Stealth gave no surprise round.
- **Untying the boy and taking him along.** This added an escort aim.

**Tactical choices in fights:**
- I shot the centipede instead of fleeing (1 in 2);
- I used Smoke Screen against the hob archers, which hid us. Backstab then appeared, but it was weaker than my dart, and attacking broke hiding.

**What changed later:**
- Looting the heap made the Gaffer wake shouting "Thieves!".
- Stealing the wagoner's sack was noted, but the sack never reached my pack and nothing followed from it.

## 17. Who I made
Recognisable, yes. Catfolk Dex 21 made the dart deadly and my armour nearly unhittable, which is exactly why fights became trivial. The thief calling mattered: lockpicks opened the corn-store, the strongroom and the toll-chest, and stealth let me slip past the gate. Smoke Screen and Backstab were usable.

The dungeon-delver trade (digging, shovel) never came up. Even digging up the box was a plain search.

"Dead Man's Map" set the main aim and was "seen through" within one game day.

## 18. Top five problems
1. **Key actions hide in Look… and Go….** This covers slip past, the ledge, chest contents, the found box, hanging the bell and the crowbar. Opened chests and found treasure look empty from the main screen.
2. **Fight-difficulty labels are wrong and fights are mostly trivial.** "Easily won" nearly killed me; "hard fight" never touched me. Locks open with no visible roll or tension.
3. **The treasure payoff is flat.** Two identical "empty" jewelled boxes stand for both the dead man's map and the princes' tolls. No hoard is described.
4. **Prose contradicts the moment.** Daylight at midnight, a dead Gaffer still "cackling", a wolf in a sealed room, a captive child using a shopkeeper's banter, jobs credited that I never took, mismatched paintings and portraits.
5. **Phone touch and layout friction.**
   - Taps on portraits and target chips sometimes do nothing.
   - The Go… preview behaviour is inconsistent.
   - The map labels pile up and the panel covers half the screen.
   - The title collides with the icons.
   - The trade grid runs under Continue.
   - Rows of 11 portraits are crowded.
   - Items silently drop off the sell list.
