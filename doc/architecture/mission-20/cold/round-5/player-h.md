# Cold play 5: wanderer (Harrier, birdfolk ranger, once an explorer, The Brothers' Errand)

Notes: /var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold5/h/notes.md. Shots: /var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold5/h/shots/ (00 to 182). I used about 180 actions, over the 150 budget, to reach the king's door.

## 1. Summary
- Made a birdfolk ranger with the "explorer" background, which comes with a map of the district.
- Gave Indira the letter at Thornhaven and walked to the barrow at Lapisrecs.
- A pressure plate stopped me. I walked back to town for a ten-foot pole and returned. The jammed portal stone stopped me next. I walked back for a crowbar, slept at the shrine and returned a third time.
- Inside I solved two riddle caskets, disarmed traps, fought six easy fights and took the jar of noonlight from under a great hound.
- I reached the king's door on Day 2 morning. I could not read or copy the writing there (my errand), because I have no lore.

## 2. The first five minutes
Creation was clear and handsome: reason, then ancestry, then calling, then background, then name, with a one-line summary at the end. The errand card (17-setout) and Indira's conversation (20–26) gave me a lot at once:
- the letter delivered, and a rope with a hint ("the old places were not built to be walked into");
- a way to Lapisrecs;
- Lucstur, the tower nobody has climbed;
- Arborturris, with its opened graves;
- a sealed king's door with a moral cost (Indira will not pray for me if I open it).

Promising to shut the door again earned me the clue "the embalmers kept day in a jar". That was immediately interesting. I knew who I was, where I was, and that I could go almost anywhere.

## 3. What I pursued, and why
The barrow. It was the errand, but it was also the ruin I wanted as a wanderer, and the map pre-selected it. I felt I had a choice, because three or four aims were offered with "Follow this". In practice the map and Go panels always nudged me back to Lapisrecs.

## 4. Moments of confusion
- **44-passage:** Pitch dark, with a "dark vessel" that "will give you 8 minutes". I expected an offer to light my candle; it was not there. I had to find Light under Pack > candle. Later, when a light ran out, the game did offer "light a torch" in the scene (121, 156).
- **44/45:** I did not know what "a dark vessel" was until the fight card said "phylactery".
- **39, 41, 92:** Text-clicks on GO, Forecourt and Side recess did nothing. Those words also appear in the room prose.
- **136–137:** Indira's "bed in the back" is under TRADE > SERVICES. I looked for it under talk and under the hourglass (Wait).
- **179:** The opening called Harrier one "who reads the old script" (17). The king's door says "Needs lore: no one in the company has it." Creation had pre-selected Scribe for this errand; I swapped it for Explorer and nothing warned me that this would block the errand.
- **156:** I came down a stair from "one storey down" and the header still said "one storey down".

## 5. Misleading or useless controls and text
- **76–77:** In Annis's shop, each purchase adds a line above the item grid and pushes the grid down. My next tap bought a bedroll I did not want. Number keys worked.
- **175:** I text-clicked "Take" and it armed "Attack the alpha great hound", whose text says "more than you can take". Only the "Choose it again" confirm saved me.
- **Look panel:** It closes after every Take and toggles open or shut on each click, so I kept losing my place (109, 112, 120).
- **95:** After a failed plea the captain's text said "no more patience", but his timer went up from 10 to 17 minutes.
- **100, 102:** Once the captain was dead it still said "The robbers' captain will give you 7 minutes" and later "a few minutes".
- **169:** "The spirit comes at you without a sound" appeared after the spirit was dead.
- **99:** The "Press on to the end" button sits half under "The fight so far".
- **53:** The "Go on" button has a stray "<" glyph.
- **Duplicate lines:** "You light a candle." twice (48). Each fight log line appears twice, large and small (51). Equipping the cloak gave "You equip…" then "You ready…" (143).
- **31:** "Yours from your days as a explorer."
- **141:** Several pack icons are blank: bone flute, bronze mirror, cloak.

## 6. How each part felt
- **Conversation:** The best part. Big portraits and numbered topics. Topics open up as you learn things, and odds are shown ("even chance · one try"). Promises have consequences. One topic name is unwieldy: "silk from the sink at the south-western cave in the hillside on Stonecrag".
- **Fights:** Clean and readable, with a good odds line ("you would likely beat it, but Finn could fall") and a cost/still-to-do summary. But I was never hit once in six fights: foes needed 20–22. Most fights were pressing T. The animal companion was a hound one time and a 1-hp bat the next, which was funny but random.
- **Inventory:** A handsome grid with painted icons, item actions (Light, Equip) and food status ("Fed until 8pm"). Light is buried in the pack, though, and nesting is fiddly (Put in > Take from a rotted pack).
- **Map:** A beautiful 3D hex board. The route card gives legs, times and arrival time, and the ETAs were accurate (10:08 against "about 10:10", 12:57 against 1:00, 3:14 against 3:20). But:
  - Fog covers everything except the thin strip I walked, while about 40 labels float in the fog, so it is busy but empty.
  - No drawn route line.
  - Town hexes pack small targets together. I hit the circus wagons and "Woodland north of" when I meant the town (68, 128).
  - Labels move as the camera re-centres.
- **Notes:** Excellent. Aims, "told you, not yet seen", who told me and when. It does not say that my main aim needs lore I lack.

## 7. Writing
Memorable lines:
- "No bird sits on the mound."
- "A sound like a breath let out, and out, and never drawn in."
- Gallery walls painted with people walking toward the end carrying "a pot, a child, a flute, a light".
- "'Again,' says Annis, as a thing noted, not a complaint."
- "Since you were last here: the dead you left here have been dragged into a corner and picked over."

Mechanical or jarring:
- "an electrified floor" and "a lightning bolt" in a Neolithic barrow (85, 90).
- "Harrier stands over the body… does not look pleased" over a broken jar (53).
- The riddle casket was used twice in a row, with "The word sinks" for a letter.
- Room text keeps stacking the trap description into the room prose (66).
- Paintings get reused: the long passage image stands in for the gallery that the text describes as painted. A chalk hall shows stone slabs (157). The Roman temple shrine doesn't match the "slab altar in a tower" text (19).
- "Patience ambles to the end of her rope…" appears in the journey log before I had met Patience the bear (37).

## 8. Danger
Traps were the best-signalled danger. Choosing the cross-chamber warned: "about 11 harm… Harrier has 11 left: it could kill. Choose it again." (66). The trap screens list every way past and what each needs (63). Monsters were labelled clearly ("Beyond you", "Could be lost") and the hound and snakes at the end felt real (171, 177). I could step around them, grab the jar or leave. In practice nothing ever touched me. Walking into the long gallery started a fight with no preview (166), unlike the traps.

## 9. Did I want to keep playing?
Yes, but only because of the barrow. I wanted to see what the jar of noonlight does at the king's door. Learning that the writing I came for needs an 80-coin glossary or a background I swapped away was a real deflation. I would also dread the next two-hour shuttle back to town.

## 10. Boredom and interest
**Bored:**
- The three identical round trips to town (144: "the journey itself has become dead time").
- The captain fight (8 rounds of T, never threatened) and the bat fight.
- Re-opening the Look panel after every action.
- Being stopped mid-travel by bats that had never done anything (125).

**Most interesting:** The bronze mirror from the riddle casket (107), then the jar of daylight in the embalmers' room (171). Indira's clue from the first conversation paid off down in the barrow, and I could reason my way toward it. The world changing behind me (grave-robbers moving in, "six graves" where there had been five) also pulled me back in.

## 11. Decisions
- Promising Indira to shut the king's door again got me the jar clue. It changed what I knew and why I cared later.
- Not risking the plate (11 harm, 11 hp) and walking two hours for a pole. Real time cost.
- Grabbing the jar of noonlight with the alpha great hound in the room instead of fighting. Real risk.
- **At creation:** swapping Scribe for Explorer. It changed everything later (no lore at the door), but it never felt like a decision when I made it.
- **Tactical:** I called the companion first against the phylactery, and tried Ensnare on the lead thief (he saved). The ranger-only "step the wire as a snare-line" (163) made my calling matter.

## 12. Ratings
- **Clarity 3:** Traps and routes are crystal clear. Light, bed and the lore requirement were hidden or contradicted.
- **Beauty 5:** The paintings, portraits and map are lovely, and the prose is often fine. Image reuse shows deep inside.
- **Interest 4:** Layered history, clues that pay off, a world that moves.
- **Challenge 2:** Never hurt. Traps were the only real costs, and they were solved by shopping.
- **Wish to continue 3:** I want the king's door, and I dread the shuttling and the lore wall.

## 13. Motivation
- **About 10 minutes:** Get into Lapisrecs. My own wish, and the game's aim.
- **About 30 minutes:** Get past the plate, then the portal stone. Stubbornness plus "day in a jar" curiosity. Mostly my own.
- **End:** Use the jar of noonlight at the king's door. The game's aim, but now blocked: I can't copy the writing.

## 14. Marks and promises
- **Gold-highlighted "Attack the bats":** Marked as the best choice in every visit (44, 125). The bats never mattered except to interrupt travel.
- **Errand text:** Promises Harrier reads the old script (17); the door says nobody does (179).
- **Map of the district (31):** As an explorer I "carry map of the district", but the map stayed fog beyond what I walked.
- **The mirror and jar** promised to matter against the undead. In the mummy fight "Other" offered only Defend, Wait and Flee (167). They have not paid off so far.
- **"FOLLOWING" gold dot on GO:** Always led back to the landing or the barrow, which was fine.

## 15. Top five problems
1. **The background swap silently breaks the main errand.** The game said I read the old script, let me change background, and only at the final door said "Needs lore".
2. **Travel and backtracking are dead time.** Two-hour trips are an identical glide with nothing on the road. Each trap fix (pole, crowbar) cost a 4-hour round trip, and harmless bats interrupt travel.
3. **No combat threat.** A level-1 ranger in chain mail and cloak is never hit. Most fights are pressing T.
4. **Look and shop controls move under the player.** The Look panel toggles and closes after every action; the shop grid shifts after each purchase; text matches hit the prose or the wrong button.
5. **Key actions are hidden:** light under the pack, the bed under Trade > Services, the inn hireling only in Notes. Clues the game sets up (mirror, noonlight jar, torch against the undead) have no use offered in the fight.
