# Cold play report: Westernesse, a caster (Valanthe, elf evoker, "What is owed")

Notes: /var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold5/g/notes.md · Screenshots: /var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold5/g/shots (00–201). About 200 actions. I reloaded once (163); the save held.

## 1. Summary
- I made Valanthe: an elf, an evocation mage and once a trader, owing Tuck 50 coin by market day. I hired Xyn, a soldier, for 6 coin.
- I cleared the rats from the corn-store with one Magic Missile, then killed the buck rat and a great centipede. In the undercroft I read a lettered flag and opened a hidden stair.
- I travelled to Seolforfyrd by accident (a misclick), talked my way past the hobs and freed a captive shepherd's boy. He told me hobs fear bells. I found the stolen bell, rang it to rout them and burned the Gaffer to death.
- I took the boy home to Elmham. A widow there paid me for the Gaffer's death, and I sang in the taproom for coin. I talked a deserter sergeant out of his toll twice, hung the bell back in Thornhaven and paid Tuck on day 2 of 10.
- I ended at level 2 (it is "taken up after a night under a roof"), with 11 coin and a new lead to Nocstumul.

## 2. The first five minutes
Character creation was fast and handsome: icon cards, plain summaries, and a chosen spell list (00–16). The intro card stated my goal well: 50 coin, owed to Tuck, by market day (17–18). I knew who I was, whom I owed and when.

Tuck's yard was lovely but sparse (19). Tuck's portrait is only a line-drawn acorn. Talking to him gave me four aims and three distant places in one conversation (21–26). It was informative but felt like working through a menu. The landing (39–40) was the first scene that caught me: a painted quay, eleven portraits, a stolen bell, a bear asleep by the players' wagon, and a notice board with three jobs.

## 3. What I pursued, and why
- **The rats.** The game pushed this, it was concrete and it paid.
- **The undercroft.** This was my own curiosity: I followed the drain past the job's end. When a flag cut with "letters nobody here can read" appeared, my mage's lore read it. That was the moment the game felt made for my character.
- **Seolforfyrd.** I did not choose this. A misclick set me travelling (96).
- **The bell, the boy and the widow.** From then on I chose for myself, pulled by the boy's clue and by people I had met.

I always felt I had choices. If anything there were too many: about 8 aims by the end of the first hour.

## 4. Moments of confusion
- **42–43:** I clicked the Taproom in the Go list, then "Go there", and arrived at the corn-store. The game had walked me through the taproom without showing it. Sometimes a click on a list item travels straight away, sometimes it only previews.
- **88:** After "Walk there" to Tuck, I found myself at the scrivener's at 12:32 pm. The Go panel was evidently still open under my click.
- **96–97:** I clicked MAP again to close the map; it stayed open. My next click, where the hourglass usually sits, hit "Leave and set out for Seolforfyrd". That travelled me 3 hours without asking, with no spell slots left.
- **117, 177:** I never found how a mage gets spells back. The Wait panel has no sleep option. Camping in the open gave only "1 spell slot" with no reason given.
- **170:** "Travel to Elmham" closed the map and left me at the cave mouth in front of now-hostile hobs. Nothing said why the journey was blocked.
- **73 vs 142:** "A tarnished silver ring" in the rat nest carried the description of a string of rings on a grave-cord. My "Take" silently did nothing, and the ring never reached my pack.
- **126:** Leaving the toll-hall "to Seolforfyrd" dropped me onto the world map. The cave, "10 minutes" away, is drawn far north near Crowmeet.

## 5. Misleading or useless controls and text
- **59:** The gold-highlighted first option was "Lay a spell of sleep over the rats". I have no Sleep spell.
- **08:** "Wears robe of elvenkind, robe of the archmagi" reads like items I would own.
- **37:** Magic Missile "may cause fatigued" — unclear who becomes fatigued.
- **83, 181, 195:** Button tags leak into the story text: "You read the ring of letters · likely", "...the road is not his · an even chance".
- **121:** One lantern lighting is reported three times in different words.
- **93, 201:** Selling is listed under "SERVICES" below "Take your leave" and is off-screen in every screenshot. I only found it from the text dump at the very end. Tuck's promise to buy grave-goods was true but invisible.
- **The MAP header button** stays gold-highlighted in places where the map is not open (114 onward).
- **Text clicks** on "Take your leave", "Book", "Back to…" and Go list items often did nothing. Clicking directly on the control worked.

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
- **Conversation:** clean and numbered, and topics chain into new ones. NPCs in danger (the hobs, the raiders) offer only "Attack / Take your leave". Several stock personality lines clash with the situation. A tied, frightened boy "slaps the nearest flat surface by way of welcome. 'Well? Out with it!'" (138, 140, 161).
- **Fights:** a good layout, with foe targets shown, a turn order, and full control of my hireling. Xyn's list of abilities (guard, sweep, taunt) is rich, and the dice detail is readable. Every fight ended in 1–2 rounds.
- **Inventory:** a nice icon grid. Item descriptions are generic ("a small bell ... to scare off wildlife"). The list of items to take reorders after each take.
- **Map:** beautiful 3D hex islands in fog. Routes are previewed with times and an arrival hour, and journeys stop for roadside events. The dark around the islands is crowded with dozens of "way into X" labels.
- **Notes:** the best screen in the game. Aims are grouped, each has a Follow button, and every "told you" fact is logged with its source and time.

## 7. Writing
- **Memorable:**

  - "Gaffer says come in and wipe your feet. Don't touch nothing."
  - The sergeant: "Nobody is coming to relieve you, and nobody is coming to hang you either... Just the once."
  - The widow: "She doesn't cry. She counts the coins into your hand one at a time, and then puts the ring on top, and goes inside and shuts the door."
  - Tuck: "I didn't think you would."
- **Repetitive:**

  - The boy's "SINCE YOU WERE LAST HERE: rubs his wrists" appears in every place, including places he has never been.
- **Paintings that contradict the text:**
  - Seolforfyrd is described as a stone toll-house, then "brambles across the cave mouth", and painted as a classical arch (101).
  - The toll-hall is painted as a gothic cathedral (106).
  - The "dry side-cave" is painted as a flooded grotto (141).
  - The "smoky cavern" has no fire in its painting (133).
  - The bonded cellar reuses the corn-store's grain sacks (120).
  - The old drain is painted with iron pipes and lanterns (75).
  - The boy's portrait is a young woman (138).

## 8. Danger
The game flagged danger clearly: a red DANGER box, "hard fight: Valanthe could fall", escape odds, and "THEY WILL NOT WAIT". I could always see it coming, except the Gaffer, who jumped me with no warning on entering the ledge (148). Preparation mattered once: I had swapped Light for Magic Missile, so the cellar was pitch dark (55).

Real threat never arrived. The "hard" centipede died to one Spark. The Gaffer aimed both blows at my 4 HP mage; Xyn's Guard took them and my Scald cantrip burned him down. As a caster I was never hurt once. With a soldier in front, a 4 HP mage is not fragile in practice.

## 9. Did I want to keep playing?
Yes, moderately — more than I expected after the first rat fight. The boy's bell clue, ringing the bell and watching the hobs run, the widow, and hanging the bell back were a real chain I had earned. What would stop me: the deadline was meaningless (paid on day 2 of 10); fights are too easy to fear; and the fiddly controls (misclicks, invisible selling, uncertain list clicks) wore at me.

## 10. Boredom and interest
- **Bored or just clicking:**
  - Walking back from the undercroft to Tuck: about 7 rounds of Go…, then Go there (85–87).
  - Leaving Seolforfyrd: tapping out room by room through Go lists (123–126).
  - The trivial rat fights (61–69).
  - The second toll scene (195).
- **Pulled back in:** the lettered flag (82–84), the captive boy (137–139) and the widow (185–186).
- **Most interesting moment:** ringing the bell (145–146). A clue from a person, an object I found, a promise kept, and a fight avoided by wit.

## 11. Decisions
- **Hiring Xyn for 6 of my 19 coin,** money against my debt. It changed everything: he tanked every fight.
- **Fighting the centipede with one spell left,** after a "Valanthe could fall" warning.
- **Persuading the sergeant instead of paying 4 coin.**
- **Tactical:** Xyn guarded Valanthe while I used Scald on the Gaffer, who had targeted me; Sweeping Strike against the centipede (it missed).
- **Consequences that carried forward:**
  - Swapping out Light left me in the dark.
  - Parleying at the gate let me in peacefully until I touched the plunder.
  - Freeing the boy gave me the bell clue, which unlocked the cave without a fight.
  - Killing the Gaffer satisfied a widow I had not yet met.

## 12. Ratings (1–5)
- **Clarity 3:** goals and notes are crystal clear; navigation clicks, resting for spells and selling are not.
- **Beauty 4:** gorgeous paintings, type and map. Paintings often contradict the text, and portraits are reused.
- **Interest 4:** dense, connected world and lore; every place has a story thread.
- **Challenge 2:** danger is well signposted but never bites; the deadline never pressed.
- **Wish to continue 3:** the bell, boy and widow chain earned it; the friction and the ease drag it down.

## 13. Motivation
- **About 10 minutes:** clear the rats for 10 coin and hire a bodyguard. The game's aim, adopted sensibly.
- **About 30 minutes:** mine. Read the undercroft letters and see the sealed vault. Then the misclick yanked me to Seolforfyrd, and I wanted to see the bell and the hobs.
- **End:** a mix of mine and the game's. Go back to the sealed vault under the undercroft ("Something keeps it"), where I had already opened the stair. Nocstumul and the star-iron are the game's pulls. Getting my second spell slot back is my own pressing need.

7. **Mismatched.** "A tarnished silver ring" (72–73): its description was of a whole string of rings, and Take silently failed.
8. **True.** Gold-dot "Hang the bell back in its frame" (197–198): paid 10 coin with a warm scene.

## 15. Top five problems
1. **Clicks land somewhere other than intended, and nothing confirms.** List items sometimes travel and sometimes preview, panels stay open under clicks, and the map's set-out button sits where the rest control usually is. This produced an unasked 3-hour journey (96), a wrong destination (88) and a lost item (73).
2. **Selling is invisible.** It sits under "SERVICES", below the fold and under Take your leave. The loot the game kept pointing me toward seemed unsellable for an hour.
3. **Fights pose no threat and the deadline does not press.** "Hard fight" warnings ended in one round. My 4 HP mage was never touched. The 10-day debt was paid on day 2.
4. **A caster cannot find how to recover spells.** There is no sleep or rest option in Wait, camping gave 1 of 2 slots with no explanation, and inns hide the bed under "services".
5. **Paintings and stock lines contradict the scene.** A cathedral for a toll-hall, a flooded cave labelled "dry", a woman's portrait for the boy, jaunty greetings from a terrified captive, and button tags ("· likely", "· an even chance") leaking into the story text.
