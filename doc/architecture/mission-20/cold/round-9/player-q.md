# Cold play, round 9, player Q: Linnet, a catfolk illusionist (once a drifter)

An agent's cold play, not a human's. Build 505b002a (12 November, night), a random world, the "Dead Man's Map"
origin. Brief: an explorer drawn to somewhere dangerous. About 142 actions. Running notes in `player-q-notes.md`;
screenshots were kept outside the repository (/var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold9/q/shots). Numbers in brackets are those
screenshots. The report is the agent's own, as returned.

## 1. Summary
I made Linnet, a catfolk mage of the illusion school with the "Dead Man's Map" origin, and saw off two robbers on the
road. In Lassëlëa I read the well-post notice, took the crypt job from the priest Niamh and hired Jassin, an elf
ranger, for 6 of my 7 coins. In the ossuary under the churchyard a grave-robber with a black candle was marked "beyond
you", so I backed out. I took the rat job instead, put the rats to sleep with a charm and killed the old buck rat.
From there I went on through a drain (a centipede) to an undercroft, where my lore read a lettered flag that opened a
hidden stair. Three storeys down I beat the keeper of a sealed vault, looted it, climbed out, was paid, sold the loot,
slept at the inn and waited a night in the churchyard for a lead that never came. About 142 actions; I never
travelled across the country map.

## 2. The first five minutes
The origin cards ("Why have you come to Greenweald?") are a strong opening: eight short hooks over a painting. "You
won a map at dice off a drover who was dead by morning" got me at once. Ancestry, calling, school, spells, background
and name were each one clear screen with a one-line summary and DETAILS. The road scene (two men robbing a pedlar,
four choices, one "an even chance · one try") showed straight away that choices carry risk. I knew who I was, where I
was going (Týrcnyll, a cross at its door) and that the town was in front of me. The most interesting thing was the map
in my shirt and the dead drover.

## 3. What I pursued and why
I ignored the map aim, which was far away and needed travel I wasn't ready for, and chose the catacombs because the
brief said "somewhere dangerous", and an underground crypt is what an explorer wants. I found it myself on the notice
and took it from Niamh. When the crypt proved too hard I switched to the rats, which was my own choice. Following the
rat runs took me into a connected underworld (drain, undercroft, sealed vault). I chose to go deeper rather than hand
the job in, and that was the best part of the session. I always felt I had a choice. The gold "your aim" markings
pushed, but I could ignore them.

## 5. Misleading or useless controls / text
- The six toolbar icons have no hover hints (30). I learned them by clicking.
- "beyond you" appears as a bare line inside the DANGER box (69, 135). It reads like a stray fragment.
- The churchyard at night offers "To Thamior Galanodel's chandlery / To Thia's weapon-shop" but not "Down to the
  crypt" (129, 132). The crypt shows up as a scene choice only while I'm following that aim.
- **69**: The ossuary gave one choice only (ask to be let by, one try). My lore had told me the dead are held only
  while the candle burns and the warding line is missing, and nothing let me use that (put out the candle, show him
  the mistake). Charm Person wasn't offered either, because it wasn't prepared.
- **94**: "shoots… for 6", then "takes 3 bludgeoning damage (roll 6)". The two numbers disagree.
- **ENTER LASSËLËA** on the first map (26) just returned me to the road scene.
- **Peren's lodger** "goes out after dark… grave-dirt on his boots" (61). I could never find, see or ask about him,
  and watching the churchyard all night produced "Nothing comes, and nothing changes" (131).

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
**Conversation**: The large-portrait talk page is handsome and quick, with numbered choices, "Ask again…", and TRADE
built in. Most townsfolk have one or two quips and nothing more, so talking to them quickly felt like a formality.
Niamh, Erevan and Peren had real content. Important topics sometimes hide under "SOMETHING ELSE…": Erevan's rats,
which the notice sends you to her for, was hidden there (82).

**Fights**: These were the best surprise. The screen is clear (company left, foe right with its intended target, a
pre-fight estimate like "Should be won… though not unhurt"), "T" to let a character fight, and Dancing Lights
blinding a foe felt like a real tactic. The log is too noisy with save arithmetic.

**Inventory and trade**: Buying gave no confirmation line (38). Selling grave-goods to Erevan was clear and
satisfying. I didn't open the Pack tab much.

**Map**: The map is pretty but I never used it for anything. It's a small 3D island in fog ringed by many "way into X"
labels. Týrcnyll and Umbrarechies weren't visible marks, and it didn't change over my session.

**Notes**: The Notes page is excellent. "Told you, not yet seen for yourself" versus "What you know", each with who
told you and when, was genuinely useful.

## 7. Writing
Memorable lines:
- "The road's all right… It's the people on it. And the weather. And the road."
- Wapshott's bill "NOW PLAYING… on a strip pasted over the word COMING".
- The undercroft "older than anything above it: the corn-store, the church and the quarry were all dug down into it,
  from three sides".
- The keeper "stands up… slowly, as if it had been waiting a long time".

Repetitive or mechanical: every danger says "X stands its ground and watches you. It will not have you here for long.
X will give you a few minutes, no more." Every farewell is "Look after yourself… I mean it." Several paintings don't
match the text: a classical temple for a painted mural wall, a sunny spring churchyard at night, Victorian iron pipes
in a medieval drain, and the same rat-runs painting for the nest. The "pedlar" portrait is a well-dressed gentleman,
not a bleeding man in a ditch.

## 8. Danger
Yes, I was threatened. The black-candle man was flagged DANGER and "beyond you", and Jassin warned me before I
attacked: "Choose it again to do it." The warning was clear and the way back was always offered. In the deeper
dungeon each danger came with an honest estimate, and the estimates matched what happened. The ghoul keeper did hurt
Jassin badly. I could prepare in principle (sleep, change prepared spells), but I couldn't find a way to turn what I'd
learned about the black candle into an advantage.

## 9. Did I want to keep playing?
Yes, moderately. The underworld beneath the town (cellar → drain → undercroft → sealed vault, opened by my own lore)
made me want to see the "stair below the vault" and the charnel passage. Umbrarechies and the crown, and Týrcnyll, are
good hooks. What cooled me was the dead-end crypt, where I had found the key fact and couldn't act on it, and the
lodger lead that went nowhere.

## 10. Boredom and interest
I was bored clicking through the one-line townsfolk (Laucian, Adrie) and Peren's long topic list. The most
interesting moment was the undercroft: "a ring of letters nobody here can read", then READ (LIKELY), my mage's lore
roll, pressing the letters, and a stair opening (102–103). It was the one place where who I'd made opened something
the game had hidden.

## 11. Decisions
- Hiring Jassin for 6 of my 7 coins. Without him I couldn't have survived the vault, so it mattered.
- Backing away from the black-candle man after the "beyond you" warning, instead of attacking.
- Going on past the rats into the drain and the vault rather than collecting my pay. That brought loot and XP.

Tactics: I opened two fights with Dancing Lights (blind and distract), then Spark plus sling. Earlier choices did
change later ones. The crowbar I bought added "Pry… with the bar" options. My mage calling gave "put the rats to sleep
with a mage's charm". My lore read the chalk ring and the vault flag.

## 12. Ratings
- **Clarity 3/5**: Scene choices and fights are clear. The toolbar has no hints, the ways out of underground places
  hide in the Go panel, and the blue pips mean nothing findable.
- **Beauty 4/5**: The paintings and typography are lovely. Some paintings contradict the text or the time of day.
- **Interest 4/5**: The connected undertown and the hooks are strong. Townsfolk are thin.
- **Challenge 3/5**: The warnings are fair and the estimates honest. The crypt dead-end offered no clever way through.
- **Wish to continue 3.5/5**: I want the vault stair and the barrow. I'm wary of more dead leads.

## 13. Motivation
- **At about 10 minutes**: I wanted to go into the catacombs. That was my own pick from the game's notice.
- **At 30 minutes**: I wanted to see how far down the rat-runs went. That was entirely my own curiosity, and the game
  rewarded it.
- **At the end**: I wanted to take the stair below the vault and to try Charm Person on the black-candle man after
  studying it. That was my own plan, with the crypt being the game's aim.

## 15. What was offered
The notice's three jobs were worth doing. The rats led far beyond themselves. The crypt led to a wall I couldn't pass.
The quarry spiders I never tried. The best thing I found that the game did not put in front of me was the drain →
undercroft → vault chain, which appeared only as "another aim" options. The Look panel's small things (Wapshott's
bill) were nice flavour. Trade services (bed, shrine care, work for your keep) were useful to know about.

## 16. Colours
- **Gold**: my aim or the step toward it. Consistent, except that it sometimes landed on the way back out (87, 104) or
  on hiring (54b).
- **Silver/white**: "another aim". Consistent.
- **Red/orange**: danger or attack. Consistent.
- **Green**: loot. Consistent.
- **Rose**: someone who might join. Consistent.
- **Blue**: on people it seemed to mean "not met yet", which promised more than it gave. On the Look list it marked the
  corn-store padlock (the job), which made sense. I couldn't reconcile those two uses.

## 17. Who I made
Creation let me make someone recognisable (a catfolk illusionist drifter with a sling), and several choices mattered
later. Mage lore read the chalk ring and the vault letters. The mage's charm handled the rats. Dancing Lights was the
key tactic in fights. Dex 20 made the sling deadly. Catfolk had little visible effect apart from a droll "looks away
solemnly". The school list puzzled me: why are Firebolt and Drench under Illusion? And the portrait in creation and on
the map is human.

## 18. Getting about
In town, the Go panel (a list with minutes) works well, and arriving is immediate. I never set out across the country,
so I can't judge drawn routes. On the first map, "ENTER LASSËLËA" put me back on the road scene rather than in the
town. Underground, the gold "toward your aim" choice led me reliably out from three storeys down (119–121), and the
danger scenes always listed "the way you came". In the plain crypt, though, the scene had no "Up" at all, only the Go
panel's "↑ Churchyard". The depth label ("ONE/TWO/THREE STOREYS DOWN") is very good.

## 19. Waiting
After I pressed SET OUT, the first story card appeared within a second or two. "Enter to go on" showed up within about
15 seconds; I wasn't timing it closely because I was reading. From SET OUT to the first playable scene was about 72
seconds, mostly me reading and pressing Enter. The page never seemed frozen, because there was always prose on
screen. Reload → Continue took about 5 seconds and kept everything.

## 20. My aim
Following the crypt aim, the gold markings led straight to the ossuary and then had nothing past the impassable man.
The Notes page meanwhile showed the wrong next step for that aim: "Ask Peren about the old quarry" (78). With the rat
aim, gold reliably led back out and to Erevan to hand in. After handing in I had no followed aim, and the crypt
dropped out of the churchyard's scene choices (129).

1. **The crypt aim dead-ends** (69, 135): one persuasion try, then "beyond you", with no way to use what my lore
   revealed (put out the candle, the missing warding line) and no Charm Person route. The lodger lead that should open
   it never appears (61, 131).
2. **Ways out and in of underground places are inconsistent**: the crypt scene offers no "Up" and still offers to pry
   the door I came through (67, 74). The crypt and ossuary vanish from scene choices unless they're the followed aim
   (129, 134), so I had to dig in the Go panel.
3. **Blue pips on people promise something that isn't there** (29, 39, 41). Gold sometimes lands on non-aim choices
   (54b, 87).
4. **No hover hints on the six toolbar icons**, and keypresses are silently ignored while a panel is open (30, 65).
   Text-clicks sometimes hit hidden elements (11, 76, 113).
5. **Mismatches that break belief**: a human portrait for a catfolk on the map and in the creation preview (16, 137);
   a night scene under a sunny spring painting (132); a sleep that ends the same evening (128); the deadline
