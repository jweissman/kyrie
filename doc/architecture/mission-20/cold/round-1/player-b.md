# Cold play, round 1, player B

Build `7302392c` site (the M19 freeze), `?world=kahitude`, under automation: **the prologue's beats were skipped**
(the page spares a webdriver them unless `&opening`), though the road meeting was played as a place. A clean-context
agent with only screenshots and clicks; no source. Screenshots: `/var/tmp/kyrie/m20/cold/b/shots` (00–139), notes
alongside. About 140 actions; in-game day 1, 8:30 a.m. to 7:11 p.m.

Character: Merle, birdfolk ranger, once a cartographer; *A dead man's map*; hired Xyn (human warrior).

## 1. Summary
Made a character, got a wagoner out of a ditch, spent a long time in Thornhaven talking to ~15 people and reading the
well-post notice. Took the spider job in the quarry: bought supplies, hired Xyn, burned the brood, killed the spider,
returned to Aiden, paid. Paid the stranded players' reckoning ("Gosse takes off his hat to you"). Reloaded the tab:
progress kept. Travelled 3 hours to Seolforfyrd (where the map marked), dug up the "dead man's box": empty. The hobs
invited me in but I never found a way in; killed the gate-hobs instead and gave up on the hold. Travelled back to
Thornhaven's circus and stopped. Never deeper than one storey, never hurt, still level 1.

## 2. The first five minutes
Creation clear and handsome (names fit a bird person). The background screen partly repeats the reason screen ("Once
a drifter" then "The Roads / Drifter"). Opening prose very good (the greased-hide map inside your shirt; the drover
"dead in the straw, and nobody in the inn would say of what"). Knew who and where I was. What I could *do* was less
clear: first scene has Go…, Look… and an unlabelled hourglass; "YOUR FIRST GOAL" was below the fold, never seen. The
first map: a pretty diorama, a dozen labels in the fog ("way into the Corrow") that mean nothing yet. Immediate interest:
the wagoner, then the landing — a sleeping bear with a clown for a pillow, a stolen bell blamed on "Hobs", a shepherd's
boy's rumour.

## 3. What I decided to pursue, and why
I chose; not pushed. The notice offered three jobs, the castellan 300 coin for a barrow crown, the innkeeper and the boy
filled in the quarry job. Took the quarry first (key, antidote, warning, in town). Then Seolforfyrd because three threads
pointed there — my map's cross, the hobs with the bell, and the road-toll grumbling that turned out to be a "toll-house".
**That convergence felt earned and was the best moment of the hour.** So much choice it became noise: by 11 a.m. 9 aims
and ~20 leads; Seolforfyrd alone was 3 different aims.

- **"Something else…"** hides important actions behind trivia: hiring Xyn (58), "Pay the players' reckoning" option 8

- **"To the woods of Asholt…"** (112) looks like a menu but leaves the site.

- **"Leads: heard, not yet seen"** (125) lists things already done (freed the wagon, paid the players).
- **Purchases** silently go to Xyn ("Buy an oil firebomb for Xyn", 71); found items too ("collect the dead man's box

  with no webs (80); glowing intact egg sacs after the burning (91); stags in a sunny glade for a stone toll-house on the
  water (111); sheep and a cottage for the circus wagons (137). A painting caption shows on the butcher (39).

## 6. Conversation, fights, inventory, map, notes
**Conversation** — the best part. Gosse, Pudding and the bear, Hepzibah, Dannel Hobb, Snig and Hench have real voices.
Ordinary townsfolk (Hugh, Alys, Cuthbert, Annis) have one quip. Great hooks have no handle: can't ask Gosse about Lem,
help Hench, or find Lem.
**Fights** — clean: portraits, HP bars, enemy intent ("→ Merle · shoot with the dart"), "what it cost". Too easy and
short: a swarm of dog-sized spiders died to one arrow, two hobs to two. A bite rolled three saves, one a "magic save".
"Takes 1 true damage" is jargon. Calling my animal companion gave a rat with 2 HP.
**Inventory** — nice icon grid; "Fed until 8 p.m." The wagoner's sack of meal never appeared.
**Map** — the route card (four legs, times, arrival) is excellent. Otherwise fog with floating labels; travel is a
silent camera move: 3 hours with nothing seen, said or met.
**Notes** — "What you mean to do" plus folded other aims with Follow / On the map works. Leads list (who, when) useful
but huge. Story tab: a transcript of the day.

## 7. Writing
Memorable: the opening map, Gosse's "tragedy in the classical sense", the bear "young for a duchess", Dannel's "I
dropped Dad's knife. Don't tell him.", bells hung "every one with its clapper torn out", Snig's invitation, Hench's
button, the Colonel's "KNOCK. WAIT. KNOCK AGAIN." Mechanical: "The spiders in the old workings is here.", "…will give
you 8 minutes, no more.", names repeated four times in a block with singular verbs for plurals (80, 89); "Merle looks
away solemnly" after shooting a spider; "YOU KNOW NOW: On the way into Thornhaven you got a wagoner out of the ditch";
loot listed twice (134). Told after the fact: the firebomb, the spider mother's death. Biggest letdown: the dead man's
map — my reason for coming — ends in "a small empty box", +300 XP, no word on who emptied it or why the drover died.

## 8. Danger
Warned well ahead ("SOMETHING HERE IS DANGEROUS", odds of getting away, "Easily won / Should be won"); preparing felt
good. Retreat trivially easy (walked away from beetles, no pursuit). Nothing threatened me; forecasts were wrong in my
favour, so the signals felt hollow.

## 9. Want to keep playing?
Partly. People and place-writing good enough that I wanted to meet the Colonel, find Lem, go down Lapisrecs. But every
adventure was flat (one or two presses, no exploring, no risk), and the one interesting payoff failed (empty box, an
invitation I couldn't accept, a pointless killing). "I would keep playing for the writing, not for the game."

## 10. Top five problems
1. Seolforfyrd has no visible way in after a successful parley; the only exit leaves the site.
2. Adventures resolve in one or two clicks with no tension; key events happen off-screen and are reported after.
3. Key actions hidden or unlabelled (hire and pay under "Something else…"; search and wait bare icons).
4. Story payoffs don't land (the map's empty box); aims and leads pile up with duplicates and done items.
5. Pictures and text disagree; state bugs (duplicated prose, hobs turn into goblins, a key that can't be used on its
   gate, the followed aim lost on reload, title and card disagreeing on where you are).
