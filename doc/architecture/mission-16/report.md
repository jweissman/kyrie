# Mission 16 report

Mission 16 set out to make Kyr a fantasy RPG someone could open, understand and play for an evening. That meant
beginning as one person, finding an adventure from the page alone, going in, seeing it through, coming back to a
world that remembers, and choosing what to become. This report says where that stands at the mission's close. The
running record is the [ledger](ledger.md). What the mission inherited, and what became of it, is in the
[reconciliation](reconciliation.md). The [plan](plan.md) holds the decisions.

No one outside this session has played this build. Every play described here is mine, or a policy's I wrote.

## 1. Aesthetic decisions

**One white page, read like a book.** A running head gives the country, the place, the hour and the weather. Under
it come the place's name and its telling, with the people about it named in the telling and spoken to from there.
Under the telling there is a line each: danger first, then people, animals, things to look at, and whoever is with
you. The ways about the place go in a line, and the roads out are routes: by what way, how long, whether you have
been. The walled field, the portrait grid, the cream paper and the dark theme are gone. Darkness is told in the
fiction. The one dark page is a death.

**The conversation page is kept**, as the owner asked and the cold player praised: the painting large with the name
on it, the dialogue beside it, and replies by number. A painting is used only where it fits:
- a role is read by the work it names, so the priest of the Chapel of the Ford is a priest and not a ford;
- a title gives sex and age (Mother, Gammer, Sir), and so does kin;
- forty-two paintings are refused, each with its reason: faces a player may know, saints with their emblems,
  severed heads, sketches.

Where nothing fits, or the painting cannot be loaded, the page is the same in type: the name large, the role under
it. Merrow's people, Mother Ysolt among them, greet in their own manner and are painted for what they are.

**Rhythm.** Ordinary happenings run in the story's serif. Coming into a new country is a chapter heading ("Into
Merrowdale"). Something seen through ends a chapter: a heavy rule, the adventure's name large, what it was worth,
and when the level is taken up. A level gained is a lighter moment of the person, under a blue rule: who and the
level, what it gave, and the development it brings. A death is the dark page. Still told in the ordinary register:
a body found, a revelation.

**Inspect, select, preview, commit, consequence.**
- **Inspect.** A thing has its own page, set large, with its acts grouped (work it, look, take). What a close look
  finds becomes part of how the thing is told.
- **Select.** The keys move a visible cursor.
- **Preview.** The previews are the rules' own:
  - a journey's legs and times before setting out;
  - each development's effect, worked out by doing it to a copy ("Strength 12 → 13: to land a blow 9 → 8");
  - a fight reckoned in words before it starts ("beyond you", "It is more than the company can take");
  - a grave act said before it is done.
- **Consequence.** What came of an act is told as what happened, and what was learned is noted as known.

**The crowded scene.** The place names its people in its own telling. Danger comes first. Seven "take up" acts
became one line, "Aldith: a development to spend", which opens the character. "Set out for" appears only where
there is somewhere known to go. Still crowded: a market's twenty goods, and ways sorted by how long they take rather
than by whether they leave the place.

**Keys, narrow screens, no images.**
- Every choice can be reached by the arrows and Enter.
- A conversation's replies are numbered.
- The sheets have letters: Character C, Notes J, Pack I, Map M, Story H.
- The first arrow shows where the cursor is before it moves.
- What happened is announced to a screen reader (`aria-live`), and the keyed choice is marked (`aria-current`).
- The Bushweald play went from the title page to the Long Man's storm by keys alone.
- Without images the conversation page is now in type. Before, it was a black slab with a broken picture and the
  painter's credit.

## 2. The opening route, with time

**Placement and lead.** Every world Begin draws holds Merrowdale beyond the north march of its first country, and
one independent adventure on another side. Every town of that country can tell of it. The keeper of travellers can
be asked by name ("Ask Fargrim about the forty crowns in Merrowdale"), and gives the way from that town. The
steward's notice is nailed up where travellers are kept, and the room's telling says so: "half the talk is of the
forty crowns it offers and the other half of why nobody has gone". Read or heard, the lead puts the march-stone on
the map, and the country's roads, which its folk know, take you there. "Set out for" shows the legs and their times,
then walks them as ordinary commands. It stops for danger, a fight, a hurt, nightfall between legs, weariness,
hunger, or something going on.

**The times.**
- **Baseline, at `0e066e5`.** In Palereach the only lead was the innkeeper's eighth topic of eleven, "the road
  east": 96 seconds for a player who already knew to ask, and no way for one who did not.
- **Lonelymoor.** A dwarf warrior on the brothers' errand. From Begin to Merrow green took 4½ minutes.
- **Bushweald.** A human cleric carrying feverwort, played by keys alone at 1440 wide.
  - Begin was pressed at 04:00:50; making the character took a minute.
  - The second page, the reed-yard at Lótëtîr, already told of the notice.
  - The notice was read and the march-stone offered at 04:04:22.
  - After a detour to some ruins in the woods, the journey took six legs and a night's camp, and "Into Merrowdale"
    came at about 04:06.

  These are a driver's times: the driver reads each page at once. A person would take longer, but the lead is in
  the telling of the second page they reach, so it is met within a page or two of reading, not ten minutes.
- **A newcomer who sees only the page.** A policy that names nothing: set out for anything newly offered (again if
  stopped); else read; else ask the first question not asked; else go somewhere not yet been.

## 3. Character and advancement

**The Character view exists from the first page, for one person.** Every figure on it is worked out by the rules the
game plays by:
- each quality, with its modifier in a fight and in a check, and what rests on it;
- what the person strikes with, and what a blow does;
- what they need to land a blow, and what a common foe needs to land one on them;
- saves, skills and where each skill comes from;
- techniques in full, the spells prepared today, and what they carry.

Two things were added in the last play. A specialist says what it is sworn or schooled to: "Deathspeaker. You are a
herald of death and the afterlife. Aldith's first prayers are of death." The play met a cleric praying curses and
drains under nothing but "Prayers that heal and protect". And a spell above any slot the member has says "not yet".

**Background and origin change what happens.**
- A scribe's lore reads the Mower's breastplate, and the page says whose lore it was.
- An apothecary carries the herbs that break the fever at Lótëtîr.
- The drover's child comes home to their own family: their mother at the hearth, their brother with the spear, and
  their father under the cart. "It is your father. You know the coat first, and the hands, before you will let
  yourself know the face."

**Companions arrive through encounters.** The drover's son goes with his kin. Inns have people for hire.

**A level is one development**, spent in the Character view: a point of any quality, or a skill the calling teaches.
- Each option says what it changes.
- A skill says what it opens among the places the company has been: "Climbing … Opens the gauge shaft, to the foot
  of the gauge shaft". Taken up, it opened the shaft.
- In the Bushweald play the cleric took up Lore at level 2, from the Character view, by keys.
- Techniques are not among the choices, since a level-1 warrior already knows all ten of its calling's. That is in
  the ledger as deliberately left.

Still open:
- an option can be shown that cannot be taken, with no reason given ("Reading the dead");
- rolled qualities cannot be placed;
- techniques are written in the rules' shorthand ("End on yourself; 3d8 + 1 + 1"), under names like "Vigor Up";
- a recruit is not seen before hiring.

## 4. The principal expedition

**The Long Man** is a dale under a long straight ridge, which is a dam, with a lake behind it and a storm three days
off. The steward's notice offers forty crowns for laying the Mower.

The geography holds together. The green, the Drowned Ox, the chapel and Gammer Coller's kitchen lie below; the Hall
yard, the cellar and the steward's study under the ridge's north end. The crest runs along the ridge, with the gauge
stone and a sinkhole. Inside it are the galleries: the grey gallery, the sluice chamber, the keeper's niche, the wet
gallery and the clay core. Beyond lie the lakeshore, the deep water, the drowned town and its Water-House.

The mechanisms explain themselves when watched:
- by night the Mower walks the crest and cuts every sapling before its roots can crack the wall;
- it goes after bronze taken from its wheels;
- the steward had that bronze prised out, and at nightfall it is moved from his cart to a barrel in the cellar;
- the iron bar beside it is "bright with scraped bronze".

**Endings without a fight.** The dale is saved in any of three ways:
- give the Mower back its bronze, and it mends the leak gate (played to the end three times from Begin);
- draw the lake down with the dyke-reeve's rod, which saves the wall and drowns the mill;
- show Mother Ysolt what the notches on her font are, and Merrow goes up the hill before the wall breaks.

Around these the people act on their own: the steward, shown his own ledger, leaves on the barge, and the Mower can
be laid by day with the steward's own bar, which leaves the wall with no one to keep it. Each way is a test.

**No empty answers.** Every close look in the expedition is answered in its own words, and a try that finds nothing
says so in the words of the try.

**Treasure with identity and use.**
- The bell-teeth are bronze off the sluice wheel. Given back, they mend the gate; to a barge-master they are money.
- The grey mask earns the forty crowns, or Mother Ysolt buries it.
- A second face, fired and never fitted, lies in the drowned town's moulds and buys the bounty too.
- The notched rod turns the great wheel.
- Pell's lantern, brought out of the wet gallery to Dunnet, has the parish told by nightfall.

**Seen through for a level.** Seen through, the Long Man is 1,000 experience to each of the company, which is a
level, taken up after a night under a roof. Leaving and coming back, a reload in the middle of the dungeon, and a
game kept by an earlier build all hold.

**The storm, played for the first time from Begin.**
- The wind went round to the north on the third night.
- Afterwards: "The beck runs brown but low between its banks, and nobody on the green is talking about the Long
  Man. The springs at its foot, people say, have nearly stopped."
- The play found two faults, now repaired:
  - the dale counted its days from the hour its country was reached, so the storm's end, "The rain stops before
    dawn", was told at nine in the evening; a country now counts from the morning after it is reached;
  - the Ox still talked of the low field going under the evening after the leak was shut; it now talks of the
    springs running clear.
- Still open: the region's sky stays as it was through the storm (the running head said "fair").

**What a company without the right skills meets.** A lone cleric cannot push the punt ("Needs brawn: no one in the
company has it") or get into the galleries ("You would need a coil of rope"). That is honest, but nothing says where
brawn or rope may be had.

## 5. Continuing play

**The return is acknowledged.** When Merrowdale is seen through, the country below calls its news old:
- the talk where the notice hangs is of the word down the north road;
- the forty crowns are no longer asked about;
- whoever keeps travellers can be told "you have been to Merrowdale", and answers with what the road brought down
  first ("There was word of it already… Nobody said who"), apart from the company's own part;
- then the notice goes on the fire.

**Own history, reports and traces are kept apart.** The brothers' expedition was the diagnostic.
- They go up to the deep site after its prize on the second morning, and down a level every two days.
- Their going is heard where it is talked, and the Notes file it as heard.
- Their prior says where they have gone.
- Their mark is on each level from the day they reach it, and their fate is heard where it is told.
- In the same way, the drover's son kneeling by the cart is seen, not filed among things known.
- The Notes keep seen, read, found and heard apart.
- One leak was found and repaired: the Notes asked a company that had never heard of a brood to tell its keepers
  it was quiet. What the company is asked to see to is now what it has heard of or met.

**The game is kept whole.**
- The Story is the whole story across a reload.
- A game is kept in two slots with the pointer written last, and stamped with its build.
- A game kept by an earlier build was opened by the next, twice in the last play.

**A second adventure in the same game.** In Coldwaste, Saltgrave (Thrume) was entered after Merrowdale, by its own
lead and road.

## 6. The independent adventure

**Low Water** is merged whole (81eaa26), as its author marked it complete. The Bowl of Ammerlin is one of the
independent adventures a world may hold; of the eight worlds above, thorn, mere and e5 hold it beyond their south
march. The country below tells of it in its own terms: a city whose Mere is falling, wanting someone from outside to
measure it. Two shared changes came with it: underground ways are told without the sky, and state names are shown
as words. Its 36 journeys pass. Its friction list of 27 entries is in the reconciliation. The mission did not work
through it, and one entry is partly met.

**Thrume** was entered from the world that held Merrowdale.

**Not done.** Low Water was not played from Begin to an ending in a generated world in this mission. *What Tessary
Owes*, a new independent adventure, arrived on its own branch on 26 October. It is not assessed here.

**Assessed separately.**
- **Mechanics.** The way in, travel, the expedition's endings, advancement and saving all work as designed.
- **Intelligibility.** The page says what a choice will do and what came of it, and the Character view explains
  every figure. A player can still be stopped without knowing where to turn (the punt, the galleries), and some
  pages are crowded.
- **Literary quality.** The authored dale is the best writing the game shows, and the new moments sit well. The
  generated country is still visibly generated:
  - everyone is asked "about their work";
  - two neighbours share a name;
  - weather lines recur;
  - a body found is told in the same voice as a cart.

**Independent feedback.** None. No player or reviewer outside this session has played Mission 16's build. The cold
playtest and the reviews were of earlier builds, and are evidence of what this mission set out to repair, not of
whether it did.

## 9. The next artistic obstacles

1. **The people of a generated country are alike.** The authored dale shows what a manner, a voice and a likeness
   do. The generated towns still give everyone the same questions, and sometimes the same name.
2. **The exceptional register is unfinished.** Chapters, levels and deaths are set apart. A body found, a betrayal
   or a revelation is not, and those are the moments a reader remembers.
3. **The callings are a catalog's, not the game's.** A cleric's domain, a warrior's ten techniques and the
   shorthand of their effects come from Orsino. The calling's own words ("Prayers that heal") and the repertoire
   it actually has can disagree, and the game cannot yet say so in its own voice.
4. **Time and weather are not yet one fiction.** A placed adventure keeps its own clock and its own storm, and the
   region keeps its own sky.
5. **The map is not yet a pleasure.** It is legible at a desk, cramped on a phone, and confused where two places
   share a name.
6. **Companions are not yet someone.** They come and fight; they do not yet remember, know things the company does
   not, or speak of what they have seen.
7. **A person, not a policy.** The next mission should begin with someone new playing this build from the title
   page, and should be shaped by where they stop.
