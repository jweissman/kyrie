# Mission 10: a campaign that goes on

The brief asked for Kyr to become a compelling, sustainable, open-ended fantasy campaign RPG rather than a collection
of integration demonstrations, and to prove it: two generated worlds, about ten freely chosen expeditions each, the
experience tracked, and an honest answer to whether an RPG player would spend several evenings on it.

This report starts with what playing it is like, then says what was built, measured and published, and ends with
what is still not good enough. No one but its builder has played any of this. Both worlds were played on the final
build (`d4e2495`) by a script that plays as a careful player would:

- it hears what is said at the inn, the shrine and the shop, and buys light and food;
- it goes where it has been told of, reads what it can, and buys a glossary of the old script when nobody can read
  it;
- it fights what the company reckons it can beat, and leaves what it cannot;
- it sells, has the dead raised or buries them, and sleeps;
- it crosses the march when a region is done.

The script only chooses. Nothing in either world was arranged for it, and every transcript was read afterwards. The
transcripts are in [`a1-expeditions.md`](../playtests/mission-10-2026-10-22/a1-expeditions.md) and
[`b2-expeditions.md`](../playtests/mission-10-2026-10-22/b2-expeditions.md).

To play: open **https://jweissman.github.io/kyrie/**, or add `?world=a1` (or `?world=b2`) to start in one of the
worlds described below.

## Playing it

### Merscfaenn: twelve expeditions (seed `a1`)

Merscfaenn is fen country. Murkstede, its one town, is *"a town of human folk, living by salt"*, with the shrine of
Nodens across the green. Three companions have come to it, each because someone sent word:

- Elen, an elf ranger, came because Cyril, the traveller at Murkstede, sent word of a green light in a lone tower;
- Bran, a warrior, because a merchant at Faennslím sent word of an old well behind it;
- Dori, a dwarf cleric, because the priest at Wǽttún sent word of opened graves.

1. **A cistern of the Water-Wardens** (days 1–3). At the Lucky Phoenix, Cyril offers to come along for three coin
   and his keep. A search of the heart of Bruinforwest finds the way to *"a square doorway in a hillside"* and to
   *"a roofless abbey"*. Paliduslac goes down *"a hundred steps and more, each tread worn hollow in the middle"*, to
   a pillared hall standing in black water. Winding the sluice-wheel round: *"Deep in the hill a gate grinds open.
   The black water in the hall begins to fall, and goes on falling."* A moonstone and a jeweled box sell to Mik, the
   salt-merchant, and their worth, 165 coin, is experience for each of them. While they sleep, *"Amrod has taken
   his father's old spear and gone out on the road alone. Nobody stopped him."*
2. **The abbey** (days 3–5). No one in the company reads the old script, so Elen buys a glossary of it at the shrine
   for 80 coin. At Wannhaff the abbey's chronicle tells how the kingdom after the Wardens fell: *"It fell to raiders
   from over the sea, who came every summer until there was no one left to raid."* With the glossary Elen reads the
   brothers' copies of the Wardens' inscriptions. The journal then holds the way to something nobody in the region
   speaks of: *"a dam of dressed stone across a valley in Glōmmeós."*
3. **The Wardens' dam** (days 5–7). There are three great wheels in the gate-tower: *"Far down in the dam something
   gives with a sound like a bell, and then the roar begins: the mere going out of the valley through gates nobody
   has opened in a thousand years. When at last it stops, there are houses where the water was."* In the drowned
   street *"the water in the gutters of the street runs together, and stands up, and is taller than you"*. The
   first-level company beats the keeper of the mere. The still water in the hall shows how the Wardens ended, and
   the way to the lone tower. Back in Murkstede, Mik pays 800 coin for a kraken claw from the vault. After that he
   has too little left for an aquamarine until morning. Level 2.
4. **Umidusspira, the lone tower** with the green light (days 7–9), an authored adventure set into the region. In the
   bone arch is *"something the shape of a hound … made of many dead men's bones wired together."*
5. **Obscurusfons** (days 9–10), the Wardens' second cistern.
6. **Nocswigil, a long mound east of Wǽttún** (days 10–12). The marks on the seal-stone tell the Barrow-Kin's craft.
   Rolling the stone aside: *"Out on the land, under the dark, something comes out of the barrow and goes along the
   old bound, as it used to."* The barrow-dead wait in the end chamber.
7. **Lapisrechies, and Sir Hamo's outlaws** (days 12–14). *"A horn sounds above you, two long notes."* Elen reckons
   the outlaws could be beaten, but that someone could fall. A search finds a passage from the ring-fort's store-pit
   out into the gorse. Elen falls in the fight in the yard. Bran takes her lantern and her glossary, and the others
   go on: the sentry, Sir Hamo Ashdown, a fat purse of road-money, then the barrow-dead. At the shrine, *"Eogan keeps
   the vigil over Elen all night, and at first light Elen draws a breath, and then another."* Level 3.
8. **Ísencepan, a broken keep** (days 14–15). Wolves are in the bailey. From the wall-walk, the way to another
   mound.
9. and 10. **Palustumulo** and **Gloom Howe** (days 15–18): a ghoul in each, as the folk had said.
11. **Over the march** (days 18–20). The old road east ends at a march-stone, and the road goes on *"into Rindwudu,
    forest country, the folk say. Few of them have been."* The country beyond is drawn as the company gets there,
    with its own land, towns and works. At the Merry Rose in Lilieburg, Warryn joins (*"Ribbons, pins and news. The
    news sells best."*). The talk is of:
    - a stranger who pays in old coin and asks the way to the fallen walls;
    - a sick child at Blóstmabearwas whose herbs have run out.

    Oratoport, a singing hall of the Stone-Singers, opens to the notes cut on its door. In the silent hall, an ear
    against the stone: *"They sang the last of themselves into the stone of their hall, all of them at once."* The
    journal now has the way to *"a cliff cut into a face in Albemunt."*
12. **Altuscarmen, the Stone-Singers' choir** (days 20–21). On the way: *"In the morning the shrine bell is rung once,
    slowly. The child on the green has died."* Nobody had gone to Blóstmabearwas with herbs. In the choir *"one of
    the figures in the wall steps out of it. It has no face, only the smooth place where the Singers had not finished
    it."* Warryn falls, and the warden is broken. A hand on the last singer: *"in the greatest of them they are
    singing still."* Rindwudu's priest raises Warryn. Level 4.

### Karkatzeh: twelve expeditions (seed `b2`)

Karkatzeh is fell country of dwarven folk. Ramavor, its town, lives by tin (*"the name is Khuzdul for 'tall
pass'"*), with the shrine of Rhiannon. The same three companions come. Elen came because Gimgen sent word of ewes
taken at a pit in the ground; Bran and Dori both came about the silk from the same sink.

1. **Stanumtener, a gallery of the Delvers** (days 1–3). Gimgen joins: *"Mending pots. There are more locks than pots
   in this country, and they pay better."* The way goes over the Telegavor pass to the roof of Zhargeithhar. In the
   galleries the company breaks the rust off the wheel and winds the lift-cage up. Then it lifts the bars off an
   iron door: *"The iron door swings open of its own weight, and the cold comes out of the dark behind it, and has a
   shape."* The first-level company kills the cold below, and Elen dies. There is not coin enough to raise her:
   *"You dig a grave for Elen and lay them in it, and say what can be said over them. Then you go on without
   them."* The next morning the shrine bell rings for a child at Barzelmizela whose herbs ran out.
2. **Lapistener, the second galleries** (days 3–5). *"The goblins slip away from you,"* and fight only when cornered.
   Behind the second iron door is a second cold, and Dori dies. A jeweled dagger sells, and *"Orsik keeps the vigil
   over Dori all night."* One coin is left.
3. and 4. **Caelumspir** and **Steletur**, towers of the Star-Readers (days 5–8). *"The lookouts stand their ground
   and watch you. They will not have you here for long."* Level 2.
5. **Gefeohtcepan, a broken keep** (days 8–10). Bran buys the glossary at the shrine on the way. *"A horn sounds
   above you, two long notes":* Sir Hamo's outlaws are close by. Bran reckons a fight with them could be lost, and the
   company leaves them be. In the keep: wolves, the lord's strongbox, and the castle roll:
   *"After the Delvers came the kingdom of Aergodríce, where the power lay with warlords who built keeps on every
   height and fought one another for the roads. It fell to a plague that came up the river in a wool-ship and left
   whole villages empty."* At the inn, Taklinn says what has moved into the emptied galleries: *"'The spiders, that's
   what… Somebody cleared it out once. Somebody had better do it again.'"*
6. **Gold Hole** (days 10–12): spiders.
7. **Fyrsteport, a toll-house by the water** (days 12–14). Rats come out of the bales, and a golden idol is in the
   toll-chest. Eberk, the smith at Ramavor, pays 400 coin for it. Level 3.
8. **Tin Sink** (days 14–15). The company comes too late. Garth has burned the spiders out himself: *"'Saw the smoke
   from here,' says Garth. 'Good riddance.'"* At the silk-frames: *"'Twelve years,' says Odile. 'Twelve years I fed
   them.' She does not say anything else."*
9. **Argentumturris, a lone tower** (days 15–18). There is a cold night on the pass: *"Night comes down on the
   Telegavor, and the cold with it. There is no shelter, and nothing to burn."* In the crypt are the risen dead and
   a knitted hound.
10. **Over the march, and Solspir** (days 18–20). At Greneheald, Hilda, a charcoal-burner, joins (*"The burn is done
    and the charcoal sold. I have a month before the next."*). The talk at Greneheald:
    - the innkeeper tells of a howe where *"the dead in it sit up if you take so much as a pebble"*;
    - the priest wants what the Star-Readers wrote at their tower at Solwis: *"I would pay well for it."*

    At Solspir, with the glossary, Bran reads the tablets: *"Two of their towers came to keep two counts, a day apart,
    and each called the other false. The war of the reckonings burned every tower but the ones that stand."* Setting
    the bronze ring to its sight-line gives the rest of their story, and the way to *"a tower taller than any other in
    Genipfullmunt."*
11. **Altustur, the Star-Readers' great tower** (days 20–22). The last reckoner is killed. At the top is the orrery,
    and the way to another of their towers.
12. **Lunaspir** (days 22–23), another of the Star-Readers' towers.

## The eleventh evening

The brief asked what a player would want to do on the eleventh expedition. After twelve, this is what each world
still holds.

**Merscfaenn and Rindwudu (a1).**

- *Six march-stones* lead to countries nobody has drawn yet: three from Merscfaenn, and three from Rindwudu. Each will
  have its own land and works, the same world's history, and troubles of its own.
- *Most of Rindwudu.* Its second singing hall, two barrows, a keep and an abbey are unvisited. A stranger who pays
  in old coin is asking the way to the fallen walls, and Garth's ewes are still being taken.
- *Whatever has moved into the works the company emptied.* The inns will talk of it, and the shepherds pay to have a
  work cleared again.
- *The people left as they were:* the toll-reeve on the Deopex, Amrod gone out with his father's spear, and the
  quarrel over the moved stones at Meóswaeter.

**Karkatzeh and Rindwudu (b2).**

- *The Delvers' story is still unread.* The company went through both galleries before it had a glossary, and the
  tally by the furnace is in the old script. Now it has the glossary, and the tally is the way to the Delvers'
  greatest work, which nobody in Karkatzeh has spoken of.
- *Sir Hamo's outlaws* still hold their cave. The company left them alone at second level, and is third level now.
- *Most of Rindwudu.* The priest at Greneheald still wants what the Star-Readers wrote at Solwis, their one tower
  there the company has not climbed. Two barrows are unvisited, one of them the howe whose dead sit up if you take a
  pebble, and so are a keep and a toll-house.
- *The spiders in Stanumtener,* which moved in after the company emptied it.

That is a real answer, and it did not exist at the mission's start. Then a region held five to eight things to do,
every world the same eight, and none of them led to another.

## What was built

The review ([`mission-10-review.md`](mission-10-review.md)) found thirteen ways the world's richness was lost before
it reached play. The brief's elements, and where each stands:

| The brief asked for | Where it stands |
|---|---|
| A review | Done first: thirteen findings (`2e520a6`). |
| Proof: two worlds, about ten expeditions each | Twelve in each, on the final build (above). |
| A cosmology, and a history of making in strata | A history in three strata, one per world: makers, the kingdom after them, the folk now. No cosmology beyond each region's faith. |
| Geography as a medium, and an honest map | Land generated from heights and water, with settlements, roads and wild country laid on it. The map draws only what the company knows, one region at a time. |
| Orsino's breadth | 356 item kinds; 18 creature kinds met in eight worlds (11 before). Traps and wonders do not reach the works yet. |
| Settlements by scale | Purses, raising the dead and the glossary differ by size, and nothing else. |
| Substantial adventures whose discoveries open outward | Thirteen work plans, each with a mechanism of its own. Inscriptions, rolls and walls point to other works, and the great work comes at the end of each people's story. |
| Interactive freedom | Where to go and in what order, whether to fight, wait, sell, raise or bury. No free-form verbs. |
| Authoring fluency | Work plans are Kyr templates, and errors name the problem. |
| A perception and narration model | Not built. |
| Openings and companions | Not built, beyond recruits who say what brings them and who sent word. |
| Combat and advancement | Folded choices, reckoned odds, treasure as experience, raising. Levels 1 to 4 in three weeks of play. |

| Aesthetic completion | Each work's text was written for it, with map labels and region titles. It is uneven; see the critique. |
| Performance, before and after | Measured; see below. |
| GitHub Pages and the iPad | Published, and checked at iPad size in Chromium, not on an iPad. |
| Rigorous QC | The expedition player, and the defects it found (see *Quality*). |

### 1. The core loop (findings 11, 12)

- **Waiting until something happens.** The company can wait until first light, until dark, or *and watch, until
  something changes*. That wait ends when someone comes or goes or something new is seen, not after an hour.
- **Fights.** A long list of actions folds to the three a companion would most likely use; the rest are one choice
  away. Before a fight, a companion reckons the odds from simulated fights: *"Bran reckons a fight with them could
  be lost."*
- **Light.** Dark places are dark: without a light there is nothing to see or do. A torch burns for an hour, and any
  merchant sells a lantern for seven coin.
- **Treasure is experience.** Orsino's goods are Kyr's now: 356 item kinds, with its junk and gems as treasure.
  - A dealer pays what a treasure is worth. The first time each one is sold, its worth is experience for each of
    the company.
  - A dealer's coin comes back by morning, and a town's chest goes furthest.
  - A dealer short of coin says what a thing is worth, and whether to come back or take it to a town.
- **Death.**
  - A fallen companion's gear can be taken from them.
  - A town's priest raises them (Orsino's Raise Dead) for a night's vigil and 300 coin.
  - Otherwise they are laid to rest and leave the company, and someone at the inn can be asked along in their place.
- **Rumours.** What the company already knows is not offered again. Two sites that look alike are told apart by
  where they are: *"a long mound east of Wǽttún"*, *"the north-eastern timbered adit on the roof of
  Zhargeithhar"*.

### 2. A world to campaign in (findings 1, 2, 3, 5, 6)

**Land.** A region is generated as land: heights, water that runs downhill, and cover by height and wetness. On it:

- settlements where people would settle;
- roads routed by cost;
- wild country with an edge, a deep and a heart.

The map draws only the land the company knows.

**History.** There are five peoples of makers: the Water-Wardens, the Delvers, the Star-Readers, the Barrow-Kin and
the Stone-Singers. Each has its craft, its belief, and two possible ends. A later kingdom came after them, ruled by
warlords, an order of abbots or merchant-princes. It fell to plague, a long winter, its heirs' war or raiders. The
history belongs to the world, not to each region: the Barrow-Kin end the same way on both sides of a march, and one
kingdom's works stand in every region. The makers' works are named in the old tongue (Aenean), and the kingdom's in
the folk's tongue.

**Works.** Sites are now the works of that history. They are written as Kyr templates
(`data/generation/works/*.kyrt`), thirteen plans in all:

- the makers' cistern, galleries, watchtower, barrow and singing hall;
- the later kingdom's keep, abbey and toll-house;
- five great works, one per people of makers.

Each has a mechanism of its own:

- a sluice-wheel that drains a hall;
- a jammed lift-cage;
- iron doors barred on the near side;
- a seal-stone;
- a door that opens to a sung note;
- a bronze ring that sights the next tower.

Each also has:

- what the later kingdom made of it, and how that kingdom fell, each shown its own way (an abbey's chronicle, a
  keep's roll whose last entries break off);
- what lives there now;
- treasure;
- folk who tell of it.

A region holds about ten sites, against three to five before.

**An arc that opens outward.** The greatest of the first makers' works in each region is hidden: no one speaks of it,
and no search finds it. The way to it opens when the company knows its makers' whole story, from inscriptions read
in their other works or from an abbey's copies of them. The old script is read by a scholar, or with a glossary,
either an abbey's or one bought at a town's shrine. Each great work is the grandest form of its art, and its keeper
is of level five. At its heart is the truth of how its makers ended, which differs as their end did. For the
Barrow-Kin it is one of two:

- the Barrow-Kin sealed themselves into the great howe, and wrote *NOW WE ARE THE BOUND*;
- or the howe was broken into, and *LET THEM WALK* is cut on every wall.

**The world beyond.** Every region has an old road out to a march-stone on each side, where the folk say what the
country beyond is called. The first time the company stands there, that country is drawn from the world's seed and
from where it lies. It is installed in about 1.6 s and joined by the road across the march. It has its own land,
works and scenarios: no scenario is repeated from those already placed, and no work is named like one already drawn.
A world goes on as far as anyone walks.

**A world that goes on without the company** (finding 6, in part):

- *Emptied works are taken again.* Some nights after the company empties a work, the beasts of its kind of country
  move in. The nearest settlement brings its flocks in and shuts the gate on that road, and its innkeeper talks of
  it. Clearing the work again earns the shepherds' purse.
- *Weather.* The weather is a Markov chain on the day's die, and it lasts as long as the campaign does (before, it
  stopped after twelve days). The season turns on the calendar, and rivers rise the morning after rain.
- *The authored situations run on their own clocks.* In a country drawn late, those clocks count from when it was
  drawn.
  - Garth burns out the spiders himself if no one else does.
  - A child dies if no one brings herbs.
  - Sir Hamo's men hold up the road and tithe the market.
  - A lord's reeve takes a toll at the bridge. A company that attacks his toll-house is outlawed, and the inn bars
    its door to it.

### 3. Imagination and the look of it

Every work's text was written for it, as was what each kind of country looks like, how each people ended, and what
the folk say. Generated text is assembled from those pieces and never written at run time. A band is spoken of as
many (*"the lookouts stand their ground"*).

On the land map, names no longer write over one another: each tries four places beside its mark, or keeps its name
for hover. The page is headed with the region the company is in. A rumour echoes the words it was asked in, not a
name learned by asking.

### 4. Authoring and architecture (finding 9, in part)

- **The work plan format.**
  - A header (`@art`, `@kind`, `@ground`, `@near`, `@seen`, `@names`, `@danger`, `@great`, `@lair`).
  - Sections by group, with weights and conditions (`if fallplague`, `unless twin`).
  - Placeholders for the work, its makers, its kingdom, its treasure, its tellers and the site in sight of it.
  - Kyr's own `{refs}` are left alone.

  A new kind of work is a text file.
- **Installing modules is an ordinary operation in mid-campaign** (crossing the march). It is saved with the
  campaign. A module installed late is projected against the company as it stands, and its clock counts from when it
  arrived.
- **Errors that say what is wrong.** An unknown calling names the callings there are. A rule or observation that
  compares a `?variable` nothing in its condition binds is now an error. That mistake had kept dealers' purses from
  ever refilling.
- **The expedition player** plays a world end to end, and it is the quality-control tool this mission most needed.
  It found most of the defects fixed here (see *Quality*). It is kept with this mission's working notes, outside the
  repository, for now.

### 6. Performance (finding 10)

This was measured with the same script on the same four worlds, before the mission and at `2ad21b2`. The worlds are
a third larger now (1,153–1,309 entities then; 1,572–1,816 now).

| World | Load before → after | One screen before → after | An action before → after | Save |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| thorn | 1,631 → 1,720 ms | 164 → 86 ms | 51 → 28 ms | 1.4 → 1.6 MB |
| ash | 1,648 → 1,559 ms | 154 → 50 ms | 66 → 45 ms | 1.3 → 1.8 MB |
| a1 | 1,253 → 1,548 ms | 147 → 45 ms | 40 → 33 ms | 1.1 → 1.7 MB |
| b2 | 1,488 → 1,483 ms | 151 → 40 ms | 64 → 48 ms | 1.5 → 2.1 MB |

What made the difference:

- the solver used to match every fact of a relation for every query, copying as it went; it now uses the index;
- screens and commands stopped copying the whole world;
- creature facts are written as whole sets per member, not a fact for every item and ability against every member of
  every group: a million changes per load became fifty-seven thousand.

In the browser a kept game is gzipped: three regions keep in 290 KB, well within the browser's storage. The full test
suite, which plays many worlds, went from about 260 s to about 165 s. Each world's twelve expeditions now play in
about two minutes.

### 7. Publishing (finding 13)

Kyr is published at **https://jweissman.github.io/kyrie/**, and every push to `main` deploys it.

- *What was wrong.* The Pages workflow had failed on every push: it never checked out the `languages` package, and
  one of the tests it named no longer existed. Both are fixed (`languages` and `deem` are public now).

## What is still not good enough

1. **The works repeat.** Each plan has one layout. a1's two barrows are the same four rooms, and its two cisterns
   the same stair and hall, and b2's company climbed one tower four times under four names. What the later kingdom
   made of each, and what lives there, differ, but the second barrow is a walk through a place already known. This is
   the largest obstacle to a third and fourth evening. Each plan needs alternative layouts and mechanisms, and
   traps.
2. **The great works fall too easily, and danger does not rise.**
   - In a1 a first-level company drained the dam and killed the keeper of the mere on the fifth day.
   - In b2 the first galleries hold something of the same level, beside a first-level company.
   - Every great keeper is of level five, and the reckoning judged each one winnable for the company that met it.

   The end of a people's story should be the hardest thing in its country, and nothing should stand at the door of
   the first adventure that is as deadly as what stands at the last.
3. **Companions say nothing.** Recruits are a name, a people, a calling and one sentence about what brings them. The
   brief's companions with identity, who recognise, remark, object and want something of their own, were not built,
   and neither were its openings. Two of b2's three companions came about the same sink.
4. **There is no model of perception.** Observations appear whenever their condition holds:
   - nothing decides which detail a ranger notices that a warrior does not;
   - a scene's lines come back every time the company walks in, and a band's warning is given again each time the
     company comes back into its room;
   - a line read on a wall and the journal's record of it often say the same thing twice.
5. **The people's troubles repeat from world to world, by name.** There are seven authored scenarios and three kits.
   - Garth's ewes and Odile's silk are in b2's first region and in a1's second.
   - The drover who did not come home, the boy who took his father's spear, the sick child and the quarrel over the
     moved stones turn up in both worlds.

   Within a world nothing repeats, so by the third region the people's troubles run out, and the regions beyond are
   works without people's stories. The situations do not always track who did what: Garth pays the company eight
   coins for the fire he set himself.
6. **Names recur across worlds.** Rindwudu lies east of both a1 and b2, and Nocswigil is a barrow in both. The
   kingdom after the makers is Aergodríce in five of eight worlds, because it is drawn from eight names.
7. **The old script is the only key.** Every great work is found by reading its makers' story, and only a scholar or
   a glossary reads it. The town shrine's glossary was added after b2's first run showed a world where none existed;
   it is a patch, not a design. There is no sage to ask, no rubbing to carry home, and no old woman who remembers.
8. **Settlements differ only a little by size.** A town's purse is bigger, its priest can raise the dead, and its
   shrine keeps a glossary; that is all. There are no institutions, no households, and no trade between
   settlements.
9. **There is no strategic layer.** The renewal of emptied works is local and happens once. No power pursues an intent
   across a region. Sir Hamo's survivors do not move into the keep the company emptied, and no lord answers the
   killing of his toll-men except by barring the inn. The review's faction layer is still to do.
10. **Orsino's traps and wonders still do not reach the works.** Magic items are rare: the great treasures are junk
    and gems of great worth, not things to use.
11. **The map is one region at a time.** There is no map of the world across the marches.
12. **Waits are still felt.** Loading takes 1.5–1.7 s on a desk, and crossing a march pauses for about 1.6 s. A save
    grows by about 0.6 MB unpacked for each region.
13. **Small untruths remain.** Dealers trade at two in the morning. A rumour is of *"the ewes taken on a pit in the
    ground"*.
14. **The proof is a script.** It plays carefully, but it cannot be bored, delighted or lost. Its fights and choices
    are a careful player's, not a person's, and only Chromium has seen the published site at iPad size.

## Would an RPG player spend several evenings on this?

Yes, two or three evenings, if they like what old-school play offers:

- a region of about ten places to find and open;
- treasure that is the way to grow;
- dark that matters;
- the dead who walk if a seal-stone is moved;
- a companion raised for a night's vigil and three hundred coin;
- a history pieced together from stones, which leads somewhere hidden;
- a march-stone with another country beyond it.

The first evening is the strongest. The second has the history and the great work, and the third crosses the march.

More than that, not yet. By the fourth evening the same plan has come round too often in the same rooms, the
companions have never said a word, the great works have fallen too easily, and nothing in the world has acted against
the company of its own accord. The first of those is the cheapest to fix and matters most.
