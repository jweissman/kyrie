## Design Note for M23 — Beginning in the Dark

An emerging possibility for Westernesse's opening is to begin immediately in play, with a largely generated protagonist in a small, enclosed predicament.

Rather than first presenting a lengthy world-selection or character-building experience, we could begin somewhere almost completely dark: an oubliette, a deep ravine, the interior of a ruined arcology, or another plausible enclosure.

A brief passage establishes how the character came there. The initial affordances are extremely limited. Perhaps the first obvious action is to light a candle, torch, or lamp.

Illumination reveals the surroundings and makes further possibilities apparent.

The player subsequently encounters a physical or investigative obstacle, perhaps a mechanism or missing key; an occasion for danger or combat; something worth recovering; another person; and eventually a means of escape into the wider world.

Along the way, the player might establish aspects of the character's background, discover abilities, choose a specialization or perk, and acquire relationships. Ideally, these decisions arise naturally from the situations in which they become relevant.

**I am not commissioning this opening yet.** I would like you to think about its implications as a candidate direction for M23.

### Questions to consider

**1. Can we introduce the interface through perception?**

Could the available lenses and foreground actions arise naturally from what the character can currently perceive and do?

In darkness, we might show almost nothing. Lighting a lamp then reveals objects, ways, and inhabitants.

This should not mean that darkness abolishes hearing, touch, memory, or agency. The presentation must respect actual senses and capabilities, including darkvision and other nonstandard bodies.

Could the existing observation and focus architecture express this without special tutorial logic?

**2. Can character creation happen during play?**

What is the minimum identity and mechanical completeness required to begin a valid campaign?

Could subsequent choices establish additional background facts, specializations, relationships, or capabilities as durable world commitments?

How might those choices remain compatible with a generated character, the existing world, and SRD rules?

Preserve the option to begin with a fully specified character.

**3. Can several introductions share a dramatic structure without becoming identical?**

Consider three possibilities: the oubliette, the ravine, and the arcology.

Their common shape might be isolation, perception, investigation, danger, discovery, and emergence.

But their actual circumstances, mechanisms, choices, and consequences should differ substantially.

What could Kyr author? What could Deem vary or place? What would require additional shared capabilities?

Avoid creating an elaborate procedural introduction framework simply for its own sake.

**4. Can the opening be a real part of the world?**

The enclosure should have an existing purpose and history. Its inhabitants should have reasons for being there. Anything discovered or changed should persist.

A player should be able to return later or encounter consequences of the opening.

The encounter with danger need not require killing an opponent. Alternative approaches should be meaningful where the circumstances support them.

**5. Could we perfect the opening in very small cinematic increments?**

Consider developing the experience beat by beat:

- The first account of the predicament.
- The first meaningful action.
- The first change in perception.
- The first investigation.
- The first consequential obstacle.
- The first danger, reward, and relationship.
- The revelation of the wider world.

At each step, consider what the engine must support, what the player should understand, and what emotional effect the presentation should achieve.

The goal would not be to spend an entire mission implementing a tutorial sequence, but to perfect a small section of actual play while strengthening the reusable capabilities beneath it.

### Requested response

When appropriate, provide a short design assessment:

- Which existing Kyr, Deem, perception, character, and presentation capabilities would already support this approach?
- What genuine architectural or authorability gaps would it expose?
- Which aspects seem artistically promising, and which risk becoming a conventional tutorial dungeon?
- What would be the smallest credible vertical slice with which to test the idea?

Please do not begin implementing it, create a large new register, or interrupt the current M22 plan.

Treat this as an artistic and architectural hypothesis for future consideration, not an approved replacement for the opening.

**The aspiration is that the player enters Westernesse by doing something meaningful, discovers the world through attention and action, and gradually becomes a particular person within it.**
