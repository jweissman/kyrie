# Spatial storyboard: the Hall, the old stones, and the Empire's posting-house

*11 November 2026. Written before the rooms were (the method is the deliverable: see the last section), then revised against what the playtests found. Status of every room: **built** means written in Kyr, compiled in every seed the audit checks and walked through the page's own choices in a spec; **drawn** means it has a painting that matches it; **designed** means only described here.*

Three places, one dramatic geography. The Hall (a lived-in great house, a walk from the first town) and the old stones (the first makers' ruin on the hill above it) already stood; the posting-house is new, an Aenean road station a day or so out on the old paved road. They share a spine: **a road nobody owns, a hill nobody understands, and a house that stands between them with a chain across its own avenue.**

| **Muniment room** | The house's memory, unread for forty years. Dry, windowless, dark: needs light, and a key, a pick or a crowbar | `scene-library` via kind word `muniment` (a muniment-room painting is wanted) | open it three ways (key / pick / force), each with a different standing in the house; read the grant (needs old-script lore), or have the steward read it | built, drawn (approximately) |

**What the Hall asks of a visitor that a dungeon does not:** to be seen. The steward notices a stolen thing (the existing silver music box) and shuts both doors; the kitchen door is not shut, and the cook has something to say about that. Forcing the press is noticed. Everything the company takes from this house, it takes in front of someone.

## 2. The old stones (ancient ruin) — `outskirts.kyrt` + `outskirts-deep.kyrt`

| Room | Dramatic function | Visual | Ways to engage | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hill-path, judgement hall, tree | Orientation, the view (the nearest of the country's works, shown from the hill), the dais letters, the rag-tree and its bronze bracelet | `scene-ruins-hill`, `scene-ruins-tribunal`, `scene-ancient-tree` | look, read (lore), tie a rag, search the roots | built before this work, drawn |
| Dome | The folk's story: pale lights after dark (or only owls; drawn per country). Where Wimble went | `scene-ruins-cenotaph` | go by day; go by night; search the graves and find the moved slab | built, drawn |
| **Undercroft** | What the dome was the roof of: a ring of niches, a bronze chime with its tongue tied, and a ring of dust where the least of the dead's offerings stood | `scene-undercroft-dark` | enter by the slab stair (strength or a crowbar), by the tunnel from the Hall cellar, or not at all; meet what lives there (one of three, drawn: a keeper of the bounds, bats, or nothing); take the cup, or strike the chime, or both; put the cup back | built, drawn |

**Three ways to meet the undercroft, and two things you can do with the cup.** The *keeper of the bounds* (a revenant) guards the room as a courtesy to the dead; the *bats* are only a nuisance the first time; *nothing* is a different story: the room is silent and the print of one small foot is in the dust, and the boy's tale is the only account of what happened. In every case the cup is gone from its niche; setting it back mends Wimble (his humming stops and the cook's face changes). Taking it away leaves the boy as he is and lets someone else be the thief.

**Four ways in, by what a person is:** the *brawny* heave the gate; the *climber* takes the loft; the *clever* find the culvert by searching at the milestone; and where someone lives in the house, the *persuasive* are let through (the road-wardens can be talked past: "likely · one try"). **Three ways to the strong-room, each leaving a different trace:** the seal (the Empire's own way; nothing is broken), the pick, the crowbar. And a fourth, the heating-void, which closes if the cistern is drained.

## 4. Encounter density, and what is a balance problem rather than a design one

Not more monsters: more *situations*. What changed in the rooms above (all through existing rules):

- **Territorial vs peaceable vs wary occupants, by draw.** Posting-house: carters (peaceable, talk), wardens (road-robbers, can be talked past: "likely · one try"), boars (territorial in the granary), wolves (in the loft; come down at dusk), the lamp-keeper (peaceable, knowledgeable, resents the loft) or a copyist (peaceable, recruitable).
- **Terrain that matters.** The granary's raised floor, the stable's loft, the cistern's steps, the void: positions that open or close. (The combat system has no battle grid, so these are routes and doors rather than cover; see below.)
- **Retreat is real.** Every occupied room is one door from the yard.
- **Alternatives to combat.** The wardens can be paid or persuaded; the carters are friendly; the keeper of the bounds can be left alone by not taking the cup, or met by taking it.

**Generic balance observations for M20 (not scenario design):**
1. *Rats and bats as "encounters".* In the cellar and the undercroft they are fights the company wins by attrition at level 1; they teach nothing about the room. A hazard (a loose floor, a dropped lantern, the heating-void's soot) would be a better first danger than a swarm.
2. *The auto-fight is one button.* A scene with three kinds of danger plays as three presses of Attack. The tactical difference between the keeper, the bats and the boars lives in the text, not the resolution.
3. *Light.* Four of the new rooms are dark by design (cellar, muniments, undercroft, strong-room, stokehold, cistern). On a fresh character one torch is an hour of play (M19-170); the stone-and-key route through the Hall needs two lights. Torches are cheap in the town shops, but the page does not say so when the player first stands in the dark room.

## 5. A storyboard-first method for the next image-led adventure

1. **Choose the dramatic question for the place** ("who owns a road?", "what happened to the boy?", "why did the stage close?"), not the monsters.
2. **List the rooms by what each one *does* to the player's knowledge or position** (orient, mislead, reveal, cost, reward, return). A room that does none of these is deleted.
3. **Fix the spine:** one route anyone can take, and at least two that depend on what the player is, has, or knows. The routes cross at least once.
4. **Write each room's one-line image first, as a painting brief** (what is in the foreground, what is lit, what state it is in: *light and state matter more than subject*, the 10 November commission list). Check the existing registry (`doc/paintings/registry.md`) for a painting that already matches before commissioning.
5. **Then write the Kyr.** Each room's `Observe` is that image in prose; each `Thing` is something in the image that can change; each person wants something that is not the player.
6. **Test by walking the routes** with the page's own labels (`test/outskirts-deep.test.ts`, `test/roadstation.test.ts`), then by a route that skips the lead and one that refuses the favour.

## 6. Painting commissions this work leaves

*In priority order; none is needed for the places to play.*
1. A **milestone with a dish on top, on a straight kerbed road** (the posting-house's approach, 3 coins in the water). The current approach shows a generic wood.
2. A **wine-cellar with a bricked-up bay of older stone** (the Hall's cellar; also reusable for any cellar with a wall that is not like the others).
3. A **muniment room**: presses and tagged chests, a fan of papers under a green paperweight.
4. A **cobbled courtyard ringed by a single range of low buildings** (the posting-house's yard; any inn-yard or abbey garth).
5. A **ring of burial niches round a chime** (the undercroft).
