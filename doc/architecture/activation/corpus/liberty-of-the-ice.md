# The Liberty of the Ice (Pellam)

*Corpus review. Read in full: `quarle`, `ice` (printer), the endings, the placed rules and role list; the rest by structure.*

**Premise.** The Lisk has frozen for the first time in thirty-one winters and a Frost Fair is held on it under the ice's own law: a lie is no crime, a handshake binds. The company has three days before the thaw, and everything shaken on the ice is "landed" and holds for ever once the river goes out.

**What works.** A legal fiction turned into a map: every deal is a thing you can hold, melt or land, and eleven endings are what the company let go of. The cast is large and well turned (Ursel the professional liar, Callow the preacher charged with unswearing, Quarle with his tub, Gant on the ice to escape a writ).

**Shared forms.** Comic brevity ("Observe!") is Quarle's own and does not recur. "He looks at you." as a beat shows three times; I did not read enough of Harker and Noll to say whether they share a cadence.

**Weak or thin.** Quarle had one topic but four actions of consequence (buy, expose, ice, cream) and no conversation about them. Twenty-nine people hold one or two topics each. No link out to any other adventure.

**Danger and density.** Wolves on the downs, wharf men with cudgels, bailiffs; the real risk is losing a bargain to the thaw.

**Connections.** First region: the rumour only; no after. Others: none.

**Done.** Quarle got three more topics that answer his three states (share-holder, ruined, frost-cream); `lore.kyr` ties Noll's printing booth to the almanack woman's ferryman (the Wray); the `after` is wired by ending (sold / unsworn / fled / landed), so the first region speaks differently of the Fair depending on what was shaken.

**Left.** A scene for the Fair's second night to differ from its first; a third thing for Meg and Bel to do.
