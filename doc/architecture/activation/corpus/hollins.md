# Behind the Wainscot (Hollins)

*Corpus review. Sampled, not read whole: `bedroom`, `kitchen`, `lady`, `library`, `hall`, `bootscrape`, `pye`, the placed rules and the simile census.*

**Premise.** A giantess has come home to die in a house shut for three hundred years; her companion wants a ratcatcher. The company goes in by the Slot at Bootscrape, and finds a society of small folk living in the walls who never knew they were kept.

**What works.** The scale is held with unusual discipline (the Lady's eye in the window, a sleigh bell as a rung bucket, the chart as a field) and the Lady is the best antagonist-who-is-not-one in the set: twenty topics, and she dies on a clock. The Wainscot's politics (Pinmen, Regiment, Jarmen) are each distinct.

**Shared forms.** "As big as" was the book's tic: 34 uses, every other simile a size. I replaced thirteen with different figures (a room's worth of silence for the bell, a family you could lose in an acanthus leaf, a wine-vat you could drown in); the browser spec's "glass as big as a millpond" is untouched. The remaining 21 are mostly short and doing real work. Nan, Mrs Pyecroft and the Lady each open too many speeches on a look (about eight), worth a pass.

**Weak or thin.** Bootscrape is four people and an inn for a village built on a doorstep, which should be the point of the title. The ratcatcher job at the mat is a line, not a trade.

**Danger and density.** Rats, the Bishop (an 18-foot cat), the hazards in the cellar: a fair share for a story about trespass.

**Connections.** First region: the carters' notice in `countries.json`, plus the `after`. Other adventures: none until now. I added `lore.kyr` (Widow Arkle on the almanack woman who asked the carrier the time) so Hollins joins the Cattermole line to Merrowdale.

**Left.** Bootscrape's ratcatching as a thing the company can actually do for pay at the mat before going in.
