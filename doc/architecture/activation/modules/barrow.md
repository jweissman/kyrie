# Module 3: The Bound Howe (a dangerous expedition)

*11 November 2026. Branch `adventure/fs-expedition`. Written before the Kyr (storyboard first: image, then dramatic function, then mechanism), revised against what the build and the playing found; the last section says what is proved and what is not.*

**One line.** A king's howe on a wind-scoured ridge, half-robbed already: a salvage crew in the lee of the hill dig for its gold; a warden keeps the dead's rest with a hearth and a tally-stick; the dead keep their law in three halls; and beneath the Barrow-Kin's floor something older than the Kin lies in the dark, which the Kin were put there to keep.

**Intended party and what it can do.** Three or four, **levels 3 to 5** (a prepared company of four at level 4). It stands in the starting country's *deep* end: beyond the dens, the towers and the Hall's cellar. A level-1 company can reach the ridge, bargain with the salvagers, hear the warden and read the survey stone, and should leave the howe alone; a level-2 company can fight the lookouts but not the household. The howe is not a gauntlet: every danger is foreshadowed in the outer works, can be met by preparation (rope, salt, oil, barley, a torch more than you think, silver or holy water, a pole), and the way out is always the way in. The *price* of the prize is moral, not lethal: see the torc.

**What it is not.** Nothing here is a copy of any published trap. The mechanisms are the Barrow-Kin's own: they believed the dead keep the bounds of the land (`arts.json`, art `bone`), spoke to their dead through flutes of bone, and laid kings with their horses. Every law of the howe is a consequence of that belief.

## 5. Resource pressure

- **Light.** A torch burns an hour (M19-170). The howe has no sun; the flutes' draught gutters a flame; the foul drift burns a candle blue. A company that comes with one torch comes out in the dark. Oil, a lantern, a spare.
- **Cold and wounds.** The ridge is cold; the grey grass takes warmth; there is no healer below the camp.
- **Time.** The dead walk at night if the howe is open. The company decides when to go in, and when to be out.
- **Carry.** The torc and the other metal are heavy. A company that carries all of it moves at half pace.
- **Supplies.** Rope (two coils: shaft and steps), oil, salt, barley, rations, a pole, spikes, chalk, a candle.
- **Unsafe rests.** The forecourt and the riders' bench are not rests; the harness-store is quiet; the warden's hearth and the camp (if the captain tolerates the company) are safe.

## 6. Approaches (none is the only one)

1. **The front:** up the steep path, past the lookout, round the ring, through the seal-stone and the gallery.
2. **The bargain:** pay or work for the salvagers; take the winch to the shaft; arrive in the middle.
3. **The quiet way:** the sheep-way, over the trip-cans, round the ring, rite at the seal.
4. **The warden's way:** salt, the greeting, the tune; she is the only one who knows the roll; she gives the tally-stick.
5. **The back:** the sump and the first folk's stair up to the room behind the seat, hard going, a shortcut that stays open.
6. **Retreat and return:** the harness-store door bars from within; the steps up are a place the king does not follow below; the shortcut up from under; the seal can be heaved back. Death is a fall back to the nearest town; the howe keeps every change.

## 7. Persistent consequences (outside the site)

1. **The levy.** The torc (or a lesser one) taken to the clerk in the nearest town: the levy is met, a clerk's notice is struck, and the company is paid by the weight. The king's torc is the expensive answer: the bound breaks.
2. **The bound.** Broken, the household and the last walk each night along the bound to the country: the town's green is visited at night, the priest finds the churchyard stirred, and the nearest town says so. Kept, the clerk says the countryside is quiet.
3. **The salvagers.** Dead, bought, or turned: if the captain is bought she goes to the town's taproom and sells what she has; if killed, the crew scatter and the clerk loses his licence-holder; if turned (the company sides with the warden), the camp breaks and the winch is given.
4. **The warden.** Alive on the ridge with the howe sealed: the shrine's priest thanks the company with a blessing (holy water) and the clerk is told. Gone with it broken: her hearth cold.
5. **The survey stone.** What it says (a wheel with a wing, cut over the Kin's marks, and a bench-mark that fits no measure in use) is known thereafter; the first town's companions notice the pattern (this is the campaign-seeds motif and is not resolved).
6. **A companion.** If Wystan Hale (the Hall's keeper, once the castellan's spearman) is with the company, he knows the clerk's colours and takes the sealed howe as a debt paid: he says so at the forecourt.

## 8. Variation by country (Deem: tables.json, templates.json)

Identity is fixed: the same twenty-six places, the same laws, the same prize. What a seed changes:

- the dead king's **name and epithet** (labels) and the captain's, the warden's and the clerk's names (casting);
- **which of three flutes** is the king's (fact), and the stave that tells it;
- **the fate**: the Kin sealed themselves in (the room behind the seat holds the last, who keep the bound), or strangers broke the stone before (the room is empty, the dead already walk by night, and the torc is all that holds the king);
- the **weather of the ridge** by terrain (mist in the marsh, snow in the frost, dust in the desert, rain in the forest);
- **how the water lies** in the sump (drained once, or never).

## 9. How to run it

Reachable from the starting town by the page's own labels: the nearest town's people speak of the ridge, the camp and the clerk's levy; the site is one of the country's sites on the map (a barrow-kind site generated for the scenario); set out from the town or the road to its foot. Verified in `test/bound-howe.test.ts` for 6 seeds; played, in `doc/architecture/activation/transcripts/bound-howe.txt`.
