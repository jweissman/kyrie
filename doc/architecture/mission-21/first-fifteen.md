# The first fifteen minutes, deepened (the owner, 14 November)

Seven properties the first fifteen minutes should show. Each is an acceptance property of the laboratory (the first
town's undertown, `mission-21/laboratory/`), proved there by a walk the runner takes and a spec that would fail
without it. None of them asks for a new system. Each is the smallest use of machinery the game already has, placed
where a first player meets it.

| Id | Property | What already exists | The smallest proof in the laboratory |
|---|---|---|---|
| FF-01 | **Time and absence matter** | The Bound Howe's week moves whether or not the company comes; Rules on the clock | Something in the undertown or above it moves on without the company: the rats take the second store by the second night; the merchant sells the key on if the job is not taken by the third day. A player who leaves and returns finds it changed, and is told why in one sentence |
| FF-02 | **Knowledge enables better decisions** | Lore that reads a ward (the cold playtest's best moment); signs noticed by a skill; leads | A fact learned above (the innkeeper on the drain, the old notice) opens a better way below: a safer way in, the big rat's habit, the centipede's warmth. The walk that knows it fares measurably better than the one that does not |
| FF-03 | **Retreat can be satisfying** | Flee; the reckoning; BP-04 (warnings kept) on the rules line | Going back up is an act with a result: the company keeps what it learned (the danger is named on return), the threat stays where it was, and a second descent is better prepared. Retreat is never a loss screen |
| FF-04 | **Two materially different solutions genuinely work** | The lock picked or forced; the hasp wrenched and noticed (BP-06) | Two routes to the job's end with different costs and consequences: by force (the hasp, the merchant's anger, a fight with the big rat) and by craft (the key, the drain, the rats led off by bait). Both are paid, and the world remembers which |
| FF-05 | **NPC actions stay legible** | The signal before malice (the silent lord's); barks tied to the moment | Whatever a person does in the first quarter-hour is announced by something seen first: the merchant checks the hasp before blaming you; the rat-catcher is seen setting traps before the traps are found. No act without a visible cause |
| FF-06 | **Consequences create the next undertaking** | The ring of letters; aims from facts | What the first job leaves (the torn hasp, the undercroft found, a debt paid or not) becomes the next aim in the world's own words, not a list's. One undertaking leads into the next |
| FF-07 | **A dead character becomes part of the continuing country** | `Memorials`: a cairn heaped where a company fell; CO-06 (begin again in this country) | The next character meets the first one's death as a fact of the country: the cairn, their pack in the undercroft where they fell, the merchant who remembers them, the debt still owed. Their unfinished job is a lead |

**Order:** FF-04 and FF-06 first, because they make the laboratory's sequence whole. Then FF-02 and FF-03, which make
going down twice better than going down once. Then FF-05, FF-01 and FF-07.

**Owners:**
- The playability line owns the laboratory and these properties.
- The rules line supplies FF-03's warning and retreat (BP-04) and the reckoning's confidence (BP-01).
- The lead keeps the engine pieces general: whatever is built for one is authorable for any situation.

**Holding them:**
- Each property gets a spec on the laboratory (`test/small/laboratory-*`, about 1 s a start) and a walk in the matrix
  that shows it.
- The prose stays under the clause-load ratchet (§36).
