# Interaction research: the first touch and the fundamental action (the owner, 15 November)

A bounded, independent investigation within the aesthetics line, meant to inform current decisions and give M22 a
better starting point. The owner's current thinking is that M22 may be maximalist about polish but extraordinarily
narrow in scope: make the first touch magnificent, and make the fundamental action extraordinarily satisfying. The
investigation tests whether that is the right focus, what its principles should be, and what the implementation must
support. It does not displace register repair, performance, or the aesthetic changes being landed.

**The two subjects:**
- **The first touch:** the continuous experience from the studio stab through the title, character creation and the
  first few interactions.
- **The fundamental action:** the repeated experience of focusing, considering, pressing or holding, committing,
  resolving and continuing.

**Questions:**
- What makes choosing an action pleasurable rather than administrative?
- What are the best patterns for focus, hover, preview, confirmation, cancellation and transitions?
- How can input respond instantly while the scene reacts more deliberately?
- How should a meaningful long-press commitment work, for acts that spend a daily Energy budget? Which acts commit at
  once, and which invite deliberate commitment?
- How can repetitive animation and confirmation friction be avoided over hundreds of interactions?
- How can the opening feel like one continuous work rather than a succession of web screens?
- What can creation do to establish agency and identity from the first choice?
- What common semantics can serve title, creation, exploration, combat, conversation, backstage and exceptional
  devices without forcing one layout?
- What must the browser, the terminal and an eventual controller share, and what belongs to each?

**To assess:**
- timing, focus stability, touch, and keyboard or controller navigation;
- reduced motion, accessibility and narrow viewports;
- duplicate or accidental commands;
- a candid review of the deployed opening: which moments are ugly, sluggish, awkward, confusing or inconsistent, and
  where it feels like a website.

**Outputs:**
1. **A small comparative study** of a few genuinely relevant exemplars: controller-first games, interaction-heavy
   RPGs, excellent menu systems, restrained interactive fiction, and satisfying selection and confirmation feedback.
   Study behaviour, not screenshots. Name the specific techniques and what might transfer, and keep established
   practice, aesthetic judgment and hypotheses still to test apart.
2. **A common interaction grammar:** states, transitions, input behaviour, and their relation to authoritative
   commands. The engine keeps legality, cost and effects. No new browser gameplay controller.
3. **A working experiment:** one or two competing versions of the principal action control inside the game,
   demonstrating focus, press, optional hold-to-commit, response and continuation. Test it over many repetitions,
   and record the whole opening and repeated use of the control, unedited.

Where an architectural problem shows, name it precisely; start no large rewrite. This is not a feature list or a
redesign of every screen. The central question: can entering Westernesse and choosing an intention feel like
operating a distinctive, exceptionally well-designed game, rather than a sophisticated web application?

**An input from the design review `history/reviews/design-pull-and-astonishment-2026-11-13.md`** (§7 and finding G):
exceptional moments should take an exceptional form. A small dramatic vocabulary (ordinary, speech, revelation,
reckoning, loss) would govern composition and pacing:
- unrelated controls suppressed;
- the person, object or place kept;
- a deliberate continuation required;
- the next lead's promotion delayed, so a death or a homecoming is not overwritten by the next job.

This bears directly on the fundamental action: when an act resolves at once, and when the game slows and asks for a
deliberate continuation. Take it as an input to the grammar, not a separate deliverable.
