# The owner on a place worth returning to, and earned instruments (14 November, night)

One focused playability and aesthetic experiment, kept as given. Register §31 (RI-01 to RI-05). It serves M21's
central objectives under the doctrine (`doc/design/DOCTRINE.md`); it is not a new exploration engine or widget system.

> **The larger goal is for Westernesse to reward repeated attention to places, and for a character's growing
> capabilities to change what they can perceive there.**

## A place worth returning to

> Please investigate how existing Kyr/Deem authoring facilities support an explicitly explorable location with
> repeatable, time-consuming investigation and context-dependent encounter or discovery tables. Use a real existing
> wilderness or other suitable location. A visit should be able to produce meaningful discoveries, inhabitants,
> events, or dangers. Subsequent visits should reflect what has already been established, the passage of time,
> environmental circumstances, and character knowledge or capabilities. Preserve durable discoveries. Do not create
> infinitely resettable treasures, arbitrary encounter farming, or generic repeatable quests. Distinguish Look,
> Examine, Search, and sustained Explore. An ordinary scene shouldn't require a dungeon entrance or an authored golden
> undertaking before the player can find something worth doing. Ensure interesting sites can actually be reached and
> revisited through reliable spatial navigation.

## A restrained instrument experiment

> Explore a small family of elegant, optionally visible observation instruments, especially on slow surfaces such as
> Look, preparation, camp, and Backstage. Candidates include a celestial-sign glyph and moon phase, party condition,
> expedition resources, local compass, field catalogue, and progress toward a non-quest activity. The principles are:
> - **Earned:** A character's knowledge, experience, equipment, or situation determines what they can legitimately
>   perceive.
> - **Contextual:** Information appears when relevant, without permanently cluttering the resting scene.
> - **Optional:** Players can inspect or pin useful instruments, with a very small default visibility budget.
> - **Authoritative:** The underlying values and knowledge permissions come from Kyrie, not from browser-specific
>   estimates or hardcoded Westernesse widgets.
> - **Honest:** An instrument should not display a numerical precision or completion percentage the character could
>   not reasonably know.
>
> Please prototype only a few contrasting instruments rather than building a comprehensive dashboard framework.

## Connect both experiments to the existing interaction architecture

> The terminal and browser must agree semantically about available exploration actions, action outcomes, learned
> information, and instrument values. Retain the four-promoted-choice convention and the existing action vocabulary;
> make further exploration accessible through lenses and expanded actions. The atlas's semantic colors should continue
> to indicate actual opportunities. Explorable location markers, compass arrows, and widget categories should not
> create accidental new atlas semantics. Use a small Kyr fixture and terminal walks to test repeat exploration,
> persistent discoveries, conditional encounters, skill-sensitive observations, and concise narration. Complement this
> with actual browser review of the visual treatment.

## What would constitute a useful result

> I'd love to see one existing place where the first visit is interesting, subsequent visits remain meaningful without
> simply resetting the scene, and two different characters discover somewhat different possibilities. I'd also like a
> beautiful but restrained example of an earned instrument appearing when it becomes relevant, without adding
> persistent clutter. This is a bounded experiment supporting M21's central objectives, not a commission to build a
> whole new exploration engine or universal widget system.
>
> **The desired experience is that knowing more about the world gives the player more interesting ways to inhabit
> it.**
