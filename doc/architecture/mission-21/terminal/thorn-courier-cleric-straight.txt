
the causeway into Misthythe · 18 Deepwinter, morning · cold
The causeway runs on its piles across the last of the fen, and at the end of it is Misthythe: low roofs against the sky, masts at the landing, eel-traps stacked against the walls, smoke going straight up from the chimneys.
The scroll-case has not been off your shoulder in eleven days: a letter under the seal of a magistrate who did not give his name, for Lindal at Merscstede and to be put into no other hands.
A cart stands canted at the side of the road, one wheel down in the ditch to the hub, the horse blowing in the shafts, a sack of meal burst on the bed.
Aim: Carry the sealed letter to Lindal at Merscstede, into no other hands.

‘A share,’ says the wagoner, and looks at you a long moment. ‘A sack, then, and a coin, and be quick about it.’ Between you and the horse the cart comes up out of the ditch. He counts the coin into your hand as though each one hurt him. ‘You’ll go far,’ he says. He does not say it kindly.
The cart stands square on the road again, its load roped down, the horse quiet.

Boats lie drawn up keel to keel on the shingle at Misthythe, and the houses come right down to them, nets hung on every fence.
Magot is crying wares to nobody in particular: pots mended, knives ground, news for nothing.
Patience ambles to the end of her rope to see who you are, and goes through your hands for honey.
