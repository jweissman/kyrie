
the road into Newmeet · 25 Sowing, morning · foggy
The road comes out from under the trees, and there is Newmeet in its clearing: the market cross and the roofs of the stalls, reed drying in ricks, smoke from a dozen chimneys.
You have come over the hills into Acornweald with your trade and not much else.
A cart stands canted at the side of the road, one wheel down in the ditch to the hub, the horse blowing in the shafts, a sack of meal burst on the bed.
Aim: A child at the market-place of Newmeet has a fever; it needs herbs, or willow bark from where willows grow by water.

the shrine of Lugh · Day 1, 9:45 a.m. · foggy
A long stone under a roof of shingles: the well-kept altar of Lugh, God of Storms. Drovers leave a stone before it when they go up into the hills, and take one away when they come down. Offerings of reed lie before it. At the back of the shrine, under the lamp, a young man in the grey of the house is copying a column of figures out of the offering-book with a ruler, tutting.
A stair goes up inside the tower wall, worn hollow in the middle of every step, to where the bell hangs.
A man lies on the shrine’s bier under a grey cloth, his boots still on. A wood-warden’s mark is tied at his belt.
The offering-box by the lamp is shut, its lock whole, and light in the hand: the little bronze things that were left in it have gone, and the coppers have not.
Here are Carmine, the priestess of Lugh; and Brother Anselm Tarr, the priest’s acolyte, who has read everything the shrine owns and wants to know what it leaves out.
Aim: Lay the dead in the crypt under the churchyard at Newmeet, and find out who raised them.
