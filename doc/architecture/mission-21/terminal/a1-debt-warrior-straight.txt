
Mik's salt-house · 25 Haysel, morning · humid
Salt in sacks stacked to the rafters, barrels of fish in brine along the wall, and a smell of tar and the sea that gets into everything.
You owe Mik fifty coin, for a cart of salt that went into the river at the ford last spring, oxen and all.
‘Wynn,’ says Mik, and does not get up.

‘They’ve got into the old corn-store from underneath,’ says Mik. ‘Rats, a whole nest of them, and bold with it. I’ve lost a third of the grain.’ A key comes off a nail behind the counter. ‘There’s a trapdoor in the floor. Kill them, and find where they’re getting in, or they’ll be back by harvest. Ten coin, and I’ll see you right for a lantern besides.’ And, looking you over: ‘Or lay ratsbane where they come up, if you’d sooner not be bitten. It wants a night.’

Boats lie drawn up keel to keel on the shingle at Lowmouth, and the houses come right down to them, nets hung on every fence.
Lambert is crying wares to nobody in particular: pots mended, knives ground, news for nothing.
Patience ambles to the end of her rope to see who you are, and goes through your hands for honey.
