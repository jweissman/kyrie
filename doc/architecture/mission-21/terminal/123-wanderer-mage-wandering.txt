the taproom of the Green Phoenix · Day 1, 8:19 a.m. · cloudy
A long room with a fire at either end and talk going on all down it, under hams hung from the beams. The boards are tacky with spilled ale. Reed-cutters sit at the long table, their hands cut and bound.
The dead man kept the count of oak cut behind the Hall’s chain, and who bought it.
Piers the drover is at the window, counting the cattle in the pens before the road, and not liking the count.
Jankin the carrier is at the table with his coat on, eating fast: the horse is put to, and the road is waiting.
Rows of notches: so many oaks, by girth, felled in the road-wood and carried by night to a buyer. At the head of each row is one mark, a pierced circle: the same on every row. The merchant’s trade-mark, as the sheds know it.
Here are Carmine, the innkeeper; Dannel Hobb, a boy of ten with a white streak in his hair, kept indoors; Isaure Venn, a scholar of the old script, who has been turned back from the hill twice; Osmund Pyle, an old man with a stick and a bad hip, who means to walk to the place his wife used to leave a coin, and has been told by everyone that he will not get there; Cillian, a factor of the Founders’ Exchange, who buys old bronze; the factor’s hands; and Imogen, the factor’s carter, who takes the lots south.
